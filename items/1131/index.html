WeMM-Embedding: WeChat Multi-Modal Embedding Technical Report

📰 Latest · 2026-08-27 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-27

Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-08-25
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24053

Tencent released open-weight 2B/4B/9B universal multimodal embedding models built on Qwen3.5 that claim a new MMEB-v2 SOTA of 80.6, with the 2B matching the best 8B open baseline — useful for self-hosters, but the headline margins are 0.1-0.4 points over leaderboard-reported and closed-source competitors.

Executive summary

WeMM-Embedding is a family of three universal multimodal embedding models (2B, 4B, 9B) from WeChat/Tencent, built on natively multimodal Qwen3.5 backbones, embedding text, images, video, visual documents and interleaved inputs into one space with Matryoshka-truncatable dimensions. Training is two-stage: large-scale contrastive alignment on several hundred million pairs, then a curated stage about a tenth the size adding reranker-derived fine-grained relevance supervision and cross-scale distillation, where a frozen 9B teacher supervises the 2B and 4B students. Reported MMEB-v2 averages are 77.9 / 79.2 / 80.6. The practitioner caveats are real: baseline numbers are copied from the MMEB leaderboard, the nearest rivals are closed-source or proprietary submissions, the winning margins are fractions of a point, no training hyperparameters or hardware are disclosed, and 14 production A/B tests are reported without a single percentage.

Problem and positioning

The paper targets a single embedding space covering text, images, video, visual documents and interleaved inputs, motivated by retrieval, recommendation, classification and agentic use inside WeChat. The pitch is a 'universal' encoder rather than modality-specific towers, with a size ladder so the same recipe serves both a cheap 2B online model and a 9B quality ceiling.

  • Three sizes: 2B, 4B, 9B, described as built on 'the corresponding natively multimodal Qwen3.5 backbones' (Section 3).
  • Authors: Junjie Zhou, Ke Mei, Lei Li, Tianyi Wang, Fengyun Rao, Jing Lyu. arXiv cs.CV (also cs.CL, cs.IR), submitted 25 Aug 2026.
  • The open-weight claim lives only in the abstract and the links (a Hugging Face collection under tencent/wemm-embedding, github.com/Tencent/WeMM-Embedding). No numbered section states which checkpoints ship, and no license is named in the paper text.

Architecture (what you actually load)

Section 3.1: 'WeMM-Embedding is built upon the Qwen3.5 architecture.' Pooling is last-token via a dedicated appended <embedding> token whose final-layer hidden state is L2-normalized; because attention is causal, multiple such tokens can be inserted at different positions to get several representations from one forward pass. Output dimensionality is elastic through Matryoshka Representation Learning: take a prefix of length d and re-normalize.

  • Last-token pooling with a dedicated <embedding> token, L2-normalized.
  • MRL prefix truncation; concrete dims analysed run 64 to 2048.
  • At 256 dims the 2B keeps 98.7% of its 2048-dim image and video score; 512 dims gives 99.2% (image) / 98.8% (video). Visual-document tasks degrade fastest.
  • No audio encoder: the models 'do not support audio input' (Section 4.1.2), which is why every MMEB-v3 audio column is 0.0.

Training data and the two-stage recipe

Stage 1 is large-scale contrastive alignment over 'several hundred million source-target pairs across diverse modalities, domains, and tasks' drawn from 'public datasets and web-scale weakly supervised sources', 'further supplemented with task-oriented synthetic data and in-house collections'. No individual public dataset is named on the training side and there are no per-source counts — a provenance gap if you care about contamination against MMEB. Stage 2 uses a curated dataset roughly a tenth the size, built by semantic-ID resampling (a three-level residual k-means quantizer with density-based down-weighting of frequent codes), then an MLLM pass that filters mismatches and corrects factual inconsistencies.

  • Hard negatives: for text targets an MLLM generates plausible-but-wrong candidates; for image/video targets, intermediate WeMM-Embedding checkpoints retrieve semantically similar candidates. False positives are suppressed by duplicate-aware masking plus task-consistent batching.
  • Fine-grained relevance: Stage 1 uses human discrete relevance levels with a score-gap-weighted CoSENT-style objective; Stage 2 swaps human labels for scores from dedicated multimodal rerankers, compared only within a query.
  • Cross-scale knowledge transfer: the frozen 9B is the embedding teacher for the 2B and 4B, distilling online soft targets from batch-wise source-target similarity distributions via a bidirectional KL over row-wise softmaxes.
  • The 9B has no larger teacher, so it instead trains multiple specialized Stage-2 variants on complementary data mixtures and combines them via TIES-style model merging. Number of variants and merge weights are not disclosed.
  • Reproducibility gap: no batch size, learning rate, epoch count, optimizer, numeric temperatures, or GPU/hardware details appear anywhere in the paper.

Results, and how much to trust them

MMEB-v2 (78 datasets: 36 image, 18 video, 24 visual document) averages are 77.9 (2B), 79.2 (4B), 80.6 (9B). The paper states Table 1 baselines are 'taken from the official MMEB leaderboard' as of August 24, 2026 — vendor/leaderboard-reported, not re-run by the authors. That matters because the headline claims are thin: WeMM 2B at 77.9 versus Qwen3-VL-Embedding 8B at 77.8 is a 0.1-point 'beats the best 8B open-source model', and the 9B's 80.6 SOTA sits 0.4 above DME-Large (80.2, a closed-source leaderboard submission with no public weights) and 0.5 above Octen-VL-Large (80.1, proprietary, undisclosed size). On MMEB-v3 the margin is wider and less contestable: 59.5 for the 9B versus 53.5 for Qwen3-VL-Embedding 8B.

  • The biggest genuine gap is video: WeMM 9B 74.3 vs Qwen3-VL-Embedding 8B 67.1; the 2B's 70.8 already beats every open 8B row.
  • The 12-benchmark cross-modal retrieval table is asymmetric by construction: Gemini Embedding 2 / Nova MME / Voyage Multimodal 3.5 numbers are copied from the Gemini Embedding 2 report, while 'Qwen3-VL-Embedding and WeMM-Embedding are evaluated by us'.
  • In-house 26-task WeChat benchmark: WeMM 2B 72.0 vs Qwen3-VL-Embedding 2B 60.9 — a single baseline on a private, unverifiable benchmark.
  • Deployment: 'WeMM-Embedding has delivered consistent gains in 14 online A/B tests across these systems' (Section 4.3) — with zero reported effect sizes.

Ablations: what is actually carrying the gains

The Stage-1 ablation (2B, MMEB-v2) shows task-consistent batching is by far the dominant design choice: removing it costs 3.4 points (71.9 to 68.5), versus 0.8 for dropping task-specific instructions and 0.5 for dropping duplicate-aware masking. The cumulative Stage-2 ablation is more revealing about the marketed contributions: curated data buys +0.9, the much-discussed reranker supervision buys +0.1, embedding-teacher distillation buys +0.9, and expanding the visual input budget buys the final +0.3.

  • Reranker supervision — one of the abstract's headline ingredients — moves the 2B average from 76.6 to 76.7, and the authors admit stable gains appear only on a limited subset of tasks.
  • Curated data actually lowers the visual-document score (78.9 to 78.5) while lifting image and video.
  • Cross-scale distillation is the single most defensible Stage-2 contribution at +0.9 for the 2B, and it is the mechanism that makes the small models competitive.
  • The paper also notes 'reranking candidates retrieved by our embedding models does not consistently improve performance' — an unusually honest negative result.

Key findings

  • Backbone is Qwen3.5 at 2B/4B/9B with last-token pooling over an appended <embedding> token and Matryoshka prefix truncation; 256 dims retains 98.7% of 2048-dim image and video quality on the 2B.
  • The claimed 'small model beats the best 8B open-source' result is a 0.1-point gap (77.9 vs 77.8), and the 9B's 80.6 SOTA is 0.4-0.5 above DME-Large (80.2) and Octen-VL-Large (80.1), both closed-source or proprietary entries.
  • Table 1 baselines are copied from the official MMEB leaderboard, not re-run; in the cross-modal retrieval table the proprietary competitors' numbers come from a competitor's own report while WeMM's are self-run — an asymmetric comparison.
  • Cross-scale knowledge transfer is real and load-bearing: the frozen 9B teaches the 2B/4B via bidirectional KL on batch-wise similarity distributions, worth +0.9 MMEB-v2 average for the 2B; the 9B instead merges multiple Stage-2 variants with undisclosed weights.
  • Reranker-based fine-grained relevance supervision, prominent in the abstract, is worth only +0.1 average in the Stage-2 ablation, and the authors concede stable gains appear only on a limited subset of tasks.
  • Data provenance is vague: 'several hundred million' Stage-1 pairs from public, web-scale, synthetic and in-house sources, with no dataset named and no per-source counts; no learning rate, batch size, epochs, or hardware reported anywhere.
  • Production claims are unquantified: 14 online A/B tests with 'consistent gains' but zero reported effect sizes.

Notable quotes

WeMM-Embedding is built upon the Qwen3.5 architecture.
For the 2B and 4B variants, the frozen 9B WeMM-Embedding model serves as the embedding teacher during Stage 2.
Task-consistent batching has the largest impact among the evaluated designs.
At 256 dimensions, the model retains 98.7% of its 2,048-dimensional performance on both image and video tasks

Limitations

  • No audio support: the 11 MMEB-v3 audio tasks are scored as zero, so the V3 lead comes entirely from text/image/video/agent tasks.
  • Benchmark honesty caveat: MMEB-v2 baselines are leaderboard-reported rather than author-run, the nearest rivals are closed-source submissions, and the winning margins are 0.1-0.5 points; the retrieval table compares self-run WeMM scores against numbers copied from a competitor's report.
  • Not reproducible as published: no training hyperparameters, optimizer, hardware, named training datasets, Stage-2 sample count, merge configuration for the 9B, or license for the released weights — so contamination against MMEB cannot be checked from the paper.

Open questions

  • Which checkpoints are actually released open-weight and under what license — the numbered sections never say, and the release claim exists only in the abstract plus the Hugging Face/GitHub links.
  • Do the MMEB-v2 margins survive an independent re-run of both WeMM and the open baselines under one harness, given that the 2B-over-8B claim rests on 0.1 points of leaderboard-reported data?
  • How much of the gain is contamination-free? With hundreds of millions of unnamed web-scale and in-house pairs and MMEB-style task families in the training mix, overlap with the 78 evaluation datasets is unaudited.
