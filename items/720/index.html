LOB-ID: Evaluating Synthetic Market Data by Inception Distances

📰 Latest · 2026-08-14
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-14

Source: arXiv · 2026-08-13
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13082

LOB-ID adapts FID and MIND to domain-specific DeepLOB embeddings, exposing temporal and cross-level failures that selected market statistics can miss. MIND proves markedly harder to fool than Gaussian-moment FID, making it a promising complement to interpretable microstructure diagnostics.

Executive summary

LOB-ID evaluates synthetic L2 book trajectories in a 96-dimensional representation learned by DeepLOB from four months of HKEX data. The paper tests stability across stocks, dates, and embedding checkpoints, then mounts a moment-matching attack against FID and a deep-book rearrangement attack against statistic-based evaluation. MIND remains sensitive to both and produces a generator ordering consistent with the temporal and cross-level dependencies each model can represent.

Domain-specific inception representation

A DeepLOB network processes 100-event windows containing ten bid and ask levels. Its Inception-module output is averaged into a 96-dimensional embedding. The associated mid-price classifier reaches 75.44% accuracy and 75.29% macro-F1, providing evidence that the representation captures meaningful book structure.

FID versus MIND

FID fits Gaussian approximations to real and generated embeddings, so it only sees means and covariances. MIND averages squared one-dimensional Wasserstein-2 distances over 256 random projections and can detect differences beyond the first two moments.

Adversarial evaluation

The moment-matching attack directly suppresses FID without repairing higher-order distributional discrepancies. A separate attack rearranges deeper bid and ask trajectories while preserving many marginal statistics and the price-impact response. MIND still detects the broken temporal, cross-level, and bid-ask structure.

Generator ranking

Five non-oracle generators are evaluated on 18 stock-days. Null-conditioned DiffLOB ranks first by MIND, followed by LOBS5, LOBGAN, Compound Hawkes, and Zero Intelligence. Future-conditioned DiffLOB scores still better but receives realized future regime variables and is explicitly excluded from the fair non-oracle ranking.

Key findings

  • The DeepLOB embedding classifier obtains 75.44% test accuracy and 75.29% macro-F1.
  • MIND remains sensitive after approximate embedding-moment matching substantially weakens FID.
  • LOB-ID detects a deep-book rearrangement that preserves many selected marginals and the price-impact response.
  • Null-conditioned DiffLOB obtains the best non-oracle MIND score, 6.0251, versus 9.1201 for LOBS5.
  • Relative generator rankings remain stable once the embedding network is sufficiently trained.

Notable quotes

LOB-ID does not directly evaluate the full message-level order-flow process.
At the population level, MIND is zero if and only if the two embedding distributions are equal.
The attacked data therefore appear realistic when individual features are examined separately, but not when the orderbook is viewed as a joint time series.
Future-conditioned DiffLOB receives regime variables computed from the future trajectory, giving it information unavailable to the other generators considered.

Limitations

  • The embedding and experiments cover only five liquid HKEX equities and one four-month period.
  • LOB-ID measures L2 trajectory structure, not the complete message-level generation process.
  • Absolute scores depend on the embedding network, preprocessing, sample size, and projection protocol.

Open questions

  • How well does LOB-ID correlate with downstream execution-policy or risk-model performance?
  • Can embeddings be trained across exchanges, asset classes, and stressed regimes without erasing important market-specific structure?
  • How does MIND compare directly with LOB-Bench's trained discriminator under adaptive attacks?
