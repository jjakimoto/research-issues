Data-Centric Post-Training for Financial Reasoning: Mining, Distillation, and Verifiable Learning

📰 Latest · 2026-09-11 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-11

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-09
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10113

A production-style recipe showing that ordinary SFT on financial data actively destroys 3.2-4.0 points of existing financial capability, while self-distilled SFT and weight merging turn the same data into net gains — with three released classifiers (finance relevance, missing context, answer verifiability) that decide which questions can even carry a rule-based RL reward.

Executive summary

A five-author technical report (FINESSE-Bench authors) building a financial post-training data pipeline from three sources: mined Glaive Reasoning v1 20M traces (44,652 kept), distilled Finance-Instruct-500K (4,475 kept), and GraphGen knowledge-graph synthesis over CFA books (329,828). Curation is factored into three explicit binary decisions, each a Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B classification head rather than a generative judge. On a fixed 778-question FINESSE-Bench subset, ordinary SFT loses 3.2-4.0 points versus the starting checkpoint, self-distilled SFT gains 1.0-2.8, equal-weight merging recovers 3.0 over its SFT parent, and GRPO adds 0.4 (hard examples, LLM-judge reward) or 3.0 (verifiable tasks, rule-based reward). Notably the paper never claims trading alpha — the whole evaluation is exam/QA accuracy graded by an LLM judge.

Data pipeline: three branches, hard thresholds documented

Each branch is deduplicated at the prompt level with Qwen3-Embedding-8B in a NeMo Curator workflow (100 clusters, cosine-distance threshold 0.1), then normalized-exact-dedup, common-prefix suppression (to kill template families), fastText English ID plus explicit Chinese-character rejection.

  • Glaive: ~700,000 finance candidates mined from the 20M pool, 645,232 after dedup, p(finance)>0.99 leaves 220,707, self-containment >0.9 leaves 111,655, prefix filter 93,570, language checks 93,427, and a 1,000-token reasoning-length floor leaves 44,652.
  • Finance-Instruct: 287,572 after semantic dedup, down through task-family and relevance filters to 11,809, then parsable single-turn only = 4,475. Traces regenerated by DeepSeek-R1-0528.
  • GraphGen/CFA: 1,024-token chunks with 100 overlap, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct extracts the KG and writes QA. 785,149 unique prompts down to 331,458. A 40,000-question shuffle is re-answered by Kimi K2.6, and a GPT-5.5 judge keeps only answers factually consistent with the GraphGen reference: 32,000 verified.
  • Released as scored candidate pools Fin-Glaive (645,232) and Fin-CFA-GraphGen (785,149); the Finance-Instruct-derived subset is not redistributed.

The three classifiers and the verifiable-reward gate

All three are two-class heads on Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B over the question truncated to 4,096 tokens, trained one epoch, BF16, LR 1e-5. Labels are LLM-generated: Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct for finance relevance (443,336 labeled, 57.9% positive), GPT-4.1-mini for missing context (259,432 labeled). The verifiability head is what gates rule-based RL: it selects numerical, multiple-choice, or regex-checkable answers so the reward is not measuring formatting or stylistic overlap.

  • Checkpoints public on HF: whoisjiji/fin-relevance, whoisjiji/lack-of-context-detector, whoisjiji/verifiability-classifier.
  • Self-containment score is defined as one minus the missing-context probability.
  • Best saved events: step 1,600 (relevance), step 300 (missing context), step 200 (verifiability).

Training and RL setup

SFT: one epoch, global batch 32, BF16, FSDP2, AdamW, LR 1e-5 cosine to 1e-7, LM parameters only. Self-distilled SFT uses a frozen copy of the starting checkpoint as teacher, loss weighted 0.8 distillation / 0.2 cross-entropy — motivated by Kaplan et al. 2026 on fine-tuning-induced hallucination. Merging is a WiSE-FT-style equal-weight linear interpolation. GRPO: one epoch, LR 1e-6 cosine, KL 0.001, 8 completions per prompt.

  • Hard-example GRPO reuses the SFT training questions: generate 4 responses, keep questions where at least 3 are judged incorrect, reward is a binary GLM-5.2 judge — an LLM reward, not a verifier.
  • Verifiable-task GRPO uses the verifiability classifier's selection with compact rule-based rewards, applied directly to unadapted Qwen3 checkpoints with no SFT stage.

Evaluation splits and the leakage question

A fixed 778-question subset of FINESSE-Bench, scored as micro-accuracy: 117 CFA Level I, 110 CFA Level II, 117 CMT Level II, 109 VLigaBench-ru, 96 technical-analysis trading, 115 derivatives-trading, 114 FinQA. Grading is GPT-5.2 as a binary correctness judge at temperature 0.

  • FINESSE-Bench is the authors' own prior benchmark, and the largest training corpus is synthesized from CFA curriculum and exam-preparation books while 227 of the 778 eval questions are CFA Level I/II — the report contains no contamination analysis against the eval set.
  • Component-level results are acknowledged as mixed but are not tabulated; only the aggregate micro-accuracy is reported.
  • No trading-performance, PnL, or out-of-sample market evaluation appears anywhere.

Key findings

  • Ordinary domain SFT is net-negative: -3.2 points for Qwen3.5-4B (75.2 to 72.0) and -4.0 for Gemma-4-12B-IT (77.5 to 73.5).
  • Self-distilled SFT (0.8 KD / 0.2 CE against a frozen copy of the start checkpoint) flips the sign: +2.8 (Qwen3.5-4B), +1.7 (Gemma-4-12B-IT), +1.0 (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B).
  • Equal-weight WiSE-FT-style merging lifts the Qwen3.5-4B SFT parent from 73.14 to 76.09 (+2.96) and finishes +0.90 over the untouched 75.2 baseline — recovery, not domain gain.
  • The two RL branches behave very differently: hard-example GRPO with a GLM-5.2 binary judge adds only 0.39 points (79.18 to 79.56); verifier-selected GRPO with rule-based rewards adds a consistent 3.0 points (56.0 to 59.0 and 61.0 to 64.0).
  • The Glaive funnel is brutally lossy by design: ~700,000 finance candidates become 44,652 kept examples (roughly 6%).
  • Classifier-based screening replaces generative judging at scale: three 0.6B heads with positive-class F1 0.94-0.97, though their training labels themselves come from Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and GPT-4.1-mini.

Notable quotes

The initial mining pass yields approximately 700,000 finance candidates.
This classifier does not judge whether a supplied answer is correct; it judges whether correctness can be evaluated reliably with a compact verifier.
Candidate answers are scored by GPT-5.2 as a binary correctness judge using the question, reference answer, and model response.
Benchmark construction was outside the scope of this work, so we describe only the evaluation set and scoring procedure needed to interpret our model comparisons.

Limitations

  • Every result is judged accuracy on the authors' own FINESSE-Bench, graded by an LLM judge; there is no contamination check even though the largest training corpus is synthesized from CFA books and 227 of the 778 eval questions are CFA Level I/II.
  • No ablation isolates the contribution of each of the three data sources, no error bars or multiple seeds are reported, and the 0.39-point hard-example GRPO gain is well inside plausible judge noise.
  • Component-level results are described as mixed but never shown, so aggregate gains may mask regressions on specific task types.

Open questions

  • Does any of this transfer to decisions with a market P&L, or only to exam-style QA? The paper offers no trading or forecasting evaluation.
  • Would verifier-selected rule-based GRPO stack with self-distilled SFT (the two best ingredients were tested only in separate chains, on different model families)?
  • How much of the 3.0-point verifiable-RL gain is genuine reasoning improvement versus the model learning the answer-format the compact parser rewards?
