Self-Supervised Auxiliary Task Discovery for Stable Reinforcement Learning in Stock Trading

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-16
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15841

Instead of hand-picking auxiliary prediction tasks to regularise a trading RL agent, QUESTrader lets a second network invent them as General Value Functions and tunes them with a non-myopic meta-gradient through K unrolled PPO updates. On four indices it beats plain PPO and handcrafted-auxiliary variants on return, Sharpe, Calmar and Sortino simultaneously (DJI Sharpe 1.459 vs 1.076) - but the ablation selects its recommended settings on the same test window it reports.

Executive summary

QUESTrader (Question Enhanced Stock Trader) is a two-network PPO framework for multi-stock daily trading in which auxiliary tasks are discovered automatically rather than handcrafted. A 'question' network emits per-timestep cumulant and discount vectors defining d_q on-policy General Value Functions; the main network outputs the PPO policy, the value function, and one scalar answer head per question, trained on truncated TD targets. The question network gets no market supervision at all -- its parameters are meta-parameters updated by a non-myopic meta-gradient of the summed PPO loss across K unrolled inner updates. Evaluated on DJI, FTSE, Sensex and TAIEX (train 2010-2023, test Jan 2024 - Mar 2025, 5 seeds), it tops annual return, cumulative return, Sharpe, Calmar and Sortino on all four datasets while keeping max drawdown competitive.

Problem: handcrafted auxiliary tasks don't survive regime change

Trading RL suffers from noisy, sparse, non-stationary reward. Auxiliary tasks are the usual fix, but they encode prior assumptions about what to predict and over what horizon. The authors argue an auxiliary task that helps in one market regime can hurt in another, and that designing them needs domain knowledge that does not scale across markets. Their answer is to make the auxiliary tasks themselves learnable, extending the GVF-discovery principle of Veeriah et al. to trading.

  • State is a (10n+1)-dimensional vector for n stocks: cash balance, plus per stock the share count, close price, and 8 technical indicators (30/60-day SMA, MACD, upper/lower Bollinger bands, RSI, CCI, ADX). With n=30 that is 301-dim.
  • Action is an n-vector with a_{t,i} in {-m,...,0,...,m} shares, so the action space is (2m+1)^n.
  • Reward is costed mark-to-market P&L: r(s_t,a_t) = (P_{t+1}-P_t)*a_t - delta*P_t*|a_t - a_{t-1}|, so the cost term penalises churn directly.
  • Stated idealisations: zero slippage, negligible market impact, immediate settlement.

Method: two networks, GVF questions, and a bi-level meta-gradient

The question network (eta) takes a short future state slice s_{t+1:t+j} and emits cumulants c_t and discounts gamma_t; each pair defines one GVF question. The main network (theta) outputs the policy, value function, and d_q scalar answers regressed onto truncated TD targets with horizon W. J_main = J_PPO(theta) + lambda_aux * J_ans(theta;eta); the question network's own loss is simply J_ques = J_PPO(theta) -- never a market target. Optimisation is bi-level: K inner PPO updates on theta, then one outer update on eta against M_t(eta) = sum_{k=1..K} J_PPO(theta_{t,k}), differentiated through the whole unroll. K=1 is the myopic variant; K>1 is what the paper advocates.

  • Question network input is a forward window of states -- a training-time self-supervised signal, since questions are only judged by how much they help PPO.
  • The meta-loss sums the PPO loss over the entire inner unroll, not just the final iterate.
  • lambda_aux is tuned in [0.01, 0.5].

Experimental setup: 4 indices, 30 stocks each

Daily closes from Yahoo Finance, 1 Jan 2010 - 31 Mar 2025. DJI, FTSE 100, Sensex, TAIEX -- all 30 DJI and Sensex constituents, top 30 by market cap for FTSE and TAIEX. Single non-overlapping holdout: 2010-2023 train, Jan 2024 - Mar 2025 out-of-sample. Initial capital 1,000,000, fees 0.1% per side, all trades at the close. Hyperparameters tuned by Bayesian optimisation with Hyperopt for QUESTrader and all DRL baselines; 5 seeds each, mean +/- std. Baselines: Buy-and-Hold, index, MVO, random, plain PPO, FinRL DDPG/SAC, VS-DRL, SRRS, Adaptive, DREB, DeepScalper, and handcrafted-auxiliary PA-AXT and PPO-AXT.

  • Table 1 reports search RANGES only, never final values: K in {1,2,4,10,20,50}, d_q in {2,4,8,16,32,64,128}, LR [e^-8, e^-1], PPO epochs [5,50].
  • Classical baselines are deterministic, reported without error bars.

Results: best on every risk-adjusted metric, on all four markets

QUESTrader tops annual return, cumulative return, Sharpe, Calmar and Sortino on all four indices; max drawdown is competitive rather than best (MVO and Adaptive go lower at much lower return). Over plain PPO: DJI 21.785% vs 15.674% annual and Sharpe 1.459 vs 1.076; FTSE Sharpe 1.124 vs 0.630 with drawdown 17.814% -> 12.165%. The handcrafted-auxiliary baselines are also beaten on every risk-adjusted metric -- the paper's core claim. Action timelines show fewer long/short whipsaws and more holds, which matters under the 0.1% cost model; the risk-return scatter puts it above the baseline cluster at comparable annualized volatility, so gains are attributed to timing rather than sizing.

  • Section 6.1: wins 5 of 6 metrics on 4 of 4 datasets (drawdown the exception).
  • TAIEX strongest: 30.279% annual, Sharpe 1.803, Calmar 2.559 vs DeepScalper 26.833% / 1.236 / 1.653.
  • Sensex hardest: the edge is mostly loss avoidance (drawdown 10.584%, Sharpe 1.003 vs DREB 0.916).

Ablation and significance

The ablation (DJI only) sweeps d_q and K, retraining the full pipeline per configuration and scoring on the fixed test window. Sharpe is an inverted-U in d_q: ~0.9 at 2, peak ~1.4 at 16, ~1.2 at 32, ~1.3-1.35 at 64, ~1.15 at 128 -- too few questions underrepresent market structure, too many fragment the signal and amplify bootstrap noise in the GVF heads. Total return peaks later at d_q=64 (~27-28%). For unroll length, Sharpe is weak at K=1-2 (~0.9-1.0) and peaks ~1.6 at K=10; total return rises to ~28% at K=20 then falls to ~23% at K=50, attributed to meta-gradient variance and stale PPO ratios. Recommended: d_q in [16,64], K around 10-20. The K=1 result is the internal evidence that non-myopic credit assignment is load-bearing.

  • Future work named by the authors: off-policy GVFs, implicit-gradient meta-updates, online regime detection to adapt d_q and K, and cross-sectional questions for portfolio/spread/sector-rotation settings.

Key findings

  • Discovered GVF auxiliary tasks beat plain PPO on all four markets: DJI 21.785% vs 15.674% annual return and Sharpe 1.459 vs 1.076; FTSE Sharpe 1.124 vs 0.630 with drawdown cut 17.814% -> 12.165%; Sensex 16.727% vs 11.948%; TAIEX 30.279% vs 21.513%.
  • They also beat handcrafted auxiliary-task baselines on every risk-adjusted metric -- vs PPO-AXT, Sharpe 1.459 vs 1.226 (DJI), 1.124 vs 0.722 (FTSE), 1.003 vs 0.870 (Sensex), 1.803 vs 0.972 (TAIEX).
  • QUESTrader leads on annual return, cumulative return, Sharpe, Calmar and Sortino on 4 of 4 datasets; max drawdown is the one metric it never wins (MVO 8.365% on DJI), but those baselines earn far less.
  • Non-myopic credit assignment is load-bearing: K=1 (myopic) gives DJI Sharpe ~0.9-1.0 and total return ~14%, rising to a Sharpe peak ~1.6 at K=10 and a return peak ~28% at K=20, then degrading at K=50.
  • The number of questions has a clear sweet spot rather than being monotone: Sharpe ~0.9 at d_q=2, ~1.4 at d_q=16, ~1.15 at d_q=128; recommended region d_q in [16,64] with K around 10-20.
  • The behavioural mechanism is turnover discipline, not leverage: fewer long/short flips and more holds than PPO at comparable annualized volatility -- which matters because the reward charges delta*P_t*|a_t - a_{t-1}| and the backtest charges 0.1% per side.

Notable quotes

Each DRL-based model is trained over five independent runs, and the mean values of the evaluation metrics along with their corresponding standard deviations are reported.
It has no direct supervised signal from the market.
For each configuration, we retrain the full pipeline and evaluate it using the fixed test window.
Unlike MVO, which minimizes drawdown at the expense of returns, QUESTrader achieves a more favorable balance between return generation and risk control.

Limitations

  • Test-window contamination in the ablation, stated outright by the authors: 'For each configuration, we retrain the full pipeline and evaluate it using the fixed test window.' The recommended d_q in [16,64] and K in [10,20] are therefore selected on the same out-of-sample period the headline numbers are reported on, so the reported edge is optimistically biased. Ablation values are also only approximate, apparently read off figures, and cover DJI only.
  • Thin statistical support: 5 seeds, a single non-overlapping 15-month out-of-sample window (Jan 2024 - Mar 2025) in a broadly rising market, no significance tests, no walk-forward or rolling windows. Several reported gaps (e.g. DJI Calmar 2.394 vs SRRS 2.371) are well inside the quoted standard deviations. Table 1 gives only search ranges, never final selected values, so the result is not reproducible as published.
  • Execution realism is limited: daily closes only, fills at the close with zero slippage, negligible market impact, immediate settlement, flat 0.1% fee per side, no short-borrow cost, no financing, no liquidity constraint -- even though the action space is (2m+1)^n over 30 names and the strategy shorts. The question network is fed a forward state slice s_{t+1:t+j}, legitimate at training time but requiring care not to become lookahead online.

Open questions

  • Does the advantage survive a proper walk-forward protocol -- hyperparameters chosen on a validation slice strictly before each test fold, over multiple rolling windows including a drawdown regime such as 2020 or 2022?
  • What do the discovered cumulants and discounts actually encode? The paper never inspects them, so it is unknown whether the learned questions recover known predictors (realised volatility, momentum, mean reversion) or something novel.
  • How much of the improvement is representation learning versus plain regularisation? A control with d_q random-but-fixed GVFs (frozen question network, no meta-gradient) would separate the two.
