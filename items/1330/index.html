ResearchAgent: Iterative Research Idea Generation over Scientific Literature with Large Language Models

📚 Foundations · 2026-09-06 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-06

Theme: Literature-grounded autonomous research
These systems automate two core research activities: generating proposals from scientific literature and synthesizing evidence from an open corpus. ResearchAgent emphasizes citation-graph expansion, knowledge augmentation, and iterative critique; PaperQA2 emphasizes agent-controlled search, evidence reranking, citation support, abstention, and expert comparison. Their strongest shared lesson is that retrieval and review improve research assistance, but judged plausibility and cited synthesis remain different from experimentally validated discovery.
Source: arxiv
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07738

A foundational agentic-science system combining citation-graph retrieval, entity-centric knowledge augmentation, and iterative multi-agent review to generate complete research proposals.

Executive summary

ResearchAgent generates research ideas as structured triples of problem, method, and experimental design. It grounds generation in a core paper, citation-linked references, and entities retrieved from a literature-wide co-occurrence store, then iteratively revises each component using specialized reviewing agents. Human and GPT-4 evaluations favor the full system over its ablations, although its evidence is based on proxy judgments rather than executing the proposed experiments.

Problem formulation

The paper frames scientific ideation as generating a problem, method, and experimental design from existing literature. This decomposition is intended to model the parts of early-stage research where an AI assistant can provide systematic help.

  • A complete idea is represented as problem, method, and experiment design.
  • Each stage conditions on the literature and on outputs from preceding stages.
  • The benchmark uses 300 post-May-2023 high-impact core papers sampled from Semantic Scholar.

Literature and entity augmentation

ResearchAgent first expands a core paper through its citation graph, filtering connected papers using abstract similarity. It then supplements that local literature neighborhood with entities retrieved from a sparse co-occurrence matrix built from titles and abstracts, aiming to introduce useful cross-domain concepts absent from the immediate citation neighborhood.

  • Core papers and citation-linked references supply focused local context.
  • BLINK extracts and canonicalizes entities from paper titles and abstracts.
  • The knowledge store was built from 50,091 papers and references dated May through December 2023.
  • Relevant references contribute more than entities in the reported ablation, but both improve scores.

Iterative reviewing agents

Fifteen ReviewingAgents assess the three idea components using five criteria per component. The criteria are induced from ten human-scored idea pairs per criterion, and ResearchAgent revises its proposal in response to the resulting feedback.

  • Problem, method, and experiment design are reviewed separately.
  • Review criteria are calibrated from researchers' five-point Likert judgments.
  • Quality initially rises with additional refinement, then saturates after three iterations.

Evaluation and results

Ten expert researchers evaluate ideas derived from their own papers, while GPT-4 supplies reference-free scores and pairwise comparisons. The full system scores 4.52 for problems, 4.28 for methods, and 4.18 for experimental designs, exceeding every reported ablation; it also outperforms SciMON and Hypothesis Proposer on four of five hypothesis-quality criteria.

  • Removing both references and entities lowers the three component scores to 4.20, 4.03, and 3.92.
  • Human-model agreement is weaker for experimental design than for problem formulation.
  • Knowledge augmentation helps substantially with GPT-4, Llama3-8B, and Qwen1.5-32B, but gains are marginal or inconsistent for GPT-3.5 and Mixtral-8x7B.
  • ResearchAgent's hypothesis scores reach 4.88 for relevance, 4.77 for originality, and 4.81 for significance.

Significance and boundaries

The work establishes an early architecture for literature-grounded, multi-agent research ideation and shows that retrieval quality, model capability, human-aligned rubrics, and bounded iteration all matter. Its main unresolved issue is validity: judged plausibility and novelty do not demonstrate that a generated idea survives prior-art checking or succeeds experimentally.

  • The entity store covers only titles and abstracts and retrieves roughly three entities per paper on average.
  • LLM hallucination remains possible despite literature grounding.
  • The fixed review criteria may not represent all disciplines or perspectives.
  • The authors identify theoretical sciences as a difficult domain requiring specialized reasoning and proof-oriented workflows.

Key findings

  • Combining citation-linked references and entity retrieval produces the strongest problem, method, and experimental-design scores among the evaluated variants.
  • Citation-linked references provide a larger contribution than entity augmentation, though both improve the full system.
  • Iterative review improves generated ideas initially but exhibits diminishing returns after three iterations.
  • Human-aligned evaluation criteria make model-based score distributions more closely resemble human judgments.
  • Knowledge augmentation depends on base-model capability; weaker models do not consistently exploit the additional context.

Notable quotes

To achieve this, we mirror the process followed by human researchers, who expand their knowledge of a paper by perusing other papers that either cite or are cited by it.
We first observe initial improvements in the quality of generated ideas with increased refinement steps.
We note that providing random elements is more helpful than providing no elements at all; we hypothesize that this may be due to the LLM’s capability to filter out noise while still gaining incidental value from random inputs.
Furthermore, since our ResearchAgent is powered by LLMs, similar to any other approaches based on LLMs, it may hallucinate the generated research ideas.

Limitations

  • The entity store is built only from titles and abstracts of a limited publication set, while the open-domain linker extracts roughly three entities per paper and therefore has limited scientific coverage.
  • The evaluation measures expert and LLM judgments of proposals rather than experimentally validating whether the generated ideas work.
  • Fifteen reviewing agents using fixed criteria cannot capture every relevant perspective across disciplines, and the approach is less suited to proof-heavy theoretical sciences.

Open questions

  • Can generated proposals be automatically checked against comprehensive prior art and then validated through real experiments rather than proxy judgments?
  • How should the entity store be scaled, continuously updated, and specialized for scientific terminology and emerging domains?
  • Can domain-specific reviewers, tools, and reasoning models make the framework reliable for theoretical mathematics and other proof-centered fields?
