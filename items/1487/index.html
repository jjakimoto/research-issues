The Analyst in the Prompt: Role, Retrieval, and Memory Biases in LLM Financial Analysis

📰 Latest · 2026-09-13
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-13

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-02
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03218

Across 3,575 SEC filings and twelve LLMs, a field explicitly defined as a neutral evidence-only judgment still moves when the user's persona changes — and ~69% of that bias survives byte-identical retrieval, so the failure is interpretive, not retrieval drift. Moving the same investor mindset from the assistant's role into a user-memory profile is the strongest mitigation (median 73% shrinkage).

Executive summary

The authors audit user-context spillover — user persona/role/memory leaking into a score defined as a neutral reading of evidence — in LLM analysis of SEC 10-K/10-Q filings. They run three matched conditions over 3,575 filings and twelve models from five families: persona-conditioned retrieval (PR), neutral pinned retrieval with persona role prompts (NR), and neutral retrieval with the mindset reframed as a third-person user-memory profile (MEM), estimating persona effects via filing-fixed-effects regressions on a two-field output. Most bias survives identical retrieval (panel mean retention 69%), so the dominant channel is interpretive drift rather than evidence selection. Role-to-profile reframing and dual-output routing both reduce spillover substantially but neither eliminates it, and effectiveness does not track model scale.

Design: three conditions, one lever per arm

Each filing is reduced to MD&A, Risk Factors, and Quantitative/Qualitative Disclosures, chunked into ~400-token segments with 80-token overlap, embedded with text-embedding-3-large (dim 3072), and indexed per filing; retrieval returns top-k=8. The model emits a fixed JSON schema with two scores in [-1,+1]: an EvidenceScore meant to be invariant to user context, and a PersonalizedScore allowed to adapt.

  • PR: the persona conditions both the retrieval query and the prompt, so personas may read different chunks
  • NR: one neutral retrieval per filing, the chunk set reused byte-identically across personas, isolating generation-stage effects
  • MEM: retrieval stays pinned, the assistant role is reset to neutral analyst, and the mindset is supplied third-person as user memory
  • Headline metrics average four matched pairs; Momentum–MomentumTrader is excluded for a near-zero base effect

Sample and estimation

A stratified sample of 3,575 SEC filings from S&P 500 constituents spans 2013-2024, preserving the index's GICS sector distribution, at most six filings per firm. All calls use deterministic decoding (T=0, top-p=1, max-tokens 1024). For each model the authors fit six filing-fixed-effects regressions, neutral frame as reference.

  • Standard errors clustered at the filing level; 95% CIs via bootstrap with B=1,000
  • Metrics: Bias = |beta_PR|; Retain = |beta_NR|/|beta_PR|; Shrink = 1 - |gamma_MEM_ev|/|beta_NR|; Leakage L where 0 is perfect routing and 0.5 is no separation
  • The PR effect is decomposed into evidence selection, context framing, and residual spillover

Result: interpretation, not retrieval, is the main channel

If retrieval drove the bias, pinning it should collapse the effect. It does not: the panel retains 69% of the PR effect on average (68% median, 44-89% across models). The ShortSeller direction is the most persistent. Bias magnitude varies widely but does not decrease monotonically with scale.

  • Eight of twelve models retain at least 74% of the ShortSeller effect; panel median 89.1%
  • Evidence selection is usually the minority component; framing plus residual spillover dominate
  • Panel mean |beta_PR| on the evidence score is 0.20 on a [-1,+1] scale, ranging 0.02 to 0.40

Mitigations: prompt framing beats output routing

Comparing NR with MEM holds retrieval fixed and varies only where the mindset lives. Reframing the assistant role as a user profile reduces the evidence-side coefficient in the large majority of cells. The dual-output schema also helps — all twelve models have mean leakage below 0.5 — but never reaches zero.

  • ShortSeller/DownsideProtection NR-to-MEM shrinkage runs 51% to 87%, panel median 73%
  • Mean leakage ranges 0.05 (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) to 0.37 (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B); gpt-oss-120b is 0.34
  • A 2x2 ablation shows profile-framing spillover remains only 16-39% of role-framing under both output formats

Corroborating evidence: rationales, document populations, and residual signal

Rationale embeddings reproduce the same ordering, residual leakage concentrates in smaller-cap filings, and the evidence scores still carry forward-looking information — which is what makes the level shift consequential.

  • Median cosine similarity of non-neutral evidence rationales to the neutral baseline rises PR 0.721 -> NR 0.805 -> MEM 0.849
  • Residual leakage on small-cap filings is 2-4x larger than on large-caps
  • Top-minus-bottom evidence-score quintiles earn +2.96% mean one-year abnormal return vs the S&P 500 (n ~= 3,393 filings); eight of nine personas clearly positive, RiskManager flat at +0.08%

Key findings

  • With byte-identical retrieved evidence, models retain 69% of the persona effect on the supposedly neutral evidence score on average (44-89% across models).
  • The ShortSeller persona is the most persistent: eight of twelve models retain at least 74% of the PR effect, panel median 89.1%.
  • Reframing the identical mindset from assistant role to third-person user-memory profile shrinks the evidence-side coefficient by 51-87% (panel median 73%).
  • Dual-output routing helps everywhere but never fully: leakage 0.05 to 0.37, and it does not track model size (a 120B model sits at 0.34).
  • Evidence-rationale similarity to the neutral baseline rises monotonically (median cosine 0.721 PR, 0.805 NR, 0.849 MEM).
  • Residual spillover is 2-4x larger on small-cap filings than large-caps.
  • The scores still carry information: top-minus-bottom quintiles earn +2.96% mean one-year abnormal return versus the S&P 500.

Notable quotes

The dominant source is interpretive drift: models assign different evidence-based scores to the same filing chunks once a role is in scope.
Prompt placement is not cosmetic; it is a major predictor of whether user context enters the neutral field.
Output separation is useful, but routing is a model-specific property that must be checked per model rather than assumed from scale.
Larger models are not uniformly safer, and MoE models are not uniformly more invariant.

Limitations

  • Confined to SEC 10-K/10-Q filings with a single retrieval encoder and one prompt-template family; the cross-domain check uses only one model.
  • The three-channel split is an accounting decomposition, not formal causal mediation.
  • Spillover is measured against a neutral-condition invariance reference rather than external ground truth, so the paper tests stability of the evidence judgment, not its correctness.

Open questions

  • Does the role-versus-user-profile asymmetry persist in agentic, multi-turn systems with genuine accumulated memory?
  • Can training-time interventions drive residual leakage to zero?
  • Why is residual spillover 2-4x larger on small-cap filings — disclosure irregularity, weaker entity familiarity, or both?
