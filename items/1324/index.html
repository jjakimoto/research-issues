SolarWM: Open Data and Scalable Training for Long-Horizon Video World Models

📰 Latest · 2026-09-05 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-05

Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-09-02
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02886

An unusually complete open release — a 1.43M-clip camera-annotated video corpus (25.85 TB, including the 549k REJECTED clips with machine-readable rejection reasons) plus four 5B-33B world models on Wan2.2 / LTX-2.5 / MiniMax-H3 trained with one three-stage recipe, and the artifacts are live on Hugging Face. The catch for anyone who cares about honest benchmarking: the paper contains zero quantitative results — no metric, no baseline, no latency number — yet claims state-of-the-art twice.

Executive summary

SolarWM (CUHK-SZ / SLAI / NUS / NVIDIA / MSRA and others, 2 Sep 2026) pairs a reconfigurable multi-source data engine with a backbone-native adaptation framework for interactive, camera-controlled video world models. The engine normalises 1.43M canonical clips from 10 source datasets (organised as 14 independently filterable 'dataset owners', 25.85 TB, 29k WebDataset shards) into one frame-aligned contract of video + metric camera poses/intrinsics + dense captions + a full metric record + provenance, and publishes accepted AND rejected clips so users can rewrite the selection policy without rerunning annotation. Four models are trained with the same three stages: bidirectional camera-conditioned adaptation, teacher-forced AnyFlow autoregressive initialisation, and DMD-based causal training; inference runs 4 sampling steps at 16 fps and sustains 60-minute rollouts despite 5-second training windows. The evaluation, however, is entirely qualitative figures — not a single number, baseline comparison, or throughput measurement anywhere in Section 7.

What the 'open data' release actually contains

The corpus is 1,425,694 canonical clips drawn from 10 source datasets — ABOT-World, DL3DV, MiraData, RealCam, SpatialVID, Sekai-Game, Sekai-Walking, MIND, MultiCamVideo, OmniWorld — split into 14 'dataset owners' (DL3DV becomes 10s and 60s temporal views; three 'Clean Plate' owners are derived from MiraData, Sekai-Walking and SpatialVID by removing people and vehicles while keeping layout and camera motion). Every sample carries video, metric camera-to-world poses, per-frame intrinsics, a Kimi-K2.6 dense caption, media metadata, the complete metric record, and provenance. The paper never states total duration in hours — only clip counts, 29k shards and 25.85 TB.

  • 876k clips kept (471,798 'high' + 404,795 'xhigh'); the other 549,101 are published as a 'rejected' partition with machine-readable failure reasons — rare and genuinely useful for re-filtering experiments.
  • Clean Plate derivatives: 543k clips; their captions, visual/semantic metrics and camera diagnostics are recomputed rather than inherited.
  • Camera annotation is Pi3X structure + MoGe-2 metric depth fused into a modified VIPE SLAM backend with per-frame intrinsics initialised by GeoCalib; ground-truth/COLMAP poses are preserved when a source has them, with a robust Umeyama Sim(3) fit on the lowest-residual 80% of frames.
  • Captions are deliberately camera-blind: Kimi-K2.6 is prompted for 60-150 English words about persistent scene content only, excluding people, actions, camera motion and shot terminology, to stop the text condition leaking camera control.
  • Selection is a frozen per-owner executable policy (Table 3), not a global threshold.
  • Recipes are indices, not copies: the released '81f short' recipe spans 10 owners with 600,320 physical rows and a repeat factor of six on ABOT / MiraData / Sekai-Game, giving 870,210 virtual occurrences per epoch.
  • Licensing is NOT discussed in the paper. Externally (checked 2026-09-05) the HF dataset card is apache-2.0 but gated, and says the raw video and pre-encoded latent payloads are distributed separately 'because of their size and upstream terms' — so upstream dataset licences still govern the actual pixels.

The four models and how each backbone is adapted

SolarWM is framed as one framework with four instantiations: shared data recipe, camera-geometry interface, and training/inference workflow, but each route keeps its native latent representation, attention layout, conditioning and optimisation objective. Sizes span 5B to 33B parameters over three backbone families.

  • SolarWM-wan2.2-5B: the TI2V member; 48-channel Wan2.2 VAE latent, no separate image-condition tensor — the first latent is the clean anchor and is excluded from the video loss. VAE downsample 4x temporal / 16x spatial.
  • SolarWM-wan2.2-14B: initialised from Wan 2.2 I2V-A14B's high-noise expert and trained as one dense model over all timesteps (two-expert routing dropped); explicit 4-channel first-frame mask + 16-channel image latent concatenated to the noisy target.
  • SolarWM-ltx-2.5-22B: used video-only — the 3.7B audio stream and 2.6B audio-video cross-attention are removed, leaving a 13.1B video core plus a frozen 1.6B Gemma connector.
  • SolarWM-minimax-h3-33B: keeps H3's multimodal packing; audio rows are retained but filled with an AudioVAE encoding of silence and given zero loss. Camera PRoPE touches only head dimensions [96:128) so dims [0:96) keep the pretrained content representation.
  • No parameter-count-versus-quality comparison is drawn between the four — they are positioned as a comparability substrate, not a scaling study.

Camera conditioning: fused-PRoPE, no side branch

Camera control is injected through fused projective rotary positional embeddings, identically across all four routes. The backbone first applies its native video RoPE; camera poses and intrinsics then determine projective rotations applied to the query, key AND value tensors; a single self-attention operation follows. Camera motion therefore enters through the attention computation itself, with no extra control branch, no additional attention pass, and no appended condition token — which is why the same conditioning code drops into four architecturally different generators.

  • Trajectories stay in metric units; no corpus-wide translation normalisation is applied.
  • Frame indices of video, poses and intrinsics must match exactly; missing or non-finite camera evidence 'fails closed' when a recipe uses a camera gate.
  • Per-frame (rather than per-clip) intrinsics are specifically motivated by clips with zoom or focal-length drift.

Three-stage recipe and how the step count gets to four

Stage 1 (bidirectional adaptation) fine-tunes the pretrained generator on camera-conditioned data with full temporal attention; that checkpoint serves both as the causal initialiser and as the frozen DMD teacher. Stage 2 (teacher-forced AnyFlow) divides the latent sequence into ordered blocks where the model attends only to the current noisy state and clean ground-truth history — producing a few-step autoregressive initialiser directly, which is what lets SolarWM skip the Causal ODE and Causal Consistency Distillation stages used by Causal Forcing and Causal Forcing++. Stage 3 (DMD) closes the exposure gap by rolling the student out under the inference-time temporal rule with a detached rollout-and-replay procedure that permits KV-cache gradients.

  • No 'from N steps to M steps' reduction and no wall-clock or GPU numbers are given. The only step figure is at inference — all reported causal results use four sampling steps at 16 fps with no attention sink.
  • The claimed compute-allocation finding is qualitative: most optimisation should happen in bidirectional training, AR adaptation converges rapidly, DMD needs even fewer steps — but no step counts, GPU-hours, batch sizes, learning rates or training resolutions appear anywhere.
  • 'Real-time interaction' is asserted in the abstract and conclusion but never measured: no fps-on-hardware, no latency, no GPU model.

What 'long-horizon' means here, and what the evaluation does not show

Training windows are short — 81, 153 or 158 frames depending on backbone, i.e. roughly 5 seconds at 16 fps. Inference rolls out continuously to minute-scale and to a 60-minute endpoint from a single image with a fixed scene prompt and a predetermined camera trajectory, with no restart from the input image, no injected reference frames, no splicing, and no attention sink. That is the headline result. The evidence is Figures 10 and 11: sparsely sampled frames from single uninterrupted sessions, judged by eye. Section 7 contains no metric of any kind — no VBench, FVD, PSNR/LPIPS, pose-following error, or drift curve — and no comparison against Matrix-Game, WorldPlay, AlayaWorld, Genie 3 or any other system, despite Section 3 discussing all of them.

  • Every causal / long-horizon figure comes from one model, the SolarWM-wan2.2-5B-fast student. The 14B, 22B and 33B routes appear only at the bidirectional stage on 10-second OOD clips.
  • Out-of-domain tests use first frames synthesised with GPT Image 2 or Krea, with several trajectories per image to separate appearance robustness from control responsiveness — a reasonable design, still scored only visually.
  • 'State-of-the-art performance' is claimed twice with no supporting number.
  • The one comparison table, Table 1, is a release-status matrix verified as of 18 Aug 2026 — and its data rows failed to render in the arXiv HTML, so only the header is visible there.

Release status, verified independently on 2026-09-05

The paper is written in the future tense ('We will release...') and Table 1's caption states that SolarWM entries 'indicate commitments for this release'. Checking Hugging Face directly three days after submission shows the artifacts are in fact live, but unevenly.

  • Dataset junchaoh-cs/SolarWM-Data exists (created 2026-08-28, last modified 2026-09-03, apache-2.0, gated 'auto', ~4.5k downloads). Raw video and pre-encoded latents ship separately.
  • Weights exist for all four routes (junchaoh-cs/SolarWM-Wan2.2-5B, -Wan2.2-14B, -LTX-22B, -H3-33B), created 2026-09-03, in safetensors/pytorch form with base backbones bundled.
  • Crucially, only the 5B repo ships the full pipeline: bid-stage0p5-81f, bid-stage0p5-153f, tf-stage1-81f and sgf-stage2-81f. The 14B, 22B and 33B repos contain base + bid-stage0p5 only — so the interactive few-step model is 5B-only today.
  • The GitHub repo (Junchao-cs/SolarWM) is linked from the paper but could not be reached from this host (proxy blocked github.com), so code completeness is unverified here.

Key findings

  • 1,425,694 canonical clips from 10 source datasets, reorganised into 14 independently filterable dataset owners, 29k WebDataset shards, ~25.85 TB. Total duration in hours is never reported.
  • 876k clips are kept (471,798 high + 404,795 xhigh) and 549,101 are published as a rejected partition with full annotations and machine-readable rejection reasons — the rejected data is a first-class artifact, not discarded.
  • Four models spanning 5B-33B on three backbone families, all sharing one camera interface (fused-PRoPE applied to Q, K and V inside the existing self-attention) and one three-stage recipe.
  • Trained only on ~5-second windows (81 / 153 / 158 frames at 16 fps), the distilled causal 5B student rolls out continuously to a 60-minute endpoint with no attention sink, no reference-frame injection, no splicing and no restart from the input image.
  • Inference is four sampling steps at 16 fps. The paper reports no before/after step count for DMD, no latency, no GPU, no throughput — so the 'real-time interaction' claim is unquantified.
  • Section 7 contains zero quantitative results: no VBench/FVD/PSNR/pose-error metric, no ablation numbers, and no comparison against any baseline system, yet 'state-of-the-art performance' is claimed twice.
  • Verified externally on 2026-09-05: dataset (apache-2.0, gated) and weights for all four routes are live on Hugging Face, but only the 5B repo contains the teacher-forced and distilled causal checkpoints.

Notable quotes

The data infrastructure contains approximately 1.43 million canonical clips from 10 source datasets, corresponding to over 25 TB of physical data.
At the owner level, DL3DV is divided into two temporal views, DL3DV-10s and DL3DV-60s, while three additional Clean Plate owners are derived from MiraData, Sekai-Walking, and SpatialVID.
The first stage adapts the pretrained video generator to camera-conditioned world data under bidirectional attention.
Second, most optimization should be performed during bidirectional training; AR adaptation then converges rapidly, while DMD requires even fewer optimization steps.

Limitations

  • No quantitative evaluation whatsoever — every result in Section 7 is a qualitative figure, with no metric, no ablation number and no baseline comparison, so the repeated 'state-of-the-art' claim is unsupported by anything a reader can check.
  • The long-horizon and OOD claims all come from a single model, the 5B causal student; the 14B, 22B and 33B routes are only shown after bidirectional pretraining, and their causal checkpoints are not on Hugging Face either, so the 'four-model family' does not yet mean four interactive models.
  • No compute, hardware, resolution or hyperparameter disclosure, making reproduction cost and real-time feasibility impossible to estimate; the paper is also silent on data licensing, deferring to upstream dataset terms on the HF card.

Open questions

  • How much drift actually accumulates over a 60-minute rollout? Without a per-minute metric (pose-following error, identity/layout consistency, frame quality), 'the 60-minute endpoints remain recognizable' is an eyeball claim.
  • Does the unified recipe hold at scale — would the 14B/22B/33B routes, once distilled, beat the 5B, or does the shared interface cap the larger backbones? The missing checkpoints prevent the community from answering it.
  • What does the four-step causal student actually cost to serve? Interactive use hinges on frames-per-second on a specific GPU at a specific resolution, and neither is reported.
