Optimal Block Time for AMM Liquidity Providers under Jump-Diffusion Prices

📰 Latest · 2026-09-02 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-02

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-31
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30321

The industry consensus that faster blocks reduce AMM liquidity-provider losses only holds under Brownian prices; once real crypto price jumps are modeled, LVR splits into a schedule-governed diffusion channel and an irreducible jump floor no block time can eliminate — at Ethereum's 12s slot only about three quarters of LP loss is addressable by block-time policy, and the LP-side optimum sits near 8s, not sub-second.

Executive summary

Nils Bundi (ZHAW) extends the Loss-versus-Rebalancing (LVR) framework of Milionis et al. from geometric Brownian motion to a Merton jump-diffusion reference price for a constant-product AMM. The main theorem shows the LVR rate separates additively into a diffusion channel scaled by the block-time-dependent trade-probability multiplier F(gamma/(sigma*sqrt(dt))) and a jump channel lambda*V*G(gamma;m,delta^2) that contains no block time at all, coupling only through an explicitly two-sided-bounded remainder. For symmetric jump laws the jump channel is an exact lower bound, so LVR does not vanish as block time goes to zero and approaches the floor only as sqrt(dt). Netting LVR against per-block consensus cost yields a closed-form cubic for the planner's optimal block time that is exactly invariant in pool size and, to the order at which the channels separate, in every jump parameter.

Problem: the 'shorten blocks' prescription rests on a GBM assumption

Under GBM with proportional fees and Poisson block arrivals, arbitrage profit scales with the stationary probability that an arriving block offers a profitable trade, which vanishes as the block time goes to zero — the standing argument behind sub-second block times on Solana, Ethereum L2s and app-chains. But crypto reference prices are empirically not GBM: they jump. A jump creates an instantaneous, block-time-independent price gap that is cleared in the very next block, and the LP eats the concavity loss regardless of how fast blocks are produced.

  • Milionis et al. give the classical CPMM LVR rate sigma^2*V/8, independent of block time at zero fees; with fees it is multiplied by a strictly increasing function of sigma*sqrt(dt)/gamma.
  • Cited empirics: Saef et al. find Bitcoin jumps on ~58% and Ethereum on ~32% of trading days in tick data from seven exchanges (Apr 2019 - Sep 2021), with systematic temporal clustering.
  • Two gaps identified: LVR has never been extended to jump-diffusion references, and the LP-side literature has no explicit planner's optimum exposing how jump parameters enter it.

Model: Merton jump-diffusion reference price against a CPMM

A single primitive is changed relative to Milionis et al.: the reference price S follows dS/S- = mu dt + sigma dW + (e^J - 1) dN with N Poisson of intensity lambda and log jump size J with finite activity (Merton J ~ N(m, delta^2) is the running instance). The CPMM holds x*y = L with pool value V(P) = 2*sqrt(L*P); its concavity is the source of LVR. A risk-neutral, capital-unconstrained arbitrageur sees S continuously but can only trade at block arrivals, and a proportional fee gamma creates a no-arbitrage band |log S - log P| <= gamma.

  • The log mispricing z = log(S/P) is driven by TWO point processes: exogenous jumps at rate lambda pushing z out of the band, and endogenous block-triggered arbitrage resets at rate 1/dt pushing it back. Every result follows from that asymmetry.
  • The mispricing is a persistent state: an arbitrage trade leaves z at the near band edge (+/- gamma), not at zero.
  • From Section 5 onward the jump law is assumed symmetric (m = 0); Remark 4 isolates exactly where symmetry is used — only to sign the remainder and prove the floor, not for Theorems 4.1, 5.1, 6.1.

Main results: additive channel decomposition and an exact jump floor

Theorem 4.1 (zero fee) decomposes the LVR rate into diffusion-LVR sigma^2*V/8 plus jump-LVR (lambda/2)*V*E[(e^(J/2)-1)^2], the latter carrying no block time. Theorem 5.1 reintroduces fees and discrete blocks: l(dt) = (sigma^2/8)*V*F(gamma/(sigma*sqrt(dt))) + lambda*V*G(gamma;m,delta^2) + E(dt), where F(kappa) = 1/(1 + sqrt(2)*kappa) is exactly the stationary probability of a profitable block and G = (1/8)*E[(|J|-gamma)^2 * 1{|J|>gamma}] is a functional of the jump law alone. Theorem 5.2 proves, with no approximation, that l(dt) >= lambda*V*G > 0 for every dt.

  • For symmetric Merton jumps G has the closed form (delta^2/8)*Psi(gamma/delta) with Psi(x) = 2[(1+x^2)Phi(-x) - x*phi(x)] — a fee discount at the jump-size scale delta, with no block time in it.
  • Proposition 1 bounds the interaction remainder two-sidedly under a numerically-checked mixing condition; at the calibration it lies in [-0.653, +0.849] bp/yr at 12 s and [-0.355, +0.464] at 50 ms, never exceeding 0.29% of the rate.
  • Corollary 1: the separated rate is strictly increasing in dt and approaches the floor only at the algebraic rate sigma^3*V*sqrt(dt)/(8*sqrt(2)*gamma).
  • Remark 7: to leading order in delta, a Poisson jump stream with std delta contributes the same zero-fee LVR as a diffusion of effective volatility delta*sqrt(lambda); fees break the symmetry because only the diffusion side gets the block-time-sensitive F discount.
  • Remark 8: fees split rather than reduce LVR, and on the jump channel the split ratio Psi(gamma/delta) has no dt in it — fees recover only 4.1% of jump-LVR at the calibration.

Planner's optimum: a cubic invariant in pool size and jump parameters

Per-block consensus cost is modeled as R(dt) = r/dt, calibrated from Ethereum's gross issuance and attributed to a pool pro-rata by value secured, so c is proportional to V. Minimizing W(dt) = l_0(dt) + c/dt reduces (Theorem 6.1) to the cubic eta*(1+eta)^2 = V*gamma^2/(8c) with dt_opt = 2*gamma^2/(sigma^2*eta^2), with a unique positive root in closed form via Cardano. Pool size cancels identically; the jump parameters never enter the first-order condition at all.

  • Four sensitivities (Remark 9): d(dt_opt)/d(lambda) = d/d(m) = d/d(delta) = 0 exactly for the separated rate; d(dt_opt)/dc > 0; d(dt_opt)/d(gamma) is U-shaped; d(dt_opt)/dV = 0 exactly.
  • Remark 10 is honest about the caveat: in the exact model the remainder carries both lambda and dt, so the invariance is not a theorem about l. The minimizer set is bounded to [7.3, 9.9] s around the separated optimum of 8.4 s.
  • Calibration: sigma and the jump parameters estimated from Binance ETH/USDT 5-minute closes over January 2020 - June 2026 (2,373 days), sigma from bipower variation, lambda and delta from the Lee-Mykland jump test at the 1% level.

Policy reading and what the paper does NOT claim

Shortening blocks can at most close the gap between the current rate and the jump floor, and that gap closes only as sqrt(dt). At every deployed block time in the paper's table, what actually limits block-time policy is the cost of consensus, not the jump floor — the diffusion channel does not become negligible until the low-millisecond range. Once the floor does bind, the remaining lever is the ratio gamma/delta (fee-tier design), plus batch auctions and LVR-rebate designs such as auction-managed AMMs, not the block schedule.

  • Ethereum's 12 s is somewhat too long under an LP-LVR-only objective, but the gain from moving to 8.4 s is under 2% (W falls from 539 to 533 bp/yr).
  • Sub-second targets pursued by other chains overshoot what LVR considerations alone would justify.
  • The conclusion is explicitly one-sided: a full welfare treatment with MEV redistribution, finality, censorship resistance and oracle freshness could push the optimum back up.

Key findings

  • The CPMM LVR rate under jump-diffusion separates additively into a diffusion channel carrying the block-time multiplier F(gamma/(sigma*sqrt(dt))) = 1/(1+sqrt(2)*kappa) and a jump channel lambda*V*G that contains no block time; the two meet only in a two-sided-bounded remainder (Theorems 4.1, 5.1, Proposition 1).
  • For symmetric jump laws satisfying a mild aggregation hypothesis, l(dt) >= lambda*V*G > 0 for every dt, exactly and with no remainder (Theorem 5.2) — an irreducible LVR floor no block-time reduction can eliminate. Fast blocks do not shrink jumps.
  • At the ETH/USDT calibration the diffusion ceiling is sigma^2/8 = 832 bp/yr and the jump floor is lambda*G = 125 bp/yr; at Ethereum's 12 s slot the rate is 471 bp/yr (73% diffusion), so only about three quarters of LP loss is schedule-addressable.
  • The floor is approached only as sqrt(dt): a 240x cut from 12 s to 50 ms removes just 89% of the diffusion term and leaves the rate 29% above the floor. The two channels are equal at dt = 0.75 s, and the diffusion term falls to a tenth of the floor only at 5.5 ms.
  • The LP-side planner's optimum solves the cubic eta*(1+eta)^2 = V*gamma^2/(8c) and is exactly invariant in pool size V and, for the separated rate, in every jump parameter; the remainder confines the shift to roughly [7.3, 9.9] s around 8.4 s.
  • At the issuance-calibrated baseline dt_opt = 8.4 s, about two thirds of Ethereum's slot, but the welfare gain is small — W falls from 539 to 533 bp/yr (under 2%). dt_opt moves nearly two orders of magnitude across the plausible consensus-cost range.
  • Fees split rather than reduce LVR: on the jump channel the split ratio has no dt in it, and fees recover only 4.1% of jump-LVR at the calibration, so the lever against the floor is fee-tier design, not the block schedule.

Notable quotes

This paper asks whether price jumps overturn that intuition, and finds that they impose a floor no block speed can breach.
Fast blocks do not imply small jumps, and the floor exists precisely because of that.
Jumps turn out to change what block time can achieve, not what it should be.

Limitations

  • The welfare objective is LP-LVR-only: MEV redistribution, finality, censorship resistance and oracle freshness are excluded, so the 8.4 s optimum bounds the LP-side contribution rather than settling block-time design.
  • The consensus cost equates observed native-token issuance with validator operating cost and attributes it pro-rata by value secured — a proxy that overstates the cost where issuance is a growth subsidy and understates it where MEV and fees fund validators; dt_opt is highly sensitive to this calibration.
  • The pool is full-range while most LVR is paid on concentrated liquidity, where concentration inflates G for contained jumps but truncates the loss for range-crossing jumps; the arbitrageur is also idealized (unlimited capital, zero latency), weakest exactly in the sub-second regime the floor argument concerns.

Open questions

  • How does the jump floor behave for concentrated-liquidity AMMs, where curvature inflation and range-crossing truncation act on G in opposite directions depending on delta relative to the tick range width?
  • Does the channel separation and the invariance of the planner's optimum survive a deterministic proof-of-stake block schedule and a latency- and capital-constrained arbitrageur, rather than the Poisson-arrival idealization inherited from Milionis et al.?
  • If fee-tier design (gamma/delta) rather than block time is the real lever against the floor, what does the jointly optimal (fee tier, block time) policy look like once MEV, finality and oracle-freshness terms enter the welfare objective?
