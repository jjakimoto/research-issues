Tabular Deep Learning for Algorithmic Trading: Cross-Regime Bayesian Optimisation for Equity Signal Generation

📰 Latest · 2026-08-28 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-28

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-27
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27076

A dissertation that does what most 'ML beats the market' papers skip: it tunes hyperparameters against three statistically distinct market regimes at once, then reports the honest negative result that no tabular deep-learning architecture beats XGBoost alone. The headline 51.26% / Sharpe 2.44 hybrid rests on a survivorship-biased universe, one 252-day out-of-sample year and ~161 trialled configurations with no Deflated Sharpe correction — so the methodology transfers, the return number does not.

Executive summary

Five model classes (Logistic Regression, XGBoost, MLP, TabNet, FT-Transformer) plus a rank-aggregation ensemble are trained on ~750,000 daily stock-day observations from ~300 large-cap S&P 500 constituents (2015-2024), holding out 2025 as the only out-of-sample year. The novelty is the objective function: Optuna/TPE Bayesian optimisation (30 trials per model) scores each configuration on backtested return, Sharpe and drawdown across three expanding-window validation folds (2022 bear, 2023 recovery, 2024 bull) simultaneously, with quadratic penalties punishing any single-regime collapse. No tabular deep-learning model beats gradient-boosted trees individually, but rank-aggregating XGBoost with TabNet — chosen for low pairwise CAPM R-squared (0.028) rather than raw performance — yields the only statistically significant CAPM alpha (0.423, p=0.011) with near-zero beta.

The core idea: regime robustness as a tuning constraint, not a detection problem

Most regime-aware equity ML detects the current state and switches models. The author argues this imports its own error source — regime misclassification propagates into model selection, and per-state tuning multiplies overfitting risk. Instead, regime heterogeneity is folded directly into the hyperparameter objective: every trial is fully trained and backtested on all three validation folds.

  • Score = 0.4*(mean_return/0.15) + 0.4*(mean_Sharpe/1.5) - 0.2*(mean_drawdown/0.10), minus penalties.
  • Penalties are quadratic on any negative-return fold, any negative-Sharpe fold, and any fold drawdown above 15%; a hard 10.0 floor penalty kills any configuration returning below -20% in any regime.
  • Targets calibrated to beat the passive benchmark over the training period: 15% return (index mean 14.3%), Sharpe 1.5 (index 0.93), drawdown 10% (index mean annual 13.1%).
  • All objective weights were fixed before experimentation specifically so the objective itself would not become an overfitting surface.
  • Portfolio size n was tuned jointly with model hyperparameters over the range 5 to 8.

Evaluation protocol and leakage discipline

A clean temporal holdout: train/validate 2015-2024, test on 2025 only. Three expanding-window folds replace k-fold, which is explicitly rejected as look-ahead-inducing. Pairwise two-sample KS tests on daily returns and 5-day realised volatility confirm all fold pairs are distributionally distinct (p<0.001), and a further test confirms train-vs-2025 shift, so the out-of-sample year is genuinely a different regime.

  • Lag schedule to block look-ahead: monthly macro shifted forward 21 trading days, quarterly fundamentals 63 days, weekly jobless claims 5 days, news and search volume 1 day.
  • One-day purge at each fold boundary; RobustScaler fitted per-fold on the training portion only.
  • Target is a cross-sectional next-day return percentile: top decile long, bottom decile short, middle 80% hold — so random baseline precision is 10%.
  • Class imbalance handled with balanced class weights for tree/linear models and class-weighted cross-entropy (label smoothing 0.1) for the deep models.
  • Multicollinearity screened via VIF (threshold 10), with some high-VIF features deliberately retained for domain relevance.

Backtest realism: costs, execution, and what is NOT modelled

Dollar-neutral, unlevered long-short portfolio: $10M split evenly across the long and short books, 100% gross exposure, rebalanced daily, executed at market-on-close. Costs are a flat 2.2 basis points per trade charged per leg on entry and exit, justified against a reported 2.84bp mean effective spread for S&P 500 constituents. The gaps are acknowledged rather than hidden.

  • Short-borrow costs are excluded and are highest in high-volatility regimes — exactly when the short signal is strongest — so short-side returns are overstated.
  • Market impact is unmodelled; the author concedes even n=6 breaches conventional issuer-concentration limits and suits only prop desks or unconstrained hedge funds.
  • Features needing same-day prices are built from a pre-close snapshot, with the close used as an execution proxy.
  • Sharpe uses excess returns with a fixed 3.6% annualised risk-free rate applied identically to strategy and benchmark.

Results: trees win individually, the ensemble wins on orthogonality

Aggregate classification metrics are near-useless here and the paper says so: accuracy spans only 0.786-0.799 across all six models because 80% of labels are 'hold'. LR vs XGBoost is the cleanest illustration — accuracy 0.789 vs 0.794, per-class precision essentially tied, but out-of-sample returns of -5.69% vs +33.61%.

  • The Hybrid's edge is attributed to error decorrelation, not accuracy: pairwise CAPM R-squared between XGBoost and TabNet is 0.028 versus 0.149 between XGBoost and MLP.
  • Rank aggregation (averaging cross-sectional ranks) is used instead of probability averaging so a poorly calibrated but overconfident model cannot dominate.
  • Friedman test across the six strategies fails to reject (p=0.352); Wilcoxon vs S&P 500 fails for every model including the Hybrid (p=0.682) — the alpha comes from a few large-magnitude days, notably the April 2025 tariff selloff.
  • Retraining cadence is architecture-constrained: XGBoost tunes in 10.8 minutes (overnight-feasible), TabNet in 19.6 hours (weekend-only); per-day inference stays under 60 ms.

Robustness: the random-null ablation is the strongest evidence

Gaussian noise is injected into standardised test features at sigma in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50} across 20 seeds, plus a random-null baseline replacing all features with noise. The null returns -8.21% with Sharpe -1.44, which rules out backtest mechanics as the source of the 2.44. SHAP is computed separately for long and short signals and aggregated by source category.

  • Quarterly Hybrid precision is stable and well above the 10% random baseline: long 23.06-25.78%, short 24.34-26.67% in every quarter, despite KS tests confirming each quarter is a distinct environment.
  • Noise degradation is NOT monotonic: sigma=0.10 (34.36%) outperforms sigma=0.05 (25.17%), and sigma=0.50 (6.66%) outperforms sigma=0.20 (2.72%) — looser than the 'gradual degradation then collapse' framing.
  • Technical features dominate SHAP attribution in every model (40-63%); macro plus alternative together are under 6% for XGBoost.
  • The pre-registered hypothesis that alternative data would account for at least 10% of mean SHAP attribution is REJECTED for XGBoost (1.63% long / 2.89% short) but satisfied by LR and MLP — attribution is architecture-dependent.

Key findings

  • The honest negative result: no tabular deep-learning architecture beats XGBoost individually on cross-sectional equity prediction — XGBoost returns 33.61% out-of-sample versus TabNet 9.30% and FT-Transformer 3.08%.
  • Value is combinatorial, not standalone: rank-aggregating XGBoost with TabNet produces the only significant CAPM alpha (0.423, p=0.011) with beta 0.048, and the partner was selected for LOW error correlation (R-squared 0.028), not standalone strength.
  • Classification metrics are actively misleading for trading: LR and XGBoost have near-identical accuracy (0.789 vs 0.794) yet return -5.69% vs +33.61%, because accuracy is dominated by the 80% 'hold' class that is never traded.
  • The random-null ablation is the strongest evidence in the paper — replacing all features with Gaussian noise drops the Hybrid to -8.21% return and Sharpe -1.44, ruling out backtest mechanics as the source.
  • Signal precision holds across all four out-of-sample quarters (23-27% vs a 10% random baseline) even though KS tests confirm each quarter is a distinct regime.
  • Alternative data is genuinely secondary: under 6% combined macro+alternative SHAP attribution for XGBoost, and the pre-registered 10%-attribution hypothesis is rejected for the best model.

Notable quotes

A fixed cost of 2.2 basis points per trade accounts for transaction costs and slippage, charged per leg on entry and exit, including both legs of a same-day flip.
A one-day purge was applied at each fold boundary, removing the final training observation whose label window extended into the validation period.
Also, reported figures reflect the selection of the best-performing configuration from approximately 161 trialled across the five model classes and their ensemble combinations.
Short selling introduces costs not captured in the 2.2 basis point assumption.

Limitations

  • Survivorship bias: the universe is ~300 S&P 500 constituents identified at the END of the sample, so delisted and index-removed underperformers are excluded and absolute returns are overstated.
  • Selection over ~161 trialled configurations with NO Deflated Sharpe Ratio correction — the author explicitly says the PSR of 0.960 must be read as an upper bound — and a single 252-day out-of-sample year gives weak statistical power.
  • No single-regime baseline was tuned under an identical objective, so the study cannot claim cross-regime tuning BEATS conventional tuning, only that its configurations generalise; short-borrow costs and market impact are also unmodelled.

Open questions

  • Does cross-regime tuning actually beat single-regime tuning? The controlled A/B — identical search space, trial count and protocol under both objectives — is never run, and it is the experiment the central claim needs.
  • Does the result survive a point-in-time universe and rolling multi-year out-of-sample evaluation rather than one 2025 window?
  • How much of the 51.26% survives stock-specific borrow costs, market-impact modelling at institutional AUM, and the concentration limits the author admits n=6 already violates?
