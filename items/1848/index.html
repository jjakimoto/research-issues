    Nested Clustered Optimization Is One End of a Schur Bridge, and the Interior Is Sometimes Provably Better

    ← Go backTaming the Option Factor Zoo: A High-Dimensional AnalysisNextAnalyzing and Mitigating Cost-Inefficient Behaviors in… →
    📰 Latest · 2026-09-28 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-28

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-18
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21271

    Lopez de Prado's NCO turns out to be the gamma=0 end of a one-parameter Schur-complement family. Under a rank-one 'gateway' model, the gamma=1 end is the unconstrained global minimum-variance portfolio, and no linear solve is larger than a cluster or the number of clusters. That gives NCO users a principled coupling knob, with theory for when an interior setting beats both NCO and GMV under estimation noise. There is no backtest yet: the evidence is theorems plus exact toy examples.

    Executive summary

    Cotton carries his Schur-complementary allocation idea (which bridges HRP and minimum variance on a bisection tree) over to NCO's flat partition. Block inversion shows the GMV portfolio already has NCO's two-tier shape, with each cluster's covariance block replaced by its Schur complement against every other asset. Conditioning instead on one 'knot' asset per other cluster (a Vecchia-style truncation) is exact under a rank-one 'gateway' model of cross-cluster dependence. Damping that conditioning by gamma in [0,1] gives a bridge with NCO at 0 and GMV at 1. Under symmetric covariance estimation noise, the out-of-sample-optimal gamma can be strictly interior or stay at full coupling. The paper gives a local theorem at the GMV end and exact examples of both outcomes, including a closed-form interior optimum.

    Problem: NCO's blind spot and the cost of fixing it

    NCO optimizes each cluster on its own covariance block, then optimizes across the resulting cluster portfolios. The only way one cluster's weights respond to another cluster is through its scalar budget. Block inversion shows the unconstrained GMV direction has the same two-tier shape, but each cluster's piece is a generalized minimum-variance direction on the cluster's covariance conditional on all other assets. All cross-cluster information enters through that conditioning, and there is no cross-cluster optimizer. NCO is the same shape with the conditioning removed. Restoring it naively costs an inverse of size n minus the cluster size for each cluster, which is exactly what two-stage methods exist to avoid.

    • All exactness results are for the unconstrained, fully invested, long-short problem.
    • With box or long-only constraints, no block decomposition of the optimum is exact.

    Knots and the gateway model (Vecchia-inspired truncation)

    Each cluster gets one 'knot' asset. Under the gateway model, each non-knot member's residual after regression on its own knot is uncorrelated with every asset outside the cluster, so every cross-cluster covariance block has rank at most one. Proposition 1 then shows that conditioning a cluster on the other clusters' knots equals conditioning it on everything, which needs only a (k-1)x(k-1) inverse. Replacing each conditioning set by a small subset is the device used in Vecchia approximations from spatial statistics. The recipe is 'Vecchia-inspired' rather than a true Vecchia approximation, because the per-cluster conditionals are cyclic and form no joint law.

    • Testing the model: check the gateway condition directly on the sample covariance. Regressing members on the knots and checking that the other-knot coefficients vanish is NOT a valid test.
    • The raw size of the residual does not measure the loss. The correct loss is the omitted Schur correction (a diagnostic that needs a large inverse, computed once).
    • Knot choice: in a latent cluster-factor model the gateway model holds to the extent the knot is the factor. The member most aligned with the cluster's first principal component approximates it.

    The bridge construction

    Damp the conditioned pair: Q_i(gamma) = Sigma_ii - gamma * Sigma_{i,P-i} Sigma_{P-i,P-i}^{-1} Sigma_{P-i,i}, with b_i(gamma) defined the same way. Take the direction d_i = Q_i^{-1} b_i and form w_gamma proportional to D(D'Sigma D)^{-1} D'u. This is the same problem Sigma^{-1}u solves, restricted to the span of the k cluster directions. With u = 1, gamma = 0 is exactly NCO (minimum variance at both tiers). Under the gateway model, gamma = 1 is the global minimum-variance portfolio.

    • With u = mu the direction is max-Sharpe, but its fully invested multiple is max-Sharpe only if 1'Sigma^{-1}mu > 0.
    • A risk-parity outer tier still runs, but nothing is exact at gamma = 1.
    • The flat bridge agrees with the tree (HRP-to-MV) Schur recursion at gamma = 1 but differs in the interior, because the tree compounds damping along the path.

    Structure theorems under the gateway model

    Proposition 3: the outer covariance equals the knot covariance scaled by each cluster portfolio's knot beta, plus a diagonal of member residual variances. That diagonal is a ridge that raises the smallest eigenvalue, which the paper offers as one explanation of why NCO's outer tier is stable. Proposition 4: along the bridge only the knot exposures move. The member part depends on neither gamma nor any other cluster, so each cluster collapses to its knot plus one independent asset. Proposition 5: a common gamma damps clusters unequally, so a knot that the other knots nearly explain barely moves until gamma is close to 1. Proposition 6 gives the noiseless variance lost at each gamma in closed form: gamma = 1 is the unique minimizer unless the whole bridge is already optimal.

    • With u = 1, a member earns weight in proportion to its shortfall in beta to the knot (1 - beta), per unit of residual variance.
    • Equicorrelated knots: the knot exposure falls from 1 (NCO) to 1/(1+(k-1)rho) (GMV). More knots and higher correlation mean a longer bridge.

    Where to sit under estimation error

    Let the estimate be Sigma + tau*E, with E sign-symmetric noise, and F(gamma, tau) the expected out-of-sample variance with the partition and knots held fixed. Proposition 7: measured from the (noisy) NCO end, the extra noise cost of moving away from NCO is of order gamma*tau^2. NCO has an estimation cost of its own, because its outer tier reads cross-cluster covariance. Corollary 3: if the noiseless variance is decreasing at gamma = 0, NCO is strictly suboptimal for small noise. Proposition 8: near the GMV end, the optimum is gamma~ = 1 - (G'(1)/V0''(1)) tau^2 + O(tau^4). It is interior when G'(1) > 0 and stays at full coupling when G'(1) < 0. The gain from moving is only fourth order in the noise.

    • The architecture does not fix the sign of G'(1). Both signs occur, strictly, on open parameter sets.
    • Exact derivatives come from truncated bivariate jets in rational arithmetic. A companion verification script is at github.com/microprediction/schur.

    Rank deficiency, cost, and significance

    With a singular (knot) covariance, the gamma parameterization itself becomes singular. With duplicated knots, the bridge holds equal weights for every gamma < 1 (variance 3/8 against an optimum of 1/3), and per-cluster pseudoinverses at gamma = 1 give 1/2. Proposition 9 repairs this with a bridge in effective knot exposure lambda, which is continuous from NCO to minimum variance. On cost, no step forms an n x n inverse: the largest solve is max(k, largest cluster), as in NCO. Conceptually, NCO is shown to be a relaxation of Markowitz rather than a separate heuristic family.

    • Design choice made explicit: should clusters share a nominal gamma or an effective lambda?
    • No empirical comparison is included; the author leaves it, and extending knot conditioning to the tree, to future work.

    Key findings

    • NCO (minimum variance at both tiers) is exactly the gamma=0 end of a damped Schur-complement bridge. Under the rank-one gateway model, the gamma=1 end is the unconstrained global minimum-variance portfolio, and no linear solve exceeds max(k, largest cluster size).
    • Under the gateway model only the knot exposures move along the bridge: NCO and GMV hold the same member positions inside each cluster up to scale.
    • NCO's outer-tier covariance equals the knot covariance scaled by knot betas plus a diagonal ridge of member residual variances, which gives a structural account of NCO's numerical stability.
    • Under symmetric estimation noise, the optimal coupling near the GMV end is 1 - (G'(1)/V0''(1)) tau^2 + O(tau^4). The sign of G'(1) decides between an interior optimum and full coupling, both occur, and the improvement is only O(tau^4).
    • Exact examples: gamma* = 2/3 (k=2, c=1/4, delta=1), gamma* ~= 0.5587 in a 4-asset quartic, and gamma* = 10/11 for k=10, delta=12 at tau=1/4. A 3-cluster example with G'(1) ~= -0.00585 keeps full coupling optimal.
    • With a rank-deficient knot covariance the gamma parameterization is singular (duplicated knots: bridge stuck at variance 3/8 vs optimum 1/3). A bridge in effective knot exposure restores continuity.

    Notable quotes

    Nested clustered optimization has been treated as belonging to a different family from optimization proper: a clustering heuristic that quarantines the instability of Markowitz rather than a relaxation of it.
    NCO and the global optimum hold the same member position inside every cluster, up to the cluster’s scale.
    Neither end owns the bridge. An interior optimum is not a consequence of the architecture, and a statement that it is generic needs a noise model.
    A member may be correlated with anything, but its correlation with other clusters passes entirely through its knot.

    Limitations

    • Exactness holds only for the unconstrained, fully invested, long-short problem under the strong gateway (rank-one cross-cluster) model, which clustering alone does not imply. With long-only or box constraints, or a risk-parity outer tier, nothing is exact at gamma=1.
    • The evidence is purely theoretical: local small-noise theorems and hand-built exact examples, with no backtest or empirical comparison against NCO, GMV or HRP. The gain from moving to an interior gamma is only fourth order in the noise.
    • The analysis holds the partition and knots fixed as noise varies. That is not how ONC/NCO is used in practice, where clusters are re-estimated.

    Open questions

    • How should gamma (or a shared effective lambda vs a per-cluster gamma) be chosen honestly in practice, e.g. by purged cross-validation? Does an interior setting beat NCO and denoised GMV out of sample on real data?
    • Under realistic sampling noise (sample covariance, possibly after Marcenko-Pastur denoising), which sign of G'(1) is typical, i.e. is an interior optimum generic?
    • Can the knot-sufficiency result make Schur-complementary allocation on the HRP tree cheaper, and how robust is the bridge when the gateway model only holds approximately?
