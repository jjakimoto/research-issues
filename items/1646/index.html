    Nested Clustered Optimization Is One End of a Schur Bridge, and the Interior Is Sometimes Provably Better

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-18

    NCO is not a separate heuristic family from Markowitz: it is the gamma=0 endpoint of a one-parameter Schur-complement bridge whose gamma=1 end is the global minimum-variance portfolio, reachable at NCO's own computational cost (no solve bigger than a cluster or the cluster count) — and under estimation error the best place to sit is sometimes provably strictly inside.

    Executive summary

    Peter Cotton (Microprediction) shows that nested clustered optimization (Lopez de Prado's NCO) and the unconstrained global minimum-variance portfolio are the two ends of a single damped-Schur-complement family indexed by gamma in [0,1], built for a flat partition rather than a bisection tree. The cost problem — the Schur complement of a cluster against everything else needs a near-full-size inverse — is solved by conditioning each cluster only on one designated 'knot' per other cluster, a Vecchia-style truncation of the conditioning set; the paper gives the exact rank-one 'gateway model' of cross-cluster dependence under which this truncation loses nothing. Under the gateway model the structure collapses pleasantly: only the knots move along the bridge, each by an explicit effective damping fraction, and the variance given up at each gamma is closed form. Section 7 analyses where to sit under estimation noise, with a local theorem at the minimum-variance end and exact rational-arithmetic examples of both a strictly interior optimum and a locally optimal full coupling.

    The blind spot NCO has, and what block inversion says

    NCO's inner step reads only a cluster's own covariance block, so the only channel through which one cluster's weights respond to another is a scalar budget. Block inversion shows the true global minimum-variance direction has exactly the same two-tier shape, except each cluster block is replaced by its Schur complement against every other asset, and each cluster solves a 'generalized minimum-variance direction' problem against a companion vector rather than the ones-vector. So NCO is the global optimum with the conditioning deleted — restoring it naively costs an (n - n_i) inverse per cluster, which is precisely what a two-stage method exists to avoid.

    • Global MV direction = one generalized minimum-variance direction per cluster on the cluster's covariance conditional on everything else
    • 'There is no cross-cluster optimizer in the global optimum' — all cross-cluster information enters via conditioning
    • NCO = same shape, conditioning removed

    The gateway model and the knot trick

    Each cluster designates one member as its knot. The gateway model asserts that, for every cluster, the residual of the remaining members after regression on their own knot is uncorrelated with every asset outside the cluster — so each cross-cluster block of Sigma has rank at most one. Proposition 1 then shows conditioning a cluster on the other clusters' knots is identical to conditioning on everything, reducing the inverse to size k (the number of clusters). Under Gaussianity this is equivalent to a block-Vecchia factorization of the joint density (Corollary 1). The author is careful that the recipe is 'Vecchia-inspired rather than a Vecchia approximation' since the conditionals are mutually cyclic and no approximate joint law is formed.

    • Gateway model = rank-one cross-cluster dependence, all routed through the knot
    • Testing it by regressing members on knots does NOT test it (Remark 2); the correct check is the cross-cluster residual matrix from the sample covariance
    • Knot selection heuristic (Remark 4): under a latent cluster-factor model, pick the member with highest variance share explained by the factor, approximated by the cluster's first principal component

    The bridge and its two ends

    Damping the conditioning by gamma gives each cluster a conditioned (matrix, vector) pair and hence a direction; the bridge portfolio solves, on the span of those k directions, the same problem the global optimum solves on the whole space. Proposition 2: at gamma=0 the recipe is exactly NCO with minimum variance at both tiers; at gamma=1, under the gateway model, the non-vanishing columns are the blocks of the global minimum-variance direction. The unnormalized form avoids the singularity when a cluster's total weight crosses zero; the two-tier 'budget times normalized cluster portfolio' reading fails only for self-financing (zero-net-weight) cluster positions.

    • Remark 7: composition of Schur complements means the tree recursion of Schur-complementary allocation agrees with the flat construction at gamma=1 but differs in the interior, because tree damping compounds along the path
    • Extends to max-Sharpe (swap the ones-vector for mu) and budget-constrained mean-variance; risk parity is not of this form and nothing is exact at gamma=1 there
    • All exactness claims are for the unconstrained, fully invested, long-short problem — box or long-only constraints break every block decomposition

    Structure under the gateway model: only the knots move

    Proposition 3 shows the outer covariance is the knot covariance scaled by cluster-portfolio knot betas plus a diagonal of residual variances — a ridge that raises the smallest eigenvalue, 'one reading of why the outer tier of NCO is stable'. Proposition 4 decouples each direction into a member part that depends on neither gamma nor any other cluster, and a knot exposure that does all the moving. Corollary 2 compresses an arbitrarily large cluster into its knot plus one independent asset. Proposition 5 gives the effective damping: a common nominal gamma damps clusters unequally, and a knot that the other knots nearly explain (small unexplained variance r_i) barely moves until gamma is very close to 1. Proposition 6 gives the lost precision in closed form via Woodbury.

    • NCO and the global optimum hold the same within-cluster member positions up to scale
    • Equicorrelated unit-variance knots with correlation rho: knot exposure falls from 1 at the NCO end to 1/(1+(k-1)rho) at the MV end — more knots and higher correlation means a longer bridge
    • Degenerate partitions (one asset per cluster, or one cluster) make the bridge constant; it has length only strictly between the two

    Where to sit under estimation error

    With Sigma-hat = Sigma + noise (symmetric in law), Proposition 7 shows the incremental cost of moving off the noisy NCO end is order tau^2, and Corollary 3 gives strict suboptimality of NCO whenever the noiseless profile is strictly decreasing at gamma=0. Proposition 8 is the local theorem at the minimum-variance end: the sign of G'(1) decides whether the optimum stays at full coupling or moves strictly inside, with the interior optimum displaced by an amount of second order in the noise while the gain is fourth order — 'a large gain at finite noise should not be inferred from the local sign'. Exact examples supply both signs.

    • Identical-clusters family: closed-form optimum gamma* = (1+2(k-2)c) / (2[1+(k-3)c]), independent of the member variance parameter delta
    • Same symmetric k=10, c=1/4 model: for delta<8 the optimum is interior under small noise, for delta>8 it stays at full coupling, delta=8 is decided at higher order
    • Mechanism (Section 7.1): the full-coupling knot allocation is convex in the estimated correlation, so unbiased noise biases expected knot allocation upward; damping toward NCO also raises it, and G'(1)'s sign is the outcome of that competition

    Rank deficiency and cost

    Section 8 shows the separation between NCO and the optimum reasserts itself when the knot covariance is singular: with two clusters whose knots are exact duplicates, the bridge holds equal weights at every gamma<1, and the gamma->1 and regularization->0 limits do not commute. The gamma parameterization is itself singular there, not merely numerically unstable. Proposition 9 offers an alternative path parameterized by effective exposure lambda that is continuous, positive definite, NCO at lambda=0 and minimum variance at lambda=1 — corresponding to cluster-specific gamma_i = lambda/(r_i + (1-r_i)lambda). Section 9: no step forms a full inverse; the largest solve is max(k, cluster size).

    • Duplicated-knot example: global optimum variance 1/3, bridge at every gamma<1 gives 3/8, and gamma=1 with pseudoinverses gives 1/2
    • Each local pseudoinverse discards its own knot; together the clusters discard a common return that is still useful
    • Design question made explicit: should clusters share a nominal gamma or an effective lambda?

    Key findings

    • Proposition 1: under the gateway model, conditioning a cluster on the other clusters' knots equals conditioning it on everything — collapsing an (n - n_i) inverse to a k x k one, so the bridge's largest linear solve is max(cluster size, number of clusters), the same as NCO.
    • Proposition 2: gamma=0 is exactly NCO with minimum variance at both tiers; gamma=1 recovers the unconstrained global minimum-variance portfolio under the gateway model.
    • Proposition 4: along the bridge the within-cluster member weights are invariant in gamma and in every other cluster — only the knot exposures reprice. Proposition 5 gives the effective damping, so a shared nominal gamma damps clusters unequally, by their unexplained knot variance r_i.
    • Proposition 8 local theorem: the sign of G'(1) determines whether full coupling stays optimal out of sample; the interior displacement is O(tau^2) while the gain is O(tau^4).
    • Closed-form symmetric family: for k clusters with knot correlation c, gamma* = (1+2(k-2)c)/(2[1+(k-3)c]); with k=2, c=1/4, delta=1 this is 2/3, with exact gains 1/576 over NCO and 1/900 over full minimum variance.
    • Duplicated knots (harmless redundancy) break the recipe: optimum variance 1/3, bridge 3/8 for all gamma<1, pseudoinverse variant 1/2; the gamma->1 and regularization->0 limits do not commute.

    Notable quotes

    A member may be correlated with anything, but its correlation with other clusters passes entirely through its knot.
    NCO and the global optimum hold the same member position inside every cluster, up to the cluster’s scale.
    The same symmetric model sits at full coupling under small noise and moves inside under larger noise.
    Neither end owns the bridge. An interior optimum is not a consequence of the architecture, and a statement that it is generic needs a noise model.

    Limitations

    • The gateway model is a strong rank-one assumption on cross-cluster dependence that clustering alone does not imply, it cannot be tested by the obvious knot regression (Remark 2), and exactness at gamma=1 holds only under it.
    • Every exactness statement is for the unconstrained, fully invested, long-short problem — with box or long-only constraints no block decomposition of the optimum is exact; risk parity outer tiers get nothing exact at gamma=1.
    • There is no empirical evaluation: the estimation-error results are a local theorem at gamma=1 plus hand-built exact examples under stylized noise, and the author explicitly declines to claim the effective-exposure path performs better out of sample.

    Open questions

    • Can Proposition 1 be pushed onto the bisection tree, taking the complement against a sibling block's knots rather than the whole sibling block?
    • The whole-matrix noise analysis of the tree relies on a scalar reduction specific to the tree — what replaces it for the flat partition?
    • How should gamma (or the effective lambda) be chosen in practice, and does an interior gamma actually beat NCO and minimum variance on real covariance estimates?
