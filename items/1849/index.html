    Taming the Option Factor Zoo: A High-Dimensional Analysis

    Date: 2026-09-28

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-25
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31263

    This paper tests 137 option-implied signals against 160 equity factors with the Feng-Giglio-Xiu double-selection LASSO. About 91% of the option factors' variance is already spanned by the equity zoo. The small part that survives is concentrated in jump tails, kurtosis and implied-volatility convexity. For a quant, that means you can skip most option-surface features, but tail and convexity measures are worth building.

    Executive summary

    The authors build 137 option-implied characteristics for optionable U.S. stocks over 2004-2023 (234 months). They screen these down to 20 representative long-short factors using gradient-boosted trees with SHAP attributions. Each factor is then tested against 160 equity factors (152 from Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen characteristics plus 8 Kenneth French factors) with the DS-LASSO of Feng, Giglio, and Xiu (2020). As a group, the option factors are largely redundant: equity factors explain 91% of their variance, the GRS test does not reject (p=0.25), and the gain in maximum Sharpe ratio (3.35 to 3.74) is not significant. Individually, however, 8 of the 20 have non-zero SDF loadings at 5%, and 4 survive Bonferroni correction.

    Problem and motivation

    Almost every factor in the equity zoo is sorted on past prices or accounting data. Option prices are different: they are forward-looking and reveal the risk-neutral distribution of returns, including the tails. Earlier work shows that option measures predict returns. This paper asks a narrower question: do factors sorted on those measures add to the stochastic discount factor (SDF) once a high-dimensional equity factor zoo is controlled for?

    • Complements Neuhierl et al. (2023), which showed option characteristics predict returns beyond firm characteristics.
    • Related to Bali and Murray (2024), who propose a low-dimensional factor model for optionable stocks.
    • Studies stock returns, not option returns.

    Data and factor construction

    Equity characteristics come from Jensen et al. (2023), built on CRSP/Compustat. Option characteristics come from CBOE data. Each stock's risk-neutral density is estimated with the Breeden-Litzenberger approach on a kernel-smoothed implied-volatility surface. The characteristics cover several families: Bakshi-Kapadia-Madan implied moments, Bollerslev-Todorov-Xu (BTX) jump and tail decompositions, a rare-disaster index, implied-volatility surface shape (smirk, convexity, wing slopes), SVIX, and option-implied estimates of physical moments. Factors are value-weighted top-minus-bottom decile sorts on S&P 500 constituents at a 30-day option maturity, rebalanced monthly. Test assets are 3x2 characteristic-by-size portfolios built from all stocks in the combined universe.

    • Final universe: 7,126 stocks and 824,081 firm-months. Each factor leg averages 42 stocks per month; each test-asset leg averages 369.
    • Returns are gross; transaction costs are ignored.
    • A daily-frequency version failed: the covariance matrices were near-singular and the DS-LASSO did not converge.

    Methodology

    The study has three steps, all in-sample. (1) Screen: 91% of the option factors have a variance inflation factor above 10, and 62% above 100. A histogram gradient-boosting regressor fits the same-month equal-weighted average test-asset return on the 137 option factor returns. Factors are ranked by mean absolute SHAP value, and the top set covering up to 90% of attribution is kept: 20 factors with 89.3% of attribution. (2) DS-LASSO: the original Feng-Giglio-Xiu code is run unmodified for each option factor, reporting the scaled SDF loading lambda_s in basis points per month. (3) Aggregate tests: a GRS test of joint alphas, maximum Sharpe ratios using Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, and Stewart-Love redundancy indices from canonical correlation analysis, each compared with its value under pure noise.

    • Multiple-testing controls: Bonferroni (|t|>3.02 for 20 tests) and Benjamini-Hochberg at a 5% false discovery rate.
    • The screen is a contemporaneous fit, not a forecast. It also favours factors with high market exposure.

    Results

    Eight option factors have significant SDF loadings at 5%. Four survive Bonferroni: ivs_convexity, BTX_EQV_relTail_k, BTX_LJV_7_5 and bakshiKurt. Benjamini-Hochberg adds RND_kurt_eps. Measures of tail size, kurtosis and convexity load positively. Right-tail jump intensity and option-implied physical variance load negatively. For 5 of the 8 significant factors, the SDF loading has the opposite sign to the factor's average return. As a group, the option factors do not measurably expand the investment opportunity set.

    • Only bakshiKurt (t=-3.25) and ivs_convexity (t=-2.57) have average returns significantly different from zero.
    • Six-factor alphas are significant and negative for bakshiKurt (-10.5%), ivs_convexity (-16.7%), ivs_smirk (-12.6%) and SVIX2_put_annual (-9.1%).
    • Several factors have maximum drawdowns above 90%, and some are strongly negatively skewed.

    Robustness

    The full analysis, including the screen, is rerun at option maturities of 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 30 and 60 days. The aggregate conclusions hold at every maturity. Which individual factors get selected does change. BTX_LJV_7_5 is selected at five maturities and is positive and significant at each. The kurtosis results appear only at 30 days, and the sign of the P_implQ_var loading flips across maturities.

    • The GRS test rejects only at 60 days (p=0.043).
    • Out-of-sample RMSE of the screen ranges from about 3.5% to 8.2% per month across windows.

    Key findings

    • The 160 equity factors explain 91.0% of the 20 option factors' variance in-sample, against a noise level of about 69%.
    • The option factors explain 35.4% of the equity factors' variance, against a noise level of about 9%.
    • The GRS test of joint option-factor alphas does not reject (statistic 1.25, p=0.25). The rise in maximum Sharpe ratio from 3.35 to 3.74 is not significant.
    • 8 of 20 option factors have non-zero SDF loadings at 5%. Four survive Bonferroni: implied-volatility convexity (t=3.92), share of variance from large jumps (t=3.65), left-tail jump variation (t=3.59) and risk-neutral kurtosis (t=3.15).
    • Across the seven maturities, equity factors explain 91-94% of option-factor variance, and GRS rejects only at 60 days (p=0.043).
    • The option characteristics are highly collinear: 91% have a VIF above 10 and 62% above 100. The SHAP screen keeps 20 of the 137 factors, covering 89.3% of attribution.

    Notable quotes

    We read this as a property of the redundant option zoo: when several measures carry nearly the same information, the screen picks one of them, and which one it picks varies.
    Option prices thus appear to add information about tail risk that characteristics built from past returns and accounting data do not fully capture, while most other option-implied information is already reflected in the equity factor zoo.
    With 20 test assets, 160 factors, and 234 months, the test has few residual degrees of freedom and limited power, so this non-rejection is weak evidence in either direction.
    Left-tail jump variation (BTX_LJV_7_5) is selected at five maturities and has a positive and significant loading at each of them.

    Limitations

    • Everything is in-sample. The screen and the tests use the same 234 months, so the test results depend on the screen, and there is no hold-out validation.
    • The 20 tested factors were chosen from 137, so the true number of tests exceeds 20. Even the Bonferroni correction understates the multiple-testing problem.
    • Factors are built only from S&P 500 constituents with gross returns and no transaction costs. The maximum Sharpe ratios are biased upward, and the choice of individual factors varies across maturities and subsamples.

    Open questions

    • Do the tail, kurtosis and convexity factors keep their SDF loadings in a proper out-of-sample design, with the screen fitted on one period and the tests run on another?
    • Does the extra tail-risk information extend beyond S&P 500 constituents, and is it concentrated in particular market conditions?
    • Why do bakshiKurt and ivs_convexity have positive SDF loadings despite significantly negative alphas?
