    The two square root laws of market impact and the role of sophisticated market participants

    Date: 2026-10-03

    Theme: Market impact as a trading constraint
    These papers derive concave market impact from explicit market mechanisms rather than treating costs as an arbitrary penalty. Together they connect latent liquidity, persistent order flow, execution rate, and participation to strategy capacity and manipulation-free execution models.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18283

    A microstructural derivation of volume- and participation-rate impact concavity, making square-root impact a concrete capacity and execution-cost constraint for quantitative strategies.

    Executive summary

    This paper extends a no-statistical-arbitrage Hawkes market-impact framework by adding a sophisticated participant who estimates an efficient price and submits opposing market orders. Under nearly unstable, long-memory Hawkes scaling, rescaled impact converges to a nonlinear Volterra system in which the Hawkes kernel produces temporal and cumulative-volume concavity while the reaction function determines the participation-rate response. Impact is increasing and, for a convex reaction, concave in participation; a quadratic reaction yields a large-participation square-root law, while the small-participation expansion is linear at leading order.

    Question and the two impact laws

    The paper seeks to disentangle cumulative volume from participation rate in the price response to a meta-order. Its targets are a square-root profile in cumulative volume at fixed participation and a crossover from approximately linear impact at small participation to square-root impact at large participation.

    • The meta-order volume by time t is represented separately from its intensity relative to ordinary market volume.
    • At fixed participation, cumulative volume grows with execution time, allowing temporal impact and volume impact to be linked.
    • The distinction matters because two schedules with the same total quantity can incur different costs when their participation rates differ.

    Hawkes market and sophisticated participant

    Ordinary buys and sells are independent Hawkes processes with a shared self-exciting kernel, while the buy meta-order is an independent inhomogeneous Poisson process. Because the meta-order is not self-exciting, a sophisticated participant can distinguish its long-run price contribution from the market's initial response, compute an efficient-price signal, and submit offsetting sell orders.

    • The no-arbitrage price anticipates future signed order flow.
    • The sophisticated trader's sell intensity is an increasing reaction to the gap between market and efficient prices; convexity supports the participation-concavity result.
    • The paper motivates a quadratic reaction by optimizing signal profit against square-root impact costs.
    • Pathwise meta-order impact is the transient-kernel convolution of meta-order trades minus the sophisticated participant's opposing trades.

    Scaling limit and nonlinear Volterra equation

    The asymptotic regime combines a nearly unstable Hawkes process with a power-law kernel tail, a meta-order whose expected size scales with ordinary market volume, and a similarly scaled reaction intensity. The main convergence theorem gives a deterministic nonlinear convolution system for normalized pathwise impact.

    • The Hawkes kernel approaches criticality and has a regularly varying tail.
    • The participation parameter controls meta-order intensity relative to background flow.
    • Limiting impact convolves net meta-order flow with permanent and transient response terms.
    • The reaction rate is the unique solution of a nonlinear Volterra equation obtained by applying the reaction function to transient net impact.

    Volume and participation-rate results

    The singular long-memory kernel controls the within-execution volume law, while the nonlinear reaction controls the participation law. For the empirically relevant one-half memory exponent and locally constant execution, integrating the transient kernel gives a square-root-in-time profile; because volume is proportional to time at fixed participation, this becomes square-root impact in cumulative volume.

    • After execution, the model produces power-law relaxation and separates permanent from transient impact.
    • Impact is increasing in participation and is concave when the reaction function is convex.
    • For a power-law reaction with exponent β, large-participation impact scales as participation to the power 1/β; β=2 yields a square root.
    • For a quadratic reaction, the small-participation expansion has a linear leading term and a negative quadratic correction.

    Significance and practical use

    The construction ties square-root impact to identifiable market mechanisms rather than imposing it as a reduced-form cost curve: persistent endogenous order flow shapes impact through time, and informed opposing flow shapes sensitivity to participation. The paper also develops comparison bounds, Euler simulation, and an Adomian expansion for evaluating the otherwise implicit Volterra solution.

    • After calibration, practitioners can simulate impact profiles for alternative schedules and substantial participation rates.
    • The scaling-limit cost replaces a random execution-cost term with an integral involving deterministic macroscopic impact and the schedule.
    • The mechanism differs from latent-order-book explanations because extra liquidity arrives as opposing market orders.
    • Participation rate becomes an explicit capacity constraint rather than impact depending only on total quantity.

    Key findings

    • A sophisticated participant reacting to the gap between market and efficient prices supplies a mechanism for participation-rate dependence in a Hawkes impact model.
    • The large-meta-order limit is characterized by a deterministic nonlinear Volterra equation coupling net flow to the participant's reaction rate.
    • Nearly critical, long-memory order flow generates the temporal concavity that becomes square-root cumulative-volume impact under a one-half memory exponent.
    • Impact is increasing in participation and becomes concave when the reaction function is convex.
    • A power-law reaction produces large-participation impact with reciprocal reaction exponent; quadratic response gives the square-root exponent while small participation remains linear to first order.

    Notable quotes

    In practice, the orders coming from a large metaorder induce a price drift and a liquidity imbalance which can be detected by some sophisticated market participant.
    Since trades are anonymous, the market digests the metaorder in the same way as the original order flow.
    This switch in regime is coming from the fact the metaorder is detected and the sophisticated participant starts reacting to this metaorder.
    The first is to use Monte-Carlo simulations to compute this expectation and deduce the cost of the underlying execution strategy.

    Limitations

    • The model normalizes all market-order sizes to one, treats the meta-order as an independent inhomogeneous Poisson process, and focuses on market-order execution.
    • The sophisticated participant is idealized as efficiently computing the fair-price signal and has no explicit inventory or risk constraints.
    • The central results are asymptotic and the paper does not calibrate or validate the full mechanism on an empirical meta-order dataset.

    Open questions

    • How do the scaling laws change when meta-orders use limit orders and displayed liquidity feeds back into order arrivals and price sensitivity?
    • Do noisy detection, heterogeneous sophisticated participants, and explicit inventory or risk limits preserve the predicted participation-rate crossover?
    • Can transaction-level data jointly calibrate the Hawkes kernel and reaction function and distinguish opposing-market-order and latent-liquidity mechanisms?
