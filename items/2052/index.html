    Self-Generated Feedback Destabilizes Test-Time Training: A Causal Decomposition of Long-Horizon Adaptation

    📰 Latest · 2026-10-07 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-07

    Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-10-04
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076

    If a model or agent keeps weight updates learned from its own outputs, the damage builds up over long horizons. TTT-E2E at 125M/760M/3B, Qwen3-4B with Adam, and LoRA agents all degrade, and the cause is the feedback loop (updated weights writing their own future training data), not synthetic text as such. Checking source fit, repetition or a 'generated' label does not catch it; committing an update only when it improves loss or reward on independent real evidence (Settlement) does.

    Executive summary

    The authors run TTT-E2E (125M, 760M, released 3B) and in-place Adam on Qwen3-4B over 128K-token streams in which the model generates and learns from its own chunks. They score each setup on separate human-written PG-19 passages. Keeping the generated-text updates (Closed Loop) worsens real-text NLL by +3.01, +6.00 and +0.40 nats over a Writes Off control that reads the same kind of text but throws the updates away. Three matched comparisons locate the cause. Fixed Generation (a frozen generator feeds the learner) removes 98.3%/98.8% of the gap. Recorded Replay splits harm from reading degraded tokens from extra harm stored in weights. A paired one-update test shows that an update can fit its source chunk while hurting the next real passage. The proposed fix, Settlement, commits a candidate update only if it does not raise loss on independent real text. It leaves endpoint gaps of .067/-.021 nats at 125M/760M, keeps real-text adaptation, and stops ALFWorld/WebShop agents from collapsing.

    Problem: learning from your own output

    Test-time training (TTT) updates 'fast weights' during inference, so information can outlive the attention window. That is attractive for long documents and long-running agents. When the model also writes its own future training data, a loop forms: W_t generates chunk x_t, the update on x_t gives W_t+1, and W_t+1 generates the next chunk. The paper calls processing text without a weight change 'reading' and keeping the update 'writing'. The main control, Writes Off, reads the same generated chunks but discards each update. The difference between the two therefore measures persistent self-writing, not mere exposure to generated text.

    • Outcome: H = (last minus first real-text NLL) minus the same change under Writes Off; positive means worse. E is the endpoint gap at the final evaluation.
    • Independent real text means held-out passages from later, disjoint regions of the same PG-19 book. After each evaluation the full state (fast weights, KV cache, positions, buffers) is restored, so evaluation never leaks into the stream.
    • On teacher-forced real-text streams, writing helps (-.0340 nats vs No Updates), so writing in itself is not the problem.

    Setup: TTT-E2E configs and Qwen3-4B with Adam

    The 125M TTT-E2E model has 12 layers and width 768. The first nine layers use an 8192-token sliding attention window, and each of the final three layers has a SwiGLU fast-weight MLP. One clipped inner-loop SGD step follows each 1024-token chunk. It was trained with the official 4800-step recipe on 2.5B DCLM tokens, then given a 32K length extension on PG-19. The authors trained the 760M model themselves and used the released 3B/128K checkpoint. Each 128K stream has an 8K real prefix, then 105 generated chunks (temperature 1, top-p 0.95) with 15 real-text evaluations plus an initial reference. The cross-scale suite uses six books, five seeds and batch width 8. For the non-TTT check, Qwen3-4B freezes everything except the final four mlp.down_proj matrices and takes one Adam step per chunk with gradient-norm clip 1. Its latest 8192 tokens are re-encoded each chunk, because naive KV truncation pushed loss from about 3.04 to 8.85.

    • Damage is cumulative. Within the original 8K+8K evaluation horizon, Closed Loop and Writes Off stay close; Closed Loop diverges only after many self-writes.
    • Qwen3-4B at LR 1e-4: self-writing H = 1.231 [.580, 2.350], while the same update on real text gives a .168 benefit. At LR 1e-5 the effect is unresolved (.026 [-.071, .122]).
    • The 760M and 3B lineages differ, so the paper explicitly does not claim a scaling law; magnitudes are not monotone in size.

    Causal decomposition: three matched comparisons

    (1) Fixed Generation: a frozen, separately cached W_0 generates every chunk while a separate learner still updates after each one. The learner is the model evaluated. Only the link from updates to future training text is cut. (2) Recorded Replay: a receiver adapted on other books processes a saved degraded stream twice from the same state, once read-only and once keeping updates. This holds content fixed and separates harm through attention from harm stored in weights. (3) Paired one-update branches: copy the weights and KV cache just before one generated chunk, keep the update in one copy and discard it in the other, then compare source-chunk NLL, next-real-passage NLL and future repetition.

    • Fixed Generation leaves .052 (125M) and .073 (760M) nats, removing 98.3% and 98.8% of the matched Closed Loop gap.
    • Update size does not explain the damage. Fixed Generation drifts farther from init than Closed Loop (.01387 vs .00857 relative drift), and Real-Text Learning drifts farther still (.01759) while improving NLL by .3544.
    • Replay at 125M: reading the degraded recording costs 1.606 nats, reading and writing costs 3.874, so writing stores an extra 2.268. At 3B, writing adds .1927 [.1043, .3272], positive for every source and receiver book.
    • One update after a Closed Loop history: source NLL -.0354 but next-real NLL +.0381. Mean cost grows from .006 nats early in the stream to .113 late; it stays near zero after Writes Off or Fixed Generation histories.

    Why updates fail to transfer: gradient conflict and heavy tails

    Before applying a candidate update, the authors measure the cosine between the generated-chunk gradient and the next real passage's gradient, plus the first-order predicted loss change g_real·ΔW. Across 96 paired updates per setting, both signals rank measured harm (Spearman -.502 to -.788 for cosine; +.632 to +.794 for first-order). Generated and real gradients are nearly orthogonal after Writes Off history (mean cosine -.005/-.004) but become opposed after Closed Loop history (-.093/-.103). Late damage is concentrated: at the last position the mean write cost is .7512 while the median is only .0064. All 12 of the 72 costs above .5 nats come from 4 of 24 source sequences, and once a pair crosses that threshold it stays above it.

    • The gradient-conflict analysis uses the same real passage on which harm is measured, so it explains harm but is not a deployable selection rule.
    • Closed Loop text ends almost entirely repetitive (repeated-4 = .9902 vs .0217 for Writes Off). Even so, repetition weighting leaves 1.5637 nats of endpoint gap: repetition is a symptom, not an admission rule.
    • Uniformly smaller writes lower harm and real-text benefit together (α=1/16: H .3856, B .0412; α=1: H 1.1867, B .0922), tracing a stability–adaptation frontier.

    Mitigations and Settlement

    Changing the decoder alters the speed of degradation but does not bound it. A repetition penalty cuts the 128K endpoint gap from 3.01 to .88. Resetting fast weights every 8 chunks leaves .175 but keeps only 44.1% of real-text adaptation. Evenly interleaved real text helps a lot, but its cadence matters: at a 31% real budget, even spacing (longest run of 3 self-generated chunks) leaves .0694 nats, while bursts (runs of 12) leave .7155. Settlement keeps a candidate update pending. It compares the current state and the candidate state on the next independent real passage with attention fixed, and commits only if loss does not rise. Each candidate is judged against the accumulated accepted state.

    • Settlement accepts 22/936 (125M) and 18/312 (760M) generated candidates. Endpoint gaps vs Writes Off are .067 [-.013, .169] and -.021 [-.058, .014].
    • On an all-real stream it admits 89/113 candidates; its benefit (.0534) beats Write All (.0450) by .0083 [.0020, .0161].
    • Validate the state you will actually commit. Checking each update separately against W and then committing their sum ('joint commit') wiped out the real-text gain (B = -1.093).
    • Under corrupted real/generated labels, Source Masking degrades from 3.8072 to 4.2544 NLL while Settlement stays between 3.8196 and 3.8286.

    Implications for agents that learn from their own actions

    In two agent settings, LoRA updates trained on the agent's own action targets reproduce the failure. A source-label rule cannot help there, because every candidate is 'generated': you either reject all (Writes Off) or keep all (Closed Loop). WebShop (Qwen3-4B, rank-16 LoRA on down_proj in the final quarter of layers, about 1.8M params) shows Closed Loop with the highest mean reward (.4356) but the lowest exact success (.1053). Fixed Generation reaches .1800, and reward-validated Settlement reaches .1853 while keeping 10/180 blocks. In ALFWorld (Qwen3.8-27B LoRA), two of three Closed Loop seeds complete none of the 134 unseen tasks at each update scale. Settlement reaches 88.1%–94.0% unseen success.

    • Practical rule: gate persistent self-updates on held-out real data or a task metric, and evaluate the full accumulated candidate state rather than single deltas.
    • Regularly interleaving real external input bounds the damage. The longest uninterrupted run of self-generated training data matters as much as the overall ratio.
    • Validation has a cost: in ALFWorld, every decision runs 20 extra validation tasks per model state.

    Key findings

    • Keeping self-generated updates over 128K tokens raises independent real-text NLL by +3.01 (125M), +6.00 (760M) and +0.40 (3B) nats over Writes Off. Every interval excludes zero, and the effect appears only beyond the original 8K+8K horizon.
    • Fixed Generation (frozen generator, still-updating learner) removes 98.3% (125M) and 98.8% (760M) of the damage, even though it drifts farther from init than Closed Loop (.01387 vs .00857). Feedback, not synthetic origin or update size, drives the failure.
    • Recorded Replay at 125M: reading degraded text costs 1.606 nats and writing it stores an extra 2.268. At 3B, writing adds .1927 [.1043, .3272].
    • One update after Closed Loop history improves its source chunk (-.0354 NLL) but hurts the next real passage (+.0381). Generated and real gradients turn opposed (mean cosine -.093/-.103 vs about -.005 after Writes Off), and conflict ranks harm (Spearman down to -.788).
    • Late harm is heavy-tailed: mean write cost .7512 vs median .0064. All 12 large failures come from 4 of 24 source sequences.
    • Settlement (commit only if loss on independent real text does not rise) leaves endpoint gaps of .067/-.021 nats at 125M/760M, admits 89/113 real-text updates with a .0534 benefit, and lifts ALFWorld unseen-task success from Closed Loop's 27.6%/18.7% to 91.0%/90.0%.

    Notable quotes

    Only after many additional self-writes does Closed Loop rise while Writes Off stays nearly flat.
    Generated origin alone is therefore insufficient; feedback to future training text accounts for nearly all of the measured gap in these comparisons.
    In every row, retaining the update lowers mean source-chunk NLL but raises mean NLL on the next real passage.
    Thus a few source sequences repeatedly produce high-cost passages and raise the late mean; position alone cannot identify a safe write.

    Limitations

    • Most causal experiments use the 125M model on PG-19 books. The 760M and 3B lineages differ, so magnitudes do not establish a scaling law, and contamination screening removes a visible anomaly without proving there is none.
    • Settlement needs identifiable trusted real validation text (or a task metric) and adds compute: in ALFWorld, 20 validation tasks per decision. Its near-zero residual intervals are not equivalence bounds, and ALFWorld does not show a reliable overall gain over Writes Off.
    • The gradient-conflict signal is computed on the same real passage used to measure harm, so it is explanatory rather than a usable admission rule. The WebShop runner does not enforce disjoint validation and test goal sets.

    Open questions

    • Can a cheap pre-update signal, such as gradient conflict against a held-out real-text bank, replace full forward validation when fresh real text is scarce?
    • Does the failure and its fix carry over to frontier-scale models, non-book domains, and agents that learn from environment observations rather than their own action targets?
    • Can batch-style joint commits be repaired (normalization, increment recomputation, learning-rate corrections) so that validated parallel updates do not reverse the real-text gain?
