Explainable Deep Learning for Price-Trade Dynamics: From Black-Box Forecasts to Effective Parametric Models

📰 Latest · 2026-09-12 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-12

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-05
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06085

Naviglio and Lillo use a neural net purely as a discovery device: SHAP decomposition of a DNN trained on high-frequency returns and signed volumes reveals two simple nonlinear mechanisms, and a hand-written parametric model using only the last (return, signed volume) pair beats a 20-lag VAR everywhere and often beats the 20-lag DNN itself.

Executive summary

The paper models the joint dynamics of high-frequency midprice returns and signed trade volumes for 5 large-tick and 5 small-tick NASDAQ stocks (LOBSTER, June 2024), comparing a linear VAR benchmark with a deep feed-forward network (3 hidden layers, 64/64/32, tanh), both at lag p=20. The DNN improves out-of-sample R^2 for returns on almost every stock. Shapley-based explainability shows contributions concentrated at the first lag, with lagged signed volume acting as a sign-preserving, saturating driver and lagged return acting as a state variable that reinforces order-flow continuation when the previous price move was near zero and attenuates or reverses it when it was large. The authors encode these two shapes into a SHAP-inspired parametric model; the one-lag version beats the VAR for every stock and matches or beats the 20-lag DNN in most cases, and a geometric-decay multi-lag extension beats the DNN on signed volume for all ten stocks.

Setup: two benchmarks, lagged information only

Both models predict returns and signed volume from lagged values only; contemporaneous volume is deliberately excluded from the return equation and left in the residuals, so instantaneous impact can be identified separately via a Hasbrouck-style triangular decomposition with a nonparametrically estimated impact function.

  • VAR(p) benchmark vs a deep feed-forward net with three dense hidden layers of 64, 64 and 32 tanh units, linear output, MSE loss, Adam.
  • Same architecture across all stocks and lag orders so differences are not driven by design choices.
  • Lag set to p = 20 after an appendix study showing performance saturates quickly.
  • Data: LOBSTER NASDAQ, June 2024, visible-limit-order executions only, first/last 30 minutes dropped, [0.005, 0.995] quantile filter, 50/50 train/test split.

Forecasting comparison

At p=20 the DNN beats the VAR on returns for almost all stocks; on signed volume it beats the VAR for every small-tick stock but the VAR stays competitive for several large-tick names. A tick-size asymmetry emerges: for large-tick stocks returns are more predictable than signed volumes, and the reverse holds for small-tick stocks.

  • Largest return gains: TSLA 0.0433 to 0.0906, NVDA 0.0166 to 0.0454, INTC 0.0814 to 0.1217.
  • DNN loses to VAR on signed volume for BAC (0.0299 to 0.0215), CSCO (0.0328 to 0.0273) and PFE (0.0183 to 0.0091).

Explainability: what the network actually learned

Shapley values are aggregated into binned conditional profiles, separated into terms for the variable on the x-axis and terms for the conditioning variable. For a linear model the latter profiles would be flat; they are not, which isolates genuine nonlinear cross-dependence.

  • Lagged signed volume: sign-like, saturating response for both targets, i.e. nonlinear (concave) price impact plus order-flow persistence.
  • Lagged return acts as a state variable: when it is near zero the contribution preserves the sign of lagged signed volume; for large absolute returns it attenuates or reverses it.
  • Small-tick stocks show sharper transitions around zero signed volume; large-tick responses are smoother and closer to linear.
  • Model-implied surfaces are cross-checked against binned averages computed directly on realized data and agree qualitatively; VAR-implied surfaces are essentially linear and cannot reproduce them.

Residual contemporaneous impact

After removing the lagged predictable component, the return innovation is regressed nonparametrically on the order-flow innovation. The estimated impact function is increasing, sign-preserving, steep near the origin and flattening in the tails.

  • Explained share averages 0.162 for large-tick and 0.179 for small-tick stocks, i.e. 16-18% of the unpredictable return variance is explained by contemporaneous order flow.
  • Ticker range: 0.117 (CMCSA) to 0.205 (PFE) large-tick; 0.116 (TSLA) to 0.266 (AMZN) small-tick.

From explanations to a parametric model

The two SHAP shapes are written as closed-form tanh terms plus a sign term on lagged signed volume. A softplus reparametrization forces the return-state correction to flip sign at a finite threshold, so the model reinforces the order-flow regime for small price moves and reverses it for large ones. Fitted by nonlinear least squares on the same training sample.

  • One-lag version uses only the previous return and signed volume, versus 20 lags for VAR and DNN.
  • Multi-lag extension shares the nonlinear shape across lags with geometric decay, so the number of nonlinear parameters does not grow with p.
  • The authors deliberately omit an asymmetric correction (visible in large-tick profiles) to keep the specification parsimonious.

Key findings

  • A one-lag SHAP-inspired parametric model using only the previous return and signed volume beats the 20-lag VAR on returns for every stock, with large-tick gains such as BAC 0.0494 to 0.0934 and PFE 0.0356 to 0.0845.
  • That same two-variable model matches or beats the 20-lag DNN on returns for most large-tick stocks and for AMZN, GILD and NVDA; the DNN keeps an edge on AAPL (0.0951 vs 0.0858) and TSLA (0.0906 vs 0.0826).
  • On signed volume the reduced model beats the VAR for all 10 stocks and dominates the DNN on all 5 large-tick stocks (e.g. PFE 0.0091 DNN vs 0.0478 reduced).
  • The multi-lag extension lifts signed-volume R^2 above the DNN for all ten stocks (e.g. NVDA 0.1068 vs 0.1141; TSLA 0.1475 vs 0.1589) while adding little for returns.
  • Contemporaneous order-flow innovation explains 16-18% of the return innovation variance left after lagged prediction.
  • Predictability is tick-size dependent: large-tick returns are more predictable than their signed volumes, while for small-tick stocks signed volume is the more predictable series.

Notable quotes

Note that small tick stocks exhibit much stronger nonlinearities and sharper transitions, while large tick stocks display smoother and more constrained dynamics, consistent with the role of tick size in limiting price adjustments.
This indicates that, even when the model response is projected onto lagged returns, the sign of lagged order flow remains the main state variable organizing the prediction.
It is steeper around the origin and tends to flatten in the tails, suggesting a nonlinear and saturating instantaneous impact.

Limitations

  • Empirical base is narrow: 10 NASDAQ stocks over a single month (June 2024), visible executions only, a simple 50/50 train/test split and no statistical test of R^2 differences.
  • The baseline parametric model imposes symmetry in the absolute lagged return even though large-tick profiles show a visible asymmetry the authors acknowledge but do not model.
  • Explainability rests on a single feed-forward architecture and binned Shapley aggregates; conclusions about what the data contains are mediated by that one model class.

Open questions

  • Would the same SHAP-to-parametric distillation survive richer architectures (CNN/RNN on the full LOB state) or longer, multi-month and cross-market samples?
  • Does an asymmetric symmetry-breaking correction recover the DNN's remaining edge on AAPL and TSLA returns?
  • Can the calibrated model generate realistic concave metaorder impact and post-trade reversion in simulation?
