Hawkes processes in finance

📚 Foundations · 2026-09-02 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-02

Theme: Event-driven market microstructure
These papers model limit-order markets as interacting event systems. Queue-reactive models condition order arrivals and cancellations on displayed liquidity, while Hawkes processes capture persistent self- and cross-excitation in order flow. Together they provide foundations for realistic execution simulators and market-impact models.
Source: arxiv
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04592

A foundational survey connecting self-exciting point-process theory to market activity, price formation, impact, execution, order books, and systemic risk.

Executive summary

Bacry, Mastromatteo, and Muzy explain why self- and mutually exciting point processes are a natural continuous-time language for transaction-level finance. The survey connects stability, branching, causality, second-order identification, and scaling limits to empirical models of volatility clustering, market endogeneity, tick prices, impact, execution, order books, and contagion. It emphasizes near-critical dynamics and slowly decaying excitation kernels while warning that exponential specifications, non-stationarity, and dimensionality can distort inference.

Problem and scope

The paper organizes high-frequency-finance applications around a common event-level model. Rather than aggregate trades and quote changes onto a regular grid, Hawkes processes model their continuous-time arrival intensities and excitation between event categories.

  • Applications include market activity, price formation, impact, execution, order books, systemic risk, news, and interaction networks.
  • The survey covers mathematical foundations as well as simulation and estimation procedures.
  • Kernel entries provide an interpretable description of how past events change future intensities.

Mathematical foundation

A multivariate Hawkes intensity combines an exogenous baseline with convolutions of past events and a matrix-valued causal kernel. Stationarity requires the spectral radius of the matrix of kernel norms to be below one.

  • The branching representation separates immigrant events from their descendants and interprets the spectral radius as average offspring.
  • For stationary linear processes, first- and second-order statistics identify the kernel through a Wiener–Hopf system.
  • Exponential kernels yield a Markov representation, while non-exponential kernels generally require retaining event history.
  • Near-critical sequences can produce richer macroscopic limits than ordinary stationary Brownian scaling.

Market activity and endogeneity

The reviewed evidence links clustered arrivals to volatility clustering and treats the kernel norm as a measure of market reflexivity. Several studies find event streams near the stability boundary and slowly decaying kernels inconsistent with routine exponential parametrizations.

  • A Euro-Bund trade-arrival study used 75 trading days from 2009 and found a process close to the stability threshold.
  • Parametric market-order studies reported excitation norms of at least 0.9 and often at least 0.95.
  • Exponential assumptions, outliers, edge effects, and non-stationarity can bias estimates of reflexivity.
  • Order splitting is a plausible source of slowly decaying excitation but lacked decisive quantitative confirmation.

Prices, impact, and execution

Upward and downward tick moves can be encoded as separate Hawkes components, permitting analytical treatment of signature plots, Epps effects, lead-lag behavior, and diffusive limits. Joint price-and-flow models distinguish direct impact, persistent order-flow excitation, mean reversion, and price-to-order feedback.

  • A four-dimensional calibration on EuroStoxx and Euro-Bund futures used 800 trading days from 2009–2012.
  • Direct trade-to-price impact lasted less than 0.1 seconds, while longer-lived impact emerged through order-flow excitation.
  • Hawkes impact models distinguish herding amplification from contrarian decay.
  • Order-flow models support optimal-liquidation policies under martingale-consistent restrictions.

Order books and dimensionality

Multivariate Hawkes models quantify interactions among market, limit, cancellation, and price-change events, while marked variants address defaults and contagion. The survey also considers latent-network and collective-mode methods for many interacting assets.

  • A ten-dimensional LSE model found the examined stock resilient in fewer than 40% of cases, with replenishment in roughly 20 seconds.
  • An eight-dimensional order-book study examined interactions over nearly eight decades of time scales.
  • A 100-dimensional S&P 100 model contained 182,037 events and imposed a 20% sparse latent graph.
  • High-dimensional inference, simultaneous jumps, inhibition, and intraday non-stationarity remain difficult.

Key findings

  • Hawkes processes provide a unified event-level framework for endogenous clustering and cross-event interactions.
  • The kernel-norm spectral radius governs stationarity and has a branching-ratio interpretation.
  • Financial excitation kernels are frequently near critical and slowly decaying.
  • Multivariate models reproduce or explain signature plots, Epps effects, resilience, meta-order impact, execution costs, and contagion.
  • Second-order identification supports non-parametric kernel recovery, but dimensionality and non-stationarity remain major obstacles.

Hawkes processes are extremely versatile processes that can be considered as the building blocks for modeling the occurrence of time-correlated discrete events, playing the same role that Auto-Regressive models have for describing continuous-valued signals.

Limitations

  • The authors describe the review as a non-exhaustive snapshot of a rapidly evolving literature.
  • Many surveyed calibrations impose exponential kernels even though empirical kernels often decay approximately as power laws.
  • Linear positive Hawkes models do not naturally capture inhibition, exact co-jumps, spread constraints, or strong non-stationarity.

Open questions

  • What mechanism determines the robust power-law kernel shape, and how much arises from order splitting rather than herding?
  • How do near-critical microscopic dynamics aggregate into macroscopic volatility and crashes?
  • How can stable, interpretable Hawkes networks handle high dimensionality, inhibition, simultaneous events, and non-stationarity?
