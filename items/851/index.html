Harness the Memory: A Holistic Evaluation of Memory Substrates in Memory Agents

📰 Latest · 2026-08-19
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-19

Source: arXiv (cs.AI) · 2026-08-18
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15008

The first controlled, cost-aware comparison of 11 agent-memory substrates shows the QA and agentic Pareto frontiers share no substrate at all — and that on BigCodeBench-Hard several popular memory designs pay 3.7–8.0x latency to score *below* having no memory. If you are choosing a memory layer for a coding agent, this is the paper that tells you the choice is a routing problem, not a ranking problem.

Executive summary

A controlled harness study evaluates 11 memory substrates spanning 7 families (flat dense/sparse indices, text records, structural stores, hierarchical trees, refinement memories, parametric adapters, and activation/context mechanisms) across 3 backbones, 4 benchmark suites, and 26 performance and efficiency metrics. The headline result is that no single substrate dominates: structural graphs lead multi-session dialogue QA but are Pareto-dominated on embodied planning, while distilled-strategy refinement memories lead planning and trail on QA. Retrieval breadth is regime-conditioned — more retrieved entries monotonically help LoCoMo but actively hurt ALFWorld — and an attention probe shows the same mass-shift mechanism underlies both. The authors conclude that substrate routing on task regime AND history depth is a necessary component of adaptive agent memory, and that universal memory must be a composed multi-substrate system.

The evaluation gap this paper is answering

The paper opens with a landscape survey of memory-augmented systems published 2023–2026, and the numbers are the strongest argument for the study itself: the field measures one thing (accuracy) on two benchmarks with one model family.

  • 52 memory-augmented systems surveyed (2023–2026).
  • 62% of benchmark–system pairs use only LoCoMo and LongMemEval.
  • Every system reports accuracy; only 21% report any efficiency metric.
  • 81% evaluate on GPT-family backbones only.
  • Half use a single benchmark with zero efficiency metrics; none combine broad coverage with comprehensive efficiency reporting.

The substrate taxonomy (11 methods, 7 families)

Substrates are split into external stores and internal (model-side) mechanisms. Production systems (Zep, Mem0, MemGPT) are deliberately kept out of the main controlled comparison because their auxiliary-LLM budgets differ substantially; they appear only in an auxiliary-cost appendix.

  • External flat: M1 Dense Vector, M2 Sparse Vector/BM25.
  • External text record: M3 Gist Index. Structural: M4 Evolving Notes, M5 Dual-Level Graph (LightRAG-style). Hierarchical: M6 Tree (RAPTOR-style).
  • Refinement: M7 Distilled Strategies, M8 Skill Bundles.
  • Internal: M9 Adapter Tuning (weight), M10 Full Context (activation), M11 Episode-Clustered Re-prefill (activation).

Harness and setup

Three backbones served on vLLM over 4xH200, with a single fixed auxiliary LLM and LLM-judge (gpt-4o-mini) so that judge variance does not contaminate the substrate comparison.

  • Backbones: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B-AWQ, Gemma-4-26B-A4B-IT.
  • User-centric: LoCoMo (10 multi-session dialogues, 1,986 questions) and MemoryAgentBench (LongMemEval-S, LRU, TTL, CR).
  • Agent-centric: ALFWorld (134 valid-unseen tasks) and BigCodeBench-Hard (148 tasks), against an explicit NoMem baseline with an empty retrieved block.
  • Ablations: top-k sweeps (k in {1,2,5,10,20} on LoCoMo; {1,2,3,4,5} on ALFWorld) and a context stress test at 6K/32K/262K tokens.
  • Design exclusions: M7 has no analog on dialogue inputs; M9 is incompatible with Gemma-4 A4B MoE routing; M10 overflows context on both agentic benchmarks.

The regime reversal — and why retrieval breadth flips sign

The central empirical claim is that substrate ranking inverts between user-centric QA and agent-centric decision-making, and the mechanism is measurable in the attention distribution rather than being merely a benchmark artifact.

  • Structural graph M5 tops LoCoMo (0.719 judge score on Gemma-4-26B, 0.683 on Qwen3-32B-AWQ) but is Pareto-dominated on the agentic side.
  • Refinement memory M7 peaks at 32.1% ALFWorld task success on Qwen3-32B-AWQ, above the 22.4% NoMem line — but has no QA analog.
  • Retrieval sweep: LoCoMo judge score rises monotonically with k; ALFWorld M7 falls from 32.1% at k=1 to roughly 25% at k=5, and M1/M2 drop below NoMem.
  • Attention probe (Qwen3-8B, last prompt token, upper-half layers): on LoCoMo, mass on task context falls 0.34 to 0.10 while retrieved-block mass rises 0.05 to 0.66; on ALFWorld the same dilution occurs (context 0.19 to 0.17, retrieved 0.34 to 0.44) but costs roughly 4% of the action-cue mass.
  • BigCodeBench-Hard inverts ALFWorld: retrieved snippets extend the prompt rather than distract, so even BM25 (M2) beats NoMem on every backbone.

Cost is heavy-tailed and frequently unrewarded

The efficiency reporting is the part most directly useful for deployment decisions: several substrates buy nothing at all for large latency multiples.

  • On LongMemEval-S, M4 (Evolving Notes) at 234 s scores below M2 (BM25) at 7.3 s.
  • M9 (Adapter Tuning) at 172 s reaches only 0.29 judge score on LongMemEval-S.
  • On BigCodeBench-Hard, M5/M9/M11 pay 3.7x–8.0x overhead yet land below NoMem, with Pass@1 deltas between -1.4% and -4.1%.
  • Pareto wins that do exist are modest: on Qwen3-32B-AWQ, M2 gives +2.0% on BCB-Hard at 1.28x latency; M7 gives +9.7% on ALFWorld at 1.23x.

Scalability is a third routing axis

Substrates that are fine at moderate history length become costly or brittle at long horizons, so routing cannot be decided from the current query alone.

  • MemoryAgentBench CR from 6K to 262K tokens: M8 (Skill Bundles) 0.32 to 0.51 with latency roughly 1 s to 10 s.
  • M5 (graph) 0.28 to 0.48 but with steep latency growth at 262K.
  • M10 (full context) 0.27 to 0.47 with linear read cost.
  • M9 (adapter) stays flat at 0.18–0.22 regardless of history length.

Key findings

  • No single substrate wins every capability; the user-centric and agent-centric Pareto frontiers share no substrate.
  • Attention dilution from retrieval is universal, but it only hurts when the answer lives in the task context rather than the retrieved block — which is why more retrieval helps LoCoMo and hurts ALFWorld.
  • Episode-clustered re-prefill (M11) roughly doubles the NoMem ALFWorld score on Qwen3-8B (11.9% vs 5.7%), showing denoising beats broad retrieval for embodied planning.
  • Cost is heavy-tailed and frequently unrewarded: three substrates on BigCodeBench-Hard pay 3.7x–8.0x latency for a Pass@1 below the no-memory baseline.
  • Refinement memories hold quality-per-second as history grows, while structural and full-context substrates hit deployment limits at 262K tokens.
  • Family labels are poor predictors of behavior, so routing must be per-substrate rather than per-family.

Notable quotes

No single substrate wins every capability, ruling out single-substrate universal memory by design.
Cost is heavy-tailed and frequently unrewarded.
Attention dilution is universal, but its consequence depends on where the answer lives.
Universal memory must route on history depth, not only on the current query.

Limitations

  • Production-grade systems (Zep, Mem0, MemGPT) are excluded from the controlled comparison because their auxiliary-LLM budgets differ, so the study does not rank what most practitioners actually deploy.
  • A single fixed auxiliary LLM and judge (gpt-4o-mini) is used throughout, and only three backbones and four benchmark suites are covered — the regime taxonomy rests on a narrow sample of regimes.
  • Implementations deviate from the reference papers in ways documented only in an appendix, and three configurations are excluded by design rather than measured.

Open questions

  • What does a learned router look like in practice, and can it be trained cheaply enough that the routing overhead does not eat the substrate gains it captures?
  • Does the retrieval-hurts-planning result survive on stronger backbones, or is attention dilution largely a small-model failure mode?
  • Would a composed multi-substrate system actually beat the per-regime best single substrate, or does composition reintroduce the dilution the paper diagnoses?
