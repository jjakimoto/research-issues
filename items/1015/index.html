Buy the Rumor, Sell the News: When Is News Priced In?

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-14
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014

A corpus-scale event study (4.57M articles, 1.68M stock-day events, 2023-2026) showing the directional move around news is essentially complete by the publication-day close (2.8x its level 20 days later) and that most apparent post-news reversal is a benchmark artifact: without a neutral-news placebo, a news model rediscovers a baseline drift of about -0.9%/month in covered small caps and calls it signal. The durable, tradable content of news flow is the event tag, coverage intensity and volatility width - not the sentiment direction.

Executive summary

The paper tests two market adages - 'news is already priced in' and 'buy the rumor, sell the news' - as measurable claims about where in event time the price move sits. The authors distill a GPT teacher into an 82M-parameter DistilRoBERTa student via active learning, tag 4.57M financial news articles with 17 event types plus five binary attributes, cluster articles into stories to separate first reports from follow-ups, and compute beta-adjusted abnormal returns around 1,681,657 priced stock-day events, using 364,405 neutral-sentiment events as a placebo. Both adages survive: the news-direction move is complete by the publication-day close, and rumor days capture the whole move while confirmations add nothing. Net of the placebo, quantified fundamentals (capital returns, earnings, guidance, analyst actions) keep drifting while soft narrative news (macro read-throughs, launches, leadership, competition) reverses - 'markets underreact to numbers and overreact to stories' - and the paper publishes the per-tag drift table as a prior for news-conditioned forecasting.

Problem: two adages as event-time claims, and the three missing measurement layers

The paper reframes market folklore as an empirical question about event-time placement of the price move, and argues the question stayed open at full-news-flow scale because three infrastructure layers were missing: (1) tagged events, since an earnings report, a lawsuit and a promotional listicle are different economic objects; (2) story structure, since the same event is syndicated and followed up for days; and (3) a benchmark for publicity itself - what happens to a stock that gets coverage with no direction. Recent LLM-for-finance work documents return predictability but builds none of these layers.

  • Direct motivation: watching a live feed, stocks often receive plainly good news and fall anyway, or drift down for weeks after upbeat coverage.
  • Categories: cs.AI (primary), cs.LG, q-fin.ST. Keywords: financial news, event study, LLM distillation, market efficiency.
  • Authors span TailState Intelligence (London), Zanista AI (London), Increase Alpha (Miami), and an independent researcher.

Data: 4.57M articles on ~3,000 US stocks, 2023-2026

The corpus is NewsWitch, a commercial financial-news product in daily production since early 2024, crawling over 250,000 URLs/day across more than 500,000 sources (~70M+ raw articles over the sample). After dedup, financial filtering and restriction to the ~3,000 most-covered US-listed stocks, 4.57M articles are retained. Vendor-supplied fields include a relevance screen, five-level sentiment, a summary, publication timestamp and source domain; sentiment is produced day-by-day with no access to later returns.

  • Prices: daily adjusted closes for 2,591 tradeable tickers; 3.4% of articles reference symbols with no price history and 9.7% of events are dropped for missing price windows.
  • The 2023 slice was backfilled in bulk and is under 3% of signed events; live years show the same patterns.

Method: LLM distillation with active learning, then articles to events

A frozen prompt gives a teacher model (gpt-5-mini on Azure, ~$0.0002/article, strict JSON schema) 17 event-tag definitions, five binary attributes (scheduled, forward looking, primary source, quantified, rumor) and four labeled examples; the taxonomy deliberately keeps two 'junk' buckets (price commentary, promotional content) and was iterated down from an earlier 63-tag system over ~500k articles. The teacher labels 29,472 random articles; a distilroberta-base student (82M params, seven heads) labels 600,000 fresh articles; the 100,000 lowest-confidence items (uncertainty sampling with a rare-tag quota) go back to the teacher; the student is retrained on 129,463 merged labels.

  • Held-out 10,000 articles: 87.5% primary-tag agreement overall, 94.0% at confidence >= 0.8 (85% of articles); attributes agree 92-99%. Routing the low-confidence 15% back to the teacher gives ~95% corpus-wide fidelity. Teacher labels cost $29; total under $200. Inference 125-160 articles/sec on an M4 Pro MacBook Pro.
  • Story clustering is two-step: within a stock-day, articles cluster on cosine similarity of title+summary embeddings at threshold 0.80 restricted to the same primary tag; day-clusters then match running stories against a frozen centroid. 55% of all articles are follow-up coverage.
  • Per-tag story lifetimes calibrated from similarity decay: M&A and legal up to 90 days, leadership/operations weeks, commentary two days (measured means 12.9 days for M&A down to 0.4 for promotional).
  • An event = (stock, trading day, tag); articles after 16:00 New York roll to the next trading day; article count is kept as an attention proxy. Event impact averages constituent article sentiment, neutral only if every article is neutral.
  • Totals: 1,862,297 events, 1,681,657 priced, of which 1,317,252 signed (73% positive) and 364,405 neutral.

Measurement design: beta-adjusted returns plus a neutral-news placebo

AR_t = r_t - beta_t * r_m,t with r_m from SPY and beta a rolling 252-day OLS estimate (minimum 126 observations; missing betas set to 1). Cumulation windows are -5..0, day 0, +1..+5 and +6..+20; signed events are stacked by sentiment sign so news-direction moves are positive for good and bad news alike. Inference uses a cluster bootstrap resampling trading dates (5,000 draws) to handle cross-sectional correlation, and size buckets are annual terciles by dollar volume.

  • Neutral events define a baseline b_k,w per size bucket and window; the adjusted statistic is s*(AR_w - b_k,w). Because baseline and events share a benchmark, benchmark misfit cancels; raw and adjusted are reported side by side.
  • Robustness: tag-matched baselines leave earnings continuation and macro/leadership/competition reversals intact; re-estimating the baseline inside each bootstrap draw yields equal or stronger significance, so reported p-values are conservative.
  • Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control across the 17 tags. At 5% FDR the macro reversal and the capital-returns and earnings continuations survive; analyst, leadership and launch sit at the boundary (q between 0.05 and 0.07); guidance is suggestive rather than decisive.
  • Additional cuts: NEW-only (1.09M events), single-tag stock-days (the median covered stock-day carries two tags), and teacher-labeled-only events.
  • Economic significance: calendar-time portfolios entering at the close of day +5 and exiting at day +20, equally weighted, rebalanced daily, on beta-hedged abnormal returns, with costs at 5/10/20 bps per side. Borrow fees and locate availability are not modeled.

Results: the move sits before publication; the placebo kills three apparent anomalies

Almost every pre- and day-0 column is positive: the market agrees in direction with the news before the news is out. Pooled across signed events the running abnormal return reaches +0.58% by the close of publication day and ends day +20 at +0.20%, a ratio of 2.8. Per-tag completeness ratios are 1.06 for earnings, 1.20 for analyst actions and 1.08 for guidance - at or above one, i.e. the move was finished, or more than finished, when publication day ended. The rumor attribute reproduces the adage literally: of 18,618 rumor-flagged signed events, 94% are followed by a same-tag non-rumor event within 60 trading days (median gap 6 days), the rumor day pays +0.36%, rumor-to-confirmation adds -0.09%, the confirmation day +0.01%, and +6..+20 gives back -0.06%.

  • The placebo became a finding: over the following month, stocks with neutral coverage trail their beta benchmark by -0.92% (small), -0.58% (mid) and -0.34% (large); quiet stock-days drift -0.74%, -0.88% and -0.59%. The drift is the residual of the single-beta benchmark, present with and without news.
  • Three anomalies dissolve after adjustment: the good/bad asymmetry (raw +6..+20 is -0.62% after positive news and +0.63% after negative news; both 0.00% adjusted, p ~ 0.9), the size gradient (small caps appear to reverse 2.4x more than large caps raw; gone adjusted), and the raw table's only continuation winner, legal and regulatory news (+0.29% raw, -0.08% adjusted, p = 0.22).
  • Because 73% of signed events are positive, the single underlying drift shows up mostly as 'reversal of good news' in pooled raw averages.
  • Sources are not interchangeable: retail analysis, portals, mainstream media and aggregators all show +0.6% to +1.5% pre-publication drift while press wires show none at all (-0.06%) because the release is the event. The two most promotional individual outlets show -0.4% to -0.8% adjusted drift per month.
  • Second moments: neutral guidance events move 1.29x the stock's normal day and 1.23x over the following week; ten-or-more-article days move 1.36x normal against 1.05x for single-article days; post-event realized volatility runs about 0.86 of the EWMA forecast.
  • Attributes: scheduled events show slightly positive adjusted drift (+0.08%, p = 0.03); quantified articles drift while unquantified reverse (-0.12%, p = 0.01); rumor-flagged events have the largest pre-windows.
  • Decay over time: the raw pooled reversal halves from -0.49% (2024) to -0.18% (2026) and the adjusted pooled drift is zero from 2025 on, 'consistent with a market that learns'.

Significance: what this means for news-conditioned ML systems

The economic-significance exercise prices the two patterns a practitioner would be tempted to trade and shows the tempting one is a mirage: fading small-cap launch/partnership sentiment earns 15.8% annualized gross at Sharpe 1.35, but simply shorting every small cap that appeared in the news at all - ignoring sentiment entirely - earns 15.9% at the same Sharpe 1.35, with cumulative curves lying on top of each other. Following legal and regulatory direction earns Sharpe 0.66 gross and dies at 10 bps. The residual tag-map alphas (earnings continuation at +0.22% per event over 15 days) are real but thin relative to plausible costs.

  • Ranking: when a day's news must be compressed into a short context, order event lines by the measured per-tag, per-attribute, per-size drift priors rather than source-reputation heuristics.
  • Representation: encode text as tagged, flagged event lines (tag, scheduled, rumor, primary source, NEW/follow-up age) rather than sentiment scalars.
  • Evaluation: benchmark any claimed news alpha against a coverage-presence baseline, or the model will rediscover the baseline and call it signal.
  • A distributional forecaster should read event tags as width signals - widening on scheduled disclosure events and narrowing after them, independent of direction.
  • The earnings continuation is post-earnings-announcement drift recovered at corpus scale from tagged news alone.

Key findings

  • The news-direction move is essentially complete at publication: pooled across all signed events the cumulative abnormal return reaches +0.58% by the close of day 0 and ends day +20 at +0.20%, a ratio of 2.8; per-tag ratios are 1.06 (earnings), 1.20 (analyst actions) and 1.08 (guidance), i.e. at or above one.
  • 'Buy the rumor, sell the news' holds literally: across 18,618 rumor-flagged signed events (94% confirmed within 60 trading days, median gap 6 days) the rumor day pays +0.36%, rumor-to-confirmation -0.09%, the confirmation day +0.01% and +6..+20 -0.06%; for M&A rumors the path is +0.24%, -0.32%, -0.10%, -0.37%.
  • A benchmark artifact, not news, drives the raw post-news picture: neutral-coverage stocks trail their beta benchmark by -0.92% (small), -0.58% (mid) and -0.34% (large) per month, and quiet no-news stock-days drift -0.74%, -0.88% and -0.59% - so the raw good/bad asymmetry (-0.62% vs +0.63%) collapses to 0.00% (p ~ 0.9) and the 2.4x small-vs-large reversal gradient disappears after placebo adjustment.
  • Net of the placebo, markets underreact to numbers and overreact to stories: capital returns +0.35%, earnings +0.22%, guidance +0.13% and analyst actions +0.10% over days +6..+20, versus macro read-throughs -0.34%, product launches -0.18%, leadership -0.18% and competition -0.17%; signs and rough magnitudes survive NEW-only, single-tag-day and teacher-label-only cuts.
  • Sentiment adds no tradable value at universe scale: fading small-cap launch/partnership sentiment earns 15.8% annualized at Sharpe 1.35, but blindly shorting every small cap that appeared in the news earns 15.9% at the identical Sharpe 1.35; following legal/regulatory direction gives Sharpe 0.66 gross and dies at 10 bps per side.
  • News carries width: neutral guidance events move 1.29x a stock's normal day (1.23x over the following week), ten-or-more-article days move 1.36x vs 1.05x for single-article days, and post-event realized volatility runs about 0.86 of the EWMA forecast.
  • The distillation pipeline is cheap and accurate: an 82M-parameter distilroberta-base student trained on 129,463 teacher labels reaches 87.5% primary-tag agreement (94.0% at confidence >= 0.8, covering 85% of articles) and ~95% corpus-wide fidelity with 15% teacher routing, for $29 in teacher labels and under $200 total.

Notable quotes

Whoever traded the rumor captured the entire move; whoever bought the confirmation bought the top.
The placebo was designed as a calibration check and became a finding.
Launch and partnership coverage is 96–97% positive, the most promotional corner of the corpus, and it is precisely where prices overshoot.
At universe scale, the exploitable object in news flow is presence and width, not direction.

Limitations

  • Labels are not ground truth: sentiment and summaries come from the corpus vendor's LLM and the distilled classifier disagrees with its teacher on about one article in eight (disagreements concentrate in the price-commentary and promotional junk buckets), and a mislabeled article can end up attached to the wrong story.
  • Returns are hedged with a single market beta, so style effects such as size are not explicitly modeled - the within-size-bucket placebo absorbs much of this, but a formal multi-factor treatment is left to future work; and daily closes cannot separate the trading-hours reaction from the overnight gap.
  • One regime and public news only: 2023-2026 with year-by-year decay suggesting the residual patterns are already fading (2023 backfilled rather than live), and because the corpus is open-internet news, part of the measured anticipation reflects information already moved through filings, terminals and professional channels; the economic exercise uses fixed windows, equal weights and flat costs with no borrow fees or locate availability, and 'is built to price the measured patterns, not to be a trading system'.

Open questions

  • Does the residual tag map survive a proper multi-factor (size/value/momentum) benchmark rather than a single market beta plus a size-bucketed placebo?
  • Does intraday data change the conclusion - how much of the publication-day move is the overnight gap versus the trading-hours reaction, and is any of it capturable?
  • How far does the source-quality signal go? The two most promotional individual outlets show -0.4% to -0.8% adjusted monthly drift, and a full source-reputation study is left to future work.
  • Given the adjusted pooled drift is zero from 2025 on, are the surviving per-tag drifts (earnings, capital returns) decaying as the market learns, and do they persist out of sample after 2026?
