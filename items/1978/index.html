    The AI Scientist-v2: Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search

    ← Go backOpen gaps — 2026-10-04NextSciAgents: Automating scientific discovery through… →
    📚 Foundations · 2026-10-04 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-04

    Theme: Autonomous science through graph grounding and experiment search
    SciAgents and AI Scientist-v2 illustrate two complementary autonomous-research topologies: graph-grounded multi-agent hypothesis generation and execution-oriented tree search over experiments. Both improve structure and traceability relative to unconstrained prompting, but neither establishes dependable end-to-end discovery. SciAgents lacks experimental validation, while AI Scientist-v2 demonstrates a selected workshop-level success alongside serious claim-to-artifact failures.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08066

    An autonomous-research architecture combining experiment tree search and multimodal review, with human evaluation exposing both capability and serious provenance failures.

    Executive summary

    AI Scientist-v2 replaces human-authored templates and a linear experiment loop with a four-stage experiment manager, parallel agentic tree search, LLM node selection, replication and aggregation nodes, and VLM figure checks. Its strongest external validation was identity-blind human workshop review: one of three selected submissions scored 6, 6, and 7 and exceeded the acceptance threshold before its prearranged withdrawal. This establishes an existential capability, not a reliability rate, because humans selected ideas and chose the best completed outputs across multiple seeds. Internal audits found citation errors, experiment-claim mismatches, dataset overlap, and figure-description errors.

    Problem and architectural objective

    AI Scientist-v1 required a human-written experimental template and refined experiments linearly. Version 2 starts from open-ended ideas, consults Semantic Scholar for novelty, generates code without a supplied template, and applies a reflection stage to the manuscript.

    • The loop covers hypothesis formulation, code, execution, analysis, visualization, writing, and review.
    • Template removal broadens scope but increases dependence on generated code and evaluators.
    • Literature retrieval is integrated despite later citation inaccuracies.

    Progressive agentic tree search

    A progress manager coordinates preliminary investigation, hyperparameter tuning, research-agenda execution, and ablations. Each stage expands multiple nodes in parallel; buggy parents spawn debugging attempts, while valid parents are refined through LLM-guided best-first selection.

    • The best node seeds the next stage.
    • Replication nodes rerun experiments with different seeds.
    • Aggregation nodes consolidate means and standard deviations.
    • Each node records plans, code, errors, runtime, metrics, figures, feedback, and bug status.

    Execution and automated evaluation

    Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2 generates code, while GPT-4o supplies LLM and VLM feedback. Successful executions advance to plotting, where a VLM checks labels, legends, and presentation. These gates are internal search heuristics rather than independent scientific validation.

    • Stage budgets are 21, 12, 12, and 12 nodes.
    • Maximum experiment runtime is one hour per node.
    • Reported generation time ranges from several hours to a 15-hour cap.
    • Dollar cost, total compute, seed count, and aggregate failure rates are not reported.

    External human evaluation

    Humans selected three ideas from roughly forty generated proposals and selected the best complete manuscript from multiple seeded runs. No human edited the selected code, results, figures, or manuscript. The papers entered an official 43-submission workshop review pool without reviewers being told which papers were AI-generated.

    • One submission received 6, 6, and 7, averaging 6.33 out of 10.
    • The other two submissions were rejected.
    • The successful paper exceeded the workshop threshold but was withdrawn under a prearranged protocol.
    • The authors concluded that none met typical top-tier main-track standards.

    Reliability and evidence grounding

    The experiment tests whether the system can produce at least one workshop-level paper, not its end-to-end success probability. Human filtering and best-of-run selection make one-of-three an invalid reliability estimate. Post hoc audits show that internal LLM and VLM review did not bind manuscript claims reliably to executed artifacts.

    • The successful manuscript had approximately 57% train-test overlap and result-description inconsistencies.
    • One rejected paper discussed calibration experiments that were never executed.
    • Another described domain adaptation even though the implementation failed and was absent from selected runnable code.
    • Every claim, table, and figure needs provenance tied to executed artifacts rather than plans or unused code.

    Key findings

    • A four-stage manager and tree search replace template-conditioned linear experimentation.
    • External human reviewers provide stronger evidence than the internal LLM evaluator.
    • One selected submission exceeded a workshop threshold but was withdrawn and was not a main-track acceptance.
    • Human idea and best-of-run selection demonstrate possibility rather than a generation success rate.
    • Audits uncover serious claim-to-artifact failures despite automated review.

    Notable quotes

    After each stage, the experiment manager selects the best-performing node using a dedicated LLM evaluator (see next section) based on clearly articulated criteria.
    At each iteration, the system selects several nodes from the existing tree to expand in parallel.
    No human edited the generated code, experimental results, figures, or manuscript text of the selected final manuscript.
    We concluded that none of the manuscripts met the quality standards typical of top-tier main-track conferences.

    Limitations

    • Only three manually selected manuscripts entered review after best-of-multiple-run selection.
    • No controlled ablation establishes that tree search, the manager, or VLM review improves scientific quality.
    • Costs, seed counts, failure rates, and calibrated agreement between internal and human evaluators are absent.

    Open questions

    • How does the system perform under preregistered fixed idea and seed budgets with complete attempt denominators?
    • Can artifact-level provenance prevent unused plans and failed experiments from leaking into manuscripts?
    • What is the cost-quality-reliability frontier across models, node budgets, verifier designs, and oversight levels?
