    Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs

    ← Go backCompact Documentation for Coding Agents: A Benchmark, an…NextTaming the Option Factor Zoo: A High-Dimensional Analysis →
    📰 Latest · 2026-09-28 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-28

    Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-09-24
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29845

    If you average the token embeddings of two unrelated prompts and feed the result to an off-the-shelf LLM, the output still keeps both next-token predictions near the top (30-40% land in the top-10). A cheap self-distillation fine-tune raises top-5 recovery above 60%, which hints at running two streams in one forward pass. Two caveats: the fine-tune costs single-stream quality, and the best decoder recovers only part of the accuracy. In the authors' throughput benchmark, the fused two-head mode matches ordinary batch-size-2 decoding (within 3%) rather than beating it.

    Executive summary

    The authors (Tikhonov, Korznikov, Mikhalchuk, Dragunov, Rahmatullaev, Druzhinina, Razzhigaev, Oseledets, Tutubalina) propose the Superposition Linearity Hypothesis. When the token embeddings of two unrelated text streams are averaged position by position, a frozen decoder-only LLM outputs roughly a mixture of the two single-stream next-token distributions. They test this on Pythia, Llama, Qwen, OLMo and Gemma models, using FineWeb and TinyStories. The property is strongest at initialization and weakens during pretraining. A KL self-distillation fine-tune largely restores it: on Pythia-2.8B, R_KL falls from 0.42 to 0.06. A 'Joint Contrastive' decoder guided by a small model can partly separate two continuations from one mixed forward pass, which the authors present as a proof of concept for parallel inference.

    Hypothesis and formulation

    The model receives the element-wise average of the embeddings of two sequences, e_t(z) = 1/2 (E(x_t) + E(y_t)). This mixed input goes through the frozen backbone with ordinary causal attention. The test is whether P_mix approximates P_target = 1/2 (P(.|A) + P(.|B)), and whether each stream's predicted next token keeps high probability.

    • Metrics: the rank of each stream's single-stream argmax token in the mixed distribution (CDF), plus KL, JS and top-256 Wasserstein distances between P_target and P_mix.
    • Superposition Approximation Ratio R_D: the mixed-vs-target distance divided by the distance between two unrelated contexts. Values below 1 mean the mixed output is closer to the ideal mixture than to chance.
    • Hidden-state additivity error: the L2 distance between the normalized mixed hidden state and the normalized sum of the two single-stream hidden states, averaged over layers.

    Evidence in off-the-shelf models

    The unmodified Pythia-2.8B, Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B keep a lot of signal after mixing. The true token is in the top-10 about 30-40% of the time, the top-50 50-60%, and the top-100 60-65%, with vocabularies of 50k tokens or more. R_D stays below 1 for KL, JS and Wasserstein on every model at L=32 and L=512. A frequency-only control shows only 2.63% top-10 and 10.41% top-100 cross-stream recovery.

    • Confident single-stream predictions (P > 0.5) almost always survive mixing, with median rank about 3.
    • Mixing three streams (N=3) raises R_KL by +0.04 to +0.09 but ratios stay well below 1.
    • Across Pythia checkpoints, hidden-state superposition error is lowest early in training and rises monotonically. The authors conclude linearity is architectural, not learned.
    • Layer-wise linearity is U-shaped: layers 0-5 are high, middle layers 6-20 fall to about 0.65, and the final third recovers above 0.95.

    Attention-patching analysis

    To see what attention contributes, the authors compare embedding mixing with two single-stream perturbations on Qwen2.5-3B (FineWeb-Edu, T=128). Donor patching swaps in an unrelated text's post-softmax attention weights. Permutation patching shuffles each attention row within its causal prefix. Positions are split into predictable (about 65%) and content (about 35%).

    • Predictable positions survive whenever attention shape is kept: median rank 3-6 under mixing and donor patching.
    • Content positions degrade: median rank 284 under base-model mixing and 111 under donor patching.
    • Permutation destroys attention shape and collapses everything (median rank 3,079 on predictable and 19,246 on content positions).
    • On 200 LAMBADA prompts, donor patching drops accuracy from 73% to 0.5%, while mixing reaches 2.25%: mixing carries more case-specific content than 'frequency prior + attention shape'.

    Restoring linearity with fine-tuning

    A student model starts from the pretrained weights. It is trained to minimize KL(P_target || M_student(z)), where the target is the average of a frozen teacher's two single-stream predictions. On Pythia-2.8B, mean KL falls from 1.86 to 0.27 and R_KL from 0.42 to 0.06. Top-5 recovery rises from about 30% to over 60%. On Qwen2.5-3B, median rank at content positions improves from 284 to 5, and top-1 agreement from 4.2% to 22.8%.

    • The cost is real: single-stream LAMBADA drops from 0.544 to 0.357 on Pythia-2.8B and from 0.602 to 0.460 on Qwen2.5-3B.
    • Single-stream FineWeb perplexity after tuned distillation: Qwen2.5 12.44 to 65.52, Llama-3.2 11.20 to 71.44, Pythia 14.09 to 43.95.
    • Compute: 114 h for Pythia-2.8B, 132 h for Qwen2.5-3B and 128 h for Llama-3.2-3B, each on 2x A100 80GB.

    Decoding two streams and throughput

    Sampling straight from the mixed distribution gives incoherent text that switches between the two streams. When logits roughly average, the resulting probabilities scale with the geometric mean sqrt(P_A P_B), which pushes down tokens likely in only one stream (the 'geometric-mean obstruction'). The proposed Joint Contrastive decoder computes l_large(z) + alpha*l_small(A) - beta*l_small(B) for stream A (and symmetrically for B). On Llama-3.2-3B it lifts mean superposed LAMBADA accuracy from 0.182 to 0.430, against 0.540 for the 1B guide model running a single stream. The fused two-head 'Separate' mode is within 3% of vanilla batch-size-2 decoding and about 2x faster than running the two streams sequentially.

    • TinyStories LLM-as-judge ratings for Pythia-2.8B: grammar 4.52 independent, 2.59 superposed pretrained, 3.69 superposed fine-tuned. Creativity barely recovers (5.72, 2.08, 2.13).
    • Memory: Separate needs 7.15 GB vs 6.34 GB for a vanilla pass on Pythia-2.8B. Guided peaks at 12.93 GB for Llama-3B+1B because both models must be loaded.

    Key findings

    • Off-the-shelf models keep each stream's predicted token in the mixed top-10 about 30-40% of the time and in the top-100 60-65% of the time. A frequency-only baseline reaches 2.63% and 10.41%.
    • R_KL is below 1 for every model tested, e.g. 0.42 for Pythia-2.8B and 0.37 for Llama-3.1-8B at L=32.
    • Hidden-state superposition error is lowest at early Pythia checkpoints and rises monotonically through pretraining, so linearity is architectural and pretraining erodes it.
    • KL self-distillation cuts Pythia-2.8B mean KL from 1.86 to 0.27 (R_KL 0.42 to 0.06), at a real cost in single-stream quality (LAMBADA 0.544 to 0.357; Qwen2.5 FineWeb PPL 12.44 to 65.52).
    • Joint Contrastive decoding lifts mean superposed LAMBADA accuracy on Llama-3.2-3B from 0.182 to 0.430, against 0.540 for the 1B guide running a single stream.
    • The fused 'Separate' decoder matches vanilla batch-size-2 throughput within 3% (e.g. 81.5 vs 81.4 tok/s on Llama-3B) and is about 2x faster than sequential decoding.

    Notable quotes

    The mixed state does not collapse into gibberish; rather, it effectively narrows down the search space to a small neighborhood containing the valid continuations for both constituent contexts.
    The fine-tuned model is therefore no longer just leaning on the frequency prior; it is genuinely processing the semantic content of both streams in parallel.
    Permutation patching, by contrast, collapses entirely across both token types
    The effect is therefore not attributable to vocabulary under-utilization or Zipf-like priors.

    Limitations

    • Analyses use short contexts (L <= 128, extended to 512 in one experiment) and mostly monolingual English text. Multimodal models are not tested.
    • The conclusion claims a 2x throughput gain, but measured throughput only matches ordinary batch-size-2 decoding. Mixed-stream accuracy and fluency stay well below single-stream (e.g. TinyStories creativity 2.13 vs 5.72).
    • Restoring linearity costs single-stream quality: tuned-distillation PPL rises about 5-6x on Qwen and Llama. There are small internal inconsistencies, e.g. fine-tuning steps given as about 200k in Sec. 4 but 150k in the appendix.

    Open questions

    • Can the geometric-mean obstruction be beaten so that one mixed pass decodes both streams at single-stream quality without an auxiliary guide model?
    • Would the effect, and the fine-tune that restores it, hold at long context, multilingual settings, or frontier scale?
    • Could a superposition objective be added during pretraining to keep linearity without hurting single-stream language modeling?
