WebWorld: The Browser as a World Model for Self-Improving Web Code

📰 Latest · 2026-09-01 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-01

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-31
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30530

WebWorld turns browser execution into a deterministic gate for autonomous web-code improvement: only repairs that demonstrate target progress while preserving previously verified behavior become training data. Removing browser certification nearly eliminates the measured benefit.

Executive summary

WebWorld addresses a weakness of VLM-driven code-repair loops: the model proposing a visually plausible change is also an unreliable judge of whether the interaction works. It compiles critiques into typed interaction contracts, replays them in a browser and admits only transitions carrying an acceptance certificate that proves progress and preservation. A 32,800-example certified SFT corpus improves a 27B model by 5.3 HTMLBench points and 14.9 MiniAppBench-Val points over matched raw supervision.

Browser as an executable world model

The browser acts as a deterministic counterparty to the VLM rather than merely a renderer. Each observation includes screenshots, DOM and console state, probe outcomes and an executed action trace. The VLM proposes a critique, but deterministic policies decide contract admission and certificate issuance.

  • The design separates hypothesis generation from proof.
  • The browser verifies transitions between artifacts, not isolated screenshots.
  • The system enforces typed claims, monotone capability memory and deterministic admission.

Contract, certificate and quality ratchet

A critique is compiled into a contract containing a target predicate, replay action, inherited preservation predicates and optionally a bounded impact scope. A candidate becomes the new baseline only when browser re-execution produces an acceptance certificate.

  • Proof levels include same-trace replay, capability gain, target-issue progress, localized visual evidence and static structural repair.
  • Previously certified predicates accumulate in capability memory.
  • Rejected candidates remain available for diagnosis but cannot enter the SFT export.

Data construction and training

The runtime permits up to ten critique–repair–verify rounds per source artifact. The reported certified pool contains 42,860 transitions; the final training set is a seed-42 uniform sample of 32,800 transitions matched to the raw-data budget.

  • Same-trace replay accounts for 42.3% of certificates and capability gain for 22.5%.
  • Qwen3.5 backbones at 4B, 9B and 27B are trained for three epochs.
  • Training uses 16,384-token sequences and an effective batch size of 128.

Benchmark results

Certificate-filtered SFT improves interactive behavior at every tested scale. The 27B model scores 52.7 on HTMLBench-400 with 43.2% test-case pass and 85.5 on MiniAppBench-Val, versus 47.4, 33.5% and 70.6 for its raw counterpart.

  • Most improvement concentrates in test-case pass and functionality.
  • Rendering, visual and code dimensions change by less than one point without a consistent direction.
  • The benchmark audit reports 400 prompts, 6,000 deterministic test cases, 65 subtypes and 338 prompts requiring interaction.

Mechanism and depth evidence

Equal-budget 9B ablations identify browser-backed admission as the main mechanism. NoCertificate reaches only 44.9 HTMLBench, 0.4 above Raw-9B, while the full gate reaches 49.3. A 5,000-example subset with certified depth of at least five reaches 48.8, within 0.5 point of the full 32,800-example result.

Key findings

  • WebWorld-27B improves over Raw-27B by 5.3 HTMLBench points, 9.7 test-case-pass points and 14.9 MiniAppBench-Val points.
  • Without certificate-backed verification, the 9B gain over raw supervision falls to 0.4 HTMLBench points.
  • The certified pool contains 42,860 transitions.
  • A 5,000-example depth-≥5 subset reaches 48.8 HTMLBench, within 0.5 point of the full-data result.
  • Removing preservation loses 3.3 HTMLBench points relative to the full gate.

Notable quotes

The contract is a claim; the certificate is the proof.
Thus the browser judges transitions, not pages: it admits only executable progress under inherited state.
The first contribution test asks whether SFT on certificate-accepted transitions produces models that behave better than SFT on raw HTML under a matched recipe.
Genuinely perceptual defects, such as aesthetic taste, brand identity, or accessibility nuance, have no executable certificate and fall back to the VLM proxy, inheriting its noise.

Limitations

  • The system is limited to single-file interactive HTML.
  • A browser is not an adequate oracle for aesthetic taste, brand identity or accessibility nuance.
  • All benchmark cells are single-seed runs, and incorrect critiques can still consume repair budget.

Open questions

  • Can contract-and-certificate verification scale to multi-file applications and persistent backends?
  • How should executable evidence be combined with reliable evaluation for aesthetics, accessibility, security and privacy?
  • Do the improvements persist across multiple training seeds and other backbone families?
