WeMM-Embedding: WeChat Multi-Modal Embedding Technical Report

📰 Latest · 2026-09-06 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-06

Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-08-25
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24053

WeMM-Embedding pushes universal text-image-video representations to a strong performance-efficiency frontier: its 2B model matches or beats much larger baselines, while its 9B model reaches 80.6 on MMEB-v2. The report is especially useful for its production-tested recipe combining task-consistent batching, curated hard negatives, selective reranker supervision, teacher distillation, and Matryoshka embeddings.

Executive summary

WeMM-Embedding is a family of 2B, 4B, and 9B universal multimodal embedding models built on natively multimodal Qwen3.5 backbones. The models are trained in two stages: broad alignment on several hundred million heterogeneous pairs, followed by refinement on a curated dataset roughly one tenth that size using hard negatives, graded relevance, selective reranker supervision, and cross-scale embedding distillation. The 9B model records 80.6 on MMEB-v2 and 59.5 on MMEB-v3, while the compact 2B model scores 79.8 across 12 cross-modal retrieval benchmarks and has been deployed in WeChat recommendation and search systems.

Data construction and semantic balancing

The training corpus unifies text, images, videos, and interleaved multimodal inputs as source-target pairs, optionally augmented with instructions, hard negatives, and graded relevance. It includes weakly supervised visual-text pairs, captions, retrieval and classification examples, multimodal QA, and graded-relevance data.

  • A curated second-stage collection—approximately one tenth the size of the broad corpus—uses a three-level residual k-means Semantic ID to reduce overexposure to dense semantic regions without forcing a uniform distribution.
  • An MLLM filters mismatched pairs and repairs factual inconsistencies in noisy descriptions while preserving their original style and detail level.
  • Text hard negatives are generated by multimodal language models; image and video negatives are retrieved from task-specific pools using intermediate WeMM checkpoints.

Universal representation architecture

Each model appends a dedicated <embedding> token to an arbitrarily interleaved sequence of text and visual tokens and uses that token's final-layer hidden state as the normalized representation. The causal formulation can place multiple embedding tokens at different positions.

  • The family contains 2B, 4B, and 9B variants based on corresponding Qwen3.5 backbones.
  • Matryoshka Representation Learning trains nested embeddings that can be truncated and renormalized at inference time.
  • Supported dimensions are generated from one forward pass, enabling storage and retrieval efficiency tradeoffs without separate models.

Two-stage objectives and distillation

Stage 1 interleaves task-specific batches from several hundred million examples. Ordinary paired data use InfoNCE with explicit hard negatives and duplicate-aware source and target masking, while native graded labels use a score-gap-weighted CoSENT-style ranking loss. Stage 2 adds selective reranker supervision and bidirectional KL distillation.

  • Task-consistent batches preserve a common candidate space and make in-batch negatives more informative.
  • Reranker supervision is restricted to task settings where it produces reliable gains because retrieved-candidate reranking did not improve all multimodal tasks consistently.
  • The frozen 9B model teaches the 2B and 4B variants; the final 9B model instead merges complementary specialized Stage-2 variants.

Public benchmark results and efficiency

The 2B model scores 77.9 on the 78-dataset MMEB-v2 benchmark, narrowly exceeding the 8B Qwen3-VL-Embedding score of 77.8. Scaling WeMM to 4B and 9B raises the score to 79.2 and 80.6.

  • On MMEB-v3, the models score 56.0, 58.2, and 59.5 despite receiving zero on all 11 audio tasks because audio input is unsupported.
  • Across 12 cross-modal retrieval datasets, WeMM scores 79.8 at 2B, 80.8 at 4B, and 81.7 at 9B.
  • At 256 dimensions, the 2B model retains 98.7% of its 2,048-dimensional image and video performance.

Ablations and production evidence

The ablations show that batch composition matters more than the other tested Stage-1 choices: replacing task-consistent batching with mixed sampling lowers MMEB-v2 average performance by 3.4 points. The cumulative Stage-2 recipe improves the 2B checkpoint from 75.7 to 77.9.

  • Removing task-specific instructions costs 0.8 MMEB-v2 points; removing duplicate-aware masking costs 0.5 points.
  • Curated data produces the largest single early Stage-2 gain, from 75.7 to 76.6; teacher distillation later raises 76.7 to 77.6.
  • On a private 26-task WeChat benchmark, WeMM-Embedding-2B scores 72.0 versus 60.9 for Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B, and the system reports gains in 14 online A/B tests subsequently rolled into production.

Key findings

  • WeMM-Embedding-2B achieves 77.9 on MMEB-v2, 4.7 points above Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B and 0.1 above its 8B counterpart.
  • WeMM-Embedding-9B reaches 80.6 on MMEB-v2 and 59.5 on MMEB-v3, the strongest values reported in the paper's comparison tables.
  • Task-consistent batching is the most consequential tested Stage-1 choice: mixed sampling reduces the MMEB-v2 average from 71.9 to 68.5.
  • The complete Stage-2 recipe adds 2.2 MMEB-v2 points over the Stage-1 checkpoint, rising from 75.7 to 77.9.
  • At 256 dimensions, image and video tasks each retain 98.7% of their performance at 2,048 dimensions.
  • On the 26-task in-house benchmark, the 2B model improves the average from 60.9 to 72.0 over Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B, accompanied by gains in 14 production A/B tests.

Notable quotes

Empirically, we find that reranking candidates retrieved by our embedding models does not consistently improve performance across multimodal tasks.
Task-consistent batching has the largest impact among the evaluated designs.
At 256 dimensions, the model retains 98.7% of its 2,048-dimensional performance on both image and video tasks; the corresponding retention rates rise to 99.2% and 98.8% at 512 dimensions, respectively.
To date, WeMM-Embedding has delivered consistent gains in 14 online A/B tests across these systems, with the corresponding improvements subsequently rolled out in production.

Limitations

  • The current models do not support audio, so all 11 MMEB-v3 audio tasks receive zero when computing the overall score.
  • The in-house benchmark and 14 online A/B tests provide production evidence, but the paper does not expose the private tasks or report per-test online effect sizes.
  • Training is described only at a high level as several hundred million examples plus a curated subset, limiting independent reconstruction of the exact data mixture and filtering pipeline.

Open questions

  • How much of the performance gain comes from proprietary in-house data versus the reusable training objectives and public data?
  • Can the selective reranker strategy be made robust enough to improve a wider range of multimodal tasks without task-by-task filtering?
  • Will the planned extension to audio and other modalities preserve the compact models' parameter and embedding-dimension efficiency?
