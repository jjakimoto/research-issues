Conformal Kelly: Conformal Prediction Intervals as the Scale in Fractional Kelly Position Sizing

📰 Latest · 2026-08-04
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-04

Source: arXiv q-fin.PM (single-author preprint, pre-registered with a sealed lockbox) · 2026-08-02
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01494

Using a conformal half-width as the Kelly denominator beats a plug-in residual standard deviation by 2.1 pp/yr at matched gross exposure — but the paper's own sealed out-of-sample lockbox shows the calibration transferred while the economics did not, and it says so explicitly.

Executive summary

Conformal prediction gives calibrated intervals; Kelly sizing needs a scale. This paper joins them by setting sigma = conformal half-width / z and sizing f = kappa * mu / sigma^2 across eight US ETFs, with a pre-registered development window (2016-2021) and a sealed lockbox (2022 to Sep 2024). The headline claim is inverted from usual CP desiderata: locally sharp, fast-adapting intervals are bad and slow, stable ones are good, because estimation variance in the denominator is charged to compounded wealth. Every fast-adaptation variant tested (vol-scaled scores, ACI, recency weighting, asymmetric CQR, Mondrian) loses. It is a useful item mainly as a model of honest evaluation: the lockbox result is a partial failure and is reported as one.

The setup: eight ETFs, a frozen harness, a sealed lockbox

Universe is SPY, QQQ, DIA, MDY, GLD, SLV, USO, DBC on a frozen Kaggle snapshot. Splits are TRAIN through 2015, DEV 2016-2021 (1,511 days), and a LOCKBOX from 2022-01-01 sealed until after the DEV work. The scoring harness (per-asset clip at +/-0.75, gross renormalised to <= 2.0, one-day lag, 5 bps per unit turnover) is immutable and identified by a blob SHA.

  • Point forecast is a deliberately weak expanding-window ridge (lambda = 10, refit every 21 days) on momentum 21/63/252d plus EWMA(20) vol.
  • A drift-only intercept forecast already reaches 0.2326, i.e. 82% of the final result — the alpha is not the contribution.
  • Horizon ensemble over H in {12,16,21,27,34}, combined by conformal inverse-variance weights.

The conformal scale

Score s = |R^(21) - mu_hat|; the quantile is the plain 0.75 empirical quantile of the last 500 landed scores, geometrically shrunk toward an expanding anchor with lambda = 0.3. sigma_hat = q_eff / z with z = 1.2816 — the paper discloses this is Gaussian-inconsistent at alpha = 0.25 (1.1503 would be correct) and equivalent to a 24% change in kappa.

  • Sizing f = kappa * mu_hat / sigma_hat^2 with kappa = 0.15, winsorised at +/-0.75 per asset.
  • Book is the equal average of target books from the last K = 5 days (staggered overlapping portfolios).
  • Config B adds a drawdown dial driven by the trailing 21-day pooled lower-break rate; the dial never touches the interval itself.

The core ablation: which sigma?

At exactly matched pre-cap gross, the rolling conformal quantile beats the rolling residual standard deviation by 2.1 pp/yr (0.2774 vs 0.2566), with mean absolute deviation in between at 0.2713 — the growth ordering tracks tail sensitivity. A sigma frozen on pre-2016 residuals (0.2669) still beats a rolling residual sd (0.2476), isolating the functional form from the updating axis.

  • Cross-sectional content in sigma is worth 6.6 pp: flattening the cross-section drops growth from 0.2773 to 0.2113.
  • Every fast-adaptation variant loses: vol-scaled scores -3.3 pp, ACI -1.6 pp, recency weighting -1.4 pp, asymmetric CQR -1.6 pp, Mondrian -2.4 pp.
  • The gross cap binds on 97.7% of DEV days, so the cap does much of the structural work — the author flags this.

The lockbox: calibration transferred, economics did not

Out of sample, realized coverage was 0.7450 against a nominal 0.75, but annualised net log growth was only +0.0847 (Config A) and +0.0701 (Config B), behind the 2x equal-weight bar at +0.1679. Both configs rank last of eleven entries on Sharpe and Calmar. Zero risk-free rate is assumed, flattering all lockbox risk-adjusted figures since cash paid 4-5% over the period.

  • The registered risk-adjusted defence explicitly failed out of sample.
  • 2020 dominates the DEV result (+0.667 vs a 0.285 mean) with skew +1.49 and excess kurtosis +7.65.
  • Roughly 200 configurations were searched on DEV — selection exposure is disclosed.

Key findings

  • At matched gross exposure the conformal quantile scale beat a plug-in residual standard deviation by 2.1 pp/yr (0.2774 vs 0.2566, Sharpe 1.32 vs 1.24), with MAD between them at 0.2713.
  • Every fast-adapting conformal variant lost: vol-scaled scores -3.3 pp, ACI -1.6 pp, recency weighting -1.4 pp, asymmetric CQR-style -1.6 pp, Mondrian -2.4 pp.
  • A sigma frozen on pre-2016 residuals (0.2669) still outperformed a rolling residual standard deviation (0.2476), separating the functional form from the updating speed.
  • Cross-sectional information in sigma is worth 6.6 pp of annual growth (0.2773 flattened to 0.2113); the per-asset cap is described as load-bearing rather than a nuisance constraint.
  • The drawdown dial bought risk, not return: max drawdown 27.7% to 20.3% with Sharpe 1.336 to 1.386, beating 40/40 circular-shift placebos on drawdown (p ~ 0.024) but losing to 31/40 on growth.
  • Out of sample the calibration held (coverage 0.7450 vs 0.750) while the economics did not: +0.0847 and +0.0701 annualised, last of eleven entries on Sharpe and Calmar.

Notable quotes

Positive in five of six years; 2018 is the loss year, shared with every comparator.
The per-asset cap is not a nuisance constraint; it is a load-bearing cross-sectional robustification.
It is the speed of the scale estimator that must be slow, not its cross-sectional content.
This is a step, not a smooth efficient frontier.

Limitations

  • One universe, one data snapshot, one development window; ~200 configurations were searched on DEV, and 2020 alone contributes +0.667 against a 0.285 mean with excess kurtosis +7.65.
  • The gross cap binds on 97.7% of DEV days, so the cap — not the sizing rule — does much of the structural work, and behaviour when it does not bind is untested.
  • The coverage guarantee is measured, not proved: overlapping horizons, geometric shrinkage, rolling windows and conformal ensemble weights each break exchangeability; a zero risk-free rate is assumed despite cash paying 4-5% in the lockbox period.

Open questions

  • Does the conformal-scale advantage survive on a second, independent asset universe — named by the author as the highest-value follow-up?
  • Would a corrected-z specification and risk-matched (rather than gross-matched) sigma comparisons preserve the 2.1 pp edge?
  • Is the variance mechanism the real explanation, or an artifact — it was measured only for the holding-period case and remains conjectural elsewhere.
