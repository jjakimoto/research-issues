How Can Rhetoric Reward-Hack AI Reviewers? Dissecting Rhetorical Sensitivity in AI-Based Peer Review

📰 Latest · 2026-08-15
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-15

Source: arXiv · 2026-08-10
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08975

Content-preserving rhetorical rewrites of real ICLR 2026 submissions move AI reviewer scores by up to +0.93 on a single dimension, and recursive joint rewriting reaches +0.62 mean overall-rating gain — with strict prompting cutting absolute scores by 1.36 points while leaving rewrite sensitivity essentially untouched (cross-protocol rank correlation of rewrite effects: 0.081). Directly relevant to anyone using LLM-as-judge in a research loop.

Executive summary

The authors take 120 anonymized ICLR 2026 submissions stratified across six human-rating bands, rewrite the full LaTeX source along six pre-specified rhetorical dimensions in both directions, and score every variant with five reviewer models under two prompts. The result is 4,200 manuscripts and 42,396 valid review records at $29,166 of API cost. Evidence framing and novelty stance move scores the most; technical register and lexical complexity barely move them at all. The most consequential negative result is that hardening the review prompt lowers absolute scores across the board without reducing susceptibility to rhetoric, and that reviewer models agree far more on paper rankings than on which papers benefit from rewriting — so ensembling does not detect the manipulation.

Controlled design

Public arXiv LaTeX sources were matched to OpenReview submissions by token cosine similarity plus 5-gram Jaccard, then anonymized by an agentic harness with source-diff checks confined to identity regions and manual PDF inspection. Every variant is generated independently from that single verified baseline, and both directions of each dimension are produced separately so directional sensitivity is separated from rewriting-induced drift.

  • Six dimensions x two directions = 12 single-dimension conditions, never stacked: 2,880 single-dimension variants, 240 joint, 480 recursive rounds 2-3, 480 reviewer-guided.
  • Rewriters: GPT-5.5 via Codex CLI and Opus 4.8 via Claude Code, operating on the whole LaTeX project including captions and tables.
  • A programmatic checker compares protected structural anchors (citation keys, labels, URLs, graphics paths, math and code environments, bib files); violations route back for repair and unresolved ones are discarded.
  • Preservation is enforced by soft prompt constraints plus anchor checks — not sentence-level semantic equivalence, so presentation of numbers could change.

Which rhetoric works

Evidence framing produces the largest overall positive-negative contrast, followed closely by novelty stance, then scope framing. Technical register and contribution salience shift scores positively in both directions (a drift signature), and lexical complexity is essentially inert. The intended ordering held for more than 100 of 120 manuscripts on the top three dimensions.

  • Peak single-dimension effects: +0.93 overall rating from positive evidence framing, and −0.73 from negative novelty stance, both under Opus 4.8 rewriting and strict Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite review.
  • Weak-accept probability (share of reviews at overall rating at least 6) shifts by 13.0 pp for evidence, 12.0 pp for novelty, 9.0 pp for scope.
  • Sonnet 5 is the notable outlier — it scores rewrites lower than originals in nearly every cell, in both directions.

The starting score conditions the direction of change

Sorting by the reviewer's own original score rather than by human rating reverses the sign: positive evidence variants average +1.05 overall-rating gain for papers the AI initially scored in [1,3] versus −0.19 for those in [8,10]. The authors flag that this may partly be scale bounds and regression to the mean rather than genuine sensitivity.

  • Joint-rewrite gains by AI-original band: +1.42, +0.51, +0.19, −0.88 across [1,3], [4,5], [6,7], [8,10].
  • By human rating band the same joint rewrites average only +0.28, +0.23, +0.10, +0.20 — the effect tracks the machine's prior, not paper quality.

Escalation buys less than it looks like

Joint rewriting (all six positive objectives at once) is rewriter-dependent: Opus 4.8 gains for every reviewer under both prompts, GPT-5.5 barely moves. Reviewer-guided rewriting — feeding a generated review back into a second pass — adds essentially nothing over plain repetition, with a mean guided-minus-unguided delta of −0.038.

  • Recursive rounds: Opus 4.8 reaches +0.410 standard and +0.624 strict by round 2, with the third round adding little; GPT-5.5 only reaches +0.080 and +0.153 by round 3.
  • Opus 4.8 joint rewrites under strict review shift weak-accept probability by +11.21 pp on average and by +20.00 pp against GPT-5 mini.
  • Same rhetorical edits load onto different rubric dimensions per reviewer — contribution for some, soundness for others — so rubric-level interpretation is not portable.

Key findings

  • Scale: 120 ICLR 2026 papers, 4,200 manuscripts, 42,396 valid reviews, $29,165.89 in direct API cost — one of the larger controlled AI-review studies to date.
  • Evidence framing is the strongest lever (+0.29 mean positive contrast overall, peaking at +0.93), and negative novelty framing is the strongest downward lever (−0.35 mean, peaking at −0.73).
  • Strict review prompting drops absolute scores by 1.36 points and pushes 95.0% of papers lower, yet rewrite effects barely correlate across protocols (0.081) — prompt hardening is not a mitigation.
  • Rewriter identity matters more than rewrite complexity: only Opus 4.8 joint rewrites gain for every reviewer under both prompts, and reviewer-guided second passes underperform plain repetition (mean −0.038).
  • Positive evidence rewrites average +1.05 for papers the AI initially scored in [1,3] and −0.19 for those it scored in [8,10] — a starting-score-conditioned effect the authors caution may include regression to the mean.

Notable quotes

Evidence framing produces the largest overall contrast, followed closely by novelty stance.
Only Opus 4.8 joint rewrites yield positive mean changes for every reviewer under both prompts.
No single-dimension intervention is applied on top of another.
Missing outcomes are retained as missing and are not imputed.

Limitations

  • Restricted to ICLR 2026 submissions with recoverable full source and public review metadata; most configurations rest on a single review per manuscript, with repeated sampling only as an auxiliary audit, so results describe the tested models and prompts rather than human review.
  • The six rhetorical dimensions are not orthogonal and each rewrite is a whole-paper intervention, so no effect is an independent coefficient for one linguistic feature; no multiple-comparison correction is applied and effects are judged by magnitude and consistency.
  • Missing review records are plausibly non-random because certain paper topics appear to trip model safeguards, and the authors explicitly flag dual use: the same pipeline can tailor manuscripts to AI-reviewer preferences without improving the science.

Open questions

  • Is the low-scores-rise / high-scores-fall pattern genuine rhetorical sensitivity or regression to the mean and scale bounds?
  • Why does joint rewriting help with Opus 4.8 but not GPT-5.5, and why does explicit reviewer feedback fail to beat a plain second pass?
  • If neither strict prompting nor multi-model agreement on rankings detects rhetorical manipulation, what does — and what should venues require before AI-assisted review is certified?
