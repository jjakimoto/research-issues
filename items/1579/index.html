    ZGCM-1: A Fully Open and Extremely Efficient Foundation Model for Math and Agentic Search

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-17

    Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-09-11
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13356

    A fully open 7.39B dense model trained from scratch on academic-scale compute that matches or beats 7B-8B peers on math reasoning and rivals 100B+ frontier systems on agentic search, with every stage's weights, data recipes, and logs released.

    Executive summary

    ZGCM-1 is a 7.39B dense foundation model from Zhongguancun Academy / Zhongguancun Institute of AI, trained from scratch under a fully open-source (not merely open-weight) paradigm and targeted at mathematical reasoning and agentic search over a 256K context. Its thesis is that compact models cannot memorize the open web but can transcend parametric limits by pairing deliberate internal thinking with active external tool use. The recipe combines gated sliding-window/global hybrid attention at a 5:1 ratio, FP8 + Muon + TWEO system co-design (~4.2x pre-training time-to-loss speedup), and a 600B-token curriculum mid-training that scales context 16K->64K->256K while reformulating agent traces into MDP state-action supervision.

    Problem: the scale and opacity barriers

    The paper frames two bottlenecks that exclude most academics from frontier work: the Scale Barrier (frontier reasoning and deep agentic search are assumed to require hundred-billion-parameter systems) and the Opacity Barrier (most competitive models ship as open-weight only, hiding filtering recipes, mid-training curricula, long-context schedules, and multi-turn agent traces). ZGCM-1 answers both with a 7.39B dense model plus a full release of per-stage weights, intermediate checkpoints, training code, data recipes, W&B logs, and eval harnesses.

    • Core thesis: compact models overcome static parametric capacity via 'deliberate internal thinking and active external seeking'
    • Release scope: pre-training, mid-training and post-training weights, intermediate checkpoints, code, data + recipes, telemetry logs
    • Code/model/data at github.com/zgcagi/ZGCM-1 and HuggingFace zgcagi/ZGCM-1-7B

    Architecture: hybrid gated SWA + global attention

    Decoder-only Transformer with GQA, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE: 7.39B params, 32 layers, hidden 4,096, SwiGLU intermediate 11,008, 32 query / 8 KV heads (head dim 128), 256K max context. 27 layers use gated sliding-window attention with a 128-token window and 5 use global causal attention (at layers 6, 12, 18, 24, 30), a 5:1 local-to-global ratio following Gemma 3. QK RMSNorm and Partial RoPE (rotary fraction 0.33) are applied. A learned sigmoid gate modulates the local-attention output element-wise before the output projection.

    • Attention-schedule bake-off at 10B tokens / seq 4,096 on 8 H100s: SWA 5:1 hit the highest throughput at 9,566 tokens/s/GPU while matching the full-attention tail loss (1.93); SWA 3:1 got the lowest tail loss (1.92); MLA was slowest at 7,645 tokens/s/GPU with no loss gain
    • SWA 5:1 throughput advantage over full attention widens with context: 1.13x at 4K to 3.94x at 256K
    • Per-token KV footprint drops from 128 KiB to 20 KiB; at 256K full attention needs 32.0 GiB vs 5.0 GiB for the hybrid (6.4x reduction)

    Training recipe: curriculum pre-training, FP8+Muon, MDP mid-training

    General Pre-Training runs two stages: 0.99T tokens (curriculum phase ordering general-language documents by lexical complexity) and 3.20T tokens (heavier on code, math and a specialized reasoning component). Matrix params use Muon (momentum 0.9, five Newton-Schulz steps, constant LR 2e-4, weight decay 0.1); scalars use Adam. Matmuls run Transformer Engine hybrid FP8 (E4M3 forward, E5M2 backward) with delayed scaling, with TWEO activation regularization to suppress outliers. Mid-Training samples a 600.51B-token schedule from a 2.86T-token pool across 16K (180B), 64K (240B) and 256K (180.51B) stages, LR 1e-4, RoPE base 10M at 256K. Agentic traces are recast as MDP-style state-conditioned next-action prediction.

    • The 16K production run sustains ~585 model TFLOP/s/GPU, ~60% BF16-equivalent MFU against the 989 TFLOP/s H100 peak
    • The ~4.2x time-to-loss claim is a product of four estimated factors: SWA 1.4x, FP8 1.5x, Muon 1.8x, Pre-LN 1.1x
    • Curriculum ordering in the 7B probe lowered coding BPB 1.99 -> 0.81 and math BPB 0.97 -> 0.94, costing 0.03-0.09 BPB on general benchmarks
    • An ops anecdote: throughput decayed 585 -> 554 TFLOP/s/GPU after job resume; periodic CUDA allocator cache clearing + GC every 100 iterations restored 585

    Post-training: tiered SFT pruning, think/no-think, mixed RL

    The SFT corpus holds 4,921,933 examples, 96.46% general and 3.54% agentic. Rule-based structural filtering is followed by a model-based evaluator scoring educational value, logical soundness, reasoning coherence, factual consistency, and safety, sorting examples into quality tiers; ~50% of raw candidates are pruned. Decontamination purges any sample with >50% 8-gram overlap against evaluation prompts. The released model is the 256K variant, 10 epochs over 19.46B packed tokens, layer-wise Muon, cosine LR 1e-4 -> 1e-6. RL uses GRPO with dynamic sampling, actor LR 2e-6, binary math rewards and test-pass-fraction code rewards.

    • Finding 4: pruning roughly half the SFT pool raised the six-benchmark mean from 67.78 to 68.83, with BBH +10.10
    • Finding 6: joint general+agentic SFT beats a sequential general-then-agentic schedule; agent-only tuning degrades interaction fidelity
    • Finding 7: long-context capability established in mid-training can be elicited at 256K using only moderate-length SFT data
    • Finding 5: oversaturating the mixture with long-CoT trajectories induces verbosity bias and hurts instruction following

    Results: strong on math, weaker on knowledge, standout on agentic search

    Evaluated in think mode, temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0, mean pass@1 over 32 runs on non-agentic benchmarks. Across 20 benchmarks against six 7B-8B reasoning models, ZGCM-1-7B leads on MATH-500, AIME 2026, HMMT 2025/2026, IMO-AnswerBench and HumanEval+, but trails clearly on MMLU, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench v6 and IFEval. Agentic results are the headline: 63.09 WebWalkerQA, 19.43 BrowseComp, 42.52 GAIA text-only, beating Qwen3-235B-A22B and Claude 4 Sonnet on BrowseComp while remaining far below GPT-5 (54.90).

    • Web deep research uses a thinking-enabled ReAct policy with at most 64 search-and-read steps
    • Binary Function Search pool has 11,000+ tasks; the 50-task eval samples 5 tasks each from 10 held-out projects (tmux, tree, GNU Wget2, XZ Utils, YAJL, zlib, libuv, libgit2, nm, Lua)
    • Web baselines other than WebDancer are taken from public reports, not re-run under the shared harness

    AI-native R&D and its measured limits

    Researchers direct agent swarms across seven stages of model development, backed by a shared harness of accumulated human context and reusable agent experience. Agents write and iterate data-filtering scripts, operate the cluster platform, and run semi-autonomous research loops. Atomic Capability Evaluation (ACE) gives 2,503 probes over 183 atomic capabilities in 18 categories in a two-to-three-minute run, used as fast ablation feedback. Nine core contributors rated 11 task categories on a five-level autonomy rubric (99 ratings total).

    • Consensus levels: experimentation/monitoring and deployment engineering at L4; model architecture design and learning algorithm design at L2; the other seven tasks at L3
    • Only 1 of 99 ratings was L5 and no task was assigned above L4; the authors call these contributor judgments, not standardized measurements
    • ZGent, a self-developed agent platform, sits in the team's meeting channels to distill discussions and dispatch tasks to agents

    Key findings

    • Hybrid gated SWA at a 5:1 local-to-global ratio cuts 256K KV cache from 32.0 GiB to 5.0 GiB (6.4x) and delivers a 3.94x throughput speedup over full attention, with the advantage growing from just 1.13x at 4K context.
    • The claimed ~4.2x pre-training time-to-loss speedup is a multiplicative estimate of four separately measured factors (SWA 1.4x, FP8 1.5x, Muon 1.8x, Pre-LN 1.1x), not an end-to-end measured run; the production 16K run holds ~585 TFLOP/s/GPU (~60% MFU).
    • Aggressive SFT pruning beat volume: removing ~50% of the candidate pool lifted the six-benchmark mean from 67.78 to 68.83, with BBH gaining +10.10.
    • On 50-task Binary Function Search, ZGCM-1-7B recovers 31/50 exact function-entry addresses (62%), matching Kimi-K2, approaching GLM-5.1 at 66%, and dwarfing Qwen3-8B at 12%.
    • On agentic web search it hits 63.09 WebWalkerQA / 19.43 BrowseComp / 42.52 GAIA text-only, exceeding Qwen3-235B-A22B on two of three, but it lags 7B-8B peers badly on parametric-knowledge tasks (MMLU 73.88 vs 85.40 for Qwen3-8B; GPQA-Diamond 47.87 vs 60.10).
    • Contributor-rated AI4AI autonomy is uneven: experimentation/monitoring and deployment reach L4, while model architecture and learning algorithm design sit at L2 and never exceed L3, with only 1 of 99 ratings at L5.

    Limitations

    • Parametric knowledge is bounded by the 7.39B dense scale: on closed-book, recall-intensive tasks without retrieval the model trails large frontier systems, visible in MMLU 73.88 and GPQA-Diamond 47.87.
    • Heavy reasoning supervision induces verbosity that slightly impairs strict non-reasoning instruction following (IFEval 75.42 vs 88.20 for Olmo-3-7B-Think).
    • General software and terminal agency remain nascent: SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.0 completion rates stay modest, and agentic execution is brittle to schema mismatches, observation noise, and search-API latency.

    Open questions

    • Does the hybrid SWA + Muon + MDP mid-training recipe transfer to sparse MoE backbones, which the authors name as their main scaling direction?
    • Can multi-turn interactive RL inside real execution sandboxes close the gap on long-horizon repository-level engineering?
    • How much of the ~4.2x efficiency claim survives an end-to-end controlled comparison, given it is a product of four independently estimated factors?
