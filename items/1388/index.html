Modeling Trade Durations under Temporal Granularity Effects in Forex Markets

📰 Latest · 2026-09-08 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-08

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-02
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02660

FX trade durations pile up at integer seconds purely because of vendor data consolidation, and ignoring this heaping biases both the fitted duration distribution and the estimated ACD dynamics; the granularity-adjusted ACD fixes it with two extra parameters and beats the standard generalized-gamma ACD on all seven major pairs.

Executive summary

Vladimír Holý documents that high-frequency FX trade durations cluster at and around integer seconds — an artifact of vendors merging venues with heterogeneous timestamp precision and clock synchronization, not of trader behavior — and proposes the granularity-adjusted ACD (GA-ACD). The duration is a two-component mixture: a generalized gamma component for regular durations and a component that redistributes generalized-gamma mass onto a truncated normal centered at the rounded integer with spread σ, mixed with weight ρ. The log scale follows a score-driven (GAS) recursion with a nonparametric smoothing-spline intraweek seasonal term. A 2,000-replication Monte Carlo shows ignoring heaping biases the dynamics nonlinearly in ρ; on 2024 Refinitiv Eikon data (>10M observations per pair) the estimated ρ runs 5-27% across the seven majors and GA-ACD raises the average log-likelihood on every pair.

The phenomenon: integer-second heaping is a data-consolidation artifact

EUR/USD durations have elevated density at 1, 2, 3, … seconds and the density of the fractional part concentrates near 0 and 1. This is not intrinsic discreteness but a reporting artifact: FX vendors combine venues that differ in timestamp precision, plus imperfect clock synchronization and vendor aggregation.

  • Heaping appears in durations but NOT in the raw transaction timestamps themselves.
  • It is FX-specific — not observed in stock markets.
  • Clustering extends to the immediate neighborhood of integers, not only exact integers.
  • Distinct from the zero-inflation literature that targets split transactions in equities.

The mixture distribution (five parameters)

X_i = Y_i with probability 1-ρ and Z_i with probability ρ. Y_i is generalized gamma with time-varying scale λ_i and static shapes γ, κ. Z_i takes the Y_i mass on [round(x)-1, round(x)) and reallocates it to [round(x)-0.5, round(x)+0.5) with a normal centered at round(x), sd σ, truncated to that unit interval. Equivalently: round durations up, then add normal timing noise.

  • ρ ∈ [0,1]: weight on the heaping component; σ > 0: tightness of clustering around integers.
  • γ, κ > 0 control hazard shape (decreasing, increasing, or non-monotonic).
  • The generalized gamma baseline is stated as non-essential — Burr, Birnbaum–Saunders, Fréchet or log-normal would work.
  • A symmetric truncated normal is used even though the empirical density is slightly higher just after an integer than just before.

Score-driven scale dynamics

ln λ_i = ω + S_i + E_i with E_i = φ E_{i-1} + α ∇(x_i), a GAS specification. S_i is a nonparametric intraweek seasonal fitted by smoothing splines. Crucially, durations are NOT pre-adjusted for seasonality — the seasonal enters the dynamic model directly so the pre-adjustment does not smear the integer clustering.

  • The mixture score is the density-weighted average of the two component scores.
  • Modeling seasonality in-model to avoid destroying the heaping signal is a practical departure from the usual diurnal-deflation step.
  • Estimated in R with a modified version of the gasmodel package.

Simulation: what ignoring heaping costs you

DGP ω=0, α=0.25, φ=0.998, γ=1.2, κ=0.8, ρ=0.2, σ=0.015; n=10,000; 2,000 replications per configuration. Fitting the standard ACD to heaped data biases both distribution and dynamics, nonlinearly in ρ. For ρ<0.20 ω is severely underestimated while γ is overestimated in compensation; the pattern reverses for ρ≥0.20. φ is underestimated for all ρ>0, worst around ρ=0.25.

  • Misspecification leaks distributional features into the estimated dynamics — not just into the shape parameters.
  • The implied score is markedly too high near integers and too low between them.
  • GA-ACD coverage of nominal 95% CIs is near nominal from n=1,000 for all parameters except ω.

Empirical results: seven majors, full-year 2024, >10M observations each

Refinitiv Eikon, calendar year 2024, seven majors; durations in seconds to three decimals. The empirical hazard falls with duration but has pronounced spikes at integer seconds. GA-ACD gives a uniformly higher average log-likelihood; ω drops markedly, φ rises on every pair, and κ falls on every pair.

  • Estimated ρ spans 5%-27%: lowest USD/JPY 0.0501, highest USD/CAD 0.2734.
  • σ is remarkably stable at ≈0.015 s across all seven majors — a ~15 ms timing jitter.
  • EUR/USD: ω -2.1386 → -3.3459, φ 0.9977 → 0.9985, κ 0.4999 → 0.4142.
  • Exotic pairs are far more heterogeneous: USD/KRW ρ=0.8347 with σ=0.2166.

Computation

Estimation on >10 million observations per pair is expensive because of the complex mixture score. Substituting the simpler generalized-gamma score for the mixture score in the GAS recursion cuts cost considerably but degrades empirical fit.

  • Score complexity, not likelihood evaluation alone, is the bottleneck.

Key findings

  • Integer heaping is pervasive across the seven major FX pairs: ρ ranges from 5% (USD/JPY, 0.0501) to 27% (USD/CAD, 0.2734).
  • σ is nearly identical across all seven majors at ≈0.015 seconds, implying a stable ~15 ms jitter consistent with a mechanical vendor-aggregation origin rather than behavior.
  • GA-ACD raises the average per-observation log-likelihood on every major pair, e.g. EUR/USD -1.2817 → -1.0465 and USD/CAD -1.4461 → -1.0455.
  • Accounting for heaping systematically shifts the estimates: ω falls sharply, φ rises on every pair, and κ falls on every pair.
  • In simulation the misspecified standard ACD underestimates φ for all ρ>0 with the worst distortion near ρ=0.25, and flips the sign of the bias in ω, γ and α around ρ=0.20.
  • Hessian-based inference is reliable from n=1,000 for every parameter except ω, whose 95% CI covers only 79.90% at n=10^3 and reaches 93.95% only at n=10^6.
  • Exotic pairs show far more extreme granularity: USD/KRW ρ=0.8347 with σ=0.2166 versus σ≈0.015 for the majors.

Notable quotes

Notably, the heaping phenomenon is observed in trade durations, but not in the transaction timestamps themselves.
A plausible explanation for the observed heaping is not trading behavior itself, but rather the way FX transaction data are reported and consolidated.
Our dataset covers the full calendar year 2024 for each of the seven major FX pairs.
Comparing the average log-likelihoods, the GA-ACD model delivers a clear and substantial improvement in fit across all currency pairs.

Limitations

  • The heaping component is forced to be symmetric around the integer, although the paper notes the empirical density is higher just after an integer than just before it.
  • The empirical estimates of ω, γ and κ resemble those from higher ρ than estimated, which the author concedes may indicate a gap between the true DGP and the GA-ACD specification.
  • No standard errors, information criteria, or out-of-sample evaluation are provided — comparison rests entirely on in-sample average log-likelihood, from a single vendor and a single year.

Open questions

  • Does heaping vanish on single-venue raw feeds, and can σ be mapped to measurable clock-synchronization error across venues?
  • How much of the documented FX duration persistence changes once heaping is stripped out — is the higher φ under GA-ACD the correct persistence?
  • Can the framework handle the far more extreme clustering of exotic pairs, and would asymmetric or multi-grid versions fit better?
