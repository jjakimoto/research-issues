FlowLOB: Efficient and Controllable Limit Order Book Generation with Flow Matching

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-13
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13096

Under a genuinely matched flow-vs-diffusion comparison (same data, same ~100M adaLN-Zero transformer, same fixed-step ODE solvers), flow matching hits its best LOB sample quality at 10 Euler steps with W1 4-120x lower than diffusion at equal NFE — and because prices are encoded tick-relative, one generator covers eight HKEX symbols and transfers zero-shot to a ninth. It also introduces the evaluation discipline the conditional-generation literature usually skips: a distributional test that a requested regime actually shows up in the generated book, not just in the conditioning vector.

Executive summary

FlowLOB (Simudyne + King's College London) is a conditional flow-matching generator of L2 limit-order-book trajectories, trained jointly across eight HKEX symbols at 0.1s/1s/10s with a tick-relative price representation and four interpretable scenario channels (trend, volatility, liquidity, imbalance). Its central methodological contribution is a controlled flow-vs-diffusion comparison — identical data, backbone, optimizer, and fixed-step ODE solver family — showing flow matching reaches peak quality at 10 Euler steps (10 NFE) while diffusion needs many more network evaluations, with a 4-120x W1 gap at matched sampling compute. At that operating point FlowLOB attains the best pooled-marginal distance in 30 of 32 metric cells at the two finer frequencies, including every cell on a held-out symbol never seen in training. Controllability is scored with an explicit distributional validity test (does the counterfactual sample land closer to the real 5% tail than the reference sample?), passing 42 of 48 cells; the model degrades clearly at 10s, which the authors report rather than hide.

The problem: four properties no existing LOB simulator delivers together

The paper frames LOB simulation around realism, sampling cost, steerability, and cross-instrument generality. Agent-based models (zero-intelligence traders, Hawkes order flow, ABIDES) are interpretable and steerable but reproduce only a subset of stylized facts; deep generative models (GANs, autoregressive event models, diffusion) close the realism gap but carry three costs the authors call out explicitly.

  • Sampling cost is structural, not incidental: one trading day at 0.1s resolution is roughly 200,000 book states, so a 50-step sampler spends 50 network evaluations on each. Fast samplers 'treat the symptom rather than the problem.'
  • Prior learned LOB generators are 'almost universally trained one-model-per-symbol,' so nothing can be said about instruments outside the training set.
  • Conditional validity is rarely measured — models are conditioned on a regime but whether the output actually belongs to that regime goes untested. This is the gap FlowLOB's counterfactual test is built to close.
  • Scope choice: following DiffLOB, the paper generates L2 book states, not order flow / message streams.

Method: matched flow/diffusion objectives and a shared ODE sampler

Flow matching learns a velocity field on the linear interpolant x_t = (1-t)z + t*x_0 with constant target velocity x_0 - z, trained by MSE on v_theta(x_t, c, t). The diffusion baseline uses the same conditional-regression template on a variance-preserving path with epsilon-prediction, converted at sampling time into the probability-flow ODE field. Both are then integrated with the same fixed-step solvers, so the only thing varying across the sweep is the learned dynamics and their ease of numerical integration.

  • Solvers: Euler / Heun / RK4, costing 1, 2, 4 network calls per step, so N steps = N, 2N, 4N NFE. Sweep is N in {10, 20, 30, 40, 50}.
  • Cost is reported in NFE rather than wall-clock deliberately, to strip out batching / hardware / kernel effects — a rare piece of measurement hygiene in this literature.
  • Conditioning tensor c is held fixed along the whole ODE trajectory; diffusion is integrated in reverse time.
  • Caveat a practitioner should notice: diffusion is only given generic fixed-step solvers, not the fast samplers (DPM-Solver, UniPC, consistency distillation) the introduction itself cites.

Representation and conditioning: why one model spans nine symbols

The target is x in R^{2 x 20 x 32} — two channels (price, volume), 20 book levels ordered outer-ask to outer-bid, 32 future time steps on a uniform grid (first observation per bin, empty bins forward-filled). Prices are never absolute: the first price coordinate is the mid-price change between consecutive states, the rest are adjacent level gaps, all in ticks; volumes are log(V+1). This removes instrument-specific scale while preserving local book geometry and depth profile, which is what makes pooled multi-symbol training and zero-shot transfer possible.

  • Conditioning tensor c in R^{7 x 20 x 32}: past-window price/volume representation, time of day, plus four scenario channels.
  • Time-of-day encodes HKEX intraday structure (midday lunch break, double-U volume profile); unlike DiffLOB, the full continuous-auction period is retained rather than dropping the first and last trading hours.
  • Scenario channels: trend and volatility are window-level summaries broadcast over levels and time; liquidity and imbalance are per-future-step and broadcast across levels only.
  • Architecture: ~100M-parameter adaLN-Zero (DiT-style) transformer; each level-time grid cell is one token (20 x 32 = 640 tokens); x_t and c are concatenated channel-wise before projection so a control specified for a future step attaches to that step's tokens; ODE time t injected through adaptive layer-norm.

Data and evaluation protocol (leakage discipline)

HKEX level-2 data, ten levels per side, eight symbols across technology, financials, automotive and semiconductors: 5, 700, 981, 1024, 1211, 1299, 1810, 9618 (.HK). Sessions 09:30-12:00 and 13:00-16:00 = 5.5 hours/day = 198,000 / 19,800 / 1,980 grid points at 0.1s / 1s / 10s.

  • Chronological split: train 2025-09-01 to 2025-11-15, validation 2025-11-16 to 2025-11-30, all evaluation on December 2025. No random shuffling across time.
  • 9999.HK is excluded from training entirely and evaluated zero-shot; 700.HK is the reported in-distribution symbol.
  • Training: AdamW, no weight decay, effective batch 1024 (micro-batch 256 x 4 accumulation), up to 500 epochs with early stopping (patience 20), conditioning dropout 0.5 for classifier-free guidance, best-validation-loss checkpoint.
  • Baselines: LOB-S5 (autoregressive S5 state-space), LOB-GAN (Coletta et al. conditional GAN), compound Hawkes and zero-intelligence. All baseline outputs are converted to the same sampled LOB representation before scoring.
  • The counterfactual test is one-sided by construction and honest about it: counterfactual conditions are drawn from the 5% tails of the TRAINING distribution, while the target regime is the matching 5% tail computed on REAL TEST windows.

Results: a low-NFE win at high frequency, a clear failure at 10s

With Euler at 10 steps, FlowLOB's W1 to the real pooled price and volume marginals is 4-120x lower than the matched diffusion sampler; even allowing diffusion up to 200 NFE, no diffusion configuration in the sweep matches FlowLOB-Euler-10 in five of six settings (the exception is the 1s price marginal, where Heun at 20 NFE matches but stays substantially worse on volume). Euler-10 becomes the fixed operating point for every downstream experiment. Realism holds at 0.1s and 1s and collapses at 10s.

  • 0.1s and 1s: best distance in 30 of 32 price/volume metric cells, median 4.5x smaller than the strongest non-FlowLOB baseline; best in EVERY cell on held-out 9999.HK at both frequencies.
  • 10s: FlowLOB no longer dominates — ZI gives the best price distances on both symbols (0.1445 vs FlowLOB's 4.942 W1 on 700.HK, a ~34x reversal) and LOB-S5 is competitive or best on several volume distances.
  • Controllability: 42 of 48 cells valid. Liquidity and imbalance are valid for every symbol, frequency and direction; all six failures are trend or volatility.
  • Ablation at 1s: a parameter-matched UNet trails the transformer on most price and volume metrics, with the largest degradation on the held-out symbol — evidence that attention over the level-time grid is what carries cross-symbol generalization.
  • Notably absent: DiffLOB, the closest conditional-generation prior, is excluded from the realism table on the grounds that its setting is not matched to the multi-symbol task.

Key findings

  • At matched sampling compute, FlowLOB's W1 to real pooled price/volume marginals is 4-120x lower than the identically-trained diffusion model; even at up to 200 NFE, no diffusion configuration matches FlowLOB with Euler at 10 steps in five of six frequency/feature settings.
  • Flow with Euler at N=10 — the cheapest configuration swept — is the best point in four of six quality-vs-cost panels, and is adopted as the fixed operating point for every downstream experiment.
  • At 0.1s and 1s, FlowLOB attains the best distance in 30 of 32 price/volume metric cells across four metrics (W1, KS, KL, JS), a median 4.5x improvement over the strongest non-FlowLOB baseline (0.1s 700.HK price W1 0.01683 vs ZI 0.1461, Hawkes 10.73).
  • Zero-shot transfer works at fine resolution: on 9999.HK, never seen in training, FlowLOB is best in every 0.1s and 1s cell (0.1s price W1 0.008337 vs ZI 0.3941, LOB-GAN 0.7901).
  • Conditioning is distributionally valid in 42 of 48 cells; liquidity and imbalance pass for every symbol, frequency and direction, e.g. 1s liquidity-high on 9999.HK moves W1 to the real tail from 0.323 (reference) to 0.047 (counterfactual).
  • The 10s regime is a genuine regression, not a shrug: FlowLOB's 700.HK price W1 of 4.942 is ~34x worse than ZI's 0.1445, generated returns are heavier-tailed than real, and both 10s trend-low and volatility-low controls fail on both symbols.

Notable quotes

Third, while conditional generation is common, conditional validity is rarely tested: models are conditioned on a regime, but whether the generated data actually belong to that regime is left unmeasured.
We use NFE, rather than wall-clock time, as the primary inference-cost unit because it isolates the algorithmic cost of sampling from implementation details such as batching, hardware, and kernel efficiency.
This criterion is deliberately distributional: it does not reward a model for merely receiving a condition, but only when the generated books themselves exhibit the requested regime more closely than the reference continuation.
We interpret this as a limitation of the current model at coarse sampling frequencies, where the same calendar span provides fewer training windows and where returns occupy a wider multi-tick support.

Limitations

  • The headline efficiency claim is measured against diffusion running only generic fixed-step solvers (Euler / Heun / RK4). The introduction itself cites DPM-Solver, UniPC and consistency distillation as the standard remedies for diffusion's NFE cost, yet none is used — so the 4-120x gap bounds 'flow beats naively-sampled diffusion', not 'flow beats state-of-the-art fast diffusion sampling'.
  • Realism is scored almost entirely by pooled marginal distances on price and volume features. There is no stylized-fact battery, no order-flow/message-level evaluation, no market-impact or response-function test, and no downstream task (RL execution, stress test) despite those being the stated motivation.
  • Scope is narrow: one venue (HKEX), eight training symbols plus a single held-out symbol, ~2.5 months of training data, L2 snapshots only, and 32-step target windows. The counterfactual test also only checks that the CONTROLLED statistic moves toward its real tail; it does not verify the remaining statistics stay realistic under intervention.

Open questions

  • Does the low-NFE advantage survive a rematch against modern fast diffusion samplers and distilled models at equal NFE, and does it survive a downstream metric (execution-policy PnL, RL agent transfer, risk-model calibration) rather than pooled marginal distances?
  • Is the 10s failure really a data-scarcity artifact, as the authors conjecture, or does the 32-step / level-time tokenization simply not carry multi-tick return support? The two explanations imply very different fixes.
  • Does the tick-relative representation transfer beyond HKEX — to large-tick/small-tick US names, decimalized venues, or futures — and how do realism and controllability scale with model size, symbol diversity and context length?
