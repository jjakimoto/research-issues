    Optimizing the Optimizer: Language Models Discover Faster Molecular Relaxation

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-07

    Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-10-05
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06577

    A Karpathy-style autoresearch loop rewrote Sella, which needed the fewest force calls of the 27 open-source optimizer configurations the authors tested. The agent could edit only one file, and two hard admission gates rejected any edit that stopped early or failed to also improve a validation split. The result, AutoSella-F, needs only 40-77% of Sella's force calls at the r2SCAN-3c DFT level, even though the search never computed a DFT gradient. The same harness driven by Grok 4.6 passed its own gates yet got worse on held-out multi-fragment systems: a validation gate only certifies the distribution it samples.

    Executive summary

    The authors point coding agents at the source of Sella 2.5.0, consolidated into one file. They ask it to cut the number of force evaluations a molecular geometry optimization needs, scoring every candidate on the cheap GFN2-xTB potential. A candidate becomes the new champion only if it (1) does not under-relax and has no evaluation errors (validity gate) and (2) also lowers cost on a separate validation split (generalization gate). Over 66 active research hours each, Claude Fable 5.1 (max) produced AutoSella-F and GPT-6 Astra (high) produced AutoSella-A. AutoSella-F's force-call savings held on four held-out molecule sets and transferred to the GFN-FF force field and to r2SCAN-3c DFT (40.24-77.20% of Sella's per-molecule force calls). AutoSella-A, and a Grok 4.6 run reported in an appendix, regressed on solvated multi-fragment systems.

    Problem and setup

    At the DFT level, gradient evaluations dominate the wall time of a molecular relaxation, so the number of force calls an optimizer needs sets the price. The authors benchmarked 27 optimizer configurations under one protocol. That protocol uses geomeTRIC-style five-criterion convergence, a 200-call cap (500 for Solvated PubChem), and each optimizer's own stopping test disabled. Sella 2.5.0 needed the fewest force calls on every benchmark, so it became the starting point. The search ran on GFN2-xTB as a cheap surrogate; the target level is DFT.

    • Training: 469 structures (225 SPICE PubChem molecules, 219 DES370K dimers, and 25 RowanSci drugs). Validation: 465 structures (250 PubChem molecules, 215 dimers).
    • Held-out sets: 500 dissimilar PubChem molecules, 673 dipeptides, 475 amino acid-ligand pairs, and 100 Solvated PubChem systems (one solute plus 20 waters).
    • Cost metrics: C_per-mol (the mean of per-molecule force-call ratios against Sella) and C_pooled. Relaxation depth: mean relative energy recovery r, where 1 means the same energy drop as Sella.
    • Sella was traced (1418 of 9136 lines on the executed path), concatenated and pruned into a single 5975-line seed file, algo.py.

    Loop topology: propose → implement → verify

    The loop follows Karpathy's autoresearch pattern: a fixed benchmark, a single editable file, and a keep-or-discard decision on the measured result. In each cycle the agent reads its records, makes one coherent edit to the champion, commits algo.py, and submits it to a remote evaluator. A rejected edit is reverted to the champion, but the attempt stays in the record. The run is single-trajectory, which the authors say is much cheaper in tokens than multi-agent teams.

    • Isolation: the agent runs in an unprivileged container with a read-only root filesystem, read-only dataset mounts, and CPU, memory and process limits. Web search is allowed.
    • Scoring is done outside the agent's environment. A run-specific SSH relay exposes only a JSON evaluation protocol. Behind it are a gateway, a Redis FIFO queue, and 192 single-thread workers on 4 Intel Xeon Gold 6348 hosts. Scoring one candidate on the 469-structure training set takes about 60-90 s.
    • Feedback: a JSON record for each molecule (force calls, relative cost, energy recovery, final geometry, convergence, stop reason). Diagnostic bundles for failed or non-converged runs hold up to 8 geometry/force frames and 200 scalar records, with SHA-256 provenance.
    • Memory: results.tsv, generalizable.tsv and non_generalizable.tsv, a narrative full_log.md, and a backlog.md of near-misses. Promising rejected code is kept under ideas/ along with its commit hash.
    • Rules in program.md: edit only algo.py; never modify the evaluator, convergence criteria or datasets; no molecule-specific special cases; give a promising but failed idea at most 3 targeted repairs, to avoid endless hyperparameter tuning.

    Verifier grounding: the two admission gates

    Validity gate (training split): mean energy recovery must be at least 1, there must be no evaluation errors (exceptions, non-finite values, malformed output), and C_per-mol must beat the champion by more than delta = 1e-4. Non-converged runs that hit the call cap stay in the averages. Generalization gate (validation split): the same three conditions must hold against the champion's validation cost. The energy condition is what stops the agent from saving force calls by under-relaxing. Some rules are not checked by the evaluator at all.

    • Under-relaxing edits are caught by the energy condition: 8 Fable candidates (7.4%) and 115 Astra candidates (17.1%) were rejected for insufficient energy recovery.
    • The 'one coherent edit' rule is not computed by the evaluator. Algorithm 1 says it is applied by the agent to its own proposal. The 'no molecule-specific recognition' rule is likewise only an instruction.
    • Neither gate looks at geometry. The authors therefore checked, after the fact, heavy-atom RMSD, the energy difference and numerical-Hessian frequencies against Sella's endpoints, all at GFN2-xTB only.
    • The same validation split gates every candidate across hundreds of cycles; the four held-out sets are the only data never seen during the search.

    What was validated externally, and by whom

    All validation was done by the authors, and the code is public at github.com/vdeshchenya/autosella. The frozen optimizers were run on four held-out GFN2-xTB benchmarks and then on two potentials the search never used: the GFN-FF force field, and r2SCAN-3c DFT through ORCA 6.1.1 (TightSCF, analytic gradients). AutoSella-F needed 39.86-73.25% of Sella's per-molecule force calls on xTB and 40.24-77.20% under DFT, with mean energy recovery above Sella on all four DFT sets. Under DFT, AutoSella-A needed 78.43-86.71% of Sella's force calls on the other three sets but regressed on Solvated PubChem (118.77% on xTB, 109.06% under DFT). Note that the held-out tables compare against fragment-enabled Sella, while the training and validation progress numbers compare against the weaker allow_fragments=False seed.

    • DFT checks measured force calls and energy recovery only. Structural checks (RMSD and frequencies) were done only at the xTB level.
    • Same-minimum agreement with Sella is high on single molecules (97.2% and 97.6% on Test) but low on fragment systems (39.8% and 69.5% on AA-ligand pairs, 1.0% and 18.0% on Solvated PubChem).
    • AutoSella-F's lower mean energy hides a mix of outcomes: it finishes more than 1 kcal/mol below Sella on 24.84% of AA-ligand pairs and 51.00% of solvated systems, but more than 1 kcal/mol above on 18.74% and 38.00%.

    Loop dynamics, cost and reliability

    Each run had 66 active research hours, covering in-container analysis plus remote evaluation. The paper reports no dollar, token or LLM API cost, and no GPU-hours; compute is described only as CPU evaluation hosts. The two agents worked at very different rhythms. Fable spent long stretches on local analysis, including a 160-minute gap between cycles 87 and 88 with hundreds of shell commands; that analysis led to a new initialization scheme. Astra committed much faster but its later gains were small.

    • Fable 5.1 (max) via Claude Code 2.1.251: 108 candidates, 37 accepted, 3 evaluation errors (2.8%), and a mean gap of 36.32 +/- 32.08 min between commits.
    • GPT-6 Astra (high) via Codex CLI 0.155.0: 672 candidates, 29 accepted, 35 evaluation errors (5.2%), and a mean gap of 5.88 +/- 3.66 min between commits.
    • Grok 4.6 (xhigh) via Cursor Agent: 900 candidates and 44 accepted, but the champion only reached 97.79% (train) and 98.14% (validation). On held-out sets it cost 148.77% (AA-ligand) and 239.85% (Solvated PubChem), with 64 non-converged runs and 4 errors on the solvated set. It was dropped before any DFT testing.
    • Fable's main mechanisms: added curvature terms for non-covalent contacts (cycles 34-35), reuse of stored gradient differences corrected for the changing geometry (cycles 53-55), and fragment pre-positioning and conformer fixes that need no potential calls.

    Significance for autonomous-researcher builders

    The paper shows that a cheap surrogate verifier can ground a code-evolving loop whose output transfers to an expensive target level (xTB to DFT), provided the gate penalises metric gaming such as stopping early. It also shows the generalization gate's blind spot. The validation split contained only small dimers, not solvated multi-fragment systems. Two of the three runs (Astra and Grok) passed every gate yet regressed on exactly that unseen regime. The ablations suggest that physically meaningful changes, rather than parameter tweaks, carried the gains.

    • Removing the model-Hessian-and-coordinates block costs +18.68 pp on validation for AutoSella-F (+15.57 on training) and +8.82 pp for AutoSella-A.
    • Fragment handling costs about +11.3 pp (F) and +5.7 pp (A). Hessian updates cost +10.16 pp in F, and the Gaussian-process step costs +4.7 pp in A.
    • Removing pairs of blocks together costs less than the sum of the single removals, so the effects of the blocks are not additive.

    Key findings

    • At r2SCAN-3c DFT, AutoSella-F needs 77.20% (Test), 73.14% (Dipeptides), 68.61% (AA-ligand) and 40.24% (Solvated PubChem) of Sella's per-molecule force calls, with mean energy recovery above 1 on all four sets. No DFT gradient was evaluated during the search.
    • On held-out GFN2-xTB sets AutoSella-F costs 39.86-73.25% of Sella. AutoSella-A costs 78.14-92.57% on three sets but regresses to 118.77% (mean r 0.999125) on Solvated PubChem, and to 109.06% under DFT.
    • In 66 active hours each, Fable committed 108 candidates and accepted 37, while Astra committed 672 and accepted 29. The energy gate rejected 7.4% of Fable's candidates versus 17.1% of Astra's, and evaluation errors were 2.8% versus 5.2%.
    • Grok 4.6 committed 900 candidates and accepted 44 under the same gates, but its champion reached only 97.79% / 98.14% (train/val). On held-out sets it cost 148.77% on AA-ligand pairs and 239.85% on Solvated PubChem, so it was never tested with DFT.
    • The same-minimum check passes for 97.2-97.6% of Test molecules but only 1.0-18.0% of solvated systems. AutoSella-F ends more than 1 kcal/mol above Sella on 38.00% of solvated systems, even though its mean energy is lower.
    • Ablation: removing the model-Hessian-and-coordinates block adds +18.68 pp of validation cost for AutoSella-F and +8.82 pp for AutoSella-A. Removing pairs of blocks costs less than the sum of single removals.

    Notable quotes

    An aggregate tells the agent whether a mechanism helped; these records tell it which molecules it helped and which it hurt, which is what turns the next cycle into a chemically-grounded hypothesis rather than a parameter sweep.
    The generalization gate then ensures that accepted improvement holds on unseen molecules, preventing the overfitting to the training set.
    Under DFT the force call dominates the calculation, so the reduction in force calls lowers the cost of the job proportionally, making the AutoSella optimizers practically useful.
    The agent must not introduce molecule-specific recognition or special cases based on known identities, exact formulas, or identifying graph features.

    Limitations

    • All validation is by the authors, with one run per model, so model comparisons are anecdotal. The DFT check measures only force calls and energy recovery; the RMSD and frequency checks were done at xTB only. Endpoints on fragment systems often differ from Sella's (1.0-69.5% same-minimum agreement).
    • The generalization gate certifies only the validation distribution, which is drug-like molecules and small dimers. AutoSella-A and AutoSella-G passed every gate yet regressed on solvated multi-fragment systems. The 'one coherent edit' and 'no molecule-specific special cases' rules are instructions, not checks the evaluator performs.
    • No LLM token or dollar cost and no GPU-hours are reported. Compute is given only as 66 active research hours per run, about 60-90 s of evaluation per candidate, and 192 CPU workers.

    Open questions

    • Would adding multi-fragment or solvated systems, or a small DFT subset, to the validation gate have prevented the Astra and Grok regressions?
    • How repeatable is AutoSella-F's gain across reruns of the same model, and what did the 66-hour runs cost in tokens or dollars?
    • Do the evolved heuristics (geometric pre-rules, fragment pre-optimization, contact curvature terms) carry over beyond drug-like organics, for example to metals, periodic systems or transition-state searches?
