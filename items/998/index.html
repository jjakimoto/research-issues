CDRL: Certification-Driven Reinforcement Learning for Neutrino Flavor Model Discovery

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-21
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20686

CDRL shows what an autonomous-discovery loop looks like when the verifier is fully execution-grounded and its failures are machine-readable: a physics pipeline rejects a candidate, a deterministic analyzer turns the rejection into a SAT conflict clause, and Boolean Constraint Propagation makes the whole failing equivalence class unreachable for every parallel worker. That single change buys up to 1.95x the valid-model rate, up to 6.33x the neutrino-model rate, and up to 4x fewer expensive evaluations over the prior SOTA (AMBer) in a >10^26 hypothesis space, with no LLM judge anywhere in the loop.

Executive summary

Jha et al. (Georgia Tech, UC Irvine, Fermilab, LBNL) attack neutrino flavor model discovery, a combinatorial search over >10^26 candidate quantum field theories where evaluating one candidate costs a full symbolic + numerical physics run. Their framework replaces the usual scalar-reward-only RL loop with a two-signal loop: the physics verifier still emits a reward, but every failure is additionally analyzed into a certificate naming the exact block assignments responsible, which is compiled into a SAT conflict clause and enforced during AlphaZero-style MCTS by Boolean Constraint Propagation. Across three theory spaces CDRL beats the prior SOTA RL system (AMBer) by up to 1.95x on valid-model rate and 6.33x on neutrino-model rate while evaluating up to 4x fewer candidates, and 40 decision-tree rules mined post-hoc from its own search trajectories give a further ~1.5-2x / 2-3x when re-injected as a soft policy bias. For anyone building an autonomous research system, the load-bearing detail is that the verifier is deterministic and execution-grounded (real physics software plus SAT), not a model-graded judge, so a failure can be converted into a hard, reusable, provably-sound pruning constraint rather than a gradient nudge.

The discovery problem and the size of the search space

A candidate model is a matrix: rows are particles (charged lepton doublets, singlets, right-handed neutrinos, Higgses, and several flavons), columns are one-hot irreducible-representation assignments under a non-Abelian flavor group, Abelian Z_N charges, and flavon VEV configurations; unassigned entries are -1 so the state can represent partial models. The paper states exhaustive search over more than 10^26 possible models is infeasible because a single candidate requires non-trivial symbolic and numerical computation. Three benchmark spaces are taken unchanged from AMBer for comparability.

  • (i) A4 x Z4 with up to 5 flavons
  • (ii) A4 x Z_N with N in [2,10] and up to 5 flavons
  • (iii) T19 x Z4 with up to 6 flavons (the hardest space)
  • Matrix state representation and physics pipeline are the same as AMBer, so the comparison isolates the certificate mechanism

RL formulation: state, action, reward

Model construction is a sequential decision process over blocks rather than individual matrix cells: first the active Z_N symmetry, then for each particle its representation and then its Abelian charge as two separate blocks, then each flavon's VEV configuration. The action alphabet at each block is small, which keeps the branching factor low. Search is AlphaZero-style MCTS with PUCT guided by a policy-value network; the policy is masked by BCP's legal-action vector so only constraint-consistent actions are ever explored. Terminal reward comes from the physics pipeline.

  • Reward: combines chi^2 fit quality and the number of free parameters, normalized and clipped, equal weighting by default (better fit with fewer parameters scores higher)
  • Invalid = rank-deficient mass matrix (no physical spectrum) or 'inelegant' (>50 free parameters); these get a strong penalty
  • 'Neutrino model' (the headline metric) requires chi^2 <= 10 AND at most 7 free parameters
  • Notably CDRL's reward has NO bonus term for higher-order Z_N symmetries while AMBer's does — yet CDRL still gets broader Z_N coverage, which the authors attribute to uniform pruning of redundant equivalence classes rather than reward shaping
  • Network: flattened state -> FC 512 -> FC 256 -> policy head (log-softmax) + value head (tanh); policy trained with cross-entropy against MCTS visit counts, value with MSE against normalized terminal rewards
  • Training: 30 iterations x 100 episodes, 150 MCTS simulations per move, c_puct 0.5, batch 512, 20 epochs/iteration, Adam; a new checkpoint is kept only if cumulative reward beats the previous one

Verifier grounding: what actually decides 'valid' (no LLM judge)

The verifier is the physics evaluation pipeline itself — real domain software, executed, not a learned or model-graded critic. A terminal model matrix is compiled by Model2Mass into a Lagrangian, from which Dirac and Majorana mass matrices are built and the light-neutrino mass matrix is obtained via the seesaw mechanism; flavorpy then fits the model's free parameters to neutrino observables from NuFit 5.3 and scores a chi-squared goodness of fit. Structural validity is a deterministic rank check. A second, formal layer sits on top: domain knowledge is compiled once into CNF and enforced by MiniSAT-style Boolean Constraint Propagation via PySAT at every MCTS node. GPT-5.3 appears only as a baseline generator, never as a judge — and it scores near random.

  • Deterministic + execution-grounded: symbolic Lagrangian construction, seesaw mass-matrix extraction, numerical parameter fitting, statistical comparison to NuFit 5.3 data
  • Formal layer: CNF + BCP, so illegal actions are masked before the policy network ever ranks them, without a full SAT solve per decision
  • NOT lab-validated: 'validation' means a chi^2 fit to existing global neutrino-oscillation data, not an experiment; the mined rules are explicitly not yet validated as physically meaningful
  • No LLM anywhere in the accept/reject path

Certification mechanics: how a failure becomes a reusable constraint

A dedicated certificate-analysis routine runs on every terminal model. It inspects the completed matrix, tests it against a fixed set of criteria, and when one fires it identifies the exact subset of assignments responsible and emits their negation as a clause — so one certificate prunes an entire class of models, not just the candidate that failed. Class (i), insufficient-rank representation: after a failure the system temporarily wildcards the whole Abelian charge sector and re-runs the rank check; if that reduced model is already rank-deficient, no charge assignment can restore full rank, so the failure is provably attributable to the representation-and-VEV combination. Class (ii), Z_N symmetry certificates, are pure group theory: reducibility when all charges share a divisor, and charge negation as an order-2 automorphism preserving the selection rule. Clauses are accumulated globally and detection falls out of BCP.

  • Certificate soundness is argued formally (rank monotonicity + two group-theoretic equivalences), not statistically
  • Clause sharing is portfolio-style, inspired by parallel SAT solvers: workers share a replay buffer AND a global conflict-clause database, so any certificate found by one worker is immediately enforced in all others; terminal evaluations are also cached globally
  • The authors are explicit that this differs from a negative reward 'in kind, not degree': a scalar lowers a probability but never rules a branch out, and says nothing about which decision was to blame
  • No CDCL-style clause deletion in the current implementation — clauses accumulate monotonically; the authors flag clause forgetting as future work for larger domains

Results, cost, and ablations

On A4 x Z_N, CDRL reaches 26.9% valid models vs AMBer's 13.79% (1.95x) and 0.19% neutrino models vs 0.03% (6.33x); gains on the other two spaces are 1.44x/5.00x and 1.65x/3.00x, while matching AMBer's best Min Params. Sample efficiency: CDRL finds MORE neutrino models with 4x fewer candidate evaluations. Numbers are mean +/- std over 4 seeds. Cost is dominated by the verifier: approx. 98% of compute is the physics pipeline, so the authors report candidate evaluations rather than wall-clock — no wall-clock or dollar figures are given.

  • Hardware: Intel Xeon Gold 6448Y (32 cores), 32 GB RAM, one NVIDIA H100 (80GB, MIG 20GB partition); parallel self-play on CPU workers, network training on GPU
  • Compute configs differ between systems (CDRL 32 CPU cores vs AMBer 250 parallel environments), which is why wall-clock is not compared
  • Removing the DNN or MCTS collapses neutrino discovery to ~0.00% — guided exploration is necessary at all in a space this large
  • Removing certificates (= the AlphaZero-style baseline) drops A4 x Z_N from 26.9% -> 16.2% valid and 0.19% -> 0.03% neutrino
  • Removing portfolio clause/replay sharing keeps the mean similar but roughly halves neutrino discovery and roughly doubles variance
  • GPT-5.3 few-shot prompting scores 0.2/0.2/0.1% valid and 0.00% neutrino — essentially random search (0.1%)
  • Qualitative: CDRL spreads discoveries across many more Z_N groups than AMBer, and none of CDRL's neutrino models exactly match AMBer's

Search knowledge discovery: 40 mined rules re-injected as soft constraints

After training (offline, not part of the per-candidate loop), the authors mine the MCTS record. They keep decision points where the dominant action received more than 95% of visits and had a positive value estimate, bucket them by construction progress, and fit one shallow decision tree per bucket (max depth 3) predicting the dominant action from earlier assignments only. Root-to-leaf paths become rules; 40 are reported. Re-injected into fresh smaller-scale runs as a soft policy bias (beta = 0.5), then masked by the hard legality constraints and renormalized, they raise valid-model rates ~2x and neutrino discovery 2-3x on A4 x Z4 and T19 x Z4, and ~1.5x / up to 3x on A4 x Z_N.

  • Soft constraints never override hard constraints: the reweighted policy is still legality-masked
  • Some rules are physically sensible (charged lepton singlets taking identical or systematically shifted Z_4 charges; coordinated triplet assignments mirroring human model-builder patterns); others are admitted to be possibly spurious
  • Important caveat the authors raise themselves: each tree is fit to a small selective high-confidence sample, so a rule can look confident merely because other values were rare in that sample
  • KD runs are smaller-scale, so their absolute numbers are NOT comparable to the main table

Key findings

  • Verifier is execution-grounded and deterministic end to end: Model2Mass compiles the candidate into a Lagrangian, the seesaw mechanism yields the light-neutrino mass matrix, flavorpy fits free parameters to NuFit 5.3 and returns a chi^2; legality is enforced formally by CNF + MiniSAT-style BCP. GPT-5.3 appears only as a baseline generator and scores at random — there is no LLM judge in the loop.
  • Certificates are sound, targeted negations rather than reward shaping: 3 types (insufficient-rank reps with the charge sector wildcarded; Z_N reducibility by a shared divisor; charge negation), each naming the exact responsible assignments and pruning the whole equivalence class for every parallel worker via BCP.
  • Up to 1.95x valid-model rate and 6.33x neutrino-model rate vs AMBer (A4 x Z_N: 26.9% vs 13.79%; 0.19% vs 0.03%), matching AMBer's Min Params, over 4 seeds and 3 theory spaces in a >10^26 model space.
  • Up to 4x fewer verifier calls for MORE discoveries: T19 x Z4 gives 7019 neutrino models from 6,000,000 evaluations vs AMBer's 6439 from 24,000,000; A4 x Z_N gives 2343 from 1,000,000 vs 1394 from 4,000,000.
  • Cost is entirely verifier-bound: approx. 98% of runtime is the physics evaluation pipeline, so evaluations (not wall-clock) are the reported efficiency metric; hardware was a single 32-core Xeon Gold with 32 GB RAM and one H100. No wall-clock or dollar cost is reported.
  • Ablations attribute the certificate contribution precisely: removing certificates drops A4 x Z_N from 26.9% -> 16.2% valid and 0.19% -> 0.03% neutrino (back to AlphaZero level), while removing the DNN or MCTS collapses neutrino discovery to 0.00%; removing clause/replay sharing roughly halves neutrino discovery and roughly doubles the standard deviation.
  • 40 interpretable rules mined post-hoc from >95%-visit MCTS decisions (depth-3 decision trees per progress bucket) and re-injected as a soft policy bias (still legality-masked) give ~1.5-2x valid and 2-3x neutrino gains in smaller-scale rerun experiments.

Notable quotes

In effect, a scalar reward changes where the agent prefers to go, whereas a certificate changes where it can go.
A model is invalid if it fails a structural check (a rank-deficient mass matrix, so no physical spectrum exists) or is inelegant (more than 50 free parameters); such models receive a strong penalty.
We therefore use BCP as a lightweight symbolic reasoning layer that filters illegal actions without requiring a full SAT solve after every decision.
the dominant computational cost (approx. 98%) arises from the physics evaluation pipeline, which is the same as AMBer.

Limitations

  • CDRL depends on the existence of a certificate-generating tool and is currently best suited to structured, discrete search spaces where constraints can be represented and reused; the authors note certificate analysis and clause management could become overhead in larger spaces (no CDCL-style clause deletion is implemented, clauses accumulate monotonically).
  • The 40 mined rules are not independently validated as physically meaningful — the authors state they describe regularities in how the search explores, shaped by their own design choices, and that each tree is fit to a small selective high-confidence sample.
  • Comparison to AMBer is normalized by candidate evaluations, not wall-clock, because the two systems ran on different compute configurations; neutrino-model rates are also tiny in absolute terms (0.09-0.19%), and 'valid' means a chi^2 fit to existing NuFit 5.3 data, not any experimental confirmation.

Open questions

  • Does the certificate loop transfer to domains where the verifier is less crisply formal than a rank check plus group theory — chemistry, materials science, theorem proving — and can generalizing a certificate into a reusable constraint itself be learned?
  • Which of the 40 mined rules reflect genuine physical structure rather than search artifacts, and does re-injecting them at full scale still yield the 1.5-3x gains?
  • How does the monotonically growing clause database behave over much longer runs or larger spaces without clause forgetting, and would combining CDRL with foundation-model priors add anything given GPT-5.3 alone performed at random?
