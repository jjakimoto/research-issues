    Trade arrival dynamics and quote imbalance in a limit order book

    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-21

    Theme: Queue state and short-horizon price formation
    Queue-level market microstructure models turn observable bid/ask depth and event flow into probabilities of price moves, fills, and spread capture. The continuous-time Markov-chain baseline provides tractable stationary and first-passage analysis, while the diffusion extension shows how imbalance and trade-arrival dynamics jointly affect passive execution.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0514

    A foundational queue-state model connecting top-of-book imbalance, trade-arrival timing, short-horizon price direction, and passive-execution slippage.

    Executive summary

    The paper shows empirically that top-of-book imbalance predicts the direction and timing of short-horizon price moves and near-side trade arrivals. It extends a two-queue diffusion model with a latent trade-arrival process, derives semi-analytic first-event probabilities, and calibrates the model to VOD.L data from the first quarter of 2012. The calibrated model explains substantial passive-fill slippage through the interaction of queue depletion and trade flow, while fitting poorly at extreme imbalance values.

    Empirical setup

    The authors define book imbalance as I=(qᵇ−qᵃ)/(qᵇ+qᵃ) and condition several stopping-time outcomes on it: the next mid-price change, the next buy trade, and the next sell trade. For each quote update, they identify the subsequent event, measure the intervening price change and waiting time, and bucket observations by initial imbalance.

    • Highly positive imbalance indicates a bid-heavy top of book; highly negative imbalance indicates an ask-heavy book.
    • The next-tick price change is approximately linear in imbalance but remains below the spread, limiting simple statistical-arbitrage interpretations.
    • Waiting times shorten for strongly imbalanced books, and outcomes differ according to the side of the arriving trade.

    Two-dimensional queue model

    Best-bid and best-ask quantities are modeled as correlated Brownian motions. Depletion of the ask queue produces an upward price move, depletion of the bid queue produces a downward move, and the depleted queue is reset from a positive distribution representing the next price level.

    • Price-direction probabilities become first-hitting probabilities in the positive quadrant.
    • A decorrelating coordinate transform followed by polar coordinates reduces the governing PDE to a one-dimensional angular equation.
    • The resulting closed-form probability gives the next upward price move as a function of both queue sizes and their correlation.

    Trade-arrival extension

    A third diffusion, φ, represents the latent clock for a near-side trade, which occurs when that process reaches zero. Correlations between φ and the two queue processes encode the joint dependence of trade arrivals, order flow, and price changes; the paper describes the resulting construction as non-Markovian because the trade-arrival state is unobserved and restarted after events.

    • The relevant alternatives are a favorable price move, an unfavorable price move, or a near-side passive fill.
    • These alternatives correspond to which boundary of the positive three-dimensional orthant is hit first.
    • The model targets the execution decision between posting passively and immediately crossing the spread.

    Semi-analytic solution and calibration

    The correlated three-dimensional hitting-probability PDE is transformed into an uncorrelated Laplace problem, then into spherical and logarithmic angular coordinates. Its solution is represented by a generalized Fourier series whose coefficients follow from matrix inversion; event probabilities are therefore computed semi-analytically, whereas conditional expected price changes are estimated by Monte Carlo.

    • Calibration estimates the latent initial state φ₀ and the three pairwise correlations; imbalance itself is observed.
    • The principal calibration uses VOD.L for every trading day in the first quarter of 2012.
    • The calibrated parameters are ρₓ𝓏=−ρᵧ𝓏=0.8, ρₓᵧ=−0.1, and φ₀=3.5 sec^(1/2).

    Results and execution significance

    The model reproduces the side-dependent gap between average mid-price moves before buy and sell trades and the broad shapes of first-event probabilities. It attributes about 60% of the theoretical spread saving lost by passive fills to the interaction between queue depletion and trade flow, beyond the usual adverse-selection interpretation.

    • At high near-side imbalance, the probability of an unfavorable move reaches almost 90%.
    • For moderate imbalance, the model captures the broad empirical trade-arrival-time shape.
    • At extreme imbalance, it misses the observed steep reduction in arrival times.
    • The findings motivate an imbalance-dependent rule that posts passively in moderate states and crosses the spread in adverse, highly imbalanced states.

    Key findings

    • Top-of-book imbalance jointly predicts short-horizon price direction and the waiting time to price changes or trades.
    • Conditioning on the side of the next trade reveals a systematic price-move gap consistent with passive-fill slippage.
    • A correlated three-process diffusion turns price moves and fills into competing first-hitting events with semi-analytic probabilities.
    • The calibrated queue/trade interaction accounts for about 60% of the theoretical spread saving lost by a passive fill.
    • Extreme near-side imbalance can imply almost a 90% probability of an unfavorable price move, supporting state-dependent execution decisions.

    Notable quotes

    The price change until the next tick is well approximated by a linear function of the imbalance and is typically well below the bid-ask spread, even for highly imbalanced order books.
    Expectations of price changes up to the stopping time defined by the arrival of a trade cannot be easily computed with analytic methods and Monte Carlo simulations were used instead.
    In the calibration described here, this effect alone is responsible for the loss of about 60% of the theoretical spread captured by a passive fill.
    Deriving such optimal spread crossing policies is left for future work.

    Limitations

    • The detailed calibration is shown for one equity, VOD.L, over the first quarter of 2012; other liquid stocks are said only to produce qualitatively similar results.
    • The model abstracts the top-of-book queues as diffusions, assumes a fixed spread after depletion, and represents trade arrival with an unobserved latent process.
    • It does not accurately reproduce the steep decline in trade-arrival times at extreme imbalance values.

    Open questions

    • How should the derived first-event probabilities be converted into an optimal, cost-aware spread-crossing policy?
    • How stable are the calibrated correlations and execution implications across assets, venues, intraday regimes, and later market structures?
    • Can richer queue-reactive or point-process models improve extreme-imbalance arrival-time estimates while retaining tractable decision rules?
