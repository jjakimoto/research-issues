    WhatWorkedBench: Benchmarking Experimental Understanding in AI Agents

    ← Go backMultimodal Thinking with Renderable ProgramsNextBetween the Commits: Process, Error, and Claim Reliability… →

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-26

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-23
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27490

    WhatWorkedBench evaluates whether research agents understand interventions, not merely whether they find a good configuration: agents must predict the complete response surface after budgeted experiments. Shared Gaussian-process reconstruction and enforced code equivalences substantially improve agent-acquired evidence, while agents often recognize program rules yet fail to apply them in their final predictions.

    Executive summary

    WhatWorkedBench supplies exhaustive reference outcomes for small computational workflows and scores agents on every conditional component effect implied by their submitted response surface. Its 36 task conditions span 30 sources and eight workflow families, producing 1,248 configuration records, 3,392 conditional effects, and 342 pair interactions. Results show that selecting the optimum is much easier than reconstructing intervention effects, and that stronger numerical inference or explicit program constraints can recover considerable value from the same measurements.

    Evaluation target

    Each task has four or six binary workflow options. After receiving two free anchor measurements, an agent buys a budgeted number of additional configurations and predicts utility for all 16 or 64 masks. The primary metric is mean absolute error over every component change in every background, normalized into an effect-recovery score; the benchmark separately reports optimum selection, response-surface error, pair-interaction error, and a strict maximum-effect-error test.

    Catalog and reproducibility

    • The benchmark covers classification, regression, clustering, forecasting, image restoration, retrieval, beat detection, and graph link prediction.
    • The catalog contains 22 four-factor and 14 six-factor conditions across 30 distinct sources.
    • Thirty-five of 36 conditions contain at least one conditional sign reversal; 24 still contain one when both directions must exceed 5% of the task's response range.
    • A prepared-input pack replays all 1,248 native records in 127.87 seconds on one computation thread, while an independent episode engine matches 4,206 stored numerical-control records.

    Agent and numerical-control study

    The core study contains 108 episodes using the dated DeepSeek V4 Flash and Pro API identifiers. Four-factor agents receive budgets of four or eight new measurements, high-effort reasoning, a 32,768-token cap, and numerical Python tools; six-factor information experiments use a budget of 20. Controls include additive and pairwise ridge models, random designs, and Gaussian processes whose acquisition targets either utility-grid or conditional-effect uncertainty.

    Optimization versus understanding

    At eight measurements across 22 four-factor sources, pair-effect ridge obtains 0.612 family-macro recovery, selects an exact optimum on 15 sources, and passes the 10%-range strict test on only three. Effect-variance GP raises recovery to 0.701 and exact choices to 16, but passes the strict test on only one. Thirteen pair-ridge cases select the optimum while retaining at least one effect error above the strict threshold, demonstrating that optimization success does not certify intervention knowledge.

    Inference and program structure

    Refitting a Gaussian process to unchanged Flash observations raises recovery from 0.632 to 0.698 in the original cohort and from 0.621 to 0.720 in the additional cohort. On six-factor workflows, enforcing code-derived equivalence classes raises effect-GP recovery from 0.248 to 0.462 at budget 20. In eight diagnostics, agents correctly report all six registered inactive-option rules but every submitted table violates an equivalence; post-hoc projection onto the known classes raises mean recovery from 0.338 to 0.507 without additional measurements.

    Added signal and graph workflows

    Six complete Flash submissions at budget 32 cover three beat-detection and three link-prediction sources. Applying the shared GP to exactly the same acquired observations improves all six tables and raises family-macro recovery from 0.303 to 0.455. Performance remains modest, especially for link prediction, showing that sparse reconstruction of interaction-heavy six-factor surfaces is still difficult.

    Key findings

    • Across the catalog, 35 of 36 task conditions exhibit conditional sign reversals, so average component effects or a single best configuration omit important behavior.
    • At budget eight, pair ridge selects the optimum on 15 of 22 four-factor sources but strictly reconstructs effects on only three, directly separating optimization from understanding.
    • Using the same observations, shared GP improves Flash recovery by 0.066 in the original cohort and 0.099 in the additional cohort; both gains remain positive under every leave-one-source-out mean.
    • Program equivalences raise six-factor GP recovery from 0.248 to 0.462 at budget 20 and from 0.537 to 0.697 at budget 32.
    • Agents can state structural rules without operationalizing them: all eight diagnostic submissions violate known equivalences, while post-hoc constraint projection raises recovery from 0.338 to 0.507.
    • On six completed beat-detection and graph submissions, shared GP improves every table and raises family-macro recovery from 0.303 to 0.455.

    Limitations

    • Tasks are deterministic binary grids with only four or six factors; they do not capture continuous hyperparameters, stochastic outcomes, long research horizons, or the much larger spaces of real autonomous-research systems.
    • Agent evidence is limited to two dated DeepSeek API identifiers, and missing deliveries are scored as zero; the study records substantial rate limiting, deadline failures, and a provider routing change between cohorts.
    • Several analyses are small or partly retrospective: the original shared-GP fit was selected after inspecting the first cohort, code-equivalence controls use six paired workflows, and the structure diagnostic contains only eight episodes.

    Open questions

    • Can agents jointly learn acquisition and reconstruction policies that match or outperform the shared GP without retrospective post-processing?
    • How should program equivalences and other executable invariants be enforced automatically so recognized rules cannot be violated by the delivered artifact?
    • Can exhaustive-effect evaluation be approximated reliably for continuous, stochastic, or combinatorially larger experimental spaces?
