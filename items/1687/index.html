    Universal Diffusion Models for Implied Volatility Surfaces: Learning Shared Dynamics Across Stocks

    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-22

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-19
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22893

    A single conditional diffusion model trained on a pooled panel of 50 US equities generates next-day IVS increments and returns for 50 completely unseen stocks with no retraining, beating a pooled VolGAN on arbitrage violations, 95% return-interval coverage (94-96% vs ~75%) and PCA variance allocation. If IVS dynamics really are shared across the cross-section, desks can maintain one scenario generator instead of one model per ticker.

    Executive summary

    The authors train one conditional DDPM with a FiLM-modulated MLP denoiser on pooled daily data from 50 US equities (2010-2022), and test on 2023-2024 for both those 50 in-sample names and 50 stocks entirely excluded from training. The model jointly emits the next-day log-return and the 99-dimensional log-IVS increment on an 11x9 moneyness-maturity grid, with four loss variants plus post-hoc scenario reweighting. Against a VolGAN benchmark retrained on the same pool, every diffusion variant wins on all three criteria, and the in-sample/out-of-sample gap is essentially nil. Counterintuitively, the MSE-only variant, not the arbitrage-penalized one, produces the lowest test-period arbitrage violations.

    What is distinct vs the already-covered single-name surface generator

    The authors cite the closest prior work explicitly and position against it. That work builds an adaptive sequential diffusion for SPX alone - one index, sequential conditioning on recent market history, static no-arbitrage imposed via post-training fine-tuning, evaluated downstream through data-driven hedging. This paper changes the axis from time-series fidelity on one underlying to cross-sectional universality over an equity panel.

    • Single-asset (SPX) vs pooled 100-name equity cross-section split 50 train / 50 zero-shot transfer
    • Arbitrage via post-training fine-tuning vs penalty terms inside the loss plus generation-stage scenario reweighting
    • Evaluation is distributional/structural rather than P&L-of-a-hedge
    • The stated research question: can one model's learned IVS dynamics transfer to tickers it never saw, without retraining

    Data and surface construction

    100 US equities, randomly split 50 in-sample / 50 out-of-sample, with a chronological split of 2010-2022 for training and 2023-2024 for test. Raw quotes are filtered to out-of-the-money contracts and smoothed with a Vega-weighted Nadaraya-Watson smoother whose bandwidths are stock-specific and tuned to minimize static arbitrage.

    • Moneyness nodes M = {0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 1, 1.05, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4}
    • Maturity nodes T = {1/252, 1/52, 2/52, 1/12, 1/6, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1} years
    • Surface flattened to a 99-dim log-volatility vector; target x_t = [R_t, delta g_t] in R^100
    • Conditioning state c_t = [R_{t-1}, R_{t-2}, RV_{t-1}, g_{t-1}] in R^102, with RV a 21-day annualized realized vol

    Model and training objectives

    The denoiser is a FiLM-modulated MLP shared across all stocks: the diffusion step and conditioning state produce per-block scale and shift parameters modulating layer-normalized hidden features. Four objectives are compared, all built on the standard DDPM epsilon-matching loss.

    • FiLM MLP, hidden width 256, 8 residual blocks; K = 1000 diffusion steps with a cosine schedule
    • AdamW, lr 6e-4, batch size 4096, 1000 epochs, EMA 0.999; 1000 scenarios per conditioning date; a single random seed
    • Static-arbitrage penalty sums finite-difference calendar, vertical-spread and butterfly violations
    • Hybrid coefficients: lambda_calendar = 0.2739, lambda_spread = 0.0671, lambda_butterfly = 0.0166
    • Scenario reweighting with beta = 50 keeps effective sample size above 995 of 1000

    Evaluation and the transfer result

    Three criteria: mean static-arbitrage violation, empirical coverage of the nominal 95% one-day return predictive interval, and agreement between generated and observed explained-variance ratios of the first three PCs. Every table reports in-sample / out-of-sample / full-universe panels side by side.

    • Coverage differs by roughly 0.1 percentage point between in-sample and out-of-sample for every diffusion variant
    • VolGAN's ranking loss also persists out of sample, so the diffusion edge is not overfitting to the training tickers
    • The authors caution that raw arbitrage levels are not directly comparable across groups because the observed Data row itself differs (1.40e-3 vs 0.56e-3)

    The regularizer puzzle

    No single penalty dominates. The MSE-only model has the lowest arbitrage violations despite never being told about arbitrage, while the arbitrage-penalized model gives the best return calibration and the best PC1/PC3 match.

    • Smoothness controls local variation but not arbitrage - DDPM_smooth is the worst diffusion variant on violations in-sample
    • Scenario reweighting lowers the mean violation for every model including VolGAN, with negligible effect on coverage
    • Only one random seed is used, so the ranking among near-tied diffusion variants is fragile by the authors' own admission

    Key findings

    • Zero-shot transfer works: on the 50 stocks excluded from training, coverage of the 95% interval is 94.00% (mse), 96.01% (smooth), 94.53% (arbitrage) and 95.72% (hybrid) - within ~0.15pp of the same models' in-sample numbers.
    • VolGAN retrained on the identical 50-stock pool undercovers badly, containing the realized return only 73.50% (in-sample) and 76.62% (out-of-sample) of the time.
    • Every diffusion variant has lower mean arbitrage violation than VolGAN in every stock group: full-universe reweighted means 0.37-0.55 x10^-3 vs VolGAN's 1.90 x10^-3, versus a Data baseline of 0.97 x10^-3.
    • The MSE-only model, with no arbitrage term at all, is the best on violations, driven by the lowest butterfly component; the butterfly term dominates total violations for all models.
    • PCA allocation: observed PC1 ~81%, PC2 ~7%, PC3 ~4%; VolGAN distorts this to 76.64/8.95/6.64, while DDPM_arbitrage (82.80/6.45/3.98) stays close.
    • Scenario reweighting at beta = 50 lowers mean violation for every generative model while keeping effective sample size above 995 of 1000.

    Notable quotes

    We instead study whether a single diffusion model trained on a pooled cross-section of equities can learn IVS dynamics that transfer to previously unseen stocks without retraining.
    This formulation anchors every synthetic scenario to the observed previous-day surface while preserving the dependence between spot returns and surface dynamics.
    The butterfly component dominates total violations for all models.
    VolGAN underestimates the PC1 share and overestimates PC2 and PC3, while the diffusion models reproduce the allocation more faithfully.

    Limitations

    • Only one-day-ahead generation on a fixed 11x9 grid, with static rather than dynamic no-arbitrage conditions, and calibration assessed at a single nominal coverage level.
    • A single random seed is used throughout, so the close ranking among the four diffusion variants is not shown to be statistically robust.
    • Arbitrage penalties are not normalized by stock-specific baselines and the observed Data baseline differs materially between groups; there is no hedging or P&L evaluation.

    Open questions

    • Why does explicit arbitrage regularization fail to produce the lowest test-period violations?
    • Does the universality claim survive beyond 100 large US equities and over multi-day horizons?
    • Does the better distributional calibration translate into economic value, e.g. hedging error or VaR backtests?
