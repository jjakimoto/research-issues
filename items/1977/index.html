    A Missing Piece for Trustworthy AI Reviewers: From Benchmarking Rhetorical Robustness to SciCore Review

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-04

    Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-09-30
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39027

    If your autonomous researcher uses an LLM judge to score papers or results, it can be steered by rewording alone, and a judge that looks stable may simply be giving every paper the same score (the paper calls this 'false robustness'). Their fix is cheap to copy: extract a neutral, structured 'science core' and average a judgment on it with a normal full-paper review. That said, the evidence is narrow: 60 ICLR papers, one review per version, the fused reviewer was tested only on GPT-5.5, and much of the gain matches what you get from averaging any two reviews.

    Executive summary

    The paper defines Rhetorical Robustness for AI peer reviewers as two requirements at once: scores stay stable across rewrites that keep the reported science, and the reviewer still tells different papers apart. RobustReview (60 ICLR 2026 submissions x 10 rhetorical conditions x 2 rewrite producers = 1,260 full manuscripts) tests 30 reviewer configurations. It finds that content-only prompting does not reliably help, that low drift often hides score collapse ('false robustness'), and that agreeing with human scores is a different property from robustness. SciCore averages a GPT-5.5 Strict full-manuscript review with a review of an extracted structured science core. It gets the best ICC (0.775), SPR (0.652), discriminability (0.726) and H-MAE (1.072), but not the lowest drift, and a three-review average beats it on ICC and Drift SD.

    Problem: stability alone is not robustness

    LLM reviewers can give different scores to manuscripts that report the same science in different words, which rewards rhetorical optimization. The authors require both within-paper stability across content-preserving rewrites and between-paper discrimination. A reviewer that gives every paper the same score would be perfectly stable and useless. Agreement with human scores is measured separately as a third property.

    • For each paper, the original x_i0 is rewritten under setting k (rhetorical condition x rewrite producer) into x_ik. A reviewer configuration is a model plus a protocol.
    • Rewrites keep claims, methods, evidence, results and conclusions, and change claim stance, evidence framing, contribution organization, technical register, or lexical/syntactic realization.
    • Cross-paper score variation is not treated as ground-truth merit. It only has to be large relative to the variation that rewrites cause.

    RobustReview benchmark construction

    The benchmark uses 60 anonymized ICLR 2026 submissions with matched arXiv LaTeX sources (text similarity > 0.8). Ten papers were drawn (seed 42) from each of six mean-human-score bins: [1,3), [3,4), [4,5), [5,6), [6,7), [7,8.5]. Each paper gets 10 rhetorical conditions, each instantiated separately by GPT-5.5 (via Codex CLI) and Claude Opus 4.8 (via Claude Code) on the full LaTeX project. Citations, cross-references, figures and bibliography are protected, and every rewritten project is recompiled.

    • Six single-dimension conditions: novelty stance, scope framing, evidence framing, contribution salience, technical register, linguistic complexity.
    • Four complex conditions: a joint rewrite, recursive rewrites over 2 and 3 rounds (R2, R3), and a reviewer-guided rewrite driven by model feedback.
    • Total: 60 originals + 1,200 variants = 1,260 PDFs, giving 1,200 matched original-variant pairs.
    • Fidelity audit: DeepSeek V4 Flash as automated auditor gave pass rates of 94.8-97.3% across table, numerical, experimental, method and result fidelity. Three human annotators on 150 comparisons gave 96.7-98.7%, with 94.6% inter-annotator agreement.

    Reviewer configurations and metrics

    The 30 configurations come from three families. Eight general-purpose LLMs (GPT-5.5, GPT-5-mini, Claude Sonnet 5, GLM-5.2, Kimi-K2.6, GPT-OSS-120B, Gemini-3.5-Flash-Lite, Qwen-3.5-Flash) each run under three protocols: Standard (ICLR criteria), Strict (a more conservative, evidence-focused rubric) and Persistent (repeated instructions to judge substance, not rhetoric). There are also three specialized models (OpenReviewer 8B, CycleReviewer 8B, DeepReviewer 14B, served locally on 8xA100) and three agentic systems (AI Scientist, OpenJudge, ProReviewer) run with their native procedures. Each manuscript version gets one review.

    • Within-paper stability (lower is better): MAD, the mean absolute score change from the original, and Drift SD, the SD of the signed changes.
    • Joint stability-discrimination (higher is better): ICC(A,1), two-way mixed with absolute agreement; SPR = Var(original scores) / (Var + mean squared drift); and discriminability, the probability that two versions of the same paper score closer than versions of different papers (0.5 = chance).
    • Human alignment: H-MAE and Spearman correlation against the mean human overall-assessment score on the original papers.
    • Total API spend was $23,575.52, including $6,688 to build the benchmark and $8,194 for the ReconstructReview ablation.

    Benchmark findings: limits of current AI reviewers

    Telling a reviewer to focus on content (Persistent) helps inconsistently: only GPT-5.5 improves on all five robustness metrics. Low drift often comes with near-chance discrimination, which is false robustness. Ranking reviewers by human alignment gives a different order from ranking them by robustness. Open-weight and specialized/agentic reviewers mostly have ICC around 0.1-0.3.

    • Claude Sonnet 5 Persistent lowers MAD from 0.468 to 0.307 but lowers ICC from 0.579 to 0.489 and discriminability from 0.646 to 0.615.
    • Gemini-3.5-Flash-Lite Standard has the lowest MAD (0.205) but ICC 0.199 and discriminability 0.511. DeepReviewer and OpenJudge also drift little but reach ICC of only 0.161 and 0.239.
    • GLM-5.2 is unstable and misaligned: MAD 1.500-1.932, with negative Spearman correlation against humans.
    • Within GPT-5.5, Strict aligns best with humans (H-MAE 1.078, Spearman 0.529) while Persistent is the most robust existing configuration (ICC 0.697, SPR 0.586, discriminability 0.682).

    SciCore: dual-branch reviewer

    The manuscript branch is the GPT-5.5 Strict review of the full PDF. The science-core branch has an LLM extract an objective, third-person, attributed record: problem, claims, formulations and derivations, methods, assumptions, evidence, results with tables transcribed, contributions, reproducibility and limitations. Uncertain content is flagged rather than inferred, and figures are dropped. A reviewer that never sees the original manuscript then scores this record with an adapted Standard/ICLR protocol. The final score is the unweighted mean of the two branches. The motivation is invariant-representation theory (maximal invariants) plus a Blackwell-garbling argument for reviewing the core directly instead of reconstructing a new manuscript. The authors present these as rationales, not guarantees.

    • The Core-Adapted prompt, the Strict manuscript branch and alpha = 0.5 were fixed before looking at results on the 60-paper panel.
    • Fusing the two branches costs no extra call: one extraction plus two reviews.
    • Ablation: ReconstructReview (core -> rewritten ICLR paper via Codex -> Standard review) does worse than reviewing the core directly. Its MAD is 0.651 and its Spearman correlation with humans 0.133.

    SciCore results, controls and caveats

    SciCore leads on ICC, SPR and discriminability, with the lowest H-MAE. Paired bootstrap intervals over paper families (5,000 resamples) support its gains over Manuscript-Strict on all five robustness metrics. Some of the gain is mechanical: averaging integer scores allows half points and cancels some drift. Two-review manuscript-only averages get close (Strict+Persistent ICC 0.758), and a three-review average beats SciCore on ICC (0.781) and Drift SD (0.681). SciCore stays Pareto non-dominated and still beats the matched two-score controls with bootstrap support. Across backbones the science-core branch transfers only partially, and the fused reviewer was tested only with GPT-5.5.

    • Same-PDF repeatability: MAD 0.294-0.329 and ICC 0.846-0.862. SciCore and Strict repeat about equally well (MAD 0.320 vs 0.329) but diverge under rewriting (0.476 vs 0.766).
    • Science-core embeddings (text-embedding-3-small): a rewritten paper's core vs its original's core scores 0.978 cosine; vs other papers' cores 0.616-0.619 (GPT-5.5 extractor).
    • Confidence scores collapse in Core-Standard and Core-Strict (MAD 0.000, SPR 0.000), a concrete false-robustness case. Core-Adapted brings confidence SPR back to 0.394.
    • Core-Adapted vs Manuscript-Standard: GPT-5.5 improves on all five robustness metrics; GPT-5-mini gets worse on SPR and discriminability; GLM-5.2 improves on all seven metrics.
    • Weight sweep: a higher science-core weight raises robustness and lowers human alignment. With Strict, alpha = 0.5 sits near the transition.

    Key findings

    • Content-only instructions (the Persistent protocol) improve all five robustness metrics only for GPT-5.5. For Claude Sonnet 5 they cut MAD from 0.468 to 0.307 but drop ICC from 0.579 to 0.489.
    • False robustness: Gemini-3.5-Flash-Lite Standard has the lowest MAD of any existing configuration (0.205) but ICC 0.199 and near-chance discriminability (0.511). DeepReviewer and OpenJudge show the same pattern (ICC 0.161 and 0.239).
    • Human alignment and robustness disagree. GPT-5.5 Strict aligns best with humans (H-MAE 1.078, Spearman 0.529), while GPT-5.5 Persistent is the most robust existing reviewer (ICC 0.697, discriminability 0.682).
    • SciCore reaches ICC 0.775, SPR 0.652, discriminability 0.726 and H-MAE 1.072. Against Manuscript-Strict it cuts MAD by 0.290 [95% CI -0.348, -0.232] and raises ICC by 0.143 [0.101, 0.185] (paired bootstrap over 60 paper families).
    • Much of the gain comes from averaging. A Strict+Persistent two-review average reaches ICC 0.758, and a three-review average beats SciCore on ICC (0.781) and Drift SD (0.681). SciCore's margin over Strict+Persistent is small (ICC +0.017 [0.005, 0.029]).
    • Reviewing the extracted core directly beats reconstructing a new manuscript from it. ReconstructReview's MAD is 0.651 vs 0.354 for Core-Standard, and its Spearman correlation with humans is 0.133, at $8,194 in API cost. A rewritten paper's core vs its original's core: 0.978 cosine; vs other papers' cores: about 0.62.

    Notable quotes

    We call low rewrite-induced drift accompanied by weak paper differentiation false robustness: invariance alone can create the appearance of robustness without the discrimination that robustness is meant to preserve.
    The two evaluations therefore favor different protocols, so human agreement cannot substitute for matched robustness evaluation.
    The original manuscript is not provided to this branch at the review stage, so its judgment is based on the retained scientific record rather than its original rhetorical presentation.
    The evidence therefore supports partial, backbone-dependent transfer of the science-core branch rather than a universal gain.

    Limitations

    • Small, single-venue sample: 60 ICLR 2026 papers with arXiv sources, one review per manuscript version. Human alignment is measured only against mean ICLR reviewer scores, and the rewrites are LLM-generated with a nonzero fidelity-mismatch rate (automated pass rates 94.8-97.3%).
    • The fused SciCore reviewer is evaluated only with GPT-5.5. The cross-backbone and criterion-level results cover only the science-core branch, and they change extraction and review together.
    • Part of the robustness gain is mechanical (averaging two integer scores gives half points and cancels drift). A three-review manuscript average beats SciCore on ICC and Drift SD, so the specific value of the science core over plain ensembling is modest.

    Open questions

    • Would an adversary optimizing against SciCore itself (instead of these fixed rewrite conditions) find rewrites that move the science-core extraction, for example by changing which claims or limitations get extracted?
    • Does the method transfer to other venues and fields, or to quant/finance papers, and does a science-core judge also stay sensitive to real substantive defects (counterfactual errors)?
    • Is reviewing a structured core better than spending the same compute on N independent full-manuscript reviews with different protocols, once token costs are matched?
