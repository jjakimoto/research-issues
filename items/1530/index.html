    Same Book, Different Fills: Partial Identification of FIFO Execution from Aggregate Order Books

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-11

    L2 data pins down displayed depth but not the queue, so a passive-execution backtest silently depends on an unobservable cancellation-allocation rule: on held-out Tokyo data, front-vs-back cancellation moves passive completion by ~8 percentage points and implementation shortfall by up to 1.01 bps, and flips the preferred policy in up to 14% of comparisons. Any L2-based passive backtest should be reported as a FIFO-sensitivity envelope, not a point estimate.

    Executive summary

    The authors (NVIDIA + Oxford) frame reconstruction of market-by-order history from price-level (L2) snapshots as a conditional partial-identification problem: many order-level histories reproduce the same observed aggregate path. They build a path-preserving 'compiler' that holds the aggregate path, reconciled L1 market removals, latent order partitions and additions fixed, and varies ONLY how a residual cancellation is allocated within a queue — front (oldest), quantity-weighted-random, or back (newest). They prove a conditional front >= random >= back fill ordering for a tagged passive order during a single touch-price spell, then measure the economic gap on seven months of synchronized 2025 Tokyo Stock Exchange L1+L2 data for RIC 1301.T and 7911.T. The aggressive benchmark is invariant across FIFO rules, but passive and hybrid execution are materially sensitive, and the lowest-cost policy changes in a non-trivial fraction of matched comparisons.

    The microstructure gap: L2 hides the queue, not the depth

    Execution simulators split a market model (evolves displayed book) from a matching engine (applies price-time priority). With L2 input, total quantity per price is observed but not the constituent orders, their arrival order, or which order a cancellation removed. Those hidden details leave displayed depth unchanged yet fully determine when a passive child reaches the front of its queue. Prior L2 studies (Dixon 2018; Vyetrenko & Xu 2019; Coletta et al. 2022) simply pick one convention — front, back, or sampled position — thereby fixing one order-level history and evaluating policy under that choice.

    • Running example: a child joins behind 300 shares at the best bid; a later 100-share cancellation may remove quantity ahead of it or behind it. Identical L2 decrease, different fill probability.
    • The observations restrict but do not select the compatible order-level history — a partial-identification problem in the Manski/Tamer sense.
    • The resulting range is called a FIFO-sensitivity envelope: conditional on a stated reconstruction procedure, NOT an estimate of the true historical queue.

    Identification argument and the compiler class

    A block of retained side-price cells satisfies a one-sided decomposition: after an L1-attributed market removal is subtracted, the residual change is either an addition or a cancellation, never an unresolved mix. Within each accepted transition, actions are applied in a common order — reconciled market removals, then residual cancellations, then residual additions. An order-level realization is compatible when its aggregate projection reproduces the path and it evolves through valid order-level updates on the displayed finite-depth support. The compiler class fixes common inputs omega (partition seed plus a separately declared random-cancellation seed) and varies only the cancellation rule.

    • front removes the oldest eligible quantity; back the newest; random samples live background orders with probability proportional to remaining quantity.
    • The evaluated sensitivity set is a SUBSET of the full compatible outcome set — front/back are endpoint conventions, explicitly not sharp bounds.
    • Proposition 3.1 gives conditional tagged-fill ordering front >= random >= back for a virtual marker during one unchanged-best-price spell.
    • A boundary-fixed view is used for the 300s policy analysis; strict blocks are used for tagged probes.

    Data, synchronization and reconciliation

    Proprietary LSEG Tick History for the Tokyo Stock Exchange, January 6 to July 25, 2025: ten-level L2 snapshots plus a synchronized L1 trade feed, for RIC 1301.T (lower turnover, TOPIX Small 2, 5 JPY tick) and RIC 7911.T (more active, TOPIX Mid400/TOPIX500, 1 JPY tick); 100-share trading unit; pre-open, lunch and auction phases excluded. Exchange time is the cross-feed key; a January orientation test chose the assignment of same-key trades to the transition ENDING at that key, which explained 99.85% of regular trade quantity by a same-price reduction versus 8.66% for the alternative, and was fixed before later months were processed.

    • Feed-eligibility gates: >=98% of regular trades inside L2 coverage with exact exchange-time match, daily p99 absolute capture lag < 20 ms, >=99% of continuous rows two-sided and uncrossed.
    • 136 feed-eligible dates for 1301.T and 137 for 7911.T; 843,135 vs 9,062,080 continuous L2 rows; 24,607 vs 319,046 regular L1 trades.
    • Chronological protocol (1301.T): Jan–May (97 dates) estimates partition inputs and parent scale, June (21 dates) selects settings, July (18 dates) is held out. All rules fixed before any July outcome was examined.

    Experimental design: tagged probes, three shadow policies, external replay

    Tagged probes register a virtual 100-share marker at the same-side best price every five seconds when both sides are present and the spread is positive, ending after 15 seconds or when that price is no longer best. Policy episodes are non-overlapping 300-second windows with identical boundaries across policies, sides, seeds and FIFO rules. Three deterministic price-taking shadow policies are evaluated: Aggressive TWAP (ten equally spaced checkpoints), Passive at touch, and Hybrid (passive but crosses any schedule deficit at checkpoints). Passive children are virtual queue markers that never divert an execution; aggressive children consume a discarded copy of the contemporaneous book.

    • Parent size = 5% of development-period median positive same-side aggressive volume: 100 shares for 1301.T, 200 shares for 7911.T.
    • Loss is completed implementation shortfall in bps, with any residual crossed against a copy of the terminal book.
    • Compute: one node with 8 CPU cores and 4 NVIDIA GB200 GPUs; final matching-engine verification on the JAX GPU backend.
    • Uncertainty: 18 held-out dates equally weighted, 95% intervals from 5,000 date-resampling replicates.

    Results: aggressive invariant, passive highly sensitive

    For each instrument the held-out evaluation contains 1,080 300-second episodes over the same 18 July dates, evaluated on two sides and three seeds = 6,480 matched units per directed rule comparison. The aggressive benchmark is exactly invariant across cancellation rules (10.010 bps for 1301.T, 2.400 bps for 7911.T), confirming the matched design isolates the queue convention. Passive-at-touch and Hybrid both degrade monotonically from front to random to back. Passive-at-touch is the lowest-cost policy on date averages for both instruments under every rule, yet the per-comparison policy choice still flips in a meaningful share of cases.

    • Episode coverage (1301.T): 97.27% of continuous-session elapsed time, 96.08% of reconciled trade volume; 7911.T: 96.80% and 94.40%.
    • Independent replay: 648 reconstructed streams from 54 background paths (1301.T) and 456 from 38 paths (7911.T) replayed through JAX-LOB; all requested market-message quantities executed (232,800 and 4,119,600 shares).
    • All 354,774 (1301.T) and 370,992 (7911.T) matched tagged-probe triplets satisfy the Proposition 3.1 fill ordering.
    • Robustness: strict-block reruns give -0.397 bps [-0.576,-0.229] for 1301.T and -0.325 bps [-0.649,0.000] for 7911.T — same direction, weaker sample.

    Practitioner implication: sensitivity envelope as an acceptance test

    The envelope separates conclusions supported by the observed aggregate path from conclusions that depend on the queue-reconstruction assumption. A practitioner can require a candidate policy to remain preferred across all matched front/random/back realizations, use worst-case loss or minimax regret, or apply a materiality threshold. A wide envelope can motivate a less passive policy or justify paying for MBO data; a narrow one shows only insensitivity to the rules considered. The same machinery is proposed as a validation or robust-training layer for learned execution agents.

    • The paper defines regret of choosing under rule R and evaluating under R', with a bps tie tolerance and a deterministic tiebreak.
    • Because the compiler is a message-stream interface validated in an external engine, it scales to larger sets of aggregate paths.
    • Cross-instrument replication shows the DIRECTION of FIFO sensitivity is not specific to one security, while the magnitude depends on how much inventory would otherwise be crossed at the horizon.

    Key findings

    • The aggressive TWAP benchmark is exactly invariant to the cancellation rule (10.010 bps for 1301.T, 2.400 bps for 7911.T), so the entire measured sensitivity is passive-queue sensitivity, not path or design noise.
    • For 1301.T, front rather than back cancellation raises passive pre-terminal completion from 10.14% to 18.15% (+8.01 pp) and gives a front-minus-back passive shortfall difference of -1.010 bps (95% interval [-1.253, -0.788]); the hybrid difference is -0.398 bps.
    • For 7911.T, front rather than back cancellation raises passive completion from 89.81% to 97.20% (+7.39 pp) and gives a front-minus-back shortfall difference of -0.384 bps ([-0.493, -0.284]).
    • The lowest-cost policy changes in 0.83%–4.49% of directed cross-rule comparisons for 1301.T and 3.63%–14.26% for 7911.T; conditional on a change the added cost is 14.277–17.354 bps for 1301.T versus only 2.366–3.723 bps for 7911.T — more frequent flips but cheaper ones in the liquid name.
    • All 354,774 (1301.T) and 370,992 (7911.T) matched tagged-probe triplets satisfy the predicted front >= random >= back fill ordering; mean 15-second probe fill for 7911.T runs 0.22% (back) to 0.50% (front).
    • Reconstruction validity was checked outside the compiler: 648 and 456 reconstructed streams replayed through JAX-LOB executed all requested market quantities (232,800 and 4,119,600 shares) with no add message triggering an immediate trade.

    Notable quotes

    We instead ask whether an execution conclusion is preserved across several FIFO-consistent order-level histories that reproduce the same observed L2 path.
    Thus, policy choices change more frequently for 7911.T but at a smaller conditional cost.
    Front and back are endpoint conventions for cancellation location, not estimates of the historical queue.

    Limitations

    • Only two RICs and seven months, with held-out evidence from a single month and the same 18 calendar dates per instrument — 36 instrument-day observations, not a market cross-section.
    • Front and back are conditional endpoint conventions within a declared compiler class, explicitly not sharp bounds over all compatible order-level histories.
    • Shadow policies assume zero latency, no fees or rebates, no endogenous market impact; parent sizes differ across instruments (100 vs 200 shares), so cross-instrument cost differences are descriptive, not causal.

    Open questions

    • How wide is the FIFO-sensitivity envelope across a broad cross-section of securities, additional months, larger parent sizes, and longer execution horizons?
    • Can execution agents be trained across multiple compatible queue realizations of the same aggregate path (robust training) so that performance does not depend on a single simulator-specific fill convention?
    • How would one estimate the full-class worst-case criterion rather than the matched fixed-seed rule contrasts reported here?
