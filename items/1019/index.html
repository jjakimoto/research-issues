AI-to-AI Code Reviews of GitHub Pull Requests

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-21
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21311

The first product-resolved, GitHub-scale census of AI agents reviewing other AI agents' pull requests: 248,641 agent-authored PRs got at least one AI review (45,269 of them cross-product), growth of over two orders of magnitude across 2025, and first-review latency measured in minutes. If you ship coding agents, this quantifies how often 'review' on agent PRs is another bot, and shows reviewer output shifts systematically with which agent wrote the code.

Executive summary

Selvanayagam and Ghaleb (ETS Montreal / Trent University, ESEM 2026 Emerging Results track) mine the CodAGE dataset of coding-agent GitHub events (2024-01-01 to 2026-04-15, from GHArchive) to build the first dataset of 'closed-loop' AI review -- PRs authored by one identifiable AI product and reviewed by one or more identifiable AI products. Of 2,830,284 attributed agent-authored PRs, 248,641 (8.8%) received at least one AI review across 10,345 repositories: 45,269 cross-product (~1.6%), 208,145 same-product, 4,773 both. Cross-product activity grew from 57 PRs in 2025-Q1 to 25,492 in 2025-Q3. Holding the reviewer fixed (CodeRabbit), comment-category mix varies sharply by authoring agent (refactor share 35.0% on Claude Code PRs vs 10.5% on Copilot PRs), three of four dual-role reviewers post 58-65% more comments on same-product PRs (but with small/negligible Cliff's delta), and median time to first AI review is 1.2 min cross-product vs 4.7 min same-product -- a gap the authors attribute to reviewer composition, not pairing.

Problem: AI now sits on both sides of the pull request

Authoring agents (Devin, OpenAI Codex, Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code) open PRs; reviewing tools (CodeRabbit, Sourcery, PR-Agent) post line-level comments and review decisions; and several products -- Copilot, Codex, Gemini Code Assist, Amazon Q, Devin, Claude Code, Cursor -- appear on BOTH sides. The authors call this 'closed-loop AI review', defined strictly observationally: AI occupies both sides of the PR, which does not imply humans were absent. Prior work studied AI code generation and AI review separately, and the classic reviewer-bot studies predate today's dual-role agents and never analyze author-reviewer pairings.

  • RQ1: how prevalent is closed-loop AI review on GitHub? Motivated by the risk that GitHub review data is increasingly not evidence of human decision making.
  • RQ2: does reviewer behavior vary with the author-reviewer setup? If so, closed-loop review cannot be treated as one homogeneous process.
  • Positioned against the 'LLM-as-a-judge' / constitutional-AI literature, which studies models critiquing model output in the lab but not in deployed public repositories.

How the dataset was built: two-tier signature attribution over CodAGE

Source is CodAGE (from GHArchive, on Hugging Face, CC BY 4.0), snapshot 2024-01-01 to 2026-04-15. A broad first pass (including branch-name evidence) yielded 4,563,819 candidate PRs; a stricter second pass required body or vendor-login evidence. Login-only attribution was judged unreliable because some agents post under generic [bot] accounts, some under the human's login with a machine-readable trailer, and some quote rival agents' text.

  • S1 (high-confidence body signature): regex/substring the agent itself emits -- e.g. the 'Co-Authored-By: Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>' trailer, the cursor.com/agents URL, the chatgpt.com/codex URL.
  • S2 (vendor-controlled account signature): logins such as coderabbitai[bot], devin-ai-integration[bot], gemini-code-assist[bot]. S1 agents need body evidence; S2 agents accept body OR login. Branch prefixes are logged but never sufficient alone.
  • Multi-agent matches (one agent quoting another's trailer) were rare: 98 of 4,141,107 review entries (0.002%) and 111 of 8,560,237 review-comment entries (0.001%).
  • Review side attributed to 12 agents; author side yielded 2,830,284 unique agent-authored PRs and adds Google Jules (authors but never reviews).
  • Quarantine: negligible on review streams (0.01%) but heavy on the author side -- 1,733,535 of 4,563,819 candidate PRs (38.0%) quarantined, 96.9% of them as branch_only; the analysed population is the remaining 62.0%.

Definitions and measures: cross- vs same-product, and three behavioral metrics

Three datasets: A x B-cross-product = 45,269 PRs / 47,259 pairs; A x B-same-product = 208,145 PRs; A x N (agent-authored, no detected AI review) = 2,581,643 PRs. 4,773 PRs appear in both A x B sets, so counts are not mutually exclusive. 'Product' means the identifiable agentic product/harness, not the parent vendor or foundation model -- Google Jules and Gemini Code Assist count as distinct (117 cross-product PRs are such same-vendor pairs). No matched human-authored control was analysed.

  • Comment content: CodeRabbit's own emoji-tagged headers (Refactor suggestion, Potential issue, Nitpick, plus an undocumented Verification prefix seen in 9,525 comments), extracted by an ordered-regex classifier. Explicitly NOT treated as an ordered severity scale, and no LLM judge is used. Sourcery and PR-Agent excluded (fewer than 250 review events together).
  • Volume: mean review comments per PR, two-sided Mann-Whitney U with Cliff's delta (<0.147 negligible, 0.147-0.33 small); reviewers with >=200 PRs in both datasets.
  • Latency: PR creation to first recorded review event; pairs missing a timestamp or with negative differences dropped, leaving 103,920 pairs; medians with IQRs because distributions are heavily right-skewed.
  • Replication package: github.com/Niruthiha/AI-AI-CodeReviews.

RQ1 results: a minority phenomenon, but exploding and sharply non-uniform

248,641 of 2,830,284 agent-authored PRs (8.8%) received at least one AI-attributed review across 10,345 repositories; 45,269 (1.6%) were reviewed across products. Volume was negligible through 2025-Q1 (57 cross, 40 same) and rose by over two orders of magnitude by 2025-Q3 (25,492 cross, 57,080 same). OpenAI Codex dominates as author (69.8% of cross-product PRs); Copilot dominates as reviewer (21,022 of 47,259 pairs); the modal pair is 'Codex authored, Copilot reviewed' at 18,114 pairs.

  • Three distinct regimes: product-internal (Copilot 95.7% same-product, Amazon Q 91.8%, Devin 76.5%); balanced (OpenAI Codex 54.1%); and cross-product-only (Cursor 0.0% same, Google Jules 0.0%, Claude Code 0.5%).
  • The authors focus on cross-product review because same-product review may just be an integrated product workflow.

RQ2 results: same reviewer, different output depending on who wrote the code

With CodeRabbit held constant, comment-category mix differs visibly by authoring agent: Copilot- and Devin-authored PRs draw far more potential_issue (49.0%, 43.9%) and far fewer refactor comments (10.5%, 9.7%) than Claude Code, Cursor and Codex (29.2-35.0% refactor), and have the highest unlabeled shares (6.1%, 12.3%). The association is significant but small (Cramer's V = 0.150; chi-square = 3177.5, df = 24, p < 10^-300); the headline contrast is a 24.5 percentage-point refactor gap (95% CI [23.1, 25.9], N = 8,757 vs 4,260). On volume, three of four dual-role reviewers post 58-65% more comments on same-product PRs, with OpenAI Codex the exception. Cliff's delta is 0.02-0.27 throughout, so the gap lives in a long right tail. Median first-review latency is 1.2 min cross-product vs 4.7 min same-product -- explained compositionally: fast reviewers (Gemini 0.5 min) are over-represented cross-product while slow Copilot (17.7 min median) dominates same-product.

  • AI review commonly begins within minutes of PR creation; there is no matched human baseline to compare against.
  • Devin and Copilot sit within 0.01 of the 0.147 negligible/small boundary, so effect-size labels are called indicative only.

Significance for builders and for empirical SE

Because AI-authored and AI-reviewed PRs are already frequent in absolute terms, empirical software engineering can no longer assume a purely human-authored population. For tool builders, the practical warning is that downstream tooling which summarizes or aggregates AI review output can produce systematically different interpretations depending on which agent wrote the code, even with the reviewer held fixed. And product boundaries need not match model boundaries, so 'cross-product' review is not proof of reviewer independence.

  • The paper explicitly makes no claim about review correctness or code quality -- CodeRabbit's self-declared categories describe reviewer output, not validated defects.
  • Future work sketched: human-annotated ratings with outcome linkage; model-level blinded experiments on self-preference; and an injected-defect benchmark comparing same-model vs related-model vs independent-model reviewers.

Key findings

  • Of 2,830,284 attributed agent-authored PRs, 248,641 (8.8%) received at least one AI-attributed review across 10,345 repositories; 45,269 (about 1.6%) were reviewed by a DIFFERENT AI product, 208,145 by the same product, and 4,773 by both.
  • Closed-loop review grew by more than two orders of magnitude inside 2025: 57 cross-product / 40 same-product PRs in Q1 versus 25,492 / 57,080 in Q3.
  • The ecosystem is concentrated: OpenAI Codex authored 69.8% of cross-product PRs, Copilot reviewed 21,022 of the 47,259 pairs, and 'Codex authored, Copilot reviewed' alone accounts for 18,114 pairs.
  • Pairing regimes are extreme rather than mixed: Copilot PRs are 95.7% same-product-reviewed while Cursor (0.0%), Google Jules (0.0%) and Claude Code (0.5%) are reviewed almost entirely across products; only Codex is balanced at 54.1%.
  • With the reviewer held constant, CodeRabbit labels 35.0% of comments on Claude Code PRs as refactor versus 10.5% on Copilot PRs -- a 24.5 pp gap (95% CI [23.1, 25.9]) -- while Copilot/Devin PRs draw the most potential_issue comments (49.0%, 43.9%).
  • Three of four dual-role reviewers post 58-65% more comments per PR on same-product PRs, but Cliff's delta stays 0.02-0.27, so the gap is a long right tail; median first-review latency was 1.2 min cross-product vs 4.7 min same-product, explained by reviewer composition.

Notable quotes

The absence of a signature is not evidence that a PR was not AI-authored or AI-reviewed: such PRs are not quarantined but simply never enter our population.
Downstream tooling that summarizes or aggregates AI review output may therefore produce systematically different interpretations for different AI coders, even under the same reviewer.
Because the reviewer populations differ, the observed difference should be interpreted as reflecting reviewer composition rather than any inherent preference for same- or cross-product review.
We make no claim about code quality, as change size, language, repository, and configuration may influence the patterns observed.

Limitations

  • Attribution is signature-based and precision-first, so it undercounts: 38.0% of candidate author-side PRs were quarantined (96.9% as branch-name-only evidence), body trailers can be stripped by squash merges, and under-attribution is asymmetric. All absolute counts are lower bounds.
  • No ground truth for review quality: RQ2 content measures are CodeRabbit's own self-declared comment headers, which the authors say are not validated measures of issue type, severity, correctness, or code quality; there is no matched human-authored control and no human latency baseline.
  • Selection and complete-case bias: only public GitHub repos that adopt these integrations are covered; latency retained only 79.2% of cross-product versus 31.9% of same-product pairs; and 'closed-loop' does not exclude unobserved human review, while 'cross-product' is product-level, not model-level, identity.

Open questions

  • Do AI reviewers exhibit self-preference at the MODEL level? Product-level attribution cannot separate product effects from model effects.
  • Do AI author and AI reviewer share correlated blind spots when they rely on related models, training data, or prompts?
  • Does closed-loop review actually improve code? Comment volume, latency, and category mix say nothing about usefulness.
