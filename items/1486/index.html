The Privacy Subsidy in Market Microstructure

📰 Latest · 2026-09-13
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-13

Source: arxiv · 2026-06-11
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10543

Any privacy-preserving venue that prices on a coarsened view of order flow while settling the true flow must hand traders a closed-form transfer — the 'privacy subsidy' — so liquidity providers can never be both informationally efficient and break-even; the resulting social loss is fourth-order in the noise scale while the transfer is second-order.

Executive summary

Nakamura unifies three earlier preprints into a single framework for privacy-preserving exchanges: a market maker committed to posterior-mean pricing on a signal strictly coarser than the settled flow earns profit equal to the executed-price wedge -Cov(v-Q, y), strictly negative under strict coarsening. This impossibility is proved from the tower property alone, then instantiated in closed form in single-period Kyle, Glosten-Milgrom with a binary flip channel, and continuous-time Kyle-Back. The subsidy is the order-flow analogue of Loss-Versus-Rebalancing. Gross of fees it is a pure transfer recovered by a break-even fee; the deadweight that fee creates is fourth-order against a second-order transfer, so a protocol trading a differential-privacy benefit against it picks an interior noise scale.

The coarse-signal impossibility theorem

A 'committed Bayesian' maker quotes posterior means conditional on a garbled signal but settles the true flow. Theorem 1 gives profit as the executed-price wedge; in the single-price case it reduces by the tower property to -Cov(v-E[v|S], y-E[y|S]) and vanishes iff the flow is measurable with respect to the signal. The proof needs no Gaussianity or functional form.

  • Strict coarsening is defined as Var(y - E[y|S]) > 0
  • If Cov(v-Q, y) > 0 then efficient pricing loses strictly and no signal-measurable rule is both efficient and zero-profit
  • The wedge identity is an accounting tautology plus the tower property; the sign condition carries the economics
  • Positioned against imperfect-information Kyle (where a filtering maker re-optimises and the wedge washes out), dark-pool welfare, and information design

Three closed-form instances

Single-period Kyle with Gaussian privacy noise yields the textbook equilibrium under the substitution sigma_u^2 -> sigma_u^2 + sigma_eps^2. Glosten-Milgrom with a binary symmetric flip channel of rate eta gives spread = mu(1-2eta)Delta and per-trade subsidy mu*eta*Delta. Continuous-time Kyle-Back has constant lambda and a cumulative subsidy exactly twice the single-period value.

  • Kyle subsidy: quadratic for small noise, asymptotically linear, single inflection at sigma_eps = sqrt(2) sigma_u
  • Incidence falls entirely on the liquidity layer: informed profit rises and noise-trader loss shrinks
  • Flip rate maps to local DP via eta = 1/(1+e^{eps_DP}), so the GM subsidy is mu*Delta / (1 + e^{eps_DP})
  • With time-varying noise the subsidy depends only on the time-averaged variance, so scheduling privacy cannot reduce it

The LVR correspondence

Both the privacy subsidy and Loss-Versus-Rebalancing factorise as (squared noise driver) x (committed-object factor). Unlike LVR, the privacy committed-object factor still depends mildly on the noise scale, so the parallel is structural, exact only in the small-noise regime.

  • LVR governs mismatched price observation; the subsidy governs mismatched flow observation
  • The quadratic-in-noise reading degrades to linear when sigma_eps >> sigma_u
  • Presented as an organising principle for fee design, not a numerical equivalence

Net welfare cost and endogenous privacy

With an allocative value of trade, a proportional fee shrinks liquidity volume, producing a Harberger triangle. The break-even fee is second-order in the noise scale, so the deadweight is fourth-order. A CARA hedger microfoundation reproduces the same scaling; letting the protocol choose privacy gives a unique interior optimum sigma_eps* = (bc/4A)^{1/5}.

  • Log-elasticities of the optimal noise scale: (1/5, -1/5, 3/5, -2/5, 1/5) in (b, kappa, sigma_u, sigma_v, Delta_DP)
  • Endogenising privacy raises the sigma_v-elasticity of price impact from 1 into (1, 7/5)
  • Amplification is about 13-20% for theta in [0.5, 1]
  • The CARA case returns the elasticity to the textbook 1

Application to deployed venues and calibration

A venue instantiates the channel only if it prices on a noise-coarsened observation while settling true flow. Uniform Random Execution randomises the executed quantity, so priced-on equals settled-against and it falls in the no-subsidy regime; batch and sealed-bid auctions are out of framework. The closest realisation is a DP-priced shielded constant-function AMM.

  • Break-even fee is scale-free in sigma_u, depending only on sigma_eps/sigma_u and sigma_v/p_0
  • Across sigma_eps/sigma_u in {0.1, 0.5, 1, sqrt(2), 2} the fee is {1.6, 33, 92, 132, 173} bps vs a conventional ~10 bp maker fee
  • A BTC/USDT calibration puts the parity-privacy subsidy at about $1.06M/day
  • The subsidy figures are gross transfers and an explicit lower bound on cost

Key findings

  • Theorem 1: a committed-Bayesian maker's profit on settled flow equals the executed-price wedge, zero iff the settled flow is measurable with respect to the priced-on signal — efficiency and zero-profit are jointly infeasible under strict coarsening.
  • Single-period Kyle: subsidy = sigma_v sigma_eps^2 / (2 sqrt(sigma_u^2 + sigma_eps^2)), with lambda*beta = 1/2 preserved for every noise level.
  • Glosten-Milgrom: spread falls linearly in the flip rate while the per-trade subsidy rises linearly to mu*Delta/2 at eta = 1/2.
  • Continuous-time Kyle-Back: cumulative subsidy is exactly twice the single-period value, and depends on a time-varying privacy profile only through its time-averaged variance.
  • Welfare: break-even fee is second-order in the noise scale while the deadweight is fourth-order.
  • Endogenous privacy: sigma_eps* = (bc/4A)^{1/5} is a unique interior optimum.
  • BTC/USDT calibration: at parity noise the subsidy is ~$1.06M/day, implying break-even fees of 92 bps versus a conventional ~10 bp maker fee.

Notable quotes

The subsidy is therefore not a generic consequence of adding noise to an exchange; it is a consequence of adding noise to the maker’s information about flow while leaving the executed flow intact.
This is an idealised benchmark, not a description of any fielded system.
LVR governs mismatched price observation; the privacy subsidy governs mismatched flow observation.
Our channel requires the reverse: noise on the priced signal, true flow on settlement.

Limitations

  • The fee-equilibrium results are leading-order and partial-equilibrium: depth is held at its no-fee value while the censored informed strategy is non-Gaussian and should feed back into it.
  • The Glosten-Milgrom instance is a single-round adverse-selection spread; privacy noise is exogenous with a single insider.
  • No empirical estimation: the BTC/USDT figures are an illustrative single-maker calibration, and the verification scripts are only available from the author on request.

Open questions

  • Can a full fee equilibrium beyond leading order confirm the fourth-order deadweight scaling?
  • How do adaptive or flow-correlated differential-privacy mechanisms change the wedge covariance?
  • Does the subsidy survive multi-insider competition, and can the impossibility be machine-checked?
