DiscoveryBench: Towards Data-Driven Discovery with Large Language Models

📚 Foundations · 2026-08-30 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-30

Theme: Autonomous research through hypothesis and reproduction benchmarks
DiscoveryBench measures whether agents can infer evidence-supported scientific hypotheses, while CORE-Bench measures whether they can execute published research and recover its outputs. Together they expose a large gap between generating plausible research narratives and producing verifiable scientific results.
Source: arxiv
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01725

Formalizes data-driven hypothesis discovery and evaluates agents with real scientific workflows, controlled synthetic tasks, and a structured hypothesis-verification metric.

Executive summary

DiscoveryBench evaluates whether LLM agents can derive specific, evidence-supported hypotheses from datasets and natural-language research goals. It combines 264 tasks reconstructed from published scientific workflows with 903 controlled synthetic tasks and evaluates agents through a structured Hypothesis Match Score. The strongest oracle-assisted configuration reaches only 24.5 HMS on the real benchmark.

Problem and formalization

The paper defines data-driven discovery as deriving a hypothesis from datasets and a discovery goal. Hypotheses are represented through context, variables, and relationships and can be decomposed into trees of sub-hypotheses.

  • Tasks require statistical analysis and semantic decisions.
  • Difficulty depends on hypothesis-tree path length, workflow length, and dataset count.
  • The benchmark evaluates discovery outcomes rather than one prescribed workflow.

Real-world benchmark

The authors reconstruct 264 discovery tasks from more than 20 published papers, preserving gold hypotheses and workflow information.

  • The split contains 25 training and 239 test tasks.
  • There are 158 unique hypotheses.
  • A total of 114 tasks require multiple datasets.
  • Workflows include more than 500 preparation operations.

Controlled synthetic benchmark

DB-Synth constructs semantic trees and generates data from their leaves upward, then adds noise and missing values before creating discovery goals.

  • DB-Synth contains 903 tasks across 48 domains.
  • Tasks use a 60/20/20 train, development, and test split.
  • Difficulty ranges from one to four.
  • Difficulty is controlled through distance between target hypotheses and observed variables.

Verification and results

Hypothesis Match Score decomposes predicted and gold hypotheses into context, variable, and relationship components. Standard planning frameworks offer little improvement, while oracle feedback helps but remains far from solving the benchmark.

  • The highest real-benchmark HMS is 24.5 for Reflexion with GPT-4o.
  • Correlation analysis reaches 55% for the best non-oracle agent, while several specialized methods score zero.
  • Performance declines with workflow length and semantic-tree height.
  • Domain results range from 0% in biology to 25% in economics.

Key findings

  • Current agents solve only a small fraction of realistic data-driven discovery tasks.
  • ReAct and DataVoyager generally do not outperform basic CodeGen.
  • Oracle evaluation feedback produces the clearest gains.
  • Correct context identification is necessary but insufficient.
  • Performance is sensitive to workflow complexity and specialized scientific methods.
  • DB-Synth provides controlled difficulty while producing patterns similar to the real benchmark.

Notable quotes

We evaluate task performance by measuring the alignment of the predicted and gold hypotheses in natural language.
Most importantly, effective reasoning prompts such as React and planning with a self-critic (DataVoyager) do not help improve the simple CodeGen agent.
Almost all agents struggle more with tasks involving complex statistical techniques, complex data preparation methods, or domain-specific models.
The performance drops significantly even for medium-length workflows, highlighting current agents’ limitations.

Limitations

  • The benchmark excludes many forecasting, simulation, and expensive-replication tasks.
  • It does not address massive datasets or safeguards against p-hacking.
  • It excludes multimodal data and complex satellite, climate, astronomy, and geospatial workflows.

Open questions

  • Can agents reproduce oracle-feedback gains using reliable self-verification?
  • How should HMS extend to novel discoveries without a known gold hypothesis?
  • Can stronger domain retrieval and tool selection close performance gaps in specialized fields?
