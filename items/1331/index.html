Language agents achieve superhuman synthesis of scientific knowledge

📚 Foundations · 2026-09-06 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-06

Theme: Literature-grounded autonomous research
These systems automate two core research activities: generating proposals from scientific literature and synthesizing evidence from an open corpus. ResearchAgent emphasizes citation-graph expansion, knowledge augmentation, and iterative critique; PaperQA2 emphasizes agent-controlled search, evidence reranking, citation support, abstention, and expert comparison. Their strongest shared lesson is that retrieval and review improve research assistance, but judged plausibility and cited synthesis remain different from experimentally validated discovery.
Source: arxiv
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13740

A foundational evaluation of agentic retrieval-augmented generation for scientific question answering, cited synthesis, and literature-scale contradiction detection against domain experts.

Executive summary

Skarlinski et al. present PaperQA2, an agentic scientific-literature system that iteratively searches for papers, gathers and reranks evidence through contextual summarization, traverses citation links, and generates cited answers. On the authors' LitQA2 benchmark, PaperQA2 attained higher precision than PhD-level human researchers while matching their accuracy; related systems built on it produced gene articles with better citation support than Wikipedia and surfaced literature contradictions at scale. The paper is also valuable as an evaluation blueprint because it compares agents with unrestricted human experts on realistic, open-corpus research tasks rather than only testing retrieval from a fixed corpus.

Problem and evaluation design

The paper argues that abstract-only or fixed-corpus benchmarks poorly represent scientific research, where a system must locate obscure evidence in the full literature, reason over paper bodies, and cite the correct source. The authors therefore evaluate one agent family on three tasks: LitQA2 question answering, WikiCrow article synthesis, and ContraCrow contradiction detection, with expert human grading or direct human-performance comparisons.

  • LitQA2 contains 248 expert-written multiple-choice questions whose answers should occur in paper bodies rather than abstracts.
  • Questions include an insufficient-information option, permitting precision to be separated from overall accuracy.
  • WikiCrow is compared blindly with matched Wikipedia content, while ContraCrow is evaluated both on a constructed benchmark and on claims extracted from randomly sampled biology papers.

Agentic retrieval and evidence synthesis

PaperQA2 decomposes scientific RAG into tools controlled by an agent: keyword-based paper search, evidence gathering, citation traversal, and final answer generation. Candidate papers are parsed and chunked; hybrid sparse-dense retrieval selects up to 30 chunks by default, after which a strong LLM performs reranking and contextual summarization. The summaries, relevance scores, and metadata become compact evidence in agent state, allowing iterative search and citation-graph expansion before cited answer generation.

  • The best runs averaged 1.26 searches and 0.46 citation traversals per LitQA2 question, showing that the agent often departed from a fixed one-pass workflow.
  • Removing the agent significantly reduced accuracy (p=0.015), and contextual summarization significantly improved retrieval accuracy over inserting untransformed top-ranked chunks (p<0.001).
  • Deep contextual summarization improved accuracy from ranking depth 1 to 10, with diminishing gains from 10 to the default depth of 30; smaller models could make summarization worse than using no summarization model.
  • The method compresses standard 2,250-token chunks into roughly 200–400-token contextual summaries, trading additional inference cost for more evidence coverage.

Scientific question answering results

PaperQA2 processed an average of 14.5 papers per LitQA2 question and achieved 85.2% precision and 66.0% accuracy over three runs. Nine PhD students or PhD-level scientists, allowed approximately one week plus unrestricted institutional paper and internet access, achieved 73.8% precision and 67.7% accuracy. PaperQA2's precision was significantly higher, while its accuracy was statistically indistinguishable from the human result; performance was also similar between the original questions and a later unseen set of 101 questions.

  • PaperQA2 exceeded human precision with t(8.6)=3.49 and p=0.0036.
  • Its accuracy did not significantly differ from humans: t(8.5)=-0.42 and p=0.66.
  • The system abstained by selecting insufficient information on 21.9% of questions.
  • Using 15 final contexts maximized precision, whereas using 5 maximized accuracy, exposing a concrete recall-versus-distraction tradeoff.

Cited synthesis with WikiCrow

WikiCrow composes four PaperQA2-generated sections and one frontier-model overview into cited articles about human protein-coding genes. Across 375 blindly graded statements drawn from matched WikiCrow and Wikipedia articles, WikiCrow had fewer unsupported citations, fewer uncited statements, and higher precision. Manual error categorization attributed much of the advantage to fewer reasoning errors, though attribution errors remained similar.

  • WikiCrow generated 240 articles averaging 1,219 words in 491.5 seconds at an average API cost of $4.48 each.
  • Cited-but-unsupported statements were 13.5% for WikiCrow versus 24.9% for Wikipedia.
  • Uncited statements were 3.5% versus 13.6%, and citation precision was 86.1% versus 71.2%.
  • The comparison is specifically about agentic RAG; the authors do not claim that an unaided frontier LLM would achieve comparable factuality.

Contradiction detection and significance

ContraCrow extracts paper claims, filters them for quality and generalizability, and asks PaperQA2 to search the broader literature for agreement or contradiction, returning reasoning and an 11-point score. On ContraDetect it reached an AUC of 0.842, and at threshold 8 achieved 73% accuracy and 88% precision. Applied to 93 biology papers, it analyzed 3,180 claims and flagged an average of 2.34 contradictions per paper; experts validated 70% of a sampled set, yielding a lower-bound estimate of 1.64 human-validatable contradictions per paper.

  • ContraCrow correctly returned lack of evidence for 98% of 42 hand-designed no-evidence statements.
  • Expert validation of 100 high-scoring candidate contradictions produced an F1 score of 0.82.
  • On a harder blinded task, human-human agreement was 75.5% while human-ContraCrow agreement was 60.42%, with the authors identifying model overconfidence as a likely source of disagreement.
  • A detected contradiction need not make either claim false; it may reflect changed conditions, context, or scientific knowledge updating over time.

Key findings

  • Agent-controlled iterative search materially outperformed a hard-coded search-gather-answer workflow, supporting agency itself rather than RAG alone as a source of performance.
  • High-quality contextual reranking and summarization was critical: strong models improved retrieval, whereas smaller models could degrade it.
  • PaperQA2 achieved 85.2% precision on LitQA2, significantly above the 73.8% mean of unrestricted PhD-level human researchers, while matching human accuracy.
  • WikiCrow's cited synthesis was more precise and less often unsupported or uncited than matched human-written Wikipedia material.
  • ContraCrow made open-corpus contradiction discovery tractable at scale, but its lower agreement with independent experts revealed meaningful calibration and overconfidence problems.

Notable quotes

PaperQA2 decomposes RAG into tools, allowing it to revise its search parameters and to generate and examine candidate answers before producing a final answer.
In summary, having a deep (>10) RCS ranking list, with a high-performing LLM, was crucial in achieving human-level accuracy on LitQA2.
Only claims scoring 8 or higher are considered.
Importantly, just because a claim is detected as a contradiction does not mean that the claim is wrong.

Limitations

  • The released PaperQA package omits the authors' bespoke full-text search access, Grobid integration, citation traversal, and production infrastructure; practical reproduction depends heavily on local or licensed full-text availability.
  • The strongest evaluations are concentrated in biology: LitQA2 uses science questions, WikiCrow covers human genes, and the contradiction deployment samples 93 biology papers, limiting evidence for cross-domain generalization.
  • Human comparisons use modest samples, and the authors had to exclude a later human-evaluation subset after published LitQA2 questions became discoverable through Google; ContraCrow also showed substantially lower human-agent than human-human agreement and apparent overconfidence.

Open questions

  • How well do the reported gains transfer to other scientific disciplines, older literature, non-English work, and corpora with less reliable full-text access?
  • Can contradiction scores be calibrated to expert uncertainty and contextual change so that genuine conflicts are separated from differences in population, protocol, time, or terminology?
  • Can open or smaller models reproduce the benefits of deep contextual summarization and agentic search at lower cost without crossing the comprehension threshold at which summarization harms retrieval?
