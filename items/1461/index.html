CryptoL: Towards Scale Dominance and Physics Constraints Mitigation in Financial Multivariate Time Series Forecasting

📰 Latest · 2026-09-12 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-12

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-10
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11206

Training a shared crypto forecaster with the MSE computed after RevIN's inverse normalization silently reweights every asset by the square of its price scale; CryptoL shows that keeping the loss in normalized coordinates (plus a scale-adaptive epsilon and a soft OHLC-validity penalty) cuts error and near-eliminates physically invalid candles across 16 assets spanning 12 orders of magnitude.

Executive summary

CryptoL is a framework for multivariate OHLC cryptocurrency forecasting built on three components: Two-Phase RevIN (TP-RevIN), which evaluates the MSE in context-normalized coordinates instead of after inverse normalization; a dynamic epsilon in the normalization denominator that scales with the squared context mean; and an auxiliary 'physics' loss penalizing violations of the candlestick inequalities, also computed in normalized space. The authors prove that original-space RevIN MSE equals a scale-squared-weighted normalized residual, so an asset's gradient contribution is multiplied by its squared context scale, effectively reweighting the sampling distribution. Experiments on ~15.5M Binance OHLC bars from 16 assets (PEPE ~1e-7 to BTC above 1e5) with three decoder-only backbones (Timer, Timer-XL, Time-MoE) on 8 TPU v5e show consistent MSE/MAE reductions from TP-RevIN, dramatic MAPE fixes for low-priced coins via dynamic epsilon, and physical-violation rates driven to around 1e-6 while keeping MAE competitive.

Problem: scale heterogeneity plus hard structural constraints

Crypto OHLC forecasting combines extreme cross-asset scale heterogeneity with non-stationarity and the fixed inequalities that define a valid candlestick. A shared model must learn from series whose numerical scales differ by more than twelve orders of magnitude, and it can emit a financially invalid candle even when its pointwise error is small.

  • Feasible set requires High >= max(Open, Close) and Low <= min(Open, Close)
  • All normalization statistics are computed only from the observed context and reused for the target and the inverse transform
  • Quoted values span below 1e-7 (PEPE) to above 1e5 (BTC)

Channel-independent vs channel-dependent normalization

CI standardizes each of O/H/L/C with its own context mean and variance, so the four channels undergo different affine maps and their original ordering is not guaranteed to survive in normalized space. CD computes one shared mean and variance over both the temporal and channel dimensions, applying a single positive affine transform to all four channels, which preserves pairwise order exactly. Under CD, normalized-space OHLC constraints are exactly equivalent to original-space constraints; under CI they are only a representation-space regularizer.

  • CD uses one mean over all entries and the same transform for all channels
  • CI can still give stronger channelwise accuracy, so the axis stays a configurable choice
  • Order-preservation proofs are relegated to an appendix

TP-RevIN and the scale-dominance proposition

Conventional RevIN training restores the prediction to physical units before computing MSE. Proposition 1 shows this equals a quadratic form weighted by the squared context scales, while TP-RevIN's gradient drops that factor. At the asset level, original-space RevIN minimizes a scale-squared-weighted sum of per-asset risks, equivalent to reweighting the asset distribution toward large-scale assets.

  • Under CD normalization the weighting collapses to a single scalar per sample
  • TP-RevIN makes the objective neutral to positive affine changes in each asset's numerical units
  • The authors caution TP-RevIN does not equalize difficulty or gradient norms, only removes the RevIN-scale factor

Dynamic epsilon and the normalized-space physics loss

The fixed stabilizer 1e-5 has an absolute scale and can dominate the empirical variance of sub-cent assets. CryptoL instead ties the stabilizer to the squared context mean, making regularization strength relative rather than absolute. The auxiliary loss sums hinge terms for each violated inequality over the horizon, and the total objective weights the TP-RevIN MSE and the physics term, both computed before inverse normalization so the auxiliary term cannot reintroduce scale weighting.

  • Both loss terms live in normalized coordinates by construction
  • Dynamic epsilon is approximately equivariant under positive unit changes

Setup and results

~15.5M OHLC rows from 16 Binance assets at multiple resolutions; backbones Timer, Timer-XL, Time-MoE; AdamW with linearly scheduled LR starting at 1e-5; one epoch on 90% of data, 10% held out; joint training across all 16 assets per (timeframe, horizon) configuration; 8 TPU v5e with FSDP in JAX 0.9.2 / Flax 0.12.6, FP32. All metrics are reported after restoring predictions to physical space. Without RevIN, MSE explodes to roughly 5e8-7.7e8. TP-RevIN beats CI/CD RevIN, FAN and SAN. The 'Unconstrained Space' baseline attains zero violations by construction but at orders-of-magnitude worse MAE.

  • FAN and SAN show far higher MAE/MAPE, indicating they handle non-stationarity but not cross-asset scale spread
  • TP-RevIN plus the physics loss reaches violation rates as low as 1e-7 to 1e-6 with competitive MAE

Key findings

  • Proposition 1: original-space RevIN MSE is a squared-context-scale-weighted quadratic form, so each asset's contribution to the shared gradient is multiplied by the square of its context scale, reweighting the effective sampling distribution toward large-scale assets.
  • Without RevIN the backbones effectively fail to converge (MSE ~5.2e8-7.73e8, MAE ~6300-8440); CI/CD RevIN drops 5m/horizon-5 Time-MoE MSE to ~9.87e3, and Two-Phase CI RevIN to 6610.0 (MAE 19.03 to 15.25).
  • Dynamic epsilon is decisive only for sub-cent assets: SHIB MAPE under standard RevIN with fixed epsilon is 17362.6 for Time-MoE, falling to 1.89 with dynamic epsilon and to 1.87 / 3.51 (horizon 5 / 30) under TP-RevIN plus dynamic epsilon; BTC is essentially unaffected (~1.12-1.16).
  • TP-RevIN beats the temporal-normalization baselines FAN and SAN on the 1h panel - e.g. Timer-XL horizon 5 MAE 50.93 (MAPE 0.95, PHY 0.01) vs FAN 113.85 (PHY 12.68) and SAN 36.03 (PHY 0.84).
  • The structurally-guaranteed 'Unconstrained Space' baseline achieves zero violations but at ruinous accuracy - Timer-XL 30m horizon 5 MAE 3307.53 vs 34.77 for TP-RevIN (CI), whose violation rate is 2.0e-6.
  • Scale of the study: ~15.5M OHLC observations from 16 Binance assets, three backbones, one training epoch on 90% of data, 8 TPU v5e with FSDP in JAX/Flax, FP32.

Notable quotes

Thus two samples with comparable normalized residuals and comparable model sensitivity contribute to the original-space update in proportion to the squares of their context scales.
Original-space RevIN therefore changes the effective optimization priority toward assets with larger context scales.
This protocol evaluates whether a shared forecasting backbone can learn transferable temporal and cross-asset patterns despite substantial differences in asset scale, volatility, and market behavior.
In contrast, TP-RevIN preserves representation stability across diverse scales and maintains lower physical violation (PHY) rates.

Limitations

  • Each model is trained for only one epoch on 90% of the data with a single learning-rate setting, and no seed variance, confidence intervals, or significance tests are reported.
  • Under CI normalization the normalized-space OHLC penalty is not equivalent to the original-space constraint; the authors concede it is only a representation-space regularizer.
  • Evaluation is purely statistical on a chronologically split 10% holdout from one exchange; there is no trading/backtest evaluation and no walk-forward protocol.
  • The two loss weights are introduced but their values and sensitivity are not discussed in the main text.

Open questions

  • Does the squared-scale reweighting argument generalize to equity or FX panels, and to MAE/quantile/probabilistic objectives rather than MSE?
  • Can CI's channelwise accuracy and CD's exact order preservation be combined, rather than left as a configurable trade-off?
  • Does near-zero violation rate translate into measurable downstream value (execution, risk, or PnL) relative to standard RevIN training?
