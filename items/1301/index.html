Global Multi-Maturity SPX-VIX Calibration Beyond Markovian Stitching

📰 Latest · 2026-09-04 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-04

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-03
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04087

The paper separates exact feasibility from path-law identification in multi-maturity SPX-VIX calibration: globally coupled and stitched models are equally feasible locally, but stitching imposes an untestable Markov restriction that can materially change cross-period prices and risks.

Executive summary

The authors formulate calibration as one law over the complete SPX-VIX maturity chain and prove that exact global feasibility is equivalent to feasibility of every monthly block. The global law class is nevertheless strictly larger because stitching discards history beyond the shared SPX state, leaving cross-period dependence unidentified by local instruments. An augmented-Bregman method keeps observable option quotes tight while explicitly exposing martingale and dispersion residuals.

Feasibility versus identification

  • The calibrated law must match SPX and VIX marginals, preserve the SPX martingale condition, and equate conditional forward log-contract value with squared VIX.
  • Theorem 3.2 proves that exact local-block feasibility, stitched feasibility, and global feasibility are equivalent when adjacent blocks share the same prescribed SPX marginal.
  • Theorem 3.4 proves strict inclusion of stitched laws within global laws for at least three SPX maturities.

What stitching removes

  • Stitching makes each next SPX-VIX block conditionally independent of earlier history given the current SPX level.
  • The removed information is quantified as conditional mutual information between future blocks and prior history.
  • In the finite-tree example, Markovization reduces Cov(V1,V2) from 0.040 to 0.011 and changes VIX-spread-call prices while preserving every adjacent calibration block.

Entropy and global coupling

  • For the standard SPX-Markov reference, relative entropy decomposes into the entropy of the Markovized law plus the information discarded by Markovization.
  • Consequently, every finite-entropy minimizer is stitched.
  • Non-Markov effects require a history-dependent prior, cross-period target, payoff objective, or dependence-risk bound.

Augmented-Bregman calibration

  • The finite-grid program represents the path law as a tensor and applies cyclic Bregman-Newton corrections to normalization, forwards, and selected option prices.
  • Conditional martingale and dispersion rows enter an augmented-Lagrangian exponentiated-gradient step as soft penalties.
  • The finite-grid penalty limit preserves hard constraints while minimizing conditional violations, but fixed-budget calculations are not claimed to reach that asymptotic limit.

Market-data evidence

  • On a 1.2-million-cell grid, raising the shared penalty from 1 to 10,000 reduced thresholded bulk dispersion error from 5.39 to 0.063 and martingale error from 0.0073 to 0.00076.
  • Worst fitted-smile error stayed below 0.70 volatility points across the reported sweep.
  • A seven-axis merged-timeline experiment improved all reported smile errors relative to a five-axis interpolated baseline.

Key findings

  • Exact monthly feasibility is neither improved nor weakened by global coupling; its benefit is preserving and interrogating cross-period dependence.
  • Block-preserving Markovization can materially alter volatility persistence and multi-window derivative prices.
  • With the standard Markov reference, KL minimization selects the stitched minimum-information completion.
  • The synthetic infeasible test kept prescribed marginals about 25 times tighter with marginal priority than with cyclic row projection.
  • Runtime across roughly 78,000 to 1.2 million cells scaled as N^1.04 with R²=0.9998 for a fixed 720-sweep schedule.

Notable quotes

The exact distinction is not feasibility but identification of the full path law.
Thus the current reference gives stitching a precise interpretation: it is the minimum-information exact completion of the local calibrations.
The proposed scheme preserves the fit to observable quote moments and exposes the remaining martingale and dispersion residuals along a finite-budget penalty path.
The independent-block seam and conditional post-projection experiments are practical finite-budget diagnostics, not evidence against exact local–global feasibility equivalence.

Limitations

  • The full coupling tensor grows exponentially with the number of maturities.
  • Headline conditional statistics exclude low-mass conditioning cells, and fixed-budget results do not certify exact feasibility.
  • The merged-timeline experiment has overlapping VIX windows and is not covered by the proved non-overlapping reference construction.

Open questions

  • How wide are robust price bounds for cross-period portfolios over all global gluings consistent with the same monthly blocks?
  • Can low-dimensional path summaries or sparse GPU methods retain non-Markov dependence without exponential tensor growth?
  • How should a rigorously feasible solver be constructed for overlapping VIX windows?
