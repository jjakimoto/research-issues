    TAT-QA: A Question Answering Benchmark on a Hybrid of Tabular and Textual Content in Finance

    ← Go backDS-1000: A Natural and Reliable Benchmark for Data Science…NextFinQA: A Dataset of Numerical Reasoning over Financial Data →
    📚 Foundations · 2026-09-27 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-27

    Theme: Executable Numerical Reasoning over Financial Reports
    FinQA and TAT-QA established financial document understanding as evidence retrieval followed by explicit, auditable computation rather than unconstrained answer generation. Their results show that mixed table-text evidence, domain-specific quantities, units, implicit formulas, and multi-step programs remain materially harder than extracting a single answer span.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07624

    A foundational benchmark for jointly retrieving evidence from financial tables and prose, then executing explicit numerical reasoning over it.

    Executive summary

    TAT-QA introduces 16,552 questions over 2,757 hybrid table-text contexts from 182 financial reports, with answer derivations and numerical scales annotated for explainable reasoning. The paper also proposes TAGOP, which extracts supporting cells and text spans before applying one of ten symbolic aggregation operators, an operand-order classifier, and a scale classifier. TAGOP reaches 50.1 EM and 58.0 F1 on the test set, substantially outperforming prior baselines but remaining far below human performance of 84.1 EM and 90.8 F1.

    Problem and benchmark scope

    Existing question-answering benchmarks generally isolate prose, tables, or knowledge bases, whereas financial analysis often requires combining semantically linked narrative disclosures and numeric tables. TAT-QA targets this hybrid setting and emphasizes arithmetic, comparison, counting, multi-span extraction, answer scaling, and composed calculations.

    • The benchmark contains 16,552 questions associated with 2,757 hybrid contexts from 182 financial reports.
    • Answer sources span tables, text, and joint table-text evidence; 5,219 questions require the joint source category.
    • The dataset contains 6,964 arithmetic questions, 7,139 single-span questions, 2,072 multi-span questions, and 377 counting questions.

    Dataset construction and quality control

    The authors began with about 500 recent financial reports, detected candidate tables, retained tables with 3–30 rows and 3–6 columns, and paired valid tables with at least two surrounding paragraphs that described, analyzed, or complemented them. Finance-trained annotators wrote questions, answers, sources, scales, and executable derivations, followed by two rounds of independent validation.

    • About 20,000 candidate tables remained after automatic preprocessing, but roughly two thirds were later discarded during manual annotation.
    • Approximately 30 finance or related-discipline students were used as annotators after passing a qualification test at 95% accuracy.
    • Splits were made by hybrid context, preventing questions associated with the same context from crossing train, development, and test partitions.

    TAGOP model

    TAGOP flattens the table by row and encodes the question, table, and paragraphs with a transformer. Inside–Outside sequence tagging selects supporting cells and text spans, after which a classifier chooses among ten symbolic operators; additional classifiers determine operand order for noncommutative operations and the answer scale.

    • The ten operators are span-in-text, cell-in-table, spans, sum, count, average, multiplication, division, difference, and change ratio.
    • Operand order is predicted for difference, division, and change ratio.
    • The scale classifier predicts None, Thousand, Million, Billion, or Percent.
    • Training jointly minimizes losses for evidence tags, operator choice, scale, and operand order.

    Benchmark results

    TAGOP clearly outperforms textual, tabular, and hybrid baselines, reaching 50.1 EM and 58.0 F1 on the test set versus 37.0 EM and 46.9 F1 for the strongest baseline, NumNet+ V2. The 32.8-point F1 gap to humans shows that the benchmark was not saturated by the proposed architecture.

    • TAGOP improves test F1 by 11.1 absolute points over NumNet+ V2.
    • Its test F1 is 67.9 for single-span, 75.1 for multi-span, 62.5 for counting, and 42.5 for arithmetic questions.
    • Adding the operators incrementally raises test performance from 14.1 EM/21.8 F1 with span-in-text alone to 50.1 EM/58.0 F1 for the full model.

    Error analysis and significance

    A manual analysis of 100 TAGOP errors attributes 84% to wrong or missing evidence, making cross-modal evidence selection a larger bottleneck than calculation or scale prediction. The authors also identify unsupported computations and questions requiring implicit financial formulas, establishing TAT-QA as a diagnostic benchmark for document-grounded numerical reasoning rather than merely answer extraction.

    • Wrong evidence accounts for 55% of sampled errors and missing evidence for 29%.
    • Wrong calculations account for 9%, unsupported calculations for 4%, and scale errors for 3%.
    • The gross-profit-margin example shows that correct reasoning may require domain knowledge not explicitly stated in the context.

    Key findings

    • TAT-QA operationalizes hybrid financial-document reasoning with 16,552 questions, explicit evidence sources, derivations, and answer scales.
    • A modular extract-then-execute architecture substantially outperforms baselines designed primarily for text, tables, or loosely connected hybrid data.
    • TAGOP achieves 50.1 EM and 58.0 F1, compared with 84.1 EM and 90.8 F1 for human experts.
    • Arithmetic is the hardest reported answer type for TAGOP, at 42.5 test F1.
    • Evidence identification dominates the model's failures: wrong and missing evidence together account for 84% of the sampled errors.

    Notable quotes

    Given hybrid contexts, we invite annotators with financial knowledge to generate questions that are useful in real-world financial analyses and provide answers accordingly.
    The answers in TAT-QA have three types: a single span or multiple spans extracted from the table or text, as well as a generated answer (usually obtained through numerical reasoning).
    This demonstrates more efforts are needed to strengthen the model’s capability of precisely aggregating information from hybrid contexts.
    How to integrate such finance knowledge into QA models to answer questions in TAT-QA still needs further exploration.

    Limitations

    • The dataset is drawn from about 500 recent reports on one financial-report website, with the final contexts coming from 182 reports, which may limit temporal, publisher, layout, and domain diversity.
    • TAGOP relies on ten predefined operators; unsupported calculations still account for 4% of its sampled errors.
    • TAGOP remains 32.8 F1 points below human performance, with evidence retrieval and implicit finance knowledge identified as major unresolved weaknesses.

    Open questions

    • Can learned, executable program induction cover calculations beyond the fixed operator inventory without sacrificing interpretability?
    • How can models jointly retrieve table cells and prose spans reliably enough to reduce the dominant wrong- and missing-evidence errors?
    • How well do methods trained on TAT-QA generalize to newer reports, unfamiliar layouts, other financial-document genres, and questions requiring unstated domain formulas?
