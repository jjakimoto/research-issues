OmniScientist: An Omni-Modal Omni-Discipline AI Scientist

📰 Latest · 2026-08-15
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-15

Source: arXiv · 2026-08-13
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13558

An AI-scientist pipeline whose distinguishing move is code-enforced gates between agent stages — reject if no reported number appears in real stdout, reject if the headline claim is not among the supported analyses (anti-HARKing), correct p-values across every test run including demoted ones. Runs cost $0.03 to $4.34 across 36 disciplines, and only one backbone (Sonnet 5) completed all 36 cases.

Executive summary

OmniScientist wraps a perception layer plus three sequential agents (ideation, experiment, writeup) in a thin deterministic pipeline: inside each stage the agent runs an open-ended ReAct loop, but code — not model text — controls stage transitions and admits outputs only after mechanical checks. The framing is that current systems are workflow-complete but evidence-incomplete: they produce paper-shaped artifacts without ever perceiving raw data. Evidence is organized into four discipline-independent families across twelve modalities, and adding a new discipline requires only a new specification file. Evaluated on a 36-case demonstration suite of real public datasets with LLM-judge rubrics, the system scores 6.3 overall with Sonnet 5, and a paired perception-vs-blind ablation shows the perceiving system wins 85% of head-to-head comparisons.

Topology: agents inside, deterministic code outside

The authors call this a "pipeline of agents," explicitly contrasted with prompt-orchestrated multi-agent labs. Code controls stage transitions, admits stage outputs only after a code-enforced check, and backtracks to ideation when an experiment collapses or returns a null result (maximum two fallbacks). The input contract is one specification file naming a dataset, a scientific subject and a target property, plus the raw artifacts; methodology is left entirely to the agent.

  • Ideation gate requires a research question, hypothesis, experiment sketch, falsification criterion, at least 5 self-filtered candidates, at least 3 focused literature searches, and computational executability.
  • Overclaims are automatically rewritten — "first" or "never explored" become hedged phrasing — because a bounded literature search cannot establish priority.
  • Writeup uses five venue structure specifications; the thesis planner picks the headline claim and demotes the rest to supporting evidence, controls or robustness.

The experiment gate is the actual contribution

Algorithm 1 rejects an experiment if there is no metric, if no run succeeded, if any reported number is absent from real stdout, if the dataset was not loaded from disk, or if the headline is not among the supported analyses. When two or more p-values exist, correction is applied across all tests run — including the ones demoted out of the narrative — while unsupported non-headline analyses remain visible in the trace.

  • Two audits run against the same execution record: a rigour check (real execution, all tests accounted, independence/leakage, headline supported) and a claim check matching every number and claim to a recorded output.
  • At least four analyses are required: main test plus baselines, ablations, mechanism probes, sensitivity sweeps.
  • The authors state plainly that LLM hallucination and overconfidence are why the checks must live in code rather than in a prompt.

Perception is measured separately from reasoning

The perception layer is hierarchical (evidence family, then modality, then registered tools) and prioritizes native numeric analysis — FFT peaks, trend points — before rendering any visual, with visual rendering budget-constrained. A blind baseline given only precomputed scalar features formulates materially different research questions, which the paper treats as the core mechanism result.

  • Four evidence families: perceptual, symbolic, quantitative-statistical, procedural/dynamic; twelve modalities from image and signal to formula, field and graph.
  • Perceptual cases dominate the suite at 28 of 36, so breadth on the other three families rests on only 8 cases.
  • Multimodal grounding is both the lowest-scoring rubric dimension (5.1 with Sonnet 5) and the one least responsive to backbone scaling (1.2-point range strongest-to-weakest).

Two validated case studies, and one refutation

The seismic audit of STEAD estimated that 21.7% of noise-labelled traces contain transients, against an amplitude-only baseline of 2.0%, with a 1.07% null false-alarm rate and stability across 50-fold FAR range, 3 null models, 4 windowing choices and 417 stations. A paediatric chest-radiograph study found a texture patchiness signature with effect size d = 1.25, holding at d = 1.29 on a held-out split. Notably, of 13 end-to-end runs one headline was refuted and five were mixed.

  • Seismology instrument-type hypothesis was refuted by the system's own analysis; channel and network chi-squared p-values were 5.7e-14 and 3.5e-14.
  • Radiograph AUC: mean entropy only 0.840, mean plus patchiness 0.851, patchiness only 0.847, raw-pixel baseline 0.634.
  • Materials informatics run reported leave-one-family-out RMSE 3.1 to 7.0 times higher than random k-fold — a leakage finding the system surfaced itself.

Key findings

  • A full three-stage run costs $0.03 to $4.34, with experimentation dominating; caps are 24 ideation steps, 50 experiment steps, 150-second execution timeout and an 8-image visual budget.
  • Only Sonnet 5 completed all 36 cases; Qwen3.5-9B completed 18 of 32 and Kimi K2.7 6 of 9 — and because failed runs are excluded from means, the high-scoring small-sample backbones look better than they are.
  • The perception ablation (5 paired cases, randomized order) has the full system winning 85% of head-to-head comparisons, with multimodal grounding +2.8 and significance +1.8, while factual accuracy is identical in both arms.
  • Component leave-one-out on seismology: removing prior-art search is the steepest drop (6.9 to 5.7); removing the novelty check costs a full novelty point; provenance enforcement does not move rubric scores at all.
  • Of 13 end-to-end runs, one headline was refuted and five were mixed — the pipeline reports null and negative results rather than manufacturing a positive.

Notable quotes

Existing systems are increasingly workflow-complete, while remaining evidence-incomplete.
The relevant question is not what can be serialised, but which relations survive the interface.
Direct observation fundamentally alters the research trajectory.
Consequently, a stronger reasoning backbone does not inherently provide the perceptual competence that these multimodal tasks demand.

Limitations

  • Grading is entirely by two LLM judges with no human expert review; verbosity bias is addressed only by reporting a 0.16 score-length correlation, and per-backbone means are computed on a biased surviving subset because failed runs are dropped.
  • Weak per-case results are real and visible: condensed matter scored 2.5 overall with factual accuracy 1.0, sleep neuroscience 4.5, gravitational waves 4.5 — the suite average hides a long tail of poor runs.
  • Provenance enforcement is invisible to the rubric (it guarantees traceability without improving judged quality), and physical or wet-lab experiments are out of scope by construction since the idea check rejects anything not executable in code.

Open questions

  • Would human expert review corroborate the LLM-judge rubric, particularly on novelty and significance where small open-weight models collapse?
  • Can multimodal grounding be improved independently of the reasoning backbone, given it is both the weakest dimension and the least sensitive to backbone scale?
  • Does the 5-case perception-vs-blind ablation hold at suite scale, and what happens on the under-represented symbolic, statistical and procedural evidence families?
