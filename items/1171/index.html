The Physical Crash Frontier: What Finite Option Quotes Can and Cannot Reveal

📰 Latest · 2026-08-29 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-29

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-24
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23274

If you convert SPX put quotes into a physical crash probability, you are reporting one point from an interval that is still 2-3x wide at its endpoints even when you use every retained OTM quote - and that interval's lower bound is exactly zero unless you impose a right-tail support cap the quotes cannot supply. The paper gives you the exact, computable set (a compact convex 'frontier' traced by finite SOCPs) instead of a false point estimate, and shows conditional depth is the one coordinate immune to the tail problem.

Executive summary

Jirong Zhuang (University of Macau, sole author) asks what a finite cross section of bid-ask option quotes actually identifies about *physical* crash risk once a CRRA pricing kernel is imposed, rather than fitting a smooth implied surface and reading off a point estimate. For each SPX cross section he collects every risk-neutral measure supported on [0,B] with unit forward mean that prices each retained quote inside its spread, maps each through the power-utility density, and characterizes the set of attainable (crash probability, expected shortfall) pairs: it is nonempty, compact and convex, and its boundary is named the physical crash frontier. A Charnes-Cooper rescaling plus a strike-partition truncated-moment representation turns each support-function evaluation into a finite second-order cone program - exact for gamma <= 2, SDP beyond. On 1,115 weekly SPX cross sections (Jan 2013-Aug 2023), going from the two puts nearest K=0.90 to all retained OTM quotes shrinks the probability interval by a median of ~80-84 percent, yet the surviving interval still has an upper endpoint two to three times its lower endpoint.

The question: what is identified before you add structure

Options price crash risk continuously, but they are state prices, so risk-neutral crash probabilities overstate physical crash risk - most severely in stress. Converting them requires a preference restriction. The standard empirical route fits a smooth implied-vol surface, reads a density off its curvature, and thereby blends market information with assumptions about payoffs that never traded. Zhuang refuses that step and keeps the ambiguity explicit: he asks what the traded, quoted contracts alone pin down.

  • The object is an identified set in the partial-identification sense, not a point estimate.
  • The construction nests the risk-neutral predecessor as the gamma = 0 member of a one-parameter family indexed by risk aversion.
  • Every statement is explicitly conditional on the maintained kernel: 'the results describe what the quotes imply for this investor.'
  • For the market index the Frechet-Hoeffding dependence problem of stock-level bounds disappears; the residual uncertainty is finite strikes, bid-ask intervals, and the unobserved right tail.

The formulation: admissible measures, the CRRA kernel, and two coordinates

State variable is the forward-normalized terminal level X = S_T / F, so E_Q[X] = 1. A retained call quote i at normalized strike k_i imposes c_lo_i <= E_Q[(X - k_i)^+] <= c_hi_i; puts enter the same form via put-call parity. Assumption 3.1 (power utility pricing kernel): the investor is marginal in the market, has CRRA coefficient gamma > 0, is fully invested in market wealth, R_m = cX. That gives the perceived physical density dP/dQ = X^gamma / M_gamma(Q) with M_gamma(Q) = E_Q[X^gamma].

  • Crash probability q = A_gamma(Q) / M_gamma(Q), where A_r(Q) = E_Q[X^r 1{X <= K}].
  • Conditional depth s = K - A_{gamma+1}/A_gamma = l/q - the only coordinate whose common denominator M_gamma cancels.
  • Threshold atoms need their own coordinate: continuous call payoffs cannot separate an atom at K from mass just above it.
  • Theorem 3.2: for gamma > 0, K in (0,1), B finite and M(I) nonempty, the joint (q, l) set is a nonempty compact convex subset. Convexity is NOT automatic - each pair carries its own denominator - and is recovered by mixing measures with weights inversely proportional to their normalizing moments.

The machinery: ratios of moments become finite second-order cone programs

Two transformations make an infinite-dimensional fractional program finite and convex. First, a Charnes-Cooper style rescaling makes both crash coordinates linear in the scaled measure. Second, a partition of [0,B] at the strikes, at K and at B makes every retained call payoff affine on every cell, so quote values are linear in cell masses and first moments; the remaining truncated moment problem has a complete answer via 2x2 localizing matrices, each of which is exactly one second-order cone constraint.

  • Corollary 3.4: under the Slater-type interior-regularity Assumption 3.3 the identified set EQUALS the image of the SOCP feasible region.
  • Corollary 3.5: checking whether a candidate (q, l) scenario is feasible is a single conic feasibility problem.
  • Second-order cones suffice exactly when gamma <= 2 (every matrix is 2x2); integer gamma > 2 requires semidefinite programming.
  • Depth needs a second scaling that, unlike rho, has NO upper bound, so the depth programs need not attain their optima.
  • The frontier is drawn by sandwiching inner hull and outer half-plane polygons over a direction grid doubling from 32 to at most 128 directions until the gap falls below 1 percent (realized max gap 0.99 percent).

What the quotes CANNOT reveal: tail dilution and the log-utility dividing line

The gamma = 2 denominator is a global second moment collecting every state, including payoffs far above the last quoted strike where nothing trades. Proposition 3.6: without a support cap, sup M(Q) = infinity and inf q = inf l = 0. The mechanism is a vanishing tail perturbation - mass of order H^-3/2 at a far state H moves fixed-strike option values by a vanishing amount but raises the second moment at order H^1/2.

  • This is why the support cap B is a SUBSTANTIVE economic assumption, not a technical convenience.
  • Remark 3.7: for gamma <= 1, Jensen plus the forward restriction give M_gamma(Q) <= 1, so the channel is blocked; for every gamma > 1 the construction works.
  • Conditional depth is immune - M(Q) cancels from its ratio.
  • Proposition B.1 orders levels (q_2 <= K*q_1 <= q_1) but is silent about WIDTHS - the empirical finding that gamma = 2 intervals are narrower is an unexplained regularity.

The S&P 500 empirics: sample, design, and what survives

OptionMetrics end-of-day SPX bid/ask quotes, January 2013 through August 2023, with quotes entering ONLY through their intervals, never through a fitted midpoint. Exact horizons of 23, 30 and 37 days, at most one cross section per horizon per calendar week: 1,115 horizon-specific observations over 537 distinct calendar weeks. Main event K = 0.90, support cap B = 5, gamma = 2. Four nested quote sets, with inference by circular block bootstrap on calendar weeks (eight-week blocks, 2,000 replications).

  • Assumption 3.3 verified in 1,111 of 1,115 cross sections; all-OTM programs solved to verified optimality in 1,098; cross-quote-set comparisons use the 976 solved under all four sets.
  • Median identified probability interval reaches [1.97, 4.53] percent at 37 days; conditional depth spans roughly 2.4 to 7.4 percent of ADDITIONAL decline below the threshold.
  • ~70-73 percent of the width reduction arrives from the six nearest additional puts alone, and OTM calls add only 2.6-5.0 percent.
  • Intervals rise in every major stress episode and BOTH endpoints jump together in March 2020 - stress raises the level and the ambiguity simultaneously.
  • Spreads dominate strike density: doubling every bid-ask spread moves an endpoint by a median 0.79pp (probability) / 1.84pp (depth), versus 0.073pp / 0.122pp for retaining only every other strike.
  • Support cap sensitivity is negligible while finite (B = 2 to B = 10 lowers the probability lower bound by a median 0.0008pp) but discontinuous at infinity: the unbounded lower endpoint is exactly 0 in every one of the 180 robustness cross sections.

Section 6: joint sets beat marginal rectangles for scenario screening

The four marginal endpoints are generally attained by four DIFFERENT measures, so a (probability, shortfall) pair drawn from the marginal rectangle may correspond to no single admissible distribution. The frontier cuts those unattainable corners away, and it cuts deepest in the shortfall direction.

  • Across the 976 shared cross sections, the horizon-level median support gap between the all-OTM joint set and its marginal rectangle runs 2.7 to 4.0 percent of the put-pair scale in the two diagonal directions weighting shortfall (pooled median 0.52 percent).
  • Median contraction from put pair to all-OTM across the four diagonal directions is 50 percent of the pair scale.
  • Plotted cross sections include 26 Aug 2015, 4 Dec 2019 (calm), 25 Mar 2020, 8 Jun 2022 at 30 days; the calm-market set lies close to the origin.

Key findings

  • The set of (physical crash probability, expected shortfall) pairs compatible with a finite bid-ask quote cross section under a CRRA kernel is nonempty, compact and CONVEX under a finite support bound (Theorem 3.2) - convexity requires mixing measures with weights inversely proportional to their normalizing moments, because each pair carries its own denominator.
  • Despite both coordinates being ratios of moments, the support function of that set is the optimum of a FINITE second-order cone program - exactly when gamma <= 2, because every truncated-moment localizing matrix is 2x2; gamma > 2 needs SDP.
  • Quotes are genuinely informative but the information is concentrated near the threshold: expanding from the two puts bracketing K = 0.90 to all retained OTM quotes shrinks the median probability interval by 79.9-84.4 percent, of which ~70-73 percentage points come from just the six nearest additional puts and only 2.6-5.0 from the entire OTM call side.
  • Identification is still loose in absolute terms: with every retained OTM quote the median 37-day interval is [1.97, 4.53] percent, i.e. the upper endpoint stays two to three times the lower one, and conditional depth spans ~2.4-7.4 percent of extra decline.
  • Spread width, not strike density, is the binding friction: doubling every bid-ask spread moves endpoints by a median 0.79pp (probability) and 1.84pp (depth) versus 0.073pp and 0.122pp for thinning to every other strike - an order of magnitude difference.
  • Tail dilution (Proposition 3.6) collapses the crash-probability lower bound to exactly ZERO without a support cap - confirmed empirically in every one of the 180 robustness cross sections - and the collapse is a strict function of risk aversion, opening only above log utility. Between finite caps the results barely move (B = 2 to B = 10 shifts the lower bound by a median 0.0008pp), so the identification problem is genuinely at infinity.

Notable quotes

Under the maintained kernel and support condition, the frontier is what the quotes pin down. Everything tighter is an assumption.
The dilution channel opens exactly when the investor is more risk averse than the log investor.
Even after every retained OTM quote is used, the upper endpoint remains two to three times the lower endpoint, so the quotes still admit economically different assessments of the same event.
A positive lower bound on crash probability is therefore a joint statement about traded prices and the maintained support condition.

Limitations

  • Everything is conditional on Assumption 3.1 - a single marginal CRRA investor fully invested in the market - and on gamma = 2, calibrated in a predecessor paper rather than estimated here. A different kernel gives a different set.
  • The headline positive lower bound on crash probability exists only because of the maintained support cap B = 5; Proposition 3.6 shows the quotes alone cannot deliver it, so the reported lower endpoints are assumption-driven, not model-free.
  • One index, one vendor (OptionMetrics end-of-day quotes, not executable prices), one decade, Wednesdays only, and three near-one-month horizons; exact SOCP computation is available only for gamma <= 2, and sample attrition is real (Assumption 3.3 fails in 4 of 1,115; only 976 cross sections support the quote-set comparison).

Open questions

  • What is the cheapest defensible right-tail restriction that restores a positive crash-probability floor - a moment bound, a tail-index condition, a far-OTM call quote, or a variance-swap constraint - and how much does each buy relative to what it assumes?
  • How much would genuinely executable data (intraday quotes, depth at the touch, realized trade prices) tighten the sets, given that spread width dominates strike density by an order of magnitude?
  • Does the width of the identified set itself carry predictive content - do wide-frontier weeks forecast realized crashes, returns, or option-strategy P&L differently from narrow-frontier weeks with the same midpoint?
