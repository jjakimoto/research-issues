    Separated Signal Libraries: Packing, Saturation, and Joint Spectral Limits

    Date: 2026-09-22

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-14
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17609

    If you grow an alpha library under a pairwise-correlation cap, the cap alone tells you nothing about whether the equal-weighted sum is the library's PC1 — this paper proves it is the admission rule (a positive IC MARGIN, not just positive IC) that makes the return target the unique leading eigendirection, and gives finite, computable diagnostics to check it on a real library.

    Executive summary

    Nunes formalizes a cross-sectional signal library as points on a product of T spheres: each demeaned, unit-normalized signal history is a unit vector, pairwise correlation caps become minimum angular separation, and growing the library is a sphere-packing problem. He asks whether a large, weakly-correlated, equally-weighted library's equal-weighted sum (EWS) converges to a principal-component quantity, and shows that separation alone decides nothing. Under the uniform product-volume benchmark, screening on positive average IC gives a target-aligned mean but an exactly isotropic second moment; a positive IC margin beta instead makes the target the unique PC1 at every finite T, with a three-level spectrum whose top eigenvalue tends to beta^2. Gilbert-Varshamov coded constructions exhibit exponentially large separated positive-IC libraries whose EWS is PC1 and others whose EWS is exactly orthogonal to PC1.

    Setup: histories as points on a product of spheres

    A signal at date t is a cross-sectionally demeaned, unit-normalized forecast over d assets, so it lives on the sphere of the neutral subspace with q = d-1 dimensions. Its T-date history, scaled by T^{-1/2}, is a unit vector in a product manifold embedded in the ambient sphere of dimension D = qT. Pairwise history correlation is the time-average of datewise cosines, so a minimum angle theta is exactly the cap c_ij <= cos(theta).

    • Intrinsic dimension of the history manifold is (q-1)T; the ambient vector-space dimension is D = qT
    • The comparison is EWS vs the top eigenvector of the UNCENTERED second moment; centering would remove the very mean term of interest
    • A 60-degree separation rule is a 0.5 correlation cap; the two-sided rule |c_ij| <= 1/2 is strictly stronger
    • Motivating case: 3,000 signals over 20 assets - large versus the 19 available neutral directions per date, small versus the number of well-separated histories

    Packing, saturation, maximum - three distinct notions

    The paper insists on separating three readings of 'filling the sphere'. A separated set merely respects the distance rule; a saturated set admits no further point; a maximum set has the largest cardinality. Greedy admission reaches saturation but not necessarily maximality. Density says WHERE points occur; an equal-weight average depends on HOW MANY occur in each region.

    • Prop. 4.1: for fixed compact domain and theta > 0 the maximum separated size is finite and attained; every saturated code has covering radius strictly below theta
    • Example 5.1 (circle): a saturated code with 3n points on the upper semicircle and 2n on the lower fills the circle in Hausdorff distance but keeps a permanent 3:2 count imbalance and a nonzero limiting centroid
    • Theorem 5.2: codes whose size divided by the maximum packing number tends to 1 have empirical measures converging weakly to normalized volume
    • On the full sphere that gives mean -> 0, second moment -> I_D/D, and a vanishing eigengap - the strongest form of 'filling the sphere' destroys both the EWS and the unique PC1

    Positive IC vs a positive IC margin - the central spectral distinction

    Positive average IC defines an open hemisphere of HISTORIES, not a hemisphere at every date: a signal with datewise ICs 0.6 and -0.2 qualifies. Theorem 3.3 shows that under uniform product volume, conditioning on c > 0 leaves the uncentered second moment exactly isotropic while producing a target-aligned mean decaying like sqrt(2/(pi q T)). Theorem 3.4 shows a positive margin c >= beta does make the target the unique PC1 at every finite T.

    • Corollary 3.5 (fixed beta, T -> infinity): mean norm -> beta, top eigenvalue -> beta^2, residual levels decay as 1/T via an exponential tilt kappa(q,beta)
    • Finite-T correction: mean = beta + 1/(kappa T) + o(1/T); top eigenvalue = beta^2 + 2beta/(kappa T) + o(1/T)
    • Cost of a fixed margin under uniform candidates: admission probability exp{-T I(beta) + o(T)} - exponentially rare
    • Proposition 3.6 (margin at CLT scale beta_T = z/sqrt(qT)): at z = 1 the limiting admission rate is 15.87% and the target-to-residual ratio 2.525, but the absolute gap and mean norm vanish
    • The coarse bound T beta^2 > 1 is far more conservative: for q=19, beta=0.03 it only turns positive at T >= 1,112

    Capacity: exponentially many separated histories

    A sign-based construction maps binary words of length D = qT into valid histories, with correlation 1 - 2 d_H / D. The Gilbert bound then gives an exponential lower bound on the packing number, at rate 0.1887218755 bits per coordinate at 60 degrees. A Varshamov-type random linear code lemma adds exact first- and second-moment balance, which makes the two contrasting spectral constructions exact rather than approximate.

    • For the single-date 20-asset case, the relevant kissing number at 60 degrees has lower bound 11,948 and a reported numerical SDP upper bound 24,417; the exact value remains undetermined
    • Gilbert bound at 60 degrees, q = 19: log10 lower bound 109.9182 at T = 100 and 1081.8841 at T = 1,000
    • A history that merely repeats one cross-section has only the original cross-section's capacity - no extra room from time

    Two exponentially large separated libraries with opposite outcomes

    Theorem 8.1 builds a balanced code with a weak common component of strength rho: the EWS is the unique history PC1 exactly when rho > 1/(N+1) with N = (q-1)T, whereas at a single date the condition is rho > 1/q. For q = 19 and rho = 0.01 the datewise test fails but the history test holds from T = 6 onward. Theorem 8.2 builds the opposite: all pairwise correlations at most 0.5, every signal with positive IC at every date, yet the unique history PC1 is exactly orthogonal to the EWS.

    • Theorem 8.2's moment matrix is M = 0.1 m(x)m + 0.3 v(x)v + (0.6/N) P_U, so the leading eigenvalue 0.3 belongs to a direction with zero payoff at every date
    • Its code rate is any R < 0.0201312433 bits per residual coordinate - still exponential in library size
    • Remark 8.3: the disagreement is Davis-Kahan stable - leading gap 0.2 for N >= 6
    • Conclusion: separation decides neither alignment nor disagreement, even within a strictly positive-IC corpus

    Finite diagnostics and estimation limits (what to actually measure)

    Section 6 gives a finite, assumption-free criterion: with rho = ||mu||^2 and eta the operator norm of the centered covariance, if eta < rho then the second moment has a simple top eigenvalue with gap at least rho - eta and the tangent of the angle between mu and the top eigenvector is at most eta/(rho - eta). Section 10.3 gives uniform correlation error sqrt((2/T) log(J(J-1)/alpha)) via Hoeffding plus a union bound, so log J = o(T) suffices.

    • The identity eta/rho = (1-rho)/(rho r_eff) shows growing K helps only if it raises the mean or the residual effective rank, and r_eff <= min(K-1, D) caps that
    • Packing gives only an UPPER bound on mean energy: rho <= 1/K + ((K-1)/K) cos(theta) - never a positive lower bound
    • For isotropic residuals on an r-dimensional subspace, the mean is PC1 exactly when rho > 1/(r+1)
    • Screening-error budget: require the estimated mean IC to exceed beta + sqrt(2 log(2J/alpha)/T) to certify the population margin
    • Section 11 checklist for a real library: minimum/average mean IC and margin persistence, rho, eta, residual effective rank, actual leading eigengap, EWS-PC angle
    • Signal, IC and PnL correlation matrices are different objects - a diversity rule on one is not a diversity rule on the others

    Key findings

    • Under uniform product volume, screening on positive average IC leaves the uncentered second moment EXACTLY isotropic for every T while giving a target-aligned mean of size ~ sqrt(2/(pi q T)) - a positive screen buys a first moment but no PC1.
    • Any positive IC margin beta makes the target the unique PC1 at every finite T, with a three-level spectrum; at fixed beta the mean norm tends to beta and the leading eigenvalue to beta^2, with finite-T corrections 1/(kappa T) and 2beta/(kappa T).
    • A margin at the CLT scale beta_T = z/sqrt(qT) keeps a positive admission rate (15.87% at z = 1, target-to-residual ratio 2.525) but drives both the mean norm and the absolute eigengap to zero.
    • Numerically (q = 19, beta = 0.03): admission falls from 0.4500 at T = 1 to 1.77e-5 at T = 1,000 while the target-to-residual ratio rises from 1.117 to 19.022 and the absolute gap SHRINKS from 0.00613 to 0.000948 - relative dominance and absolute gap move in opposite directions.
    • Separation permits both outcomes at exponential scale: a balanced code with rho = 0.01 and q = 19 has the EWS as history PC1 from T = 6 onward, while Theorem 8.2's code (all correlations <= 0.5, all ICs positive) has PC1 exactly orthogonal to the EWS at code rate up to 0.0201312433 bits per residual coordinate.
    • Geometric room is enormous but statistically unverifiable: the Gilbert bound gives >= 10^109.9 separated 60-degree histories at q=19, T=100, yet uniform estimation of all pairwise correlations needs log J = o(T) and fails once J grows exponentially in T.

    Notable quotes

    Three interpretations of “fill the sphere” must be kept apart.
    A separated set respects the distance rule. A saturated set admits no further point without breaking the rule. A maximum set has the largest possible cardinality.
    The residual factor survives in the second moment even though its signs cancel in the EWS.
    The empirical question is not whether the sphere has enough room. It is whether the admission rule spreads residual variation while preserving useful mean structure.

    Limitations

    • Pure population and finite-configuration theory with synthetic numerical checks only - no market data, and the capacity bounds count geometrically possible histories rather than any supply of genuinely predictive signals.
    • The sharp spectral results hold under the uniform product-volume benchmark and its symmetry group; an arbitrary margin-screened real corpus need not obey that conditional law, as Theorem 8.2 demonstrates.
    • No uniform packing claim is made when beta shrinks or T grows, and temporal dependence is not covered by the Hoeffding-based estimation bounds.

    Open questions

    • What measure does a real greedy admission process actually select - i.e. extend random-sequential-adsorption results to IC-screened domains with nonuniform or adaptive candidate arrivals?
    • Can packing-to-moment bounds be made useful as the history dimension grows, with certified occupancy deficits rather than an assumed near-maximum packing?
    • How does the geometry combine with spectral inference under time dependence when means and eigengaps are themselves vanishing?
