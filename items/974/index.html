Llama-Mobile: Efficient 2.7-Bit Quantization of VLMs

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-21
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21134

Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct is squeezed into a 3.73 GB file (2.68 bits/weight) that actually runs on a Pixel 8a at 3.8 tokens/s, where the INT8 version does not even fit in memory -- but the honest price is an 0.083 average VQA drop (0.744 -> 0.661), and the whole thing depends on 2048 steps of QAT distillation, not a post-training quantizer.

Executive summary

The paper introduces S3D8, a vector-quantization weight format that packs 3 weights into 1 byte (5-bit shared centroid index + 3 sign bits, ~2.68 bits per weight with bfloat16 per-output-channel scales), co-designed around the Arm CPU 64-entry TBL instruction so it decodes to INT8 in about 5 logical instructions. Because sub-3-bit post-training quantization collapses (S3D8+GPTQ scores 0.340; rate-matched INT+GPTQ scores 0.018), the accuracy comes entirely from quantization-aware distillation on data the model generates itself: ImageNet images + 495 randomly sampled generic prompts + teacher responses, trained with per-token KL for only 2048 steps. Result: 3,569 MB of weights (3.73 GB on disk with vocabulary and metadata) scoring 0.661 average across VQAv2/ChartQA/DocVQA/AI2D vs 0.744 for bfloat16, and 3.8 tokens/s decode on a Pixel 8a. Size-matched scalar formats under the identical QAT recipe score far worse (INT 0.347, student-t 0.565, lloyd-max 0.436), which is the central claim: the format, not the training, is what buys the last bit.

The S3D8 format: 3 weights per byte, sign-reflected codebook

S3D8 is a 3-dimensional vector quantizer, not a scalar one. Each byte stores a 5-bit index into a 32-entry per-matrix codebook of absolute-value centroids plus 3 sign bits (one per weight), so 32 stored centroids reflect into 256 effective signed lattice points across the 8 orthants. Dequantization multiplies a per-output-channel bfloat16 absmax scale by the sign and the centroid -- channel scaling, not block scaling. Total cost is about (8/3 + 16/k) bits per element, measured at 2.68 bits per weight for this model. Crucially the byte packs three separate OUTPUT channels, not three consecutive input weights, which removes padding and alignment logic in the fused dequantize-and-dot-product kernel.

  • 5-bit centroid index + 3 sign bits = 3 weights in 8 bits; 32-entry table, 256 signed points via sign reflection.
  • Per-output-channel bfloat16 absmax scaling, NOT block scaling.
  • Exploits the Arm 64-entry TBL lookup to fetch SIGNED values directly; sign bits are reordered so 5 logical instructions build the 3 lookup indices.
  • No outlier path, no mixed precision -- one uniform format on every 2-D weight tensor.
  • Activations are per-channel-scaled INT8 for the direct-cast and QAT variants; KV-cache quantization is not addressed.

Two-phase quantization: centroids fit once, then frozen

Centroids are constructed BEFORE QAT by normalizing each weight matrix by per-output-channel absmax, taking absolute values, reshaping into 3-element vectors, and running Lloyd-Max with k-means++ initialization to minimize squared reconstruction error, then rounding into {0,...,127}. During QAT only the channel scales, quantization indices and signs move; the codebook never updates. Index assignment in the forward pass is nearest-centroid in L2 over the scaled absolute 3-vector, with a straight-through estimator for gradients.

  • The codebook fit is a one-time, data-free (weights-only) step -- no calibration set needed for the centroids.
  • QAT updates scales, indices and signs only; centroids frozen.
  • Straight-through estimator; float32 master weights; full logits used for the KL.

Self-generated calibration data -- the part that makes it reproducible without the original recipe

The authors assume no access to the original training data or recipe. Images come from ImageNet train (1,281,167 images, deliberately generic and disjoint from the eval tasks); text targets are the bfloat16 teacher's own generations (temperature 0.6, top_p 0.9, max 512 tokens). Prompts are randomly assembled: the instruction template is applied with p=0.75 from a pool of 495 generic image-comprehension questions, and with p=0.7 an instruction block is prepended carrying optional adherence, length and format probes. No benchmark images or benchmark-specific prompts are used anywhere. The loss is per-token KL between teacher and student next-token distributions over the response tokens only.

  • Prompt diversity is load-bearing: a single fixed prompt ('Describe the image:') gives markedly worse downstream scores at matched QAT steps.
  • Training is cheap: 2048 steps, batch 128, max sequence length 512, AdamW, cosine schedule with lr = 2^-(b+14) where b = average bits per parameter.
  • Train KL loss tracks task score well (Spearman rho = -0.91 after 2048 steps) even though the training set is task-independent -- so you can tune without touching the benchmarks.
  • The GPTQ baseline used 512 synthetic image-text calibration samples, max length 1024.

Accuracy: what is actually lost

At 2.68 bits per weight the average across four VQA tasks falls 0.083 absolute (0.744 -> 0.661, about 11% relative). The loss is unevenly distributed: VQAv2 is nearly intact (0.754 -> 0.702) while ChartQA (0.747 -> 0.648), DocVQA (0.844 -> 0.740 ANLS) and AI2D (0.631 -> 0.554) -- the document, chart and diagram tasks that need fine-grained reading -- lose the most. Every size-matched scalar baseline trained with the IDENTICAL QAT recipe does far worse, and two of them are essentially broken, which is what isolates the format as the causal factor rather than the distillation.

  • S3D8 gives about 22% extra compression versus block-scaled INT at equal task performance.
  • PTQ is not an option at this rate: S3D8+GPTQ = 0.340, rate-matched INT+GPTQ = 0.018, S3D8+QAT = 0.661.
  • Direct casting below about 3.5 bits per weight causes severe degradation.
  • Mixed precision does not help: keeping the LM output projection and the whole vision encoder in INT8 costs 3,569 -> 4,513 MB (+26%) and buys +0.006 average.

On-device reality: where S3D8 wins and where it loses to INT8

The speed story is bandwidth, not math. At batch 1 (autoregressive decode) S3D8 beats INT8 because the weight read dominates: 33.8 vs 26.5 GMAC/s on a 5-core Pixel 8a, and 1031 vs 461 GMAC/s on Graviton4. At m=128 (text prefill) and m=1601 (single-tile vision encode) the operation becomes compute-bound, dequantization is pure extra work, and S3D8 is slightly SLOWER than INT8. The authors say this openly. End-to-end, a custom C++ safetensors-based runtime generates 100 tokens at a median 3.8 tokens/s on the Pixel 8a (12.5 GB/s of an observed ~25 GB/s peak); INT8 simply does not fit in the phone's memory. On Graviton4 (c8g.24xlarge, 96x Neoverse V2) S3D8 does 36.8 tokens/s vs INT8's 26.4.

  • Pixel 8a: 1x Cortex-X3 + 4x A715 + 4x A510, LPDDR5X-4266 (34 GB/s peak); tested in 5-core and 1-core modes.
  • Decode-only throughput -- prefill and model loading are EXCLUDED from the 3.8 tokens/s figure.
  • Benchmark protocol: 20 iterations per sample with the first 5 discarded, 50 samples, median plus 95% bootstrap CIs.
  • 3.8 tokens/s for an 11B VLM on a mid-range 2024 phone is usable for one-shot image Q&A, not for chat.

Is the evaluation honest? (practitioner read)

Mostly yes, with two caveats worth pricing in. Strong points: all format baselines share the identical QAT procedure so the comparison isolates the format; the calibration data provably never touches benchmark images or prompts; fixed 1024-example eval subsets; confidence intervals on every runtime number; the paper states its own numbers are a controlled relative benchmark for quantization rather than a reproduction of official model-card scores; and the negative mixed-precision ablation is reported rather than buried. Caveats: the headline table's model sizes EXCLUDE vocabulary and metadata (3,569 MB tabulated vs the 3.73 GB actually deployed, a ~4.5% understatement), and every baseline is an in-house scalar re-implementation, so there is no comparison against published sub-3-bit systems (AQLM, QuIP#, llama.cpp Q2_K) and no text-only benchmark to catch language-capability regression.

  • Baselines are all internal; no head-to-head against external low-bit VQ methods.
  • Only 4 VQA benchmarks, all from Llama's own eval suite; no text-only, reasoning, or hallucination evals.
  • One model, one modality pair, CPU only -- no GPU/NPU, no energy, battery or thermal numbers.
  • The 'INT8 does not fit in memory' claim is the strongest practical argument, and it is a memory-capacity fact rather than a benchmark artifact.

Key findings

  • S3D8 at 2.68 bits per weight gives an average task degradation of 0.083 (0.744 -> 0.661); the damage concentrates on chart/document/diagram tasks (ChartQA -0.099, DocVQA -0.104, AI2D -0.077) while VQAv2 loses only 0.052.
  • Under the identical QAT recipe, size-matched scalar formats collapse: INT 0.347 at 2.71 bits, lloyd-max 0.436 at 2.59 bits, student-t 0.565 at 2.71 bits -- so the 3-D vector format (not the distillation) is what buys the sub-3-bit regime, worth about 22% extra compression versus block-scaled INT at equal accuracy.
  • Post-training quantization is dead at this rate: at ~2.7 bits S3D8+GPTQ scores 0.340 and rate-matched INT+GPTQ scores 0.018; QAT is required to reach 0.661.
  • The deployed artifact is a 3.73 GB file that decodes at a median 3.8 tokens/s on a Pixel 8a at 12.5 GB/s read bandwidth -- and the INT8 version of the same model does not fit in the phone's memory at all.
  • S3D8 beats INT8 only in the memory-bound decode regime (33.8 vs 26.5 GMAC/s on Pixel, 1031 vs 461 on Graviton4) and is slightly slower for compute-bound prefill and vision encoding -- the authors state this trade-off explicitly.
  • Mixed precision was tried and rejected: keeping the LM output projection plus the vision encoder in INT8 grows the model 3,569 -> 4,513 MB for a +0.006 average gain, even though train KL improves (0.041 -> 0.034).
  • The self-generated data pipeline needs neither the original training data nor benchmark data, and train KL correlates with task score at Spearman rho = -0.91 after only 2048 QAT steps.

Notable quotes

We separate quantization into two phases. First, centroids are constructed once before performing QAT. Second, during QAT, channel scales, quantization indices and signs are updated while centroids are kept frozen.
GPTQ improves over direct casting throughout most of the sweep, but QAT remains stronger at lower rates.
Sampled prompting substantially improves downstream performance, suggesting that QAT benefits from diverse responses that probe a range of instruction-following behavior.
While our quantization framework is general, our experiments focus on a single model, Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct, and on CPU execution.

Limitations

  • Single model (Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct) and CPU-only execution; the decoder is an Arm-optimized 64-entry table lookup, so other architectures (GPU/NPU/x86) may need bespoke kernels and the reported speedups do not transfer.
  • Evaluation is only four VQA-style benchmarks from Llama's own eval suite on fixed 1024-example subsets, with no text-only or reasoning benchmarks, no external low-bit baselines (AQLM/QuIP#/llama.cpp Q2_K), no KV-cache quantization, and no energy, battery or thermal measurement.
  • A single uniform format is applied to all weights and higher-precision layers did not improve the trade-off; the headline table sizes exclude vocabulary and metadata (3,569 MB tabulated vs 3.73 GB actually shipped).

Open questions

  • Does S3D8 + self-generated QAT hold up on newer/denser VLMs (Qwen3-VL, Gemma 3) and on smaller models where 2.7 bits leaves far less redundancy to absorb the error?
  • The paper flags that prompt and image selection could be further optimized -- how much of the remaining 0.083 gap is recoverable by better synthetic data or more than 2048 QAT steps versus being an irreducible floor of the format?
  • Given that S3D8 loses to INT8 on compute-bound prefill and vision encoding, is the right production answer a mixed deployment (S3D8 weights for decode, INT8 for prefill/vision), and what does that do to the memory-capacity argument that is currently S3D8's strongest selling point?
