Convex Modeling of Price Cross-Impact over Time

📰 Latest · 2026-09-07 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-07

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-04
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04712

Standard multi-period portfolio models charge price impact separately per contract and per day, which systematically overprices relative-value trades; this paper gives a convex quadratic cost that couples contracts (cross-impact) and dates (power-law transient impact) while provably admitting no price manipulation, and shows on WTI index-roll data that it raises net P&L from 0.4 bp to 3.1 bp per month.

Executive summary

Wang and Udell (Stanford MS&E) propose a transaction-cost model for multi-period portfolio construction that captures both cross-impact and transient (decaying) impact in a single convex quadratic form. Each period's impact is a PSD matrix Lambda_t = Gamma_t^{1/2} C_t Gamma_t^{1/2} built from volatility/volume forecasts and a return-correlation proxy; periods are coupled by a power-law kernel k(h) = (1 + h/tau)^{-beta} using a symmetric coupling Lambda_t^{1/2} Lambda_s^{1/2}. They prove the full nT x nT cost matrix A is PSD (A >= (1/2) diag(Lambda_1,...,Lambda_T)) by writing the power-law kernel as a nonnegative mixture of exponentials, each PSD as a Cauchy characteristic function, so no round trip has negative cost even when liquidity varies over the horizon. Empirically, on 2004-2011 WTI calendar-spread trading around the S&P GSCI index roll, the model trades larger and nets 3.1 bp/month versus 2.3 bp (self-impact only) and 0.4 bp (the separable 3/2-power baseline).

Problem: separable impact costs overprice relative-value trades

Multi-period portfolio construction frameworks (Boyd et al. 2017) model impact cost as separable contract-by-contract and period-by-period. That misses two documented effects: correlated legs of a spread partially cancel each other's impact, and the entry's impact is still alive at the exit, so an unwind recovers part of the entry cost. With edges of a few basis points in commodity/macro relative value, this overpricing kills otherwise profitable trades.

  • Running example: the S&P GSCI index roll, which sells the nearby and buys the deferred on business days five through nine of the month.
  • Cited empirics: from 2004 to 2011 the WTI nearby-minus-deferred spread fell roughly 30-40 bp around the roll window and recovered within about two weeks (Irwin, Sanders & Yan 2023).
  • Liquidity is predictably time-varying: roll calendars, expiry cycles, and the EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report concentrate liquidity.

Model: PSD cross-impact matrix per period, power-law kernel across periods

Self-impact of contract i at date t is gamma_{t,i} u_{t,i} with gamma_{t,i} = l_i * sigma_{t,i} / v_{t,i} (volatility over dollar volume). Cross-impact is Lambda_t = Gamma_t^{1/2} C_t Gamma_t^{1/2} with C_t PSD with unit diagonal, so cross terms are added without disturbing the diagonal self-impact. Return correlation is used as a proxy for impact correlation, mirroring the risk matrix decomposition but with a different scale.

  • Temporal kernel k(h) = (1 + h/tau)^{-beta}, matching empirical power-law (not exponential) impact decay.
  • Cost: phi_imp(u) = u^T A u, with A = B (G kron I_n) B, G = (K + K^T)/2, K lower triangular to enforce causality.
  • Dates need not be equally spaced: they can be days or intraday windows around scheduled events.
  • Power-law decay is non-Markovian, so the whole trade sequence is optimized jointly rather than by a closed-form policy.

Convexity proof and the no-manipulation guarantee

PSD-ness of A is sufficient for the absence of negative-cost round trips under linear impact. The proof has two steps: a Gamma-function substitution writes H_{ts} = k(|a_t - a_s|) as a nonnegative mixture of exponential kernels; and each exponential kernel e^{-c|h|} is PSD because it is the characteristic function of a Cauchy distribution. Hence H >= 0, G >= (1/2) I_T, and A >= (1/2) diag(Lambda_1,...,Lambda_T) >= 0.

  • Symmetric coupling Lambda_t^{1/2} Lambda_s^{1/2} is what buys convexity; the true causal coupling G_ts Lambda_s can be indefinite.
  • Appendix A counterexample (n=1, T=2): det M = gamma_1 gamma_2 - rho^2 gamma_1^2/4, negative whenever gamma_2 < rho^2 gamma_1 / 4, an illiquid period followed closely by a liquid one.
  • Motivation is legal as well as computational: exploiting negative-cost round trips is price manipulation and is illegal (Huberman & Stanzl 2004; Gatheral 2010).

Numerical study: WTI calendar spreads around the GSCI roll, 2004-2011

n = 2 contracts per month, 96 months of EIA daily WTI settlements. The trader maximizes forecast return minus impact cost minus a bid/ask cost with a = (1.0, 1.5) bp, subject to x_0 = x_T = 0, dollar-neutrality, a $1B gross cap, and a daily CVaR_0.95 limit of 50 bp computed from 250 prior trading days. Three impact specifications are compared: the separable 3/2-power baseline, self-impact only, and the full transient cross-impact model.

  • Dollar volumes are licensed data, so simulated from a CFTC prior-year average volume figure.
  • Nearby share is logistic in business days to last trading day, with w_0 = 0.8, c = 7, s = 2.
  • Wednesday inventory-report effect simulated by scaling volume by 1.08 and volatility by 1.02.
  • EWMA decay lambda = 0.97, frozen within each month; kernel (tau, beta) = (1, 1/2); l_i = 1.
  • Averaged over 96 months the spread falls 9 bp over business days two through four, another 32 bp over the roll window, and rebounds 65 bp by day 15.

Significance

The paper supplies a drop-in convex cost for existing multi-period convex portfolio frameworks that simultaneously handles cross-impact and non-Markovian power-law decay, with a clean sufficient condition (PSD-ness) for no price manipulation that holds for arbitrary time-varying liquidity, a case where prior work shows per-period PSD matrices are not enough. The cost of the guarantee is a deliberate approximation: the symmetric coupling is not the true causal impact.

  • Practical payoff: at estimated leg correlation 0.96 the two legs' impacts nearly cancel, netting the baseline and self-impact models cannot see.
  • The authors explicitly do not attempt to reproduce the square-root law.

Key findings

  • The cost matrix satisfies A >= (1/2) diag(Lambda_1,...,Lambda_T) >= 0, so every trade sequence costs at least half the sum of its single-period costs and no round trip is profitable, regardless of how liquidity varies over the horizon.
  • Convexity is proved by decomposing the power-law kernel into a nonnegative mixture of exponentials and using the Cauchy characteristic function to show each exponential kernel is PSD.
  • On 96 months of WTI index-roll trading, the transient cross-impact model nets 3.1 bp/month vs 2.3 bp for self-impact-only and 0.4 bp for the separable 3/2-power baseline.
  • The baseline's predicted impact cost (1.0 bp) exceeds its simulated cost (0.0 bp) and its alpha is only 0.6 bp: it overprices impact so severely that it barely trades; the self-impact model predicts 6.9 bp against a simulated 2.7 bp.
  • With estimated inter-leg correlation 0.96, the long and short legs' impacts partially cancel, and neither the baseline nor the self-impact model captures this netting.
  • The measured dislocation matches the literature: the spread falls 9 bp over business days 2-4, a further 32 bp over the roll window, and rebounds 65 bp by day 15.
  • The true causal coupling G_ts Lambda_s is not PSD in general.

Notable quotes

The index roll schedule is public and fixed in advance.
Power-law decay is not Markovian, so we optimize the sequence of all trades jointly under the predicted liquidity over the planning horizon.
Cross-impact does not vanish when the period ends.
The data covers 2004–2011, when the roll effect was economically material.

Limitations

  • Circular backtest: the transient cross-impact model is both the simulator's cost model and one of the tested models; the authors note this limitation is standard in impact backtests.
  • Dollar volumes are licensed exchange data and are therefore simulated from public proxies, and the impact scaling l_i is set to 1 rather than learned.
  • The symmetric coupling is not the true causal impact, and estimates are frozen within each month with a single up-front solve rather than a receding-horizon re-solve.

Open questions

  • Can the impact correlation C_t be calibrated directly from limit-order-book data rather than proxied by return correlation?
  • How does the model perform in a receding-horizon implementation with daily re-estimation, and on larger cross-sections than n = 2 contracts?
  • Can a convex, manipulation-free cost also reproduce square-root/nonlinear metaorder impact, or is linearity essential to the guarantee?
