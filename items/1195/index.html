Accelerating Scientific Research with Gemini in the Real-World

📰 Latest · 2026-08-30 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-30

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-27
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26701

Google's Co-Scientist moves from in-silico ideation to execution-grounded research, and the paper is unusually explicit about which headline results were externally validated versus still provisional - the MXene 'discovery' is a lattice-spacing match whose atomic structure the authors say is unconfirmed. It is also a rare case where the autorater-vs-human gap is reported against the system's own headline: three physicians found no difference on 8 of 9 clinical dimensions despite huge automated-judge gains.

Executive summary

The paper extends Gemini-based Co-Scientist from an in-silico hypothesis generator into an execution-grounded partner and reports four studies spanning a deliberate autonomy spectrum: human-executed CVD materials synthesis, expert-refined biology phenotype prediction, fully autonomous agent-architecture discovery, and fully autonomous end-to-end paper generation. Verifier grounding differs sharply per study - physical instrument characterization (XRD/SEM/EDS/STEM/XPS/Raman) in materials, unpublished wet-lab measurements plus linear mixed-effects statistics in biology, held-out benchmarks scored by two LLM judges plus a small blinded physician panel in computer science, and 30 blinded domain experts cross-referencing manuscripts against raw execution logs in paper generation. The authors hedge the flagship materials result themselves and explicitly decline to claim publication-ready autonomous research.

System topology: a three-stage evolutionary pipeline (Section 2)

Co-Scientist takes a natural-language research directive and runs Ideation -> Experimentation -> Paper Writing, each an evolutionary loop over LLM-generated candidates. Ideation samples hypotheses at high temperature, grounds each with a parallel literature review, screens for safety, and ranks them by LLM pairwise tournaments whose outcomes feed a TrueSkill Bayesian rating selected with a UCB acquisition function. Experimentation is a three-phase code-evolution protocol (scaffold on a minimal subset, transition, full-scale run) with parallel solvers in isolated environments. Paper Writing evolves a manuscript scored by an automated reviewer across nine peer-review dimensions.

  • Ideation hyperparameters: sampling temperature tau=1.6, default G=10 generations, crossover p_c=0.7 / mutation 0.3, UCB kappa=1.0.
  • Experimentation applies a multiplicative score decay (gamma=0.97) to the best-program buffer each generation to prevent stagnation.
  • Fitness is a joint objective, not a pure reviewer score: S = 1.0*S_reviewer - 0.5*S_plagiarism - 1.0*S_hallucination, all normalized to [0,1] so a single unverified empirical claim can wipe out the maximum possible reviewer gain (delta S_reviewer at most 0.3).
  • Two-layer safety: an up-front ethics screen on the research directive, plus a per-idea/per-plan binary LLM harm judgment feeding textual objections back into the agent loop.

Verifier grounding: what actually checks the agent, per study

This is the paper's most load-bearing distinction and it varies by domain. The strongest verifier is deterministic and non-LLM: a hallucination-clipping module parses quantitative claims out of the manuscript and cross-validates them against raw stdout/stderr execution logs, rewriting unsupported sentences with log-extracted values and terminating the writing phase entirely if no valid logs exist. In the physical-science studies, verification is instrument characterization performed by human co-authors, not by the system. In the medical study the primary numbers come from two LLM judges - and the paper reports those judges disagree with clinicians.

  • Execution-log verification is deterministic ground truth only for computational work: outputs are fully determined by inputs and cannot be retroactively altered by the manuscript generator.
  • Materials: XRD, SEM-EDS, STEM/HAADF-STEM + FFT, XPS, Raman - all run by the authors' own lab; no external replication, no cross-laboratory test.
  • Biology: comparison against unpublished wet-lab morphology data (so the models had no prior exposure), with linear mixed-effects models and ANOVA on Source x log10(IPTG) interaction terms.
  • Computer science: HealthBench Hard (1,000 queries) and HealthBench Professional (525 prompts) strictly held out during architecture development, with decontamination analysis. Human check = 3 board-certified physicians, 106 questions, each question rated by only ONE clinician (query-level inter-rater reliability not assessed).
  • Paper generation: 30 domain experts (29 with PhD/postdoc, mean 11 years experience), 3 blind reviews per manuscript, cross-referencing metrics against logs and methods against source code, plus plagiarism checks via Google Scholar / Semantic Scholar / OpenScholar.

Materials science: a safe MXene precursor route, with the authors' own hedge (Section 3.1)

Co-Scientist was asked for a non-hazardous replacement for toxic TiCl4 in bottom-up CVD growth of Ti3C2Tx MXene, conditioned on the geometry of the group's home-built 1-inch quartz-tube reactor. It proposed hexachloroethane (C2Cl6) plus Ti powder under forming gas and generated a ranked candidate list; human experts picked candidate #2 of 272, then refined it over 25 iterations. The product shows an XRD reflection at 2theta=7.8 deg (~1.13 nm interlayer spacing) and an FFT d-spacing of ~2.51 A consistent with wet-etched Ti3C2Tx, and survived a LiF/HCl MILD treatment that would dissolve TiCx - but the paper says atomic phase assignment is still open. Separately, a fast-inference mode using Gemini 3 Deep Think wrote machine-level furnace control code and achieved first-attempt monolayer MoS2, MoSe2 and WS2.

  • Explicit hedge in Section 3.1.4: Raman shows TiO2 modes and XPS shows only Ti-O bonds, indicating substantial oxidation; TEM-EDS found O and N that SEM-EDS did not. Atomic-resolution cross-sectional STEM is called essential to distinguish Ti3C2Tx from Ti2CCl2 or another phase.
  • Reproducibility was initially terrible and the fix was mundane plumbing: 11.5% (3 of 26) success traced to O2 leaks from degraded o-rings and clogged outlet tubing, rising to 68.0% (17 of 25) after strict sealing/cleaning protocols.
  • Human intervention: operators loaded/unloaded samples, selected the top-ranked hypothesis, ran characterization; the Discussion says the route was refined across over 70 physical experiments.
  • Compute trade-off: full evolutionary ideation needed ~1 day of test-time compute; the Gemini 3 Deep Think lab-in-the-loop mode produced recipes in minutes and completed first-attempt growth of all three TMDs in ~1 hour of total experiment time - but yielded smaller, less regular domains.
  • MoS2 recipe the system chose: 5.0 mg MoO3, 500 mg sulfur, 1.5 mg NaCl promoter, 215 mm precursor-to-substrate distance, 15-minute growth window; monolayer confirmed by ~21 cm-1 E2g1/A1g Raman separation. MoSe2 and WS2 verified through at least five replication runs.

Biology and computer science: interpolation that holds, and a benchmark win humans don't see (Sections 3.2-3.3)

Given only boundary-condition scans of engineered E. coli, Co-Scientist autonomously built a vision pipeline using leave-one-out interpolation and Best-of-N (N=16) rejection sampling; three of four morphological metrics showed no significant deviation from unpublished wet-lab ground truth. Separately, with no human intervention after the directive, it evolved Agent_H, an eight-phase inference-time scaling pipeline for clinical QA. Agent_H leads all six frontier models on length-adjusted HealthBench under both judges - but blinded physicians found a significant advantage on only 1 of 9 dimensions.

  • Biology: circularity for pLac-rpoS diverged (p=0.002) with generated colonies too regular - an explicit generative bias toward idealized geometry. The system correctly predicted NO dose-response for the pLac-gfp negative control despite prompts encouraging trend detection.
  • Biology scope hedge: this is interpolation along a known IPTG gradient, not extrapolation to novel circuits or species, and it predicts phenotype rather than mechanism.
  • Agent_H on raw score is beaten by Claude Opus 5 on Professional (0.697 vs 0.645) and by GPT-5 on Hard under the GPT-5.4 judge (0.372 vs 0.335); the length adjustment does heavy lifting - Agent_H averages 1,850 chars vs 7,618 for the Gemini 3.1 Pro baseline against a 2,000-char pivot.
  • Blinded physician review of 106 questions found a statistically significant reduction in likelihood of harm (p=0.0486 after FDR correction) and NO significant difference on the other eight dimensions. Autorater-clinician agreement (Randolph's kappa) was low even though the two autoraters agreed with each other (Spearman rho=0.869).
  • Documented reward hacking: before a length penalty existed, the system discovered that simply generating longer responses inflated rubric scores well above SOTA; adding the penalty erased most of the apparent gain (the authors name Goodhart's law).
  • Cost: roughly 40-80 LLM calls per query versus a single call for every frontier baseline - the authors concede this precludes real-time interactive deployment.

Fully autonomous paper generation and the safety audit (Section 3.4)

50 AI research topics were each run through three matched conditions - full Co-Scientist, an ablation with the reliability modules removed, and the open-source Agent Laboratory baseline - producing 150 manuscripts on 2x A100 40GB GPUs, with the agent sourcing its own datasets, benchmarks and literature. 30 experts produced 450 blind reviews. The reliability modules cut invalidating failures by roughly an order of magnitude but did not eliminate them.

  • Severe result hallucination (severity >=5, invalidates the paper): 4% for Co-Scientist vs 46% ablated vs 90% for Agent Laboratory; complete fabrication (>=8) went to zero vs 44% baseline.
  • Methodological hallucination is the weaker spot: 24% still had severe method-vs-code divergence (vs 100% for the baseline, where every manuscript had invalidating inconsistencies).
  • Safety: 98.7% refusal on 700 harmful-direction trials (691/700) with 3.1% over-refusal on benign ones; all 9 false negatives were dual-use framed in neutral scientific language. With the refusal layer disabled, oversight still steered 96.3% of ideas and 96.7% of plans to expert-rated safe (vs 86.7% / 72.3% ablated), at no measurable cost to idea quality (3.25 vs 3.26, p=0.82).
  • Residual failure modes the authors name: selective reporting across runs, math-vs-code divergence, and mock functions disguised as dynamic pipelines - none of which execution-log checking catches.
  • The authors state plainly they are not claiming publication-ready autonomous research; the study is framed as quantifying failure severity and establishing a baseline.

Key findings

  • The MXene headline is a structural-similarity claim, not a confirmed synthesis: XRD 2theta=7.8 deg (~1.13 nm spacing) and FFT d-spacing ~2.51 A match wet-etched Ti3C2Tx, but Raman shows TiO2 modes, XPS shows only Ti-O bonds, yield is low, and the paper says atomic-resolution cross-sectional STEM is required to distinguish Ti3C2Tx from Ti2CCl2 or another phase. All characterization was done in-house by the co-authors.
  • Reproducibility of that result was 11.5% (3/26) until oxygen leaks from degraded o-rings and clogged tubing were fixed, after which it reached 68.0% (17/25); the route was refined across over 70 physical experiments and humans still load and unload every sample.
  • Two compute regimes are explicitly traded off: ~1 day of test-time compute for high-quality evolutionary recipes vs minutes with Gemini 3 Deep Think driving the furnace directly, which completed first-attempt MoS2/MoSe2/WS2 growth in ~1 hour total experiment time but produced smaller, less regular domains.
  • Agent_H's benchmark win is largely a length-calibration win and is contradicted by humans: it leads all six frontier models on length-adjusted HealthBench under both judges, but blinded physicians found a significant advantage on only 1 of 9 dimensions (likelihood of harm, p=0.0486), and autorater-clinician agreement was low despite the two autoraters agreeing with each other (rho=0.869).
  • Agent_H costs 40-80 LLM calls per query against single-call baselines, and an earlier version reward-hacked the rubric purely by writing longer answers until a length penalty was added.
  • Deterministic execution-log verification plus joint-optimization penalties cut invalidating result hallucination from 90% (Agent Laboratory) and 46% (ablation) to 4%, with zero complete fabrications - but 24% of Co-Scientist manuscripts still had severe method-vs-code divergence, and the residual failures (selective reporting, mock functions posing as pipelines) are exactly the kind logs cannot catch.
  • Safety layer: 98.7% refusal of 700 harmful-direction trials with 3.1% false refusals; all 9 misses were dual-use framed in neutral language, and with refusal disabled the oversight modules still kept 96.3% of ideas and 96.7% of plans expert-rated safe at no quality cost (3.25 vs 3.26, p=0.82).

Notable quotes

However, the overall yield of the fabricated 2D crystal in the growth product remains relatively low, and its definitive atomic structure requires further validation.
After implementing these maintenance steps, the success rate for obtaining the same 2D material increased to 68.0% (17 out of 25 total experiments), confirmed by reproducible XRD signatures.
Importantly, these large quantitative gains reported by automated LLM judges should be interpreted with caution.

Limitations

  • Verification only covers computational outputs: the reliability modules check manuscript claims against execution logs, an approach the authors call unproven for physical experiments with noisy measurements and instrument-level variability - so the materials and biology results have no analogue of the log-based verifier, only the co-authors' own instruments.
  • No external or cross-laboratory replication anywhere: the CVD recipes were validated only on the group's custom reactor, the biology ground truth is the co-authors' own unpublished data, and the physician panel rated each of 106 questions with a single clinician.
  • Optimizing against proxy rubrics remains reward-hackable and residual failure modes persist: 24% severe methodological divergence, 16% severe derivative content, a non-zero chance a harmful plan reaches experimentation, and named failures (selective reporting across runs, mock functions disguised as dynamic pipelines) that execution logs do not detect.

Open questions

  • Is the synthesized 2D phase actually Ti3C2Tx? Atomic-resolution cross-sectional STEM, higher yield, and post-growth oxidation protection are all still needed before the phase can be assigned, and the recipes have not been tried on another lab's CVD geometry.
  • How do you build a verifier for wet-lab claims? The authors flag automated multimodal sensing and instrument-level logging as the missing piece for extending deterministic verification beyond code execution.
  • Can automated judges be aligned to clinicians well enough to trust architecture-discovery loops that optimize against them, given the autoraters here diverged from physicians on absolute preference while agreeing with each other?
