Calibration Bets on the Past: Post-Training Quantization for Financial Time-Series Forecasting

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-12
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12259

The activation-calibration statistic — not the bit width alone — decides whether a quantized forecaster keeps its alpha: default abs-max W4A4 destroys 11-62% of mean IC on an S&P 500 cross-sectional volatility task (Transformer drops below HAR, SegRNN below naive persistence), while switching to a p99/p99.9 percentile range recovers 53-94%. The financial twist that generalises: activation ranges are calibrated on the past and frozen for the future, so the narrow-range advantage evaporates exactly on the extreme days you care about (1.5-3.6x higher damage outside the calibration envelope).

Executive summary

A systematic study of post-training quantization for cross-sectional volatility forecasting on the S&P 500, covering 7 neural architectures, 8 walk-forward test years (2018-2025) and 560 independently trained models. Eight-bit quantization and weight-only INT4 are essentially free, but static 4-bit activation quantization under the default absolute-maximum calibration removes 11-62% of full-precision mean information coefficient. Swapping abs-max for percentile calibration recovers 53-94% of that loss, though recurrent (SegRNN) and multi-scale (TimeMixer) architectures keep large residual damage. Because activation ranges are estimated on historical data and then frozen, the preferred range shifts with market regime: narrow ranges win in calm periods and lose their advantage precisely on out-of-envelope stress days.

Problem: activation calibration is a deployment bet on history

Weight quantization ranges can be computed directly from trained parameters and are robust. Activation ranges cannot — they must be estimated from a calibration set before deployment and then stay fixed. The paper frames this as a deployment decision rather than a numerical implementation detail, and argues finance is the sharpest test case because calibration always uses past market data while inference happens under future, possibly very different, conditions. Most existing PTQ benchmarks assume calibration and evaluation come from the same distribution.

  • Symmetric quantizer with scale s = A/(2^(b-1)-1); at 4 bits s = A/7 with only 15 representable levels for the whole tensor.
  • Range-resolution trade-off: wide A avoids clipping but coarsens every in-range value; narrow A improves resolution but clips tails.
  • Two calibration statistics compared: abs-max (A = max |x| over the calibration set) vs percentile (A = P_p of |x|).
  • In the walk-forward setup the calibration set is sampled from validation year Y-1 and the range is held fixed for the whole of test year Y.

Data, task and metric

A daily panel of the 501 current S&P 500 constituents from June 2008 to December 2025, roughly 2.1 million asset-days, from Yahoo Finance adjusted closes with five renamed tickers backfilled from Tiingo. Each stock gets eleven features (daily return; 5/10/20-day mean returns; 20-day volatility; 14-day RSI; MACD plus signal; 20-day max, min and skewness of returns), standardized using training-split statistics only. Each sample is a 64-day history for a single stock.

  • Target: within-date cross-sectional z-score of log five-day-forward realized volatility.
  • Metric: daily information coefficient (cross-sectional Spearman correlation), averaged over test dates.
  • Volatility rather than return is chosen deliberately because the stronger signal makes small quantization effects measurable.
  • Classical baselines: persistence forecast (FP32 IC 0.315) and a pooled HAR model (FP32 IC 0.408).

Experimental protocol (walk-forward, leakage controls, PTQ settings)

Fixed-length walk-forward: for test year Y, train on Y-8..Y-2, validate on Y-1, test on Y; test years 2018-2025 give 8 folds. Seven architectures x 10 seeds x 8 folds = 560 independent training runs. Hyperparameters are chosen once on a development pseudo-fold and frozen. Training is AdamW, batch 1024, MSE loss, early stopping on validation MSE with patience 20.

  • Architectures: DLinear, TSMixer, TimeMixer, vanilla Transformer, PatchTST, iTransformer, SegRNN.
  • Leakage control: a sample is assigned to a split by the final date of its five-day outcome window, not by the forecast date.
  • Precision treatments: FP32; W8A8 static; weight-only W4; W4A4 static; Dynamic INT8 as a reference.
  • Calibration sample: 1,024 sequence windows drawn uniformly from the validation split before each test year.
  • Coverage: every learned matmul plus its input activation, including conv token embeddings and unrolled GRU steps. Biases, norms, nonlinearities and attention activation-activation products stay FP32.
  • Execution is simulated quantization (quantize-dequantize, FP32 accumulate) — no real INT4 kernels.
  • Damage metric: paired Delta = IC_FP32 - IC_quantized, reported descriptively with no significance testing.

Main results: 8-bit is free, static 4-bit activations are not

Dynamic INT8, static W8A8 and weight-only W4 preserve nearly all skill. Under W8A8, six of seven architectures lose no more than 0.0007 mean daily IC. Weight-only W4 costs at most about 2.5%. Static W4A4 under abs-max is where everything breaks: PatchTST -11%, iTransformer -12%, TSMixer -20%, Transformer -25%, and roughly -60% for both TimeMixer and SegRNN.

  • Transformer falls from FP32 IC 0.490 to 0.367 under default W4A4 — below the HAR baseline (0.408).
  • SegRNN falls from 0.458 to 0.187 — below the 0.315 persistence baseline.
  • The best FP32 neural models beat HAR by about 0.08 IC, while default W4A4 damage is 0.096 (TSMixer) and 0.123 (Transformer): quantization erases the entire edge over a 2009 econometric baseline.
  • Damage does not follow architecture family — attention and mixer models appear at several points in the damage ordering.
  • DLinear acts as a low-signal control: near-zero FP32 IC and near-zero change.

Why abs-max fails, and how much is recoverable

A range sweep over the four worst architectures shows abs-max sits on the high-damage side of the curve; damage falls as the range narrows, bottoming between p98 and p99.9, then rises sharply at the final step to abs-max for TSMixer, Transformer and SegRNN. The paper decomposes abs-max damage into a range-recoverable share and a residual, and flags that these are an upper bound because the best percentile is picked using the same test-year outcomes it is scored on — then tests a deployable version.

  • Range-recoverable share: Transformer 94%, TSMixer 80%, SegRNN 73%, TimeMixer 53%.
  • Transformer damage falls +0.123 -> +0.007 at the best tested percentile; TSMixer +0.096 -> +0.019.
  • Layerwise attribution: quantizing only the Transformer's convolutional token embedding reproduces 72% of full-model W4A4 damage; keeping just that layer at 8 bits cuts damage from +0.123 to +0.015.
  • TSMixer has no single-layer bottleneck — protecting any individual layer still leaves damage near +0.086.
  • Deployable rule (no hindsight): pick the percentile with the smallest validation-year damage. Mean regret 0.007 IC, median 0.002 IC vs the oracle, matching or beating a fixed p99 rule in 27 of 32 architecture-fold cells; worst case SegRNN 2021 at 0.057 IC regret.

Market-regime sensitivity: the distribution-shift argument, made concrete

The shift is measured with the daily cross-sectional standard deviation of stock returns against a calibration envelope equal to the maximum observed in the preceding validation year. 2020 repeatedly breaks above its envelope; 2021 sits well below it because the envelope still reflects 2020's extremes. The two directions of mismatch produce opposite failure modes.

  • Test year more volatile than calibration year (2020, 2022): narrow percentiles cost more because they clip tails the calibration period underestimated.
  • Test year calmer than calibration year (2021): abs-max becomes the costliest setting.
  • Matched recalibration diagnostic on the 2020 fold: TSMixer p99 damage +0.077 -> +0.043 (-44%), SegRNN +0.164 -> +0.119 (-27%) — mismatch explains roughly a quarter to a half of the excess damage; the rest is irreducible clipping.
  • Under p99, damage outside the calibration envelope is 1.5-3.6x larger than inside for all four sensitive architectures; abs-max does not carry this penalty (ratios 0.8-1.4).
  • Proposed deployment procedure: measure default W4A4 damage, sweep percentiles on pre-deployment data, add a layerwise check, fall back to Dyn8/W8A8/W4 when residual damage is large, and use out-of-envelope days as a live recalibration trigger.

Key findings

  • Static W8A8, dynamic INT8 and weight-only INT4 are effectively free: six of seven architectures lose <= 0.0007 mean daily IC under W8A8, and no architecture loses more than ~2.5% of FP32 IC under weight-only W4.
  • Static W4A4 with default abs-max calibration removes 11-62% of full-precision mean IC; Transformer drops to 0.367 (below HAR at 0.408) and SegRNN to 0.187 (below persistence at 0.315).
  • Only the calibration statistic changes between abs-max and percentile, and that alone recovers 94% (Transformer), 80% (TSMixer), 73% (SegRNN) and 53% (TimeMixer) of the abs-max damage.
  • Damage is not predictable from architecture family — attention models occupy several positions in the damage ordering, as do mixer models.
  • One layer can dominate: quantizing only the Transformer's convolutional token embedding reproduces 72% of full-model damage, and keeping that layer at 8 bits cuts damage from +0.123 to +0.015. TSMixer has no such bottleneck.
  • The percentile advantage is regime-conditional: under p99, damage outside the calibration envelope is 1.5-3.6x the inside-envelope damage, while abs-max shows ratios of 0.8-1.4.
  • A hindsight-free rule (minimize validation-year damage) has mean regret 0.007 IC / median 0.002 IC vs the oracle and beats or matches a fixed p99 in 27 of 32 architecture-fold cells.

Notable quotes

Because input windows only reach backward while target windows are never split across a boundary, validation-year early stopping never has access to any test-year return, whether as a feature or as a label.
These differences do not follow a simple architecture taxonomy: attention-based models occupy several positions in the damage ordering, as do mixer-based models, so architecture family alone is insufficient to predict robustness under static W4A4.
The curve also separates two cases. For TSMixer and Transformer, most of the default W4A4 failure is range-recoverable. For SegRNN and TimeMixer, changing the range helps but does not solve the problem.
The narrow range that helps on typical days clips more heavily once conditions turn extreme. Its advantage disappears when reliability matters most, on the unusual market days a deployed model is least prepared for.

Limitations

  • Quantization is simulated in FP32, so the paper measures the predictive cost of range selection but no actual latency, memory or backend accumulation effects — there is no hardware validation.
  • Reported IC levels are survivorship-tilted because the panel uses the 501 CURRENT S&P 500 constituents; the authors argue the bias cancels since quantized models are compared against their own FP32 checkpoints on identical stocks and dates.
  • Only symmetric round-to-nearest quantization with per-tensor activation ranges is studied; stronger PTQ methods (QuaRot-style rotations, SmoothQuant-style channel scaling) are untested. Eight test years contain only two major stress episodes, and results are reported descriptively with no significance testing.
  • The headline recoverable-share figures are an explicit upper bound — the best percentile is selected using the same test-year outcomes it is evaluated on (a hindsight-free variant is validated separately).

Open questions

  • Do stronger PTQ techniques eliminate the residual, non-range-recoverable damage in recurrent and multi-scale architectures, or is that damage intrinsic to their activation distributions?
  • Does the calibration-envelope signal translate into a working online policy — how often should ranges be recalibrated, and does dynamic-range inference beat any static percentile once runtime cost is priced in?
  • Do the findings hold beyond US equity cross-sectional volatility — other markets, return targets, portfolio-level P&L rather than IC, and real INT4 hardware?
