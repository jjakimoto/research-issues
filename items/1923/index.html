    Deep Learning Statistical Arbitrage

    ← Go backPaperQA: Retrieval-Augmented Generative Agent for Scientific…NextDeep Learning in Characteristics-Sorted Factor Models →
    📚 Foundations · 2026-10-02
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-02

    Theme: From Characteristics to Tradable Residuals
    These papers make learned cross-sectional structure directly tradable rather than treating machine learning as a standalone return forecaster. One constructs nonlinear characteristic-sorted factors and conditional betas; the other removes common factor exposures and learns temporal allocations over the resulting residual portfolios. Together they connect representation learning to economic objectives, portfolio construction, and causal out-of-sample evaluation.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04028

    Shows how conditional latent-factor residuals create tradable, weakly dependent cross-sectional portfolios whose temporal structure can be converted into allocations by a convolutional transformer.

    Executive summary

    The paper recasts statistical arbitrage as three jointly optimized components: constructing tradable residual portfolios, extracting temporal signals, and allocating capital under a risk-adjusted trading objective. It combines Fama-French, PCA, or characteristic-conditioned IPCA residuals with CNN local filters, transformer attention, and a feedforward allocation network. On roughly 550 liquid U.S. stocks, its IPCA five-factor specification achieves an out-of-sample annualized Sharpe ratio of 4.16 before costs and remains profitable under the paper's simplified transaction- and holding-cost model.

    Problem and economic structure

    Statistical arbitrage is decomposed into portfolio generation, signal extraction, and allocation. A conditional factor model defines each arbitrage portfolio as a stock return minus its factor-mimicking portfolio, making the residual itself a tradable relative-value position with systematic factor exposure removed.

    • Similarity is defined through common systematic-risk exposure rather than pairwise stock matching.
    • PCA residuals compare a stock with a portfolio of historically correlated stocks; IPCA residuals additionally condition similarity on 46 firm characteristics.
    • With a sufficiently rich factor model, residuals are close to cross-sectionally uncorrelated and have near-zero unconditional means, making shared time-series signal estimation more plausible.

    Signal and allocation architecture

    The model integrates the last 30 cumulative residual returns into a residual-price sequence. A two-layer CNN learns local trend and reversal filters, a one-layer transformer with four attention heads combines them into global dependency patterns, and a feedforward network maps the resulting signal into a residual allocation.

    • The benchmark CNN has eight filters with a two-day filter size; its stacked structure captures neighboring three-point patterns.
    • The transformer provides a learned alternative to fixed Fourier bases and the parametric Ornstein-Uhlenbeck signal.
    • CNN, transformer, and allocation parameters are trained jointly using either a Sharpe-ratio or mean-variance trading objective rather than a return-prediction loss.
    • Final stock weights are obtained by mapping residual positions back through the factor-residual construction and normalizing their absolute sum to one.

    Out-of-sample design

    The study uses CRSP daily equity returns through 2016, retaining stocks whose prior-month capitalization exceeds 0.01% of aggregate market capitalization—about 550 liquid stocks on average. Residuals cover 1998–2016, while trading performance is evaluated from January 2002 through December 2016 after an initial four-year training window.

    • Fama-French loadings use rolling 60-day regressions; PCA uses a 252-day correlation window and 60-day loading regressions; IPCA uses 240 months of returns and characteristics and is re-estimated annually.
    • Deep models use rolling 1,000-day training windows and are re-estimated every 125 days.
    • Benchmarks are a Fourier signal with a feedforward allocation network and a parametric Ornstein-Uhlenbeck signal with a threshold policy.
    • All reported trading residuals and portfolio returns are generated out of sample.

    Performance and ablations

    The CNN+Transformer consistently dominates both signal benchmarks, while the precise factor family matters less once enough common variation has been removed. Ablations show that a flexible allocation network cannot compensate for an information-poor OU signal or for feeding raw residual histories to an ordinary feedforward network without an explicit time-series model.

    • For five IPCA factors, CNN+Transformer reaches a 4.16 Sharpe ratio versus 1.90 for Fourier+FFN and 0.97 for OU+Threshold.
    • For five PCA factors, CNN+Transformer produces a 14.3% annual mean return with 4.2% volatility and a 3.36 Sharpe ratio.
    • Five-factor IPCA CNN+Transformer returns have an annualized eight-factor alpha of 8.3%, a t-statistic of 16, and an R-squared of 3.9%.
    • Using raw stock returns instead of factor residuals materially weakens performance, demonstrating that cross-sectional de-factoring is part of the signal-design problem.

    Robustness, frictions, and interpretation

    The learned strategy remains economically meaningful after imposing five basis points of turnover cost and one basis point of daily short-position holding cost, though these are deliberately simplified frictions. Attention and gradient analyses suggest that the model builds allocations from smooth local trends and reversals, reacts faster to downturns than uptrends, and emphasizes roughly the most recent two weeks.

    • With trading frictions, the three-factor IPCA strategy has a 1.24 annualized Sharpe ratio, 6.6% mean return, and 5.4% volatility under the Sharpe objective.
    • The signal's Sharpe-ratio half-life is about seven trading days; overlapping one-month holding strategies retain a Sharpe ratio above one.
    • Strategies derived from different factor families have correlations of roughly 0.2–0.45, offering diversification despite similar standalone performance.
    • Sparse portfolios using only 10% of stocks retain Sharpe ratios above two, while roughly 25 stocks—the largest 5% of weights—produce a 14% mean return in the reported exercise.

    Key findings

    • Cross-sectional de-factoring is essential: applying the same models directly to stock returns performs substantially worse than applying them to residual portfolios.
    • The five-factor IPCA CNN+Transformer achieves a 4.16 out-of-sample Sharpe ratio, compared with 1.90 for Fourier+FFN and 0.97 for OU+Threshold.
    • Signal extraction is the decisive component: flexible allocation alone does not rescue a weak parametric signal, while the learned temporal filter approximately doubles performance over a fixed Fourier representation.
    • The profitable returns are largely distinct from conventional factor premia; the five-factor IPCA strategy reports an 8.3% annualized eight-factor alpha with a t-statistic of 16.
    • Under the stated turnover and short-holding costs, the IPCA strategy remains profitable, reaching a 1.24 Sharpe ratio for three factors.
    • Arbitrage signals decay but persist: their Sharpe-ratio half-life is about seven trading days, and meaningful performance remains at monthly holding horizons.

    Notable quotes

    The CNN can be interpreted as a set of data driven flexible local filters.
    Second, the CNN+Transformer model strongly dominates the other benchmark models in terms of Sharpe ratio and average return.
    The half-life in terms of Sharpe ratios is around seven trading days.
    Importantly, the arbitrage strategies retain their economic significance even in the presence of trading costs.

    Limitations

    • The evidence is a historical U.S.-equity backtest ending in 2016 and restricted to roughly 550 highly liquid large-cap stocks, so post-2016 and broader-market generalization is not tested.
    • The friction model uses universal five-basis-point transaction costs and one-basis-point short holding costs and explicitly omits market impact and stock- or time-varying costs.
    • Friction-aware training approximates recursive prior allocations using the preceding optimization epoch to preserve parallelism; the authors note that this can be suboptimal and do not retune the main hyperparameters for the friction setting.

    Open questions

    • Can latent-factor construction be trained jointly with the trading-friction objective, perhaps through sparse factor portfolios or ETF-based mimicking portfolios?
    • Would exact sequential training or a richer non-Markovian allocation policy materially improve performance after transaction and holding costs?
    • Which mechanism principally explains signal persistence: limited arbitrage capital, strategic control of market impact and information leakage, or behavioral trading by noise and uninformed investors?
