Ideation Arena: Evaluating LLM Generated Research Ideas with Battle-style Human Expert Assessment

📰 Latest · 2026-09-05 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-05

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-30
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29696

105 CS researchers cast 6,000+ double-blind pairwise votes on research proposals from 14 frontier LLMs and 5 agent frameworks, producing an Elo leaderboard where agent scaffolds help or hurt almost at random (+177 Elo for one, -155 for another). The companion meta-evaluation is the sting: the best LLM judge reaches only 72.56% Soft Accuracy against expert preference, feasibility judgments collapse to near-chance, and GPT-4o flips its verdict on 55% of order-swapped pairs.

Executive summary

Ideation Arena is a battle-style human evaluation platform for LLM-generated research ideas. Every system is handed the identical closed context — the concatenated titles and abstracts of a real seed paper's references, with retrieval and outside knowledge disabled — and asked to produce a proposal; experts then compare two anonymized proposals head-to-head on Novelty, Feasibility, Significance, Specificity and Overall Quality. From 6,000+ votes by 105 active CS researchers the authors fit a Bradley-Terry model and publish a 24-system Elo leaderboard, showing agent frameworks are wildly inconsistent: AI-Researcher lifts DeepSeek V3.2 by +177 Elo (7th to 2nd) at ~7x token cost, while MOOSE-Chem drags GPT-4o from 829.9 down to 675.2. The second half, Ideation Arena-Eval, turns the human votes into a benchmark for automated judges and finds all 14 tested LLM judges wanting: 59.52%-72.56% Soft Accuracy on Overall Quality against a 50.57% marginal-distribution baseline.

Closed-context retrospective design (Sec. 3)

Rather than free-form topics, the authors reconstruct the discovery context of a real paper. Participants supplied DOIs plus self-selected fields under an AAAI-derived taxonomy (8 primary areas, 53 secondary sub-fields); Semantic Scholar retrieval over the last five years built a candidate pool, then a three-stage filter kept only papers with >=10 citations, dropped surveys/benchmarks/evaluation-only work, and required >=10 references. For each surviving seed paper the reference list's titles and abstracts are concatenated into a single context C that defines 'the boundary of observable knowledge', and the model must propose the research direction from C alone.

  • Final pool: 2,191 unique seed-paper contexts across 8 primary CS areas
  • Largest areas: NLP 593 (27.1%), Computer Vision 497 (22.7%), ML Methods and Theory Reasoning 492 (22.5%)
  • Largest secondary subfield: Large Language Models 290 (13.2%)
  • Agents keep reasoning / iterative-refinement / proposal-generation modules but lose retrieval and pre-built knowledge bases

Arena protocol and the 105-expert panel (Sec. 4.1, 4.3)

Two proposals from distinct systems for the same seed task are randomly assigned to slots A and B and shown without system identity. Each battle routes to an expert whose self-reported expertise matches the seed-paper topic; the expert rates five dimensions and returns one of four verdicts — A Wins, B Wins, Tie, Both Bad — with no external lookup allowed. The 105-person panel spans PhD students, postdocs, faculty and industry researchers, recruited via academic social media, screened on active research experience, and paid based on expected workload.

  • Over 6,000 valid pairwise votes collected
  • 1,675 queries / 3,888 battles got >=2 independent reviews, forming the agreement subset
  • Self-reported area coverage is LLM-heavy: 89 of 105 annotators claim NLP, 81 claim LLMs; only 16 claim Planning and Symbolic AI
  • Ties count as half votes to both sides in the agreement computation

Rating math and reliability (Sec. 4.2, 4.3)

Ratings come from a Bradley-Terry model with a logistic link fit by maximum likelihood over outcomes y in {0, 0.5, 1}; both 'Tie' and 'Both Bad' are folded into 0.5. The fitted abilities are rescaled into conventional Elo numbers, and uncertainty comes from a 1,000-resample bootstrap giving 95% CIs. No K-factor, base rating or scale divisor is disclosed. Inter-annotator agreement sits near the level reported by prior expert ideation studies, and the leaderboard survives resampling of the annotator pool.

  • Krippendorff's alpha hovers around 0.59-0.61 on all five dimensions; pairwise agreement 53.8%-56.8%
  • 50% annotator subsampling over 200 runs: mean Spearman 0.980 with the full leaderboard
  • Leave-one-domain-out re-estimation: mean Spearman 0.998
  • The AI-Researcher (DeepSeek V3.2) CI is disjoint from its backbone's, but the top tier only 'marginally intersects' internally — no numeric CIs are printed, only an error-bar figure

Leaderboard: agent scaffolds are a coin flip (Sec. 5.1)

Four systems clear 1200 Elo on Overall Quality: GPT-5.1 (1293.8), AI-Researcher on DeepSeek V3.2 (1276.5), Claude 4.5 Opus (1221.2), Claude 4.5 Sonnet (1220.7). Kimi K2 (Thinking) is the best pure open-source model at rank 5. The agent story cuts both ways: AI-Researcher moves DeepSeek V3.2 from 7th (1099.5) to 2nd (1276.5), +177 points, but at 99,067.8 tokens/query versus 14,860.3 for the bare model, while ResearchAgent burns the most tokens of anything (119,009.0) and lands 17th, and MOOSE-Chem on GPT-4o finishes last at 675.2, well below the 829.9 of plain GPT-4o.

  • Elo correlates across the five dimensions at r > 0.95, so the dimensions are not very discriminative
  • In near-equal battles (rating gap <= 100) experts pick the longer proposal 52.8%-58.7% of the time
  • Length-adjusted Bradley-Terry rankings correlate with the originals at only Spearman 0.7504-0.8835
  • Seed-proximity (SPECTER2 similarity to the actual seed paper) is essentially uncorrelated with rank: Spearman -0.041 vs Overall Quality Elo, -0.082 vs Novelty Elo
  • Cross-domain pilot: 4 experts each in Biology and Physics, ~50 comparisons apiece; GPT-5.1 again leads with 1237.6 and 1375.6

Meta-evaluation: LLM judges vs. human preference (Sec. 6)

Ideation Arena-Eval reframes the 6,000+ annotated pairs as a ternary classification task scored by Soft Accuracy, where a ground-truth tie awards 0.5 to both candidates. Because tie rates differ by dimension (10.62% for Overall Quality up to 19.24% for Significance), the random baseline is not 50% but 50.57%-51.85%. Fourteen frontier models were run as judges. The best, Gemini 3 Pro Preview, reaches 72.56% on Overall Quality; the worst, GPT-4o, 59.52%. Feasibility is the hardest dimension — only three judges clear 60%, and OpenAI o3 gets 54.88%, roughly 3 points over chance. Judge skill and ideation skill are decoupled: GPT-5.1 tops the ideation leaderboard but is only the 9th-best judge, while Gemini 3 Pro Preview is the best judge yet ranks 10th as an ideator.

  • Soft Accuracy is the only metric; no hard/strict accuracy variant is defined
  • Overall Quality span 59.52%-72.56%, i.e. 9-22 points over a ~50.6% baseline
  • The authors' verdict: LLM judges 'remain unreliable substitutes for expert preference labels on this benchmark'
  • The paper does not directly compare judge Soft Accuracy against the 56.8% human-human pairwise agreement — the two are computed on different targets

Position and verbosity bias in the judges (Sec. 6.2)

The authors rerun 500 comparisons per judge with A and B swapped, for 11 of the 14 judges (Gemini 3 Pro Preview, Grok 4 and Grok 4 Fast were dropped for API unavailability — which excludes the top scorer). Aggregate rankings are stable across orders (Spearman 0.942), but individual consistency varies enormously: ten judges land between 70.6% and 92.5%, while GPT-4o agrees with itself on only 45.0% of swapped pairs — meaning its Overall Quality verdicts are dominated by slot position, not content. Verbosity bias is measured separately: across comparable-strength battles judges pick the longer proposal 50.9%-62.0% of the time.

  • Order-swap consistency leaders: Qwen3-Max 92.5%, Claude 4.5 Sonnet 90.4%, Claude 4.5 Opus 88.1%
  • Order-swap laggards: Mistral Large 2512 70.6%, DeepSeek V3.2 71.3%, GPT-4o 45.0%
  • Order-averaged Soft Accuracy reshuffles the ranking: Claude 4.5 Opus becomes best at 66.8% average
  • Longer-proposal selection rate: Significance 62.0%, Overall Quality 58.9%, Novelty 58.8%, Specificity 58.1%, Feasibility 50.9%
  • Human experts show the same directional bias but milder (52.8%-58.7% in near-equal battles)

Key findings

  • Scale and panel: over 6,000 double-blind pairwise votes from 105 active CS researchers; 1,675 queries / 3,888 battles were double-annotated. Krippendorff's alpha is ~0.59-0.61 with pairwise agreement of only 53.8%-56.8% — expert preference on research ideas is genuinely noisy, which caps what any judge can reproduce.
  • The leaderboard is stable but the dimensions are not independent: 50% annotator subsampling gives mean Spearman 0.980 and leave-one-domain-out gives 0.998, yet Elo across the five dimensions correlates at r > 0.95, so Novelty/Feasibility/Significance/Specificity largely track one global 'quality' factor.
  • Agent scaffolds are architecture-dependent, not uniformly helpful: AI-Researcher lifts DeepSeek V3.2 from 7th (1099.5) to 2nd (1276.5), +177 Elo with disjoint bootstrap CIs, at ~7x tokens; ResearchAgent spends the most tokens of any system (119,009) for 17th place, and MOOSE-Chem drops GPT-4o from 829.9 to 675.2.
  • LLM judges do not reproduce expert preference: Overall Quality Soft Accuracy spans 59.52% to 72.56% against a marginal-distribution baseline of 50.57%. Feasibility is worst — only three of 14 judges exceed 60%, and OpenAI o3 manages 54.88%, about 3 points over chance.
  • Being a good ideator does not make a good judge: GPT-5.1 is #1 on the Elo leaderboard but only the 9th-best judge (64.29%), while Gemini 3 Pro Preview is the best judge (72.56%) yet ranks 10th as an idea generator.
  • Position and verbosity bias are large and unevenly distributed: per-judge order-swap consistency runs 70.6%-92.5% for ten judges and just 45.0% for GPT-4o. Judges pick the longer proposal in 50.9%-62.0% of decisive predictions, and human experts do the same at 52.8%-58.7%; length-adjusted rankings correlate only 0.7504-0.8835 with the published ones.

Notable quotes

Due to domain overlaps in our assignment mechanism, 1,675 queries, corresponding to 3,888 pairwise battles, were independently reviewed by at least two Expert Panel members.
The re-estimated leaderboards remain highly consistent with the full leaderboard under both 50% annotator subsampling over 200 runs and leave-one-domain-out re-estimation, with average Spearman correlations of 0.980 and 0.998, respectively.
These results show that current LLM judges remain unreliable substitutes for expert preference labels on this benchmark.
The ten judges other than GPT-4o have Overall Quality order-swap consistency between 70.6% and 92.5%; GPT-4o has 45.0% consistency.

Limitations

  • The authors' own caveat: the leaderboard is 'inherently time-sensitive' — a snapshot of specific model and agent versions — and the main benchmark covers only computer science, with Biology and Physics reduced to a 4-expert, ~50-comparison pilot whose rank correlation with the CS leaderboard is never reported.
  • The evaluation measures proposal-stage preference only: nothing is executed or empirically verified, and the authors concede that under blind review formal depth and logical consistency significantly influence perceived scientific value. Combined with the measured length effect, part of the Elo signal is presentation rather than science.
  • The judge bias rerun excludes exactly the three judges that matter most for the headline claim — Gemini 3 Pro Preview, Grok 4 and Grok 4 Fast were dropped for API unavailability — so the best judge's 72.56% is never tested for position bias. No numeric confidence intervals are printed, only an error-bar figure, and the Elo rescaling constants are not disclosed.

Open questions

  • How should a 72.56% judge Soft Accuracy be read against 56.8% human-human pairwise agreement? The paper never puts the two side by side, and they are computed against different targets, so the apparent 'judges beat humans' reading is not licensed — but the true human ceiling remains unquantified.
  • Do the closed-context constraints (retrieval disabled) systematically penalise agent frameworks whose value comes from search and knowledge-base construction? The counterfactual — the same agents with retrieval on — is not measured, so 'agent frameworks are inconsistent' may partly be an artefact of the ablation.
  • Given that Elo correlates at r > 0.95 across all five dimensions and seed-proximity is uncorrelated with rank (-0.041 / -0.082), does the arena actually measure novelty at all, or a single presentation-weighted quality factor? A judge or leaderboard tuned on this signal would inherit that conflation.
