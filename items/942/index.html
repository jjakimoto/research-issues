M3: A State-Event Generative Foundation Model for Market Microstructure Dynamics

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-07-29
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19227

The first order-level market simulator to put limit-order-book state and order events in ONE token space and fit a Chinchilla-style scaling law to it (5 sizes up to 1.27B params, 31.9B order events of CSI 300/500 data) — and its closed-loop TWAP interventions recover square-root impact exponents of 0.532/0.524 as an emergent property. A scaling-law-backed counterfactual sandbox for execution research, but with weak baselines and no P&L accounting.

Executive summary

M3 (Market Microstructure Model) jointly represents LOB snapshots and raw order events as one token sequence rather than treating one as conditioning for the other. A VQ-VAE order tokenizer (K=32,768 codebook, prices anchored to the DAILY OPEN rather than the rolling mid to avoid a training/inference anchor-drift feedback loop) feeds a LLaMA2-style decoder-only Transformer whose prefix is a cross-attention-pooled encoding of the initial 2x10-level book; generated tokens are decoded to executable orders and replayed through a deterministic matching engine that produces the next LOB state. Trained on ~31.9B order events across 800 CSI 300 + CSI 500 constituents (Jan 2024 - Nov 2025, held-out Dec 2025), M3 exhibits a fitted Chinchilla-style loss surface that extrapolates to a held-out 1.27B model within 0.53%, beats Zero-Intelligence and Compound-Hawkes baselines on return-distribution fidelity at 10-60s, and produces concave TWAP market impact with log-log slopes of 0.532 (buy) and 0.524 (sell). The honest caveats are large: baselines are weak by the authors' own admission (TradeFM/MarS weights unreleased), fidelity DEGRADES relative to ZI at the 120s horizon, and the predictive results are explicitly framed as ranking diagnostics, not strategies.

The modelling gap: state and event as one modality, not two

Prior order-level generative models (Nagy et al. 2023; LOBDIF; MarS; TradeFM) pick either the LOB state or the order flow as the primary representation and demote the other to auxiliary conditioning, so the coupled feedback loop — orders consume/replenish depth, and the resulting book shapes the execution and impact of the next order — is never explicitly modelled. M3 frames the market as an event-driven state-transition system and splits the labour: the Transformer learns state-conditioned order-flow dynamics, while a deterministic matching engine realises the event-induced state transitions. The authors describe the design as analogous to a vision-language model, with the LOB encoder playing the role of the vision tower.

  • Order event = [action (add/cancel), side, price, volume, arrival time]; limit vs market orders are NOT distinguished — all events live in one unified event space.
  • Event feature vector is [open-relative price, log1p(volume), inter-arrival delta-t, action, side]; LOB state is the top d_L = 10 levels per side (2 x 10 x 2 tensor of price and volume).
  • The LOB is encoded ONCE at the start of the rollout, not recurrently re-encoded, on the argument that the realised book at every step is a deterministic function of the initial book plus the decoded events.
  • Positioned explicitly as world modelling (Ha and Schmidhuber 2018) for markets: generate many trajectories, not one next outcome.

Tokenizer: why the price anchor is the whole ballgame

The paper's sharpest methodological point is that mid-relative price discretization — the standard choice in MarS and TradeFM — creates a training-inference mismatch. At training time the true mid-price is the anchor; at generation time the mid must be recovered by replaying previously generated (and lossily reconstructed) orders through the simulated book, so reconstruction error perturbs the anchor and compounds along the trajectory. Recentring on the current mid also destroys global intraday position information. M3 therefore anchors to the daily open price, which is identical and fixed in both regimes, and absorbs the resulting wider, heavier-tailed price distribution with a learned vector-quantized codebook instead of uniform bins.

  • Tokenizer: symmetric 3-layer Transformer encoder / 3-layer decoder, ~15.2M params, d_model = 384, d_ff = 1536, 6 heads, Pre-LN + GELU, 128-dim bottleneck, K = 32,768 codes.
  • Codebook trained by EMA (decay 0.99) after 10 iterations of k-means init; commitment loss beta = 0.25; dead codes (EMA cluster size < 2) resampled from the current batch; gradients use the ROTATION-TRICK estimator, not identity straight-through.
  • Composite loss = weighted-MSE continuous reconstruction (asymmetric dimension weights e.g. [100, 1, 1] to prioritise price) + cross-entropy on action/side + VQ penalties + a domain-specific 'Open Tick Loss' (Huber, lambda_tick = 0.001) pushing reconstructed prices onto valid tick intervals.
  • Codebook health: 100% cumulative utilization (all 32,768 codes selected at some point), 22,696 codes (69.3%) per minibatch, code perplexity 16,815 — no collapse.
  • Inputs clipped at the 0.5th/99.5th percentiles; fixed 1,024-token sliding window for both tokenizer and AR training.

Backbone, LOB prefix, and the matching engine

The autoregressive model is a standard decoder-only LLaMA2-style Transformer (RoPE, pre-norm causal MHA, SwiGLU/SiLU gated FFN, final RMSNorm, untied embeddings, full MHA rather than GQA) over the 32,768-token order vocabulary. The LOB prefix encoder normalizes each of the 20 book positions to [mid-relative price clipped at +/-0.20, log1p(volume), validity flag], adds side and level embeddings plus a projected global state vector (normalized spread, time since open, return from open), runs a 2-layer / 4-head Transformer encoder over the 20 tokens, then compresses to P learned prefix embeddings via cross-attention pooling. Training is plain next-token prediction with labels masked at the prefix and BOS positions.

  • Five scales trained: tiny 10M, small 25M, base 75M, large 366M, xlarge 1.27B (hidden 128 -> 2048, layers 3 -> 20, heads 4 -> 16); all share vocab 32,768.
  • Matching engine is deliberately lightweight: aggregate volume per discrete price level, tick delta = 0.01, prices reconstructed from the OPEN anchor and rounded to nearest tick, volumes to nearest lot, add/cancel event space only, partial fills and multi-level walks allowed, unexecuted residual posted at the limit.
  • Explicit engine limitation stated by the authors: price priority across levels is enforced but there are NO order identifiers and NO FIFO priority within a level; unmatched cancellation quantity is recorded but does not touch other levels.
  • Long-horizon rollouts keep the LOB prefix pinned and evict only the oldest order-event tokens when context overflows.

Scaling law: the most transferable result

On a full 5 x 4 grid (five sizes: 9.66M, 24.99M, 74.75M, 366.06M, 1.27B; four token budgets: 1B, 3B, 10B, 31.9B) the authors fit a Chinchilla parameterisation L(N, D) = 5.3697 + 1.5987 / N^0.3795 + 1.0027 / D^0.3117 (N in millions of params, D in billions of tokens) using a Huber objective on log-loss residuals (delta = 1e-3). Validity is checked by leave-one-model-size-out refits and a 2,000-replicate fixed-design residual bootstrap, which is more diligence than most domain scaling-law claims get.

  • Mean absolute relative error 0.66% across the 20 held-out predictions; per-fold MAPEs 1.14% / 0.40% / 0.56% / 0.70% / 0.53% for held-out 9.66M / 24.99M / 74.75M / 366.06M / 1.27B.
  • Strict upward extrapolation: fitting only up to 366.06M predicts the 1.27B runs with 0.53% MAPE and 1.12% max error (signed errors +1.12%, -0.11%, -0.27%, +0.60% at 1B / 3B / 10B / 31.9B tokens).
  • Bootstrap 90% CIs: alpha = 0.379 [0.281, 0.485], beta = 0.312 [0.211, 0.423], E = 5.370 [5.065, 5.529]; 95% CIs alpha [0.259, 0.510], beta [0.193, 0.456], E [4.972, 5.562].
  • Authors flag that these intervals do NOT capture run-to-run training stochasticity — only one loss observation exists per configuration.

Downstream evaluation: stylized facts, prediction, and closed-loop impact

All price-based evaluation goes through one common protocol: initialise the matching engine from the observed LOB snapshot at the start of the sequence and replay events chronologically. Stylized facts are benchmarked against a Zero-Intelligence sampler (factorized marginals, exponential inter-arrival and volume, 5-component GMM depth) and a 4-type Compound Hawkes Process (buy/sell x add/delete, 4 exponential kernels with half-lives 0.05 / 0.5 / 5 / 60 s). Prediction is a ranking diagnostic over terciles of ensemble-forecast return. The impact test is genuinely closed-loop: intervention fills are re-tokenized and appended to the AR context so background flow reacts.

  • Prediction setup: horizons 0.5 / 1 / 3 / 5 minutes; prompts ranked into Down/Flat/Up terciles; 'tail directional accuracy' scores only the two extreme predicted groups against a 50% naive benchmark. LOB conditioning beats both a no-LOB ablation and DeepLOB.
  • Volatility: Monte Carlo realized-variance forecasts (1-second previous-tick grid) sorted into quintiles; realized volatility rises monotonically Q1 -> Q5.
  • Directional stress test: buy-side add events multiplied by m in {5, 10, 20} (sell symmetric); mid-price displacement is signed correctly and SUBLINEAR in intervention strength.
  • TWAP: participation rates 0.10% to 1.00% (11-point grid), 64 liquidity-matched prompt windows, 4 rollouts each, 300s horizon split into 20 child orders at 15s intervals, buys as aggressive limits at best ask + 10 ticks; impact normalized by security-day realized volatility and daily volume, bin-level medians on EXECUTED quantity.
  • The DeepLOB comparison is confounded and the authors say so: DeepLOB is trained on the original three-class objective but scored under a direction-only protocol that ignores the Flat class.

Key findings

  • Data scale: ~31.9 billion order events across 800 non-overlapping CSI 300 + CSI 500 constituents, Jan 2024 - Nov 2025 for training with a strictly held-out Dec 2025 test month; five model scales from 9.66M to 1.27B parameters, four token budgets (1B / 3B / 10B / 31.9B).
  • A Chinchilla-style law L(N, D) = 5.3697 + 1.5987/N^0.3795 + 1.0027/D^0.3117 fits with 0.66% mean absolute relative error under leave-one-model-size-out validation, and a fit truncated at 366M predicts the held-out 1.27B runs within 0.53% MAPE (max 1.12%) — evidence that market order-flow modelling scales predictably.
  • The open-price anchor plus VQ codebook cuts price reconstruction MAE from 4.3e-1 (Bin-S, the generation-feasible mid-anchored binning baseline) to 2.51e-2, and P90/P99 tick errors from 26/764 to 6/28 — an ~27x reduction in the P99 tail.
  • M3 has the smallest Wasserstein-1 distance to empirical log returns at ALL four horizons (1.60 / 2.85 / 4.25 / 6.69 bp vs ZI 2.77 / 3.55 / 4.83 / 7.41) and the smallest KS statistic at 10s, 30s and 60s, and its kurtosis is far closer to real than either baseline (e.g. 29.63 vs real 20.08 at 60s, against ZI 54.86 and Hawkes 49.21).
  • Closed-loop TWAP interventions (participation 0.10%-1.00%, fills fed back into the AR context) produce concave impact with log-log slopes of 0.532 (buy, R^2 = 0.908) and 0.524 (sell, R^2 = 0.819) — both within ~7% of the square-root exponent 1/2, and this is an emergent property, not a fitted one.
  • LOB conditioning is load-bearing: the LOB-prefixed model beats an otherwise-matched no-LOB ablation and DeepLOB on tail directional accuracy at all horizons, and realized volatility rises monotonically across Monte Carlo forecast quintiles Q1 -> Q5.

Notable quotes

The models are trained on data from January 2024 to November 2025, comprising approximately 31.9 billion order events, and evaluated on the strictly held-out December 2025 period.
We therefore normalize prices relative to the daily open price, which is fixed and identically available during training and inference.
We do not compare with recent large-scale generative models such as TradeFM and MarS, as their pretrained weights are not publicly released, precluding faithful reproduction.
The engine therefore enforces price priority across levels, but does not model order identifiers or FIFO priority within a level.

Limitations

  • Baselines are weak and the comparison set is incomplete: only Zero-Intelligence and a Compound Hawkes Process are benchmarked, because TradeFM and MarS weights are unreleased — so 'state of the art' is unestablished. Fidelity is also NOT uniformly better: at the 120s horizon M3's KS statistic (0.364) is WORSE than both ZI (0.287) and Hawkes (0.297), suggesting the coupled-dynamics advantage decays as returns aggregate.
  • Simulation realism is bounded by a deliberately lightweight engine: aggregate depth per level with no order IDs and no intra-level FIFO priority, an add/cancel-only event space with no market/limit distinction, no latency or queue modelling, and the LOB encoded only ONCE at rollout start rather than recurrently. Reconstruction is also lossy at the tick level (VQ exact-tick rate 0.377 versus the 0.765 oracle), and the TWAP impact study rests on just 64 prompt windows x 4 rollouts.
  • Nothing here is a trading result: the authors explicitly frame tail directional accuracy as a predictive ranking diagnostic rather than the performance of an executable trading strategy, there are no transaction costs, no fees or slippage accounting, and no P&L. Scope is also narrow — one market (China A-shares, CSI 300/500), one held-out month, 1,024-token context, and bootstrap CIs that exclude run-to-run training stochasticity since only one loss observation exists per configuration.

Open questions

  • Does the open-price anchor survive regimes it was not built for — gap opens, limit-up/limit-down days, halts, and the tick-size and T+1 mechanics specific to China A-shares — and does the fitted scaling law transfer to US equities, futures, or crypto, where TradeFM-style mid-relative tokenization was calibrated?
  • How far can the impact result be trusted for execution decisions? The 0.53 exponent emerges from background flow reacting to a single TWAP meta-order; it is untested against competing/adversarial flow, multiple simultaneous meta-orders, longer-than-300s horizons, or the transient-vs-permanent impact decomposition an execution desk actually needs.
  • Is the predictive signal real alpha or a leakage/protocol artifact? Terciles are formed by ranking within the evaluation set and labels are constructed by ranking observed returns, so an out-of-sample, cost-aware, causally-clean backtest (and a recurrent or periodically re-encoded LOB state, which the one-shot prefix design forgoes) remains the obvious next test.
