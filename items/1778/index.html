    Model-Free Passive Execution via Order-Level Shadowing

    Date: 2026-09-26

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-16
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18019

    Shadow-PPOV shows that simply mirroring the price, venue, and lifecycle of a randomly-selected resting order - with zero book modeling or fill prediction - matches a matched-participation aggressive POV to the fourth decimal on a full year of CME ES data, giving quants a dead-simple, near-zero-compute passive-placement benchmark that any predictive execution model should have to beat.

    Executive summary

    The paper introduces Shadow-PPOV, a passive percentage-of-volume execution method that sets its order-placement rate from observed third-party order-book adds rather than from traded volume or any explicit book/fill model. Instead of predicting where to post, it shadows a just-arrived resting order - posting its own limit order at the same price and venue and binding cancellation to that order's identifier (immediate on delete, delayed by a grace window on trade). Evaluated on a full calendar year (246 sessions, 2025) of replayed CME ES futures market-by-order data, it executes near the mid-price and statistically matches an aggressive-POV equivalent at matched participation, degrading far more gracefully than the aggressive style under added latency.

    The core mechanic: participant shadowing

    On observing a third-party add message, Shadow-PPOV may transmit its own limit order at that add's exact price and venue, then stores a single mapping from (venue, exchange_order_id) to its own shadow order ID. No price, quantity, queue position, book state, or timestamp is retained - only that one identifier association - making lookup and removal O(1) by construction.

    • Order-placement rate is selected either probabilistically (fixed probability per add) or deterministically (every N-th add); experiments use the probabilistic mode.
    • Shadow order quantity is derived from recent average trade sizes, then capped by the followed order's own size, per-order/per-level limits, and remaining parent quantity.
    • Because a limit order at the same price and venue joins price-time priority behind the shadowed order, a sweep must consume the participant order (and depth ahead of it) before reaching the shadow.

    Identifier-driven cancel mirroring and venue inheritance

    Cancellation needs no market-state evaluation at all: a delete of the followed order triggers an immediate cancel of the shadow; a trade against the followed order arms a delayed cancel that fires after a grace window of B end-of-burst events, since a sweep may still reach the shadow. Safety guards (two-band hysteresis, completion and over-hedge limits) can pre-empt the grace window; a modify is treated as full lifecycle termination. Because each shadow is routed to the venue of the add it follows, the cross-venue execution mix reproduces the observed add distribution in expectation, with no venue-scoring or routing optimizer - though this is proposed and unevaluated, since the CME ES corpus is single-venue.

    Experimental setup

    E-mini S&P 500 (ES) futures market-by-order data from CME channel 310, every regular session of calendar 2025 with an available PCAP file (246 sessions). Two-sided windows open every 10 minutes from 09:29 to 16:00 ET (up to 39 windows/session), each trading 100 contracts to buy and 100 to sell. One tick = 0.25 index points = $12.50/contract. The book was one tick wide at 96.3% of window openings (mean spread 1.038 ticks), giving an immediate-execution slippage of +0.519 ticks/contract as the crossing baseline.

    Results: passive vs aggressive at matched participation

    Passive Shadow-PPOV (5% of adds shadowed) and an aggressive POV baseline (2% of prints) were tuned to nearly matched realized participation (4.46% vs 4.86%) over 9,592 and 9,581 completed windows. Relative slippage came out statistically indistinguishable: +0.0955 +/- 0.0130 ticks/contract passive vs +0.0952 +/- 0.0135 aggressive, both beating immediate execution by about 0.424 ticks. The authors attribute the agreement to the Glosten-Milgrom adverse-selection account of the spread.

    • A round trip of 100 contracts bought and sold costs under a tenth of a tick against arrival mid - about $1.19 per contract on ES.
    • A latency sweep from 0 to 5 ms on order + cancel + feed shows passive cost rising only ~55% (slope ~0.011 ticks/contract/ms) while aggressive cost more than triples (slope ~0.042).
    • The cost ordering between the two styles reverses within the first half-millisecond of delay.
    • Crossed share (filled on arrival) is 1.04% passive vs 51.86% aggressive.

    Stale-information control and the participation-size model

    A control test delayed only the observed information by 30 seconds before acting on it (order and cancel still instant): cost roughly quadrupled from +0.0953 +/- 0.0130 to +0.3673 +/- 0.0989 ticks/contract and participation collapsed from 4.47% to 1.56%, confirming the inherited information is real and decays. A separate stylized Monte Carlo (Q one-lot children, exponential fill gaps) shows realized participation variance around the target shrinking roughly as 1/sqrt(Q).

    • At Q=100 child fills the 10th-90th percentile band is [0.89, 1.14] of the modeled mean.
    • At Q=1 the same band is [0.44, 9.48] - small parent orders have highly random realized participation.

    Key findings

    • At nearly matched participation (4.46% vs 4.86%) and zero latency, passive Shadow-PPOV and aggressive POV cost statistically the same: +0.0955 +/- 0.0130 vs +0.0952 +/- 0.0135 ticks/contract, both beating immediate execution by about 0.424 ticks.
    • Over 9,592 completed passive windows, a round trip of 100 contracts costs +0.0955 +/- 0.0130 ticks/contract against arrival mid - under a tenth of a tick, or $1.19 per contract on ES.
    • Under a 0-5 ms latency sweep, passive cost rises only from +0.097 to +0.150 ticks/contract (~55%, slope ~0.011/ms) while aggressive more than triples (+0.087 to +0.296, slope ~0.042); the cost ordering reverses within the first half-millisecond.
    • A 30-second stale-information control roughly quadrupled cost (+0.0953 to +0.3673 ticks/contract) and cut participation from 4.47% to 1.56%, showing the shadowed information's value decays.
    • The retained per-shadow state is a single (venue, exchange_order_id) -> shadow_order_id mapping - no price, quantity, queue position, book state, or timestamp - making state-based cancellation structurally impossible.
    • Monte Carlo participation variance: the 10th-90th percentile band around target participation is [0.44, 9.48]x at 1 child fill but tightens to [0.89, 1.14]x by 100 child fills.

    Notable quotes

    A resting limit order is somebody's conclusion, arrived at by whatever means, that this price is worth standing at.
    The whole of Shadow-PPOV's own contribution is to copy that price and watch that order's identifier.
    Inheriting a placement judgement is not the same as holding a directional view: knowing where a fill is likely says nothing about where the price is going next.
    The material limitation is that the replay does not model the market's reaction to Shadow-PPOV's own orders.

    Limitations

    • The replay simulator does not model the market's reaction to the algorithm's own orders - fills are added to the day's recorded volume rather than displacing other participants'. The authors call this mild at 4.5% aggregate participation but strained at the 6.9% reached under 5 ms latency, so participation there is an upper bound.
    • A single instrument (ES futures) with a one-tick-wide book for most of the session, so the relative-to-immediate-execution metric is close to an identity here and should not be transferred to wider-spread products without new measurement.
    • Parent order size is fixed at 100 contracts throughout, and the flow-inherited venue-selection mechanism is proposed but unevaluated because the corpus is single-venue.

    Open questions

    • How does Shadow-PPOV compare, under the same direction-neutral slippage metric, against posting at the touch, the passive legs of TWAP/VWAP/POV, and an actual predictive placement model - i.e. how much does prediction buy over the model-free benchmark?
    • What are the realized fill/completion rates, queue-position dynamics, and sensitivity to the placement rate and grace window?
    • Does the method generalize beyond a single zero-impact-simulated index-futures contract to fragmented multi-venue equity markets and to larger order sizes with real market impact?
