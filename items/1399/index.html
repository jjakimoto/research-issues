What LLM Trading Agents Actually Do in Production: A Six-Month, Population-Scale Record from Two Fleets

📰 Latest · 2026-09-09 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-09

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-04
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663

The largest production record of LLM trading agents to date (3,505 real-capital vaults plus a ~500-agent perp fleet) finds no directional edge from any model or information source — behavior and losses are governed almost entirely by the operating layer (sliders, rendered lists, order-path mechanics), so the cheap wins are mechanical, not prompt- or model-side.

Executive summary

DX Research Group reports six months of production telemetry from two LLM-trading systems sharing one design lineage: DX Terminal Pro (3,505 user-funded vaults trading real ETH on Base memecoins for 21 days) and the DXAP live alpha (500-599 Hyperliquid perp agents, 231,638 finalized turns, 14,596 fills). Four findings anchor the paper: the operating layer determines behavior more than strategy text; sizing is volatility-blind; agents fail to capture upside they reach; and neither fleet has a directional edge, with a paired replay league finding frontier models statistically indistinguishable on decision quality. The DXAP fleet loses money (-$217K realized at a common 5.5 bps fee) and trails a matched Hyperliquid retail benchmark (41% vs 50% roundtrip win rate).

Two systems, one design lineage

Both deployments expose the same five-slider configuration (activity, risk tolerance, trade size, holding style, diversification; 1-5) plus free-text strategy, and both run a one-action-per-turn loop with typed tool calls. DX Terminal Pro ran Feb 26 - Mar 18, 2026 with a frozen Qwen3-235B model on 12 Base memecoins at a 2.3%/swap fee; DXAP runs Jun 8 - Aug 15, 2026 on ~99-100 Hyperliquid perps via a 10-tool manifest.

  • DX Terminal Pro: 4,984 ETH deposited, 4,589 withdrawn (92%); ~$20M volume, ~70B inference tokens
  • DXAP: 231,638 finalized turns, 14,596 fills, 5,035 real-money (6 of 19 users)
  • Builder fee moved 4.5 -> 14.5 -> 5.5 bps/side, so all cross-era P&L is restated at 5.5 bps
  • Every claim carries an evidence class: firm / provisional / weak / retracted

The operating layer determines behavior

Chosen leverage is essentially a configuration constant, and a purely visual rendering choice causally routes which symbols agents trade. The two systems resolve text-versus-slider conflicts in opposite directions despite the shared lineage.

  • riskTolerance slider maps to leverage at +0.425x per level (p = 3.8e-279); agent fixed effects absorb 60% of variance; user-named leverage tracks realized leverage at Spearman rho = +0.836
  • 46.5% of entries land in the 9 symbols rendered on the 'movers' leaderboard vs an 8.9% random-availability baseline
  • Regression discontinuity at the rank-3/rank-4 render cut: 1.75x [1.49, 2.06]
  • Owners writing concrete numeric instructions were profitable 4.2x as often as median; the 87 UI-only owners were the highest-profit cohort (41% in profit)

Volatility-blind sizing and concentrated liquidations

Across 6,400 closed positions in entry-volatility sextiles, median chosen leverage is 5.0x in every sextile despite a 5.7x spread in volatility, and notional rises with volatility. Liquidations concentrate in one configuration cell, and the strongest prompt-side mitigation backfired.

  • Spearman(volatility, leverage) = -0.001 (p = 0.92); Spearman(volatility, notional) = +0.165 (p = 2.7e-40)
  • 205 liquidations (3.2%) carry 74% of gross loss; 128 (62%) sit in the momentum x frequency-5 cell holding ~11% of the book; OR 22.37 [12.59, 37.45]; zero liquidations in 411 momentum positions at frequency < 5
  • Forcing agents to state liquidation distance: stated in 45.0% of entry turns, identical sizing, and staters liquidated MORE (5.8% vs 1.2%)

The capture gap and mechanical exits

Positions frequently reach profitable territory and give it back. Upside is nearly as predictable as risk, but what the agent keeps is not. A fixed 2%/4% bracket at entry is the best-measured lever, working mainly by truncating the left tail.

  • 43.2% of closed positions reached >= +300 bps MFE within 24h; 49.3% of those closed negative
  • Median MFE +247.9 bps vs median realized -33.3 bps; median capture where upside existed is 2.0%
  • MFE ROC 0.751 (liquidation 0.783) but capture ROC 0.541 — chance
  • Bracket earns +39.0 bps/position [+21.3, +56.5]; ~60% of the gain is blow-up prevention

Nulls, the model league, and the canon

No information source produced a directional edge, no posture/template/cohort was day-clustered positive, and a paired replay league found three frontier models indistinguishable on decision quality while differing sharply in stability and cost.

  • A 77K-candidate signal ledger sorts at chance (0.50); 770K tokens of extra context yields -0.46 points [-2.93, +1.75]; a world-context arm went 0/84
  • Memory writes correlate negatively with P&L (rho = -0.200, p = 0.002); one agent ran 31 of 32 positions on deleted strategy text
  • League: 3 models span 263.27-264.37 bps of regret (min p = 0.46); cost $8.25 vs $203.61 (~25x)
  • Canon rule 1: the market day, never the trade, is the inferential unit

Key findings

  • Median chosen leverage is 5.0x in all six volatility sextiles across a 5.7x spread (Spearman -0.001, p=0.92) while notional moves the wrong way (+0.165, p=2.7e-40).
  • A rendering choice routes trade selection: 46.5% of entries in the 9 rendered symbols vs 8.9% baseline, RDD 1.75x [1.49, 2.06].
  • 128 of 205 liquidations (62%) sit in one configuration cell holding ~11% of the book (OR 22.37).
  • 43.2% of positions reached >= +300 bps MFE, 49.3% of those closed negative; a 2%/4% bracket recovers +39.0 bps.
  • Forced liquidation-distance restatement changed nothing but the liquidation rate, which rose (5.8% vs 1.2%).
  • Three frontier models span 263.27-264.37 bps of replay regret (min p=0.46) at ~25x cost difference.

Notable quotes

Nothing else we measured (market state, recent P&L, prompt family) moves sizing comparably.
Risk in this fleet is a configuration choice made at setup time rather than a series of bad in-context decisions.
The fleet's positions frequently go somewhere profitable; the fleet rarely keeps it
Every prompt-side attempt to fix a behavior that lives in the operating layer (forced restatement, added context, stronger wording) underperforms a one-line change to the order path or the render.

Limitations

  • Most DXAP fills are paper-engine: live-mark fills, zero slippage, zero funding, and a 0.4% maintenance-margin placeholder that liquidates ~2x later than real margining.
  • Thin positives and single venue per era: 205 liquidations underpin all tail results.
  • The neural probe reads a proxy encoder, not the production model; census counts drift (336-599).

Open questions

  • Would an atomic open-with-protection order path plus volatility-scaled leverage convert the +39.0 bps bracket gain into a fleet-level edge under real margining?
  • Can training on the environment itself produce separation where model choice cannot?
  • Does the provisional 'restraint beats added information' result replicate, or join the prior retractions?
