    Strategic Index Reconstitution: Differential Games, Closed-Loop Equilibria and Mean-Field Dynamics

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-20

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-14
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15901

    Index rebalancing is where billions change hands on a known schedule (Bloomberg reported two Millennium pods made ~$3.7bn in June 2026), and this paper gives the first globally solvable continuous-time, finite-player, CLOSED-LOOP execution game with transient cross-asset impact — so the anticipatory-trading problem can finally be solved as a feedback equilibrium rather than a fixed open-loop schedule. Its punchline for practitioners: opportunists usually REDUCE the indexer's execution cost, but only at intermediate impact resilience.

    Executive summary

    Fiechtner and Blanchet (Stanford ICME/MS&E) model n opportunistic traders who position ahead of an index reconstitution announcement, revise once membership is revealed, and trade against an indexer executing a TWAP into the close. Under a no-price-manipulation standing assumption they construct a subgame-perfect closed-loop Nash equilibrium on every finite horizon, with affine feedbacks driven by a non-standard 3d x 3d matrix Riccati ODE whose size does not grow with the number of traders. Aggregate inventory and price impact depend on beliefs only through the population-average belief, while belief dispersion drives individual positions and gross volume. A mean-field limit gives O(eps_n) path errors and C*eps_n^2 approximate-Nash bounds, and a calibrated five-asset illustration shows indexer cost savings peaking at intermediate resilience.

    Problem: who warehouses the inventory before the index fund arrives

    Index funds must trade at implementation, usually in the closing auction, and opportunists front-run that known flow. The paper motivates the question with the empirical record: announcement/implementation abnormal returns have collapsed while pre-announcement returns and implementation-day turnover have risen, suggesting the profits moved earlier and the transfer of inventory, not the price pop, is the object of study.

    • S&P 500 additions in the late 1970s/early 1980s: ~3% abnormal return on the first day after announcement, with later reversals (Harris and Gurel).
    • Greenwood and Sammon: announcement abnormal returns fell from 4.94% (1990s) to 1.02% (2010-2020); implementation from 3.55% to 0.26%.
    • Same comparison, the 100 trading days BEFORE announcement rose from 9.60% to 18.68% — anticipatory trading moved earlier.
    • Pegoraro et al.: implementation-day turnover near 20% of shares outstanding for 2010-2024 additions from outside the S&P family, despite an average abnormal return of -0.09%.
    • Press framing: The Economist called index rebalancing 'the biggest event in markets'; Bloomberg reported two Millennium index-rebalancing pods earned ~$3.7bn in June 2026 after losing ~$900m in early 2025.

    Model: multiasset transient impact, heterogeneous beliefs, closed-loop policies

    n >= 2 opportunists trade d assets on [0,T] with announcement at T_ann and implementation at T_impl. Before the announcement each trader holds a subjective probability vector pi^i over m possible post-rebalance index compositions; the indexer's scenario-k schedule g^k spreads its required purchases and sales uniformly over [T_TWAP, T_impl]. Midprice is S = S^0 + I with a decaying transient impact state dI = [-RI + Gamma(varpi_n sum_j u^j + g)]dt, and execution prices add an instantaneous Lambda term that also loads on rivals' rates (chi_u) and the indexer's flow (chi_g). Traders minimize expected execution cost net of fundamental-price inventory value plus quadratic running/terminal inventory penalties.

    • Crucially CLOSED-LOOP: strategies are feedback functions of own inventory, average inventory, midprice and the announcement process — a deviation changes rivals' realized rates, unlike the open-loop equilibria standard in execution games.
    • Traders do NOT observe rivals' individual inventories, only the average inventory and the population-average belief pi-bar_n.
    • Two scalings: fixed-market-size varpi_n = 1 (adding fixed-size traders) vs mean-field varpi_n = varpi/n (fixed total opportunist mass).
    • Assumption 2.8 (standing/no-manipulation): Lambda positive definite, Q and Q_T PSD, the self-impact cost functional C(v) >= 0 for every deterministic square-integrable v, chi_u, chi_g >= 0, and chi_u*varpi_n <= 1.

    Solution: a Riccati system whose size is independent of n

    Theorem 3.2 is a verification result: smooth solutions of the coupled HJB system with terminal and announcement conditions yield a subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium via their minimizing feedbacks, covering all admissible history-dependent unilateral deviations. The authors use a quadratic ansatz in the reduced state z^i = (X^i - X-bar, X-bar, I) in R^{3d}, which reduces the HJB system to one non-standard 3d x 3d matrix Riccati ODE for the shared quadratic coefficient H_n plus a sequence of linear ODEs for the remaining coefficients.

    • For fixed d and m only a fixed number of 3d-dimensional linear ODEs is needed — equilibrium-computation complexity does not grow with n.
    • Theorem 3.6 (uniform global solvability): under Assumption 2.8, two-sided bounds hold uniformly over n >= 2, ruling out finite-time blow-up of the backward Riccati solution.
    • No short-horizon restriction and no smallness condition on transient impact is required — the indefinite cross terms between trading rate and outstanding impact are tamed by the no-manipulation condition instead.
    • Remark 3.5 decomposes dynamics: before the announcement the common block (X-bar, I) and the private deviations X^i - X-bar satisfy DECOUPLED linear systems, forced by pi-bar_n and by pi^i - pi-bar_n respectively; after the announcement the realized schedule hits only the common block, so pre-announcement disagreement persists through carried inventories.

    Mean-field limit and quantitative convergence

    Because the finite-player feedback needs current average inventory and the exact n — both often unavailable — the authors build mean-field policies indexed by belief type pi in the m-simplex, using equilibrium aggregate paths instead of real-time aggregates. Theorems 4.2 and 4.5 construct the mean-field equilibrium and show the finite-dimensional linear system determining average inventory and impact is nonsingular, giving uniqueness within the affine class.

    • Error scale eps_n = 1/n + ||pi-bar_n - pi-bar||.
    • Theorem 4.6: from zero initial inventory and impact, trading-rate, inventory and impact errors are O(eps_n), uniformly over time, scenario and trader.
    • Theorem 4.7: mean-field policies played in the finite game are an approximate Nash equilibrium — no admissible unilateral deviation improves expected cost by more than C*eps_n^2, with C independent of n and of the belief collection.
    • i.i.d. belief types: path errors O_p(n^{-1/2}), Nash errors O_p(n^{-1}). Deterministic populations matching the limit average belief at rate 1/n: O(n^{-1}) and O(n^{-2}).

    Numerical illustration: a five-stock reconstitution

    A stylized rebalance with d = 5 (INTC, CSCO, EBAY, DOW, COST), T_ann = 10, T_TWAP = 19, T_impl = 20, T = 22 trading days. Lambda and Gamma come from published instantaneous/permanent impact estimates rescaled to millions of shares per trading day; resilience is set to rho = 2*log 2 for every asset, an impact half-life of half a trading day. The index holds exactly two of the five assets, so m = C(5,2) = 10 scenarios; beliefs are drawn from a Dirichlet with concentration 50 around a score-based mean.

    • Baseline atomic case: n = 20, varpi_n = 1, chi_u = chi_g = 1, zero initial inventory and impact.
    • q_a/lambda_a = 0.25 per day^2 (inventory half-life ~1.96 trading days in an auxiliary scalar problem); q_{T,a}/lambda_a = 13,000 per day to make terminal inventory negligible.
    • Indexer AUM: h^0_a = 0.07 f_a for the two incumbents (seven days of ADV each), USD 2.112 billion under management; f_a set to 100 days of ADV.
    • Mean-field section uses varpi_n = 30/n, which forces n >= 30 since chi_u = 1 makes Assumption 2.8 require chi_u*varpi_n <= 1; paths for n = 30, 60, 150, 300 converge visibly to the limit.
    • Indexer cost savings decompose as C_0 - C_n = -P_n + B_n + E_n: P_n is persistent pre-TWAP price displacement (a cost), B_n the transient-impact reduction from opposing trades, E_n the instantaneous execution-cost saving.

    Significance and how it differs from prior work

    The closest economic model, Pegoraro et al., is a five-period two-stock Cournot model with membership public before trading and asset-separable permanent/instantaneous impact, where the indexer optimizes its own schedule. This paper instead fixes the indexer's schedule and adds what that literature omits: pre-announcement positioning under heterogeneous beliefs, cross-asset impact that decays, and closed-loop rather than open-loop strategies.

    • Claimed first: a globally solvable continuous-time, finite-player, closed-loop execution game with transient price impact; comparable finite-player execution games are open-loop, where existence is easier.
    • Transient-impact dynamics follow the resilient limit-order-book model of Obizhaeva and Wang; the nonnegative self-impact-cost condition follows the multiasset no-manipulation literature (Gatheral et al., Alfonsi et al., Schneider and Lillo, Abi Jaber et al.).
    • The equilibrium reproduces a documented stylized fact: aggregate inventory in added stocks crosses through zero during the TWAP (opportunists go short to supply the indexer) and is covered afterward, matching the rise-then-decline in short interest around implementation.

    Key findings

    • A subgame-perfect closed-loop Nash equilibrium exists on EVERY finite horizon under the no-manipulation Assumption 2.8, via uniform two-sided bounds on the 3d x 3d Riccati solution (Theorem 3.6) — no short-horizon or small-impact condition needed.
    • Computation cost is independent of the number of traders: the number and dimension of ODEs depend only on d assets and m scenarios, not on n.
    • Beliefs enter in two separable channels: aggregate inventory and impact depend only on the population-average belief pi-bar_n, while belief DIFFERENCES move individual inventories and gross volume with zero effect on net aggregate flow or the impact path.
    • Mean-field policies are eps_n-accurate in paths and C*eps_n^2-accurate in the Nash sense (eps_n = 1/n + ||pi-bar_n - pi-bar||); for i.i.d. beliefs that is O_p(n^{-1/2}) and O_p(n^{-1}).
    • In equilibrium, aggregate opportunist inventory in every added or deleted asset passes through ZERO during the TWAP window and temporarily flips sign — the opportunists sell more than they hold and cover afterward — but with a single opportunist there is no such crossing.
    • Indexer implementation shortfall falls as opportunists are added, with diminishing marginal savings, while both mean and total opportunist terminal wealth decline; savings are non-monotonic in resilience, peaking at intermediate decay, and at low resilience opportunists are net HARMFUL (B_n - P_n < 0).

    Notable quotes

    To our knowledge, this is the first globally solvable continuous-time, finite-player, closed-loop execution game with transient price impact.
    Notably, in the case of a single opportunistic trader there is no crossing through zero: During the indexer implementation window the opportunist sells less than what he held at the start of the window.
    Gross volume increases with belief dispersion, even though aggregate inventory is unchanged.
    In panel (c), savings peak at intermediate resilience, where much of the adverse impact from earlier trading has decayed but opposing trades during the TWAP still provide substantial benefits.

    Limitations

    • The indexer's schedule is exogenous — a fixed TWAP over [T_TWAP, T_impl] — so the paper cannot speak to the index fund's own tracking-error vs execution-cost tradeoff, which is exactly what the closest prior model (Pegoraro et al.) endogenizes.
    • Beliefs are fixed until the public announcement (no information arrives in between), the fundamental price is an exogenous martingale independent of the index scenario, and in the numerics it is held constant.
    • Everything is linear-quadratic with deterministic, diagonal coefficient matrices in the illustration; the numerical section is a stylized five-asset rebalance calibrated from published impact estimates, not an empirical test against realized reconstitution data.

    Open questions

    • What happens when the indexer optimizes its own schedule against these closed-loop opportunists — i.e. a Stackelberg or full game version where the TWAP is chosen rather than prescribed?
    • Can beliefs be made dynamic, learning from observed order flow or prices before the announcement, rather than frozen until T_ann?
    • Do the model's signature predictions — the sign flip of aggregate inventory during the TWAP and the inverted-U of indexer savings in resilience — survive confrontation with actual reconstitution tape data?
