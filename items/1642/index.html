    Diffusion models for dynamic volatility surface generation and data-driven hedging

    📰 Latest · 2026-09-20
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-20

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-11
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13402

    The first diffusion-model framework for generating adapted, multi-day implied-volatility surface trajectories — and the first to prove its value economically: on 2018-2023 SPX options the diffusion-based hedge cuts tracking-error standard deviation to 10.55 USD versus 32.98 for the GAN benchmark and 41.09 for delta hedging, while an arbitrage-aware fine-tune drives static-arbitrage violations to near zero.

    Executive summary

    Han, Zhang, Torres, Acero and Xu (Stanford + J.P. Morgan QTR AI Research) extend the Adaptive Sequential Diffusion (AD-Seq) framework of Cao et al. (2026) from one dimension to the full SPX implied-volatility surface, yielding AD-Seq-Vol, which jointly generates the underlying log return and the vectorized surface conditional on realized market history, and AD-Seq-Vol-FT, a variant post-trained with LoRA against a differentiable static-arbitrage penalty. Trained on daily OptionMetrics SPX surfaces (2000-2018) and evaluated out-of-sample (2018-2023), AD-Seq-Vol produces fewer and smaller calendar/call/butterfly-spread violations than the training data itself and than VolGAN, while the fine-tuned variant reduces them to near zero. Plugged into Cont and Vuletic's (2025) one-period optimization-based hedging problem, the diffusion scenarios keep realized tracking errors centered near zero and substantially cut tail risk, including through the COVID-19 dislocation. The paper is the first to combine diffusion-based dynamic surface generation with a decision-based (hedging) evaluation.

    Problem: existing surface generators are static or one-step

    An implied-volatility surface is a high-dimensional object constrained cross-sectionally by static no-arbitrage and linked temporally to the underlying. Prior generators (VAE, GAN, neural-SDE) model the static or unconditional distribution of individual surfaces, or at best the next-day surface from the current state. What hedging actually needs is a generator adapted to the filtration — one that produces conditionally consistent multi-period trajectories.

    • GANs are unstable / mode-collapse prone; VAEs oversmooth — the motivation for score-based diffusion
    • Static admissibility is formalized as three discrete conditions from Davis and Hobson (2007): calendar spread, call spread, butterfly spread
    • The arbitrage penalty L(sigma) = l1 + l2 + l3 sums the positive parts of the three violated inequalities; L = 0 iff no static arbitrage
    • Hedging is chosen as the evaluation because hedge selection depends on the conditional joint evolution, not the realism of any single surface

    Model: AD-Seq-Vol and the arbitrage-aware fine-tune

    Each daily surface is vectorized on the moneyness-maturity grid into R^(Nm*Nt). Per time step a forward time-varying Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noising process is defined, and the corresponding time-reversed SDE's score is learned by denoising score matching with the conditioning set being the previously generated steps. At sampling time the reverse SDE is applied recursively — each generated surface is appended to the conditioning history — giving an adapted, non-anticipative trajectory. AD-Seq-Vol-FT post-trains the score network with the diffusion fine-tuning method of Han et al. (2025), using -L(.) as the reward.

    • Score function parameterized by a Transformer; attention models pairwise interactions among grid entries
    • 200 noise-injection steps, cosine noise schedule; DDPM sampler with 200 denoising steps
    • Pretraining: AdamW, 1000 epochs, batch 256, lr 7e-5, weight decay 1e-2, warm-up cosine LR schedule
    • Fine-tuning: rank-8 LoRA, KL penalty of strength 0.1 to the pretrained model, 200 epochs, AdamW, batch 32, lr 1e-4
    • Supports both one-step and block (recursive multi-day) generation

    Data and preprocessing

    Daily SPX option quotes from OptionMetrics, January 3 2000 to February 28 2023. Training window January 3 2000 - June 16 2018; held-out evaluation July 1 2018 - February 28 2023. Surfaces are fit with a vega-weighted Nadaraya-Watson estimator with a Gaussian kernel, then linearly interpolated in moneyness and maturity off-grid.

    • Moneyness grid: 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 1, 1.05, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4 (11 points)
    • Maturity grid: 1/252, 1/52, 2/52, 1/12, 1/6, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1 (9 points)
    • Market state = SPX log return plus the log-implied-volatility surface
    • Each training sample = 22 consecutive daily states (21 conditioning + 1 one-day-ahead target), overlapping by one trading day
    • Daily risk-free rate = median rate implied by put-call parity from option mid-prices

    Hedging experiment design

    Following Cont and Vuletic (2025), the target position is a long straddle with strike K = m0 * S_t1 for m0 in {0.75, 0.8, 0.9, 1.1, 1.2, 1.25}, rebalanced each trading date until expiry. At each date N conditional next-step scenarios are drawn, all instruments are valued with Black-Scholes under each scenario, and a LASSO-type one-period problem (squared tracking error plus a linear rebalancing-cost penalty) is solved by coordinate descent with the regularization parameter chosen by AIC.

    • Candidate hedges: SPX underlying, puts at 0.9/0.95/0.975 S_t1, calls at 1.0/1.025/1.05/1.1 S_t1 (straddle legs excluded)
    • Transaction cost = half the bid-ask spread of the instrument
    • N = 100 scenarios suffice for AD-Seq-Vol; VolGAN needs N = 1000 for comparable stability
    • Benchmarks: unhedged, Black-Scholes delta, Black-Scholes delta-vega, VolGAN (published and re-implemented)

    Results

    On static arbitrage, AD-Seq-Vol has fewer and smaller violations than the training data on all three terms — it attenuates rather than amplifies them — and beats VolGAN most clearly on calendar and butterfly constraints; AD-Seq-Vol-FT pushes violations to near zero. On hedging, AD-Seq-Vol's errors are centered at zero (medians -0.03 and 0.02 USD) with the lowest dispersion and best 5%/2.5% VaR in the COVID-included window; AD-Seq-Vol-FT takes the lowest 1% VaR in both windows. Against a self-implemented, stronger VolGAN (std 12.10 vs the published 32.98), AD-Seq-Vol still wins on std, 5% VaR and median while using a sparser hedge.

    • COVID window defined as 13 February - 21 July 2020
    • Correlation of tracking errors between AD-Seq-Vol and VolGAN is 0.83 — they agree on most hedge selection; the edge comes from a few VolGAN excursions
    • Instrument sparsity: re-implemented VolGAN uses 5.51 of 8 candidates on average (median 6); AD-Seq-Vol uses 4.73 (median 5)
    • Near-money m0 = 0.9, COVID included: std falls from 68.66 (VolGAN) to 10.49, 1% VaR from 80.78 to 29.36; at m0 = 1.1 the 1% VaR falls from 47.58 to 10.26
    • Fine-tuning's tail benefit: 2.5% VaR at m0 = 0.9 improves from 22.79 to 21.16 (COVID included) and 25.52 to 21.80 (excluded)

    Key findings

    • AD-Seq-Vol cuts pooled tracking-error standard deviation to 10.55 USD (COVID included) versus 32.98 for VolGAN, 41.09 for delta and 15.53 for delta-vega hedging.
    • Median realized tracking error is essentially zero for the diffusion hedges: -0.03 (COVID included) and 0.02 (excluded) for AD-Seq-Vol.
    • AD-Seq-Vol-FT attains the lowest 1% VaR in both windows (21.11 and 20.35 USD) and the lowest 2.5% VaR when COVID is excluded (16.41).
    • The plain diffusion model already produces fewer and smaller static-arbitrage violations than the training data itself; LoRA fine-tuning with -L(.) as reward pushes all three violation terms to near zero.
    • Sample efficiency: 100 generated scenarios stabilize AD-Seq-Vol hedges, whereas VolGAN needs 1000; AD-Seq-Vol also uses a sparser hedge (4.73 of 8 candidate instruments on average vs 5.51).
    • Even against the authors' own stronger VolGAN re-implementation (std 12.10, 1% VaR 20.49), AD-Seq-Vol still improves std (10.55), 5% VaR (11.16 vs 12.02) and median (-0.03 vs 0.31).

    Limitations

    • Single market only: all evidence comes from SPX index options via OptionMetrics, with no multi-asset or single-name validation.
    • The no-arbitrage fine-tuning enforces only static, per-surface conditions — dynamic no-arbitrage along the generated trajectory is not imposed, and violations are merely penalized, never guaranteed to be zero.
    • Hedging is evaluated in the local, one-period formulation with Black-Scholes valuation of all instruments; the generators' full-horizon trajectory capability is not exercised, and sequential diffusion sampling (200 denoising steps per step) is computationally heavy.

    Open questions

    • Can a joint multi-asset surface generator spanning several indices and single-name options preserve cross-asset dependence as well as this model preserves cross-sectional structure?
    • Does extending arbitrage-aware fine-tuning from static conditions to dynamic no-arbitrage along the trajectory further improve tail metrics, or does it trade off hedging accuracy?
    • Can distillation or few-step samplers cut sampling cost enough to make adaptive sequential diffusion viable at intraday/high-frequency rebalancing?
