    RECLAIM: Can Agents Reproduce the Claims of Machine Learning Papers?

    📰 Latest · 2026-09-26 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-26

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-23
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28850

    Even when an agent is handed a paper's own code, data, and weights, the best model reproduces only 41% of results and quits with most of its GPU budget unspent - for an autonomous researcher the bottleneck is not compute or access, it is the discipline to check its own work before declaring victory.

    Executive summary

    RECLAIM is a 100-paper benchmark, rebuilt yearly from NeurIPS, that asks four open-weight agents to reproduce one pinned empirical claim per paper under a fixed GPU-hour grant, stratified into three tiers (Run / Retrain / Reimplement) by how much of the artifact stack the original authors released. Grading is done by an adversarial LLM auditor (Claude Sonnet 5) that re-derives every reported number from the run's own execution evidence rather than trusting the agent's report; its verdicts agree with blind human graders at F1 0.82. Across 400 agent-paper cells the best agent per tier reproduces 41% (Run), 27% (Retrain), and 15% (Reimplement); agents leave the large majority of granted compute unspent even when they fail; and the single most common failure mode is writing a reimplementation and never checking it against the paper's own numbers.

    Benchmark construction and the three tiers

    RECLAIM starts from 3,414 NeurIPS 2025 arXiv-indexed papers. A MiniMax-M2.7 classifier agent reads each paper, extracts its central empirical claim, pins a 'match target' (the cheapest configuration the authors report that still establishes the claim), and audits four artifact signals: released code, released datasets, released weights, and whether required datasets are standard public benchmarks. An artifact counts as available only when a tool call can access it; paywalled or sign-up-gated artifacts count as unavailable. A 200-paper pool stratified equally across tiers and compute bands was human-audited, and the evaluation split was capped at 96 H100-hours per paper.

    • Run papers release code, data, and weights - the agent need only execute or evaluate them.
    • Retrain papers release no weights, so the agent trains the model before evaluating.
    • Reimplement papers release no code, so the agent rebuilds the method from the paper (datasets stay public).
    • Example match target: SharpZO pinned at 79.42% test accuracy on EuroSAT, 16-shot CLIP ViT-B/16.
    • Final tier sizes: 34 Run, 33 Retrain, 33 Reimplement (100 papers), plus a 14-paper dev split. Only 20 of 100 papers shipped a usable compute estimate; the rest were estimated by the authors.

    The verifier: an adversarial, execution-grounded LLM auditor

    Grading is hybrid: an LLM judge (Claude Sonnet 5, same auditor and frozen rubric for every run) assigns a 0-10 score from which a categorical verdict is derived deterministically, but the judge must ground every number in the run's own execution trace rather than the agent's self-report. Scores of 8+ count as reproduced; 6+ counts as partial or better. A separate anti-cheat layer forces a score of zero on high-severity flags such as an echoed paper number, a hardcoded constant, or a proxy value standing in for the real target.

    • "The auditor is adversarial by instruction and treats the agent's final report as a set of unverified claims."
    • It inspects and recomputes but never re-runs the reproduction, so a grade reflects the evidence the run produced.
    • Three human graders independently scored all 396 started runs, blind to the auditor; the auditor's reproduced verdicts reach F1 0.82 (vs PaperBench's reported 0.83 for its own judge).
    • Agents' self-assessments are unreliable in both directions: 128 self-claimed reproductions (of 359 parsable reports) against only 73 audited-confirmed.

    Results: four agents across three tiers

    Four open-weight-served agents (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.6-27B, MiniMax-M2.7, Muse Spark 1.2) each ran once per paper in an identical thin ReAct-style harness with no model-specific tuning, receiving the paper's LaTeX source, OpenReview supplement, verified artifact links, and the match target without its configuration.

    • DeepSeek-V4-Flash is strongest overall (27/100, mean score 5.13) and the only agent whose rate falls monotonically across tiers (Run vs Reimplement separated at z=2.7; Run vs Retrain not, at z=1.2).
    • Muse Spark 1.2 ties DeepSeek at Retrain and leads at Reimplement (15%).
    • All-agent totals: 31/136 (23%) Run, 29/132 (22%) Retrain, 13/132 (10%) Reimplement, 73/400 (18%) overall.
    • Partial-or-better (score >=6) at Retrain ranges from 9/33 (MiniMax) to 23/33 (Muse Spark).
    • Reproduction score falls as a paper's required compute band grows, even though no band comes close to exhausting its grant.

    Failure modes and the unspent-budget puzzle

    A nine-mode taxonomy was applied by an LLM reading each transcript (28 of 400 cells got no label). The most common process failure across all four agents is reimplementing part of the method and never checking it against a reference number the paper itself supplies.

    • "Reimplemented but did not check the result is the most common process failure, at 63 of the 400 cells, and 38 of those fall at Reimplement."
    • That mode plus 'Wrong experiment' cover 62 of 132 Reimplement-tier cells.
    • Tier-locked modes: 'Echoed a shipped number' needs released code and never occurs at Reimplement; 'Never launched an experiment' occurs 10x at Reimplement vs once at Run.
    • Agent-locked modes: MiniMax-M2.7 holds 11 of 24 build/dependency failures (partly missing aarch64 wheels on GH200 nodes); Qwen3.6-27B holds 19 of 31 'Failed before any number was produced'.
    • The 48 cells in the largest (96 H100-hour) compute band spend only 6.2% of their grant, and only 1 of them reproduces its claim.

    Cost and reliability

    The full evaluation (396 started runs across 400 agent-paper cells) was cheap in absolute terms, with GPU time dominating spend.

    • "The 396 runs that started cost $4,835, which is $12.21 per run and $66 per confirmed reproduction, and GPU time is 78% of that total."
    • GPU priced at $2/H100-hour; per-agent total cost ranged from about $610.82 (MiniMax-M2.7) to $1,837.24 (Muse Spark 1.2).
    • No human-intervention rate is reported - runs are fully autonomous within a fixed tool/session budget enforced by the harness.

    Key findings

    • Best-per-tier reproduction rate falls from 41% (Run) to 27% (Retrain) to 15% (Reimplement); overall confirmed reproduction is 73 of 400 agent-paper cells (18%).
    • Failed runs use only 29% of their granted GPU-hour budget on average; pooled across all 400 cells, agents spent 1,888 of 10,208 granted H100-hours.
    • Reproduction quality falls as a paper's compute band grows even though no band exhausts its grant - the largest (96h) band spends just 6.2% of its grant and yields 1 reproduction from 48 cells.
    • The most common failure mode is reimplementing part of a method and never validating it against the paper's own numbers (63/400 cells, 38 at Reimplement).
    • Agents misjudge their own success in both directions: 128 self-claimed reproductions vs 73 audited; MiniMax-M2.7 claimed 43 with only 10 confirmed, while DeepSeek-V4-Flash claimed just 19 despite 27 confirmed.
    • The adversarial LLM auditor's verdicts agree with blind human graders at F1 0.82.
    • Total cost across 396 started runs was $4,835 ($12.21/run, $66 per confirmed reproduction), with GPU time at 78% of spend.

    Notable quotes

    The auditor is adversarial by instruction and treats the agent's final report as a set of unverified claims.
    Taking the human label as the reference, the auditor's reproduced verdicts reach an F1 of 0.82.
    Spending stays far below the grant for every agent and every tier. The mean non-reproduced cell uses 29% of its allocation, and pooled over all 400 cells the agents spend 1,888 of the 10,208 H100-hours we granted.
    Reimplemented but did not check the result is the most common process failure, at 63 of the 400 cells, and 38 of those fall at Reimplement.

    Limitations

    • No independent human reproduction was run on the authors' own cluster, so for the 76 runs that executed a faithful pipeline but landed outside tolerance, agent error cannot be separated from seed and hardware variance.
    • Every agent postdates the benchmark papers and may have trained on their released code; a memorized fix would count as reproduced, inflating the released-code tiers.
    • Scope is one venue and year (NeurIPS 2025), one pinned experiment per paper, a 96 H100-hour cap, and no paid/closed-API papers; the roster omits Claude, GPT, and Gemini because of cost.
    • Only one run per agent-paper cell, so reported rates are point estimates and close pairs within a tier are left unranked.

    Open questions

    • Whether the results produced by increasingly autonomous idea-to-manuscript research systems hold up under independent verification - the exact capability RECLAIM measures, and one that recursive self-improvement would depend on.
    • Whether closed frontier models, excluded here for cost, would substantially change the tier-wise reproduction rates.
    • What actually drives the large gap between granted and spent compute - premature stopping, poor confidence calibration, or a harness/prompting artifact.
