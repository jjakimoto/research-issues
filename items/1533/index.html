HypoEvolve: Genetic Algorithms Enable Multi-Agent LLMs to Discover Scientific Hypotheses

📰 Latest · 2026-09-15
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-15

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-14
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15938

HypoEvolve turns multi-agent hypothesis generation into an explicit (mu+lambda) genetic algorithm whose fitness is an LLM pairwise judge fitted with Bradley-Terry — and then, crucially, holds out two external biological oracles (DepMap CRISPR selectivity, Open Targets) that never touch search, so the reported gains (0.171 vs 0.115 for the best baseline) are measured by something the optimizer could not game.

Executive summary

HypoEvolve coordinates three specialized LLM agents (generation, pairwise comparison, evolution) with a generational genetic algorithm over a fixed-size population of structured hypotheses (title, summary, statement, rationale). Search fitness comes purely from LLM pairwise judgments aggregated by a Bradley-Terry model; external biological evidence (DepMap selectivity, Open Targets association) is reserved for post-hoc scoring only. Evaluated on drug repurposing across 34 cancer types with a fixed 61-drug vocabulary, HypoEvolve beats six baselines on both external measures (DepMap 0.171 vs 0.115 for Tree of Thoughts, 0.039 for single-pass), at 206 model calls and $0.56 per run. The controlled ablation — replacing fitness-guided parent selection with uniform random selection while holding everything else fixed — is the paper's core claim that search design, not just model capability, drives hypothesis quality.

Evolutionary topology: (mu+lambda) generational GA over hypothesis documents

Each candidate is a structured document (title, summary, hypothesis statement, rationale). The search keeps mu retained candidates, produces lambda offspring per generation, runs for G generations, and returns argmax fitness in the final population. Replacement pools parents and offspring together and truncates to the top mu, so offspring must out-rank the parents they were built from. Every offspring carries parentage plus operator provenance so lineages are inspectable.

  • Default config: mu = 6, lambda = 6, G = 3 — a small population and a very short horizon.
  • Update rule: Q_t = P_{t-1} + O_t; f_t = Score(Q_t); P_t = Top_mu(Q_t; f_t) — textbook (mu+lambda) truncation.
  • Sensitivity tests at mu = 10 and G = 5 produce mixed, non-significant shifts.
  • Base model for all agents is gpt-5.4-mini; literature retrieval via Tavily.

Variation operators are semantic, not syntactic

Crossover and mutation are LLM reasoning steps over claims and rationales rather than string edits. Crossover has two variants: 'combination' (integrate compatible mechanisms/evidence from both parents) and 'inspiration' (use both parents as a springboard for a different explanation). Mutation has two variants: 'drug substitution' (swap the compound inside the allowed 61-drug vocabulary while keeping the mechanistic argument) and 'out-of-box' (revisit the hypothesis's assumptions). Each offspring: crossover with p_c = 0.6, then mutation with p_m = 0.15, or mutation with probability 1 if crossover was skipped.

  • Operator usage across all 1,692 offspring records: inspiration 27.6%, out-of-box mutation 24.8%, combination 22.9%, drug substitution 17.1%, unchanged fallback copies 7.5%.
  • Origin of the 94 final hypotheses: crossover 60 (53 of them inspiration), mutation 27 (23 drug substitution), 7 unchanged parent copies.
  • Parent selection is binary tournament — with six strictly ranked members the top hypothesis wins ~1/3 of tournaments and the 5th ~1/15, so selection pressure is mild, not greedy.

FITNESS: LLM-as-judge pairwise comparisons, NOT execution-grounded

This is the load-bearing design choice and the main thing to be skeptical about. There is no executable verifier, no simulator, no wet-lab loop in the search. The 'fitness' is a pairwise LLM scorer that reads two hypotheses and picks a winner or declares a tie, using task-specific criteria (specificity to the named cancer, evidence implicating the proposed target, whether the prediction is concrete and falsifiable). Every unordered pair in the pool is compared; outcomes are fit with a Bradley-Terry model into latent strengths, and fitness is an affine log transform anchored so the initial population mean is 50 with a 60-point spread. The reported fitness trajectory is therefore a within-run, self-referential scale — 'fitness improves in every run' is close to tautological for a monotone truncation scheme under a judge that is itself the selector.

  • Search fitness is 100% LLM judgment; the paper is explicit that DepMap/Open Targets never enter the scorer.
  • Fitness rises from mean 50.0 (gen 0) to 112.2 (gen 3), best 81.2 -> 125.7, increasing in all 94/94 runs — expected by construction, so treat it as a sanity check, not evidence of discovery.
  • The authors themselves warn that LLM-based selection 'can propagate unsupported assumptions across generations, directing attention toward persuasive hypotheses with weak evidence'.
  • The credible signal is the SEPARATE external oracle, computed only after the method has already picked its single answer.

External verification: held-out biological oracles, applied post-hoc

Because prospective experiments are infeasible, each hypothesis's rationale entails a checkable claim: the drug's annotated targets are relevant to the named cancer. DepMap selectivity subtracts each target's pan-cancer median CRISPR dependency from its median in the matched cancer and takes the max over annotated targets — the selectivity correction matters, because a constant thalidomide answer ranks first (with ties) in 30 of 31 matched cancer types under raw dependency, while under selectivity thalidomide in AML collapses from 1.0000 to +0.0040 and true positives survive (imatinib in CML +0.9674, vemurafenib in melanoma +0.9378). Open Targets gives a second, non-CRISPR channel. Each method emits exactly ONE hypothesis before external scoring — no oracle-selected max over a candidate pool — and all methods share the base model, retrieval, and the 61-drug vocabulary.

  • 34 cancer types (TCGA's 33 plus CML); 26 usable for DepMap, 29 for Open Targets in the all-method comparison.
  • Held-out protocol: 7 types (4 used in protocol development, 3 seen in an interim inspection) are excluded; the remaining 27 test a frozen configuration.
  • Real strength: the optimizer cannot see the metric, so the gains are not straightforward metric hacking. Real weakness: it is still a retrospective database lookup, not execution, and the authors concede memorized drug-disease pairs may contribute.

The controlled ablation that actually supports the thesis

The key experiment replaces fitness-guided parent selection with uniform random selection while holding generation, crossover, mutation, retrieval, evaluation, and hypothesis count fixed. Across 31 cancer types on DepMap and 34 on Open Targets, fitness-guided selection lifts the weakest member by 0.088 / 0.218 and the population mean by 0.075 / 0.128; the maximum external score shows no statistically detectable change. In other words, the LLM-judge fitness signal raises the floor and the average but does not demonstrably raise the ceiling — consistent with a noisy-but-nonrandom verifier. Other ablations are weaker: removing crossover, mutation, or both gives mixed signs and none of the six comparisons survives Holm correction over eight cancer types.

  • Final-hypothesis margin for fitness-guided over random selection: +0.090 DepMap, +0.156 Open Targets.
  • Operator ablations are underpowered (8 cancer types) and should not be read as evidence that crossover/mutation individually matter.
  • Useful negative control: in the co-scientist baseline, adding vs withholding generator feedback changes scores by -0.017 / -0.010, comparable to simply repeating the run (+0.012 / +0.028) — that scaffold's 'feedback' is within run-to-run noise.

Cost and the compute-vs-quality picture

HypoEvolve costs 206 model calls and $0.56 per run, of which 167 calls (81%) are pairwise comparisons — the fitness function dominates the budget and grows quadratically with pool size. Notably it is CHEAPER than two baselines it beats decisively: the co-scientist scaffold (288 calls, $0.71) and static reranking (227 calls, $0.55) both land within 0.007 of single-pass generation on DepMap. Tree of Thoughts is the efficiency champion among baselines at 0.115 with 31 calls and $0.09. Token budgets are not matched across methods, so the cost table is indicative rather than compute-matched.

  • For a builder: the obvious optimization is a sparse comparison schedule, freeing the quadratic judge budget for larger mu or longer G.
  • Self-consistency with 40 samples scores BELOW single-pass generation on both metrics — more sampling without selection pressure actively hurts.
  • $0.56 per run makes fanning out over 34 cancer types trivially affordable; the bottleneck is verifier quality, not compute.

Key findings

  • HypoEvolve reaches 0.171 DepMap selectivity and 0.426 Open Targets association vs 0.115 / 0.329 for Tree of Thoughts and 0.039 / 0.163 for single-pass generation; paired margins over single-pass are +0.133 (19 wins / 7 losses) on DepMap and +0.263 (26 wins / 3 losses) on Open Targets.
  • Gains transfer to 27 held-out cancer types under a frozen configuration: +0.280 Open Targets and +0.111 DepMap (on the 24 types with matched cell lines) over single-pass generation.
  • The collaboration-design claim rests on one clean ablation: swapping fitness-guided parent selection for uniform random selection costs 0.088 / 0.218 on the weakest member and 0.075 / 0.128 on the population mean (DepMap / Open Targets), with no detectable change in the maximum.
  • Variation operators do real work — 87 of 94 final hypotheses came from crossover or mutation (60 crossover, 27 mutation), only 7 were unchanged parent copies; across 1,692 offspring all four operators are used (inspiration 27.6%, out-of-box 24.8%, combination 22.9%, drug substitution 17.1%).
  • Fitness is purely LLM-judge: 167 of 206 model calls per run are pairwise comparisons, Bradley-Terry-fitted, with DepMap/Open Targets explicitly withheld from the scorer; internal fitness rises 50.0 -> 112.2 (mean) and 81.2 -> 125.7 (best) in 100% of 94 runs.
  • More compute does not buy quality: the co-scientist scaffold (288 calls) and static reranking (227 calls) score within 0.007 of single-pass generation on DepMap, and 40-sample self-consistency scores below single-pass on both metrics, while HypoEvolve wins at 206 calls / $0.56.
  • Metric design matters as much as method design: under raw target dependency a constant thalidomide answer ranks first in 30 of 31 matched cancer types, which is why the authors switched to pan-cancer-median-corrected selectivity (thalidomide in AML drops 1.0000 -> +0.0040).

Limitations

  • Fitness is LLM judgment only — there is no execution-grounded verifier in the loop, and the authors acknowledge LLM-based selection can propagate unsupported assumptions and favor persuasive-but-weakly-evidenced hypotheses; the external oracles are retrospective databases, not experiments.
  • Pairwise comparison consumes 167 of 206 model calls and scales quadratically with pool size, which is what forces the small mu = 6 population and G = 3 horizon.
  • The operator ablations cover only eight cancer types and no comparison survives Holm correction; the paper also concedes prior model exposure to known drug-disease pairs may inflate gains, and token budgets are not matched across methods.

Open questions

  • Does the architecture transfer beyond drug repurposing, where a convenient post-hoc oracle (DepMap/Open Targets) happens to exist — and beyond a single base model (gpt-5.4-mini)?
  • Can the quadratic judge cost be replaced by sparse comparison schedules or by folding real domain evidence directly into fitness, converting the LLM-as-judge into something closer to an execution-grounded verifier?
  • Would adaptive operator rates and diversity-aware replacement raise the ceiling, given that fitness-guided selection currently lifts only the mean and minimum external scores and not the maximum?
