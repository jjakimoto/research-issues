    DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression

    ← Go backHacker News — Front Page: Full front-page turnover: MiMo…NextAgora: Git as Shared Memory for Collective AutoResearch →
    📰 Latest · 2026-09-21 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-21

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-17
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969

    DeepSeek pushes global KV cache down to 890 bytes/token (~1/4 of V4-Flash, ~1/437 of V1) by combining cross-layer KV/index reuse, FP4 KV, and a causal encoder-decoder that activates only 8B params during prefill — making million-token agent serving dramatically cheaper while claiming frontier-comparable agentic scores with openly released checkpoints.

    Executive summary

    DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash is a 552B-backbone multimodal MoE (plus 196B Engram parameters) supporting 1M-token contexts, activating 8B parameters per token during prefill and 16B during decode via a Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED) in which decoder global KV is projected from the final encoder hidden state. The headline contribution is KV cache compression: Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2) shares main KV, indexer K and Top-K indices across layers in three static modes (Full / Reindex / Reuse), and FP4 quantization-aware training on the main KV cache brings global KV to 890 bytes/token; SWA Bounded Replay cuts the persistent footprint to about 1/8. Pre-training uses a 45T-token multimodal corpus with sparse attention trained from scratch at 64K and no dense warmup; post-training is explicitly claimed to contain no algorithmic novelty, with all gains attributed to synthesized agent tasks and environments. Reported results put it at or above Opus-5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on several agentic benchmarks while trailing clearly on the hardest ones.

    Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED)

    The 40-layer backbone splits into a 20-layer causal encoder and a 20-layer decoder. Decoder global KV is not derived from each layer's own hidden state but projected from the final encoder layer's hidden state with layer-dependent weights, so prefill only needs to run the bottom half. SWA remains conventional per-layer; Decoder SWA Bounded Replay replays far fewer tokens, exploiting the finding that SWA's effective receptive field is much smaller than its theoretical window.

    • Inspired by YOCO; reduces prefill complexity by roughly half
    • 8B activated params in prefill vs 16B in decode
    • First two encoder layers use SWA only; the rest use CSA2

    CSA2 and cross-layer reuse

    CSA2 attacks KV cost along three multiplicative dimensions (entry size, sequence compression, layer reuse). Each layer is statically assigned Full, Reindex, or Reuse mode. Full computes its own main KV and fresh Top-K indices. Reindex reuses the preceding main KV and indexer K but rescores with its own indexer Q. Reuse reuses both KV and the Top-K indices and skips indexing entirely. All modes keep their own global Q and SWA KV.

    • Reuse-mode layers run only 15 fused kernels in prefill and 11 in decode
    • Contrasted with IndexCache, YOIO, HySparse — none covers all three dimensions
    • V4.1-Flash is pure CSA2, dropping V4's CSA+HCA hybrid, overlapping compression windows and absolute positional embedding in the compressor

    Hierarchical Sparse Indexer

    Used only in the CED decoder. The first Full-Mode decoder layer scores all causally visible positions, selects its own Top-512 indices, and additionally performs blockwise selection to build a shared candidate pool. Later Reindex layers search only inside that pool, making their per-query indexing cost constant rather than linear in context length. The restriction is applied identically in training and inference.

    • Example given: 2,048 blocks x 8 positions = 16,384 candidate positions
    • The initial full-range pass is still paid once

    FP4 main KV cache and other extensions

    Quantization-aware training extends from FP4 indexer Q/K to the main KV cache, using E2M1 with one E4M3 scale per 16 channels, quantized after RoPE; the paper argues dynamic range is ample because RMSNorm weights and the KV latent L2 norm bound magnitudes well below the format's range. SWA KV stays FP8 due to quantization sensitivity. Other extensions: Single-Pass mHC, Engram conditional memory, and DSpark speculative decoding.

    • FP4 main KV nearly halves storage vs V4's FP8 main KV
    • Engram: 196B params over 2 modules at layers 1 and 14, 8 hash heads, ~16M entries/head, FP8 tables
    • DSpark: 3 drafter blocks, 128-token window, 5 draft positions, trained after the backbone with the backbone frozen

    Training, data and optimization

    45T-token multimodal corpus, native multimodal from the start of LM pre-training, DeepSeek-ViT vision encoder trained from scratch with 2D-RoPE, linear patch projection (for Muon compatibility), RMSNorm and SwiGLU, plus a 9x pixel-unshuffle token reduction. MoE uses DeepSeekMoE experts with modality-specific auxiliary-loss-free load balancing. Optimizers: head-wise Muon for Q/K weights, Muon for other matrices, AdamW for non-matrix params, and a Sinkhorn-balancing update for Engram tables, embeddings and the prediction head.

    • Sparse attention trained from scratch at 64K, no dense warmup
    • Vision encoder frozen until the LR decay stage, then unfrozen at a lower LR

    Post-training and evaluation posture

    Post-training is deliberately unremarkable algorithmically — SFT, then RL, then on-policy distillation — with all effort in automated synthesis of verifiable tasks and containerized agent environments. A controllable reasoning-effort scalar (25-100) trades tokens for accuracy. Scaffold-robustness is tested across eight configurations from six families, and a multi-agent configuration is evaluated against wall-clock deadlines.

    • Raising effort lifts DeepSWE v1.1 from 66.0% to 74.2% and Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 82.4% to 90.6%
    • Multi-agent on ProgramBench: Almost@1 13.59% at 1h peaking at 30.04% at 8h vs 12.79%/20.39% single-agent
    • Multi-agent on FrontierSWE v2: Mean@5 13.50% at 1h to 32.90% at 20h vs 10.50% to 28.20%

    Key findings

    • Global KV cache footprint resident in HBM is 890 bytes per token — about 1/4 of DeepSeek-V4-Flash and ~437x smaller than DeepSeek-V1; SWA Bounded Replay cuts the persistent SSD/host footprint to ~1/8 of V4-Flash.
    • Architecture: 552B backbone params + 196B Engram params, 40 layers split 20 encoder / 20 decoder, 8B activated per token in prefill vs 16B in decode, contexts up to 1M tokens, 45T-token multimodal pre-training corpus.
    • Decode FLOPs stay nearly flat with context: extending context 256-fold from 4K to 1M raises single-token decode FLOPs by only about 1/4.
    • Agentic wins over closed models are narrow in the vendor's own table: DeepSWE v1.1 74.2% vs Opus-5 74.0% and GPT-5.6 Sol 73.0%; Terminal-Bench 2.1 90.6% vs Opus-5 89.1%; CyberGym 88.1%.
    • The gap to frontier closed models persists exactly where the tasks get hard: Terminal-Bench 3.0 30.0 vs Opus-5 43.3, Terminal-Bench 4.0 31.2 vs 51.8, HLE 36.8 vs 56.3, ProgramBench 20.3 vs 37.0.
    • Base model reportedly matches DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base on world knowledge/reasoning/coding with 1/3 total and 1/4 activated parameters.
    • Efficiency engineering: CSA2 Reuse-mode layers execute with only 15 kernels in prefill and 11 in decode; Mega-mHC halves activation memory traffic.

    Notable quotes

    We therefore introduce the Hierarchical Sparse Indexer, which is used only in the decoder of CED to reduce this repeated scoring during decode.
    We allocate 196B Engram parameters evenly across two modules.
    On Terminal-Bench 2.1, it achieves 90.6%, outperforming Opus-5 (89.1%) and GLM-5.3 (88.2%).
    Potential selection errors in CSA2 and approximate state reconstruction in SWA Bounded Replay may still cause capability degradation in untested boundary cases.

    Limitations

    • Every number is vendor-reported, run on DeepSeek's own evaluation framework and harness, including the competitor columns — nothing here is independently reproduced. The strongest agentic claims are sub-1-point margins reported without variance, while the paper itself shows scaffold choice moves scores by many points; treat those as ties, not wins.
    • Weights are released (huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash) but no license terms, training data, or evaluation harness are described, so 'open' means downloadable checkpoints, not a reproducible pipeline; the reader did not verify the HF repo or its license.
    • The authors concede robustness boundaries are uncharacterized: CSA2's sparse selection and SWA Bounded Replay are lossy by construction, and claims that degradation is negligible are asserted without ablation tables in the body.

    Open questions

    • How much of the efficiency story survives outside DeepSeek's own serving stack of fused kernels and RDMA Engram prefetch — what does a vLLM/SGLang deployment actually achieve?
    • Does cross-layer KV/index reuse hurt needle-in-haystack and multi-hop retrieval at 1M tokens? LongBench-V2 base score (45.2) is below DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base (51.5), the one long-context number moving the wrong way.
    • If post-training contributes no algorithmic novelty and all gains come from synthesized environments, how much of the agentic lead is contamination or harness co-design rather than model capability?
