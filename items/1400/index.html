Regimes in the Order Flow: Duration-Aware and Multivariate Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection for High-Frequency Markets

📰 Latest · 2026-09-09 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-09

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-07
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07989

On NASDAQ order flow, swapping BOCPD's constant hazard for a log-normal duration law consistently improves regime detection and forecasting — while the fancier multivariate BOCPDMS filter loses to two plain univariate filters, because adaptivity plus heavy-tailed innovations and short regimes produces wildly over-sized forecasts.

Executive summary

The report re-derives and implements Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection, extends it with a run-length-dependent hazard from an HSMM duration law, and with the multivariate model-selection framework BOCPDMS using Bayesian VARs and online hyperparameter learning. All three are applied to signed order flow of AAPL and MSFT from LOBSTER data (Aug-Nov 2022, plus June 2022 bivariate). The duration-aware extension works: a log-normal duration law beats both the geometric baseline and the Pareto alternative across assets, months and both calibration criteria, consistent with order flow having no characteristic timescale. The multivariate extension fails: bivariate BOCPDMS (MSE 0.8707) is beaten by two independent univariate BOCPDs (0.7705).

Motivation: long memory as a regime-shift process

Signed order flow has a non-summable power-law autocorrelation, the signature of long memory, attributed mainly to order splitting of metaorders. A long-memory series can be approximately generated by a regime-shift process with short-memory regimes and heterogeneous durations — but BOCPD's constant hazard forces geometric durations, a law with a characteristic timescale that order flow does not have.

  • Persistence documented by Lillo & Farmer (2004) and Bouchaud et al. (2004), lasting up to weeks
  • Builds directly on Tsaknaki et al. (2025), who used BOCPD on NASDAQ order-flow regimes
  • The geometric constraint excludes by construction the duration heterogeneity that makes the regime story work

Method: a duration-aware hazard

The constant hazard is replaced by a run-length-dependent hazard derived from an explicit duration law inside a hidden semi-Markov model. Pareto and log-normal laws are calibrated; the hazard vector is pre-computed before the filtering loop — faster than the reference C++ code, but it rules out online re-estimation and grows memory with T.

  • Three evaluation axes: covering metric, MSE, cumulative predictive log-likelihood
  • Monte Carlo validation confirms each filter recovers the duration law it assumes
  • numba-compiled loops cut calibration from hours to about fifteen minutes on much larger grids

Univariate results on NASDAQ order flow

LOBSTER data sampled in volume clock: buckets of N consecutive executions, observation = net signed volume (MSFT N=542, AAPL N=887). Hyperparameters grid-searched on 2022-08 under two criteria, then run out-of-sample. Log-normal BOSD wins calibration under both criteria on both assets and dominates out-of-sample MSE in most cells.

  • Pareto BOSD over-segments under MSE calibration (518 changepoints on MSFT 2022-10, average regime length 6.0) vs 117 for log-normal
  • Diebold-Mariano tests check significance of MSE differences
  • Over-segmentation traced to a threshold-only detection heuristic, fixed by also requiring a drop in the most likely run length

Multivariate BOCPDMS with Bayesian VARs

BOCPDMS replaces the run-length state by a (run length, model) pair over a model universe, giving a 2D posterior and Bayesian model averaging. The UPM is a Bayesian VAR with conjugate updates; two Inverse-Gamma hyperparameters are learned online by gradient ascent on the predictive Student-t.

  • Bivariate series in fixed 60-second buckets so MSFT and AAPL align (390 bars/day, T=8190 for June 2022)
  • Three noise specifications: diagonal, full observation covariance, covariance of expanding-window VAR innovations
  • No intercept in the BVAR; the frequentist baselines also run without one

Why the multivariate filter fails

First bivariate runs gave MSE above 1 (worse than forecasting zero), yet the forecast correlated 0.36 with the target (vs 0.42 for the frequentist VAR) — the problem was over-sized forecasts, not lack of signal. Within a short regime the AR coefficient is estimated on few points; multiplied by a volume spike it produces forecasts exceeding any value the series attains.

  • Prefix scales (6.33, 11.09) vs (9.68, 13.72) on the evaluation window — a heavy-tailed short prefix under-estimates scale
  • The top 1% of squared errors carries a much larger share of total MSE for BOCPDMS than for the frequentist VAR
  • Standardization fixed the symptom without changing the conclusion

Conclusion and proposed extensions

Duration-awareness is a clear win; multivariate model selection is a clear loss here, though flagged as provisional because the model universe was never populated with competing specifications — the very mechanism BOCPDMS exists for.

  • Add an intercept: preserves conjugacy and makes the multivariate model nest the univariate one that beat it
  • Populate the model universe with level-only, AR-only and level-plus-AR specifications
  • Combine the duration-aware hazard with the multivariate framework; learn duration parameters online
  • Lesson: verify a conjugate model on synthetic data first — this revealed an inconsistent shape convention in the reference code

Key findings

  • Log-normal BOSD beats constant-hazard BOCPD and Pareto BOSD in calibration for both assets and both criteria (MSFT MSE 0.8422 vs 0.8432/0.8552; AAPL 0.9075 vs 0.9213/0.9243).
  • Bivariate BOCPDMS VAR(2) reaches 0.8707 while two independent univariate BOCPDs reach 0.7705 — a ~12% gap favouring the simpler pair on both components.
  • The noise specification barely matters (0.8874-0.8916); the best frequentist expanding-window VAR gets 0.8791, still worse than the univariate pair.
  • No Diebold-Mariano comparison reaches conventional significance; ten of 7020 bars carry ~70% of the total absolute loss differential. Winsorizing the extreme 0.5% makes the univariate advantage significant (DM 3.50 MSFT, 8.60 AAPL).
  • Pareto BOSD over-segments on MSFT (390-518 changepoints, regime lengths 5.9-7.1) versus 48-118 for log-normal.
  • numba JIT cut calibration from hours to ~fifteen minutes; without it a bivariate run on T > 8000 is prohibitive.

Notable quotes

Here is a slightly counter-intuitive finding: the adaptivity of BOCPDMS, which is its strength on regime-switching data, becomes a liability when the innovations are heavy-tailed and the regimes are short.
The forecast therefore points in roughly the right direction but is grossly over-sized, and it is this over-sizing, not a lack of predictive content, that produces the large error.
None of the pairwise differences reaches conventional significance: on this sample, no model is demonstrably a better forecaster than another.

Limitations

  • The negative multivariate result is provisional: the model universe was never populated with competing specifications, and the BVAR had no intercept.
  • Narrow scope — two NASDAQ large caps, four months univariate, one month bivariate; true changepoints are unknown so the covering metric cannot be used out-of-sample.
  • The changepoint detection rule is an ad hoc heuristic, and the pre-computed hazard precludes online re-estimation.

Open questions

  • Does adding an intercept let the multivariate BVAR match the univariate filters that outperformed it?
  • Would a genuinely populated model universe reverse the negative verdict?
  • Can the duration-aware hazard be combined with the multivariate framework, with parameters learned online?
