LigBench: A Unified and Human-Aligned Benchmark for LLM-based Research Idea Generation

📰 Latest · 2026-08-16
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-16

Source: arXiv · 2026-08-13
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13136

LigBench turns research-idea evaluation into retrieval-grounded pairwise comparisons against 11,164 scored conference papers. It provides a useful reward-model direction for autoresearch, but still depends heavily on LLM judgments and the full evaluation pipeline was not yet released with the paper.

Executive summary

LigBench evaluates generated research ideas on rating, contribution, soundness, and novelty. It formalizes every idea into four common fields, retrieves comparable papers from PAIR-IQ, uses an LLM for pairwise judgments, and updates scores through an adapted Elo process. The results show that pairwise-evaluation ability is trainable, but also reveal that elaborate ideation frameworks do not necessarily beat direct prompting with a strong model.

PAIR-IQ reference corpus

PAIR-IQ contains 11,164 papers from ICLR 2024, ICLR 2025, and NeurIPS 2024, including accepted and rejected work. OpenReview rating, contribution, and soundness scores are projected onto a 0–5 scale and mean-shifted to reduce venue-year bias.

  • 57.3% of the corpus is posters, 36.6% rejected submissions, 4.3% spotlights, and 1.8% orals.
  • The largest topic groups are LLMs at 19.3%, training methods at 15.4%, and reinforcement learning at 13.4%.
  • Venue means differ enough to motivate debiasing; for example, mean rating ranges from 2.740 for ICLR 2024 to 2.918 for NeurIPS 2024.

Evaluation pipeline

An input idea is decomposed into Main Target, Core Breakthrough, Innovative Methods, and Experimental Design. Hybrid keyword and embedding retrieval finds similar reference papers, and repeated pairwise comparisons update the target’s score until convergence.

  • Rating, contribution, and soundness are updated through an Elo-style process with scores clamped to 0–5.
  • Novelty combines an LLM estimate with Semantic Scholar similarity; the similarity-derived component receives weight 0.7.
  • Standardization is intended to reduce the judge’s preference for longer or more polished idea descriptions.

Judge reliability

The held-out test contains 269 thematically similar paper pairs whose labels come from debiased OpenReview scores. Frontier models perform substantially better than untrained small models, while PAIR-IQ training raises small-model accuracy across all three dimensions.

  • GPT-5 reaches 0.820 rating accuracy and 0.826 soundness accuracy.
  • Trained Qwen2.5-7B rises to 0.714/0.710/0.712 from 0.514/0.496/0.488.
  • On 100 expert-judged pairs, LLM-human agreement is 71% for rating, 79% for contribution, and 73% for soundness.
  • GPT-5 errors tend to occur on close pairs: mean debiased score gaps are 0.114, 0.196, and 0.138.

What the benchmark says about ideation systems

Across 110 ideas per system, direct GPT-5.2 receives the highest rating and contribution scores among the reported systems. CoI and SciPIP use GPT-5 as a backbone but do not consistently improve on standalone GPT-5, suggesting that orchestration can constrain a strong model as well as help it.

  • GPT-5.2 scores 3.974 rating and 3.392 contribution.
  • CoI scores 3.468 rating and 2.996 contribution; SciPIP leads soundness at 3.243 but scores only 1.900 on contribution.
  • Accepted NeurIPS 2025 papers receive higher LigBench scores than rejected papers across all four dimensions in a 50-paper demonstration.

Key findings

  • PAIR-IQ provides 11,164 structured research ideas with debiased review-derived scores.
  • Task-specific training turns a near-random 7B pairwise judge into a roughly 71%-accurate judge across all three dimensions.
  • Human agreement is strongest for contribution at 79%, but none of the judge dimensions approaches perfect reliability.
  • Direct prompting with GPT-5 or GPT-5.2 beats the tested GPT-5-backed ideation frameworks on several dimensions.
  • Novelty scores are tightly clustered, reinforcing that semantic-distance-based novelty remains difficult to distinguish.

Notable quotes

Consequently, there is no unified framework to benchmark idea generation systems comprehensively.

Limitations

  • OpenReview scores are treated as ground truth after a simple venue-level mean shift, despite reviewer noise and selection effects.
  • Pairwise evaluation and idea formalization remain LLM-mediated and may carry model-specific biases.
  • Human validation covers only 100 pairs, and the acceptance demonstration covers 50 NeurIPS 2025 papers.
  • The PAIR-IQ dataset is available, but the complete LigBench pipeline was only promised for a later release.
  • Semantic similarity is an imperfect proxy for conceptual novelty.

Open questions

  • Does LigBench predict expert judgments on genuinely new, unsubmitted ideas rather than reconstructed paper ideas?
  • How stable are rankings across judge models, prompts, retrieval pools, and adversarially polished descriptions?
  • Can a reward model trained on PAIR-IQ improve downstream scientific discoveries rather than merely imitate conference review scores?
