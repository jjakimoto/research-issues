OmniHallu: Unified Hallucination Detection for Cross-Modal Comprehension and Generation in Multimodal Large Language Models

📰 Latest · 2026-09-11 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-11

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-10
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11244

OmniHallu is the first hallucination-detection framework and benchmark to span six bidirectional cross-modal tasks (image/video/audio, both comprehension and generation) at claim level, and it shows a hard modality gradient — image > video > audio — while a GRPO-trained 7B verifier cuts expensive expert-model calls by 66% for ~1.5 points of Mac.F1.

Executive summary

Existing hallucination benchmarks are locked to one modality or one task direction, so nobody could compare image, video, and audio hallucination, or comprehension against generation, under one protocol. OmniHallu introduces a 10,000-sample claim-level benchmark covering I2T, V2T, A2T, T2I, T2V, T2A, plus a multi-agent decompose-verify-aggregate detector using modality-specific experts and GPT-5.2 as the aggregator. It beats the strongest per-task baseline by +3.4 to +8.1 Mac.F1, with the largest gains exactly where individual models are weakest (audio). A compact Qwen2.5-VL-7B verifier trained with GRPO reproduces most of the pipeline's decision boundary, cutting API calls from 5-8 to 1.7-2.8 per sample and cost from ~$0.12 to ~$0.05.

The problem: modality-siloed hallucination evaluation

Hallucination detection work has been fragmented by modality and by task direction. Image benchmarks (POPE, AMBER) ignore video and audio; video (FactVC) and audio (AHLALM) evaluations operate in isolation; and almost all address comprehension only, neglecting generation, despite both sharing hallucination patterns rooted in insufficient perception and reasoning.

  • Prior largest comparable claim-level benchmark, MHaluBench, has 420 instances over 2 modalities; OmniHallu-Bench has 10,000 over 4 modalities and 6 tasks.
  • A shared four-type taxonomy — Object, Attribute, Relation, Event — applies identically to all six tasks, enabling direct cross-modal comparison.
  • Unified formulation: an output is hallucinated if any extractable semantic claim is unsupported by, or contradicts, the input.

Building OmniHallu-Bench

The benchmark is stratified: 60% comprehension / 40% generation, image:video:audio at 5:3:2, and hallucination types at object 35%, attribute 25%, event 25%, relation 15%. Source media come from standard datasets (COCO Captions, Nocaps, Flickr30k; MSVD, MSRVTT, VATEX; AudioCaps, ClothoV2, AudioSetCaps). Generation samples come from DALL-E 3, SD 3.5 Large, Midjourney v6, Open-Sora 1.2, CogVideoX-5B, Make-an-Audio, AudioGPT, and AudioLCM.

  • Chain-of-Thought atomic claim decomposition with self-reflection, then three trained annotators per sample; retained only on full consensus.
  • ~24.3% of initial samples were dropped for annotator disagreement; pre-filter Fleiss' kappa was 0.89 (image), 0.86 (video), 0.83 (audio).
  • Final split: 4,000 human-curated and 6,000 model-generated but human-audited samples.
  • Average atomic claims per sample: 5.6 (V2T) down to 3.1 (T2A); overall hallucination rate 42.8%.

The multi-agent detector and the trainable verifier

Three stages: (1) Atomic Claim Decomposition via GPT-4.1, validated against human reference claims on a 300-sample subset at mean coverage 0.92 and redundancy 1.15. (2) Modality-aware expert verification: Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro for open-set object checks on images, an ensemble of Qwen2.5-VL-72B / InternVL2.5-78B / GPT-4.1 for attribute, relation, and event claims; video claims are reformulated into targeted QA queries; audio uses Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, GAMA, SALMONN. (3) Reasoning-based aggregation by GPT-5.2. Separately, a Qwen2.5-VL-7B verifier is GRPO-trained to output SUP / UNSUP / ABS with a three-term reward.

  • Equal-weight voting is chosen deliberately because no reliable cross-model confidence calibration metric exists.
  • Verifier trained 3 epochs, lr 1e-5, GRPO group size K=8, on a disjoint 6,000/1,000/3,000 split with no source-media overlap.

Results: a hard modality gradient

The framework wins on all six tasks, +3.4 to +8.1 Mac.F1 over the best per-task baseline, significant at p<0.01 (bootstrap, 10,000 resamples). Image tasks are easiest, video moderate, audio hardest. Within each modality, comprehension beats its generation counterpart. The largest absolute gains are on audio, which the authors read as evidence that multi-agent verification matters most when individual models are weak. Ablations show removing atomic claim decomposition costs more than removing multi-expert voting, making decomposition quality the pipeline's primary bottleneck.

  • Backbone matters: GPT-5.2 beats GPT-4.1 as aggregator by 4.5-6.6 points — but even with GPT-4.1 the framework still beats baselines on video/audio by 1.3-1.9 points.
  • Expert count ablation: 1 to 2 to 3 experts lifts I2T from 77.62 to 80.54 to 82.95; A2T from 68.20 to 71.18 to 74.11.
  • Swapping 72B/78B video experts for 7B/8B ones collapses Mac.F1 from 76.58/74.78 to 62.54/59.25.
  • On T2V, hallucination prevalence scales with prompt complexity: 23.2% low, 28.7% medium, 40.3% high density.

Why it matters: a cost-performance spectrum

The trainable verifier gives three operating points rather than one: verifier-only (78.94 Mac.F1, zero expert calls), filter/hybrid (80.52 with experts for only 34.2% of samples), and aggregator mode (83.15, the best overall). GRPO beats SFT by +7.5 and DPO by +2.1. Cost drops from ~$0.12 to ~$0.05 per sample and latency from ~15s to ~6s. Error analysis on 50 misclassified samples per task pins distinct bottlenecks: image detection is perception-limited, video reasoning-limited, audio tool-limited.

  • The gradient is attributed jointly to intrinsic modality complexity and to the maturity gap between 70B+ vision experts and current audio experts.
  • Removing the calibration reward barely dents Mac.F1 (-1.8) but raises ECE from 0.042 to 0.154.

Key findings

  • A consistent modality gradient image > video > audio holds across all metrics: Mac.F1 81.8-83.0 (image), 75.6-77.1 (video), 73.4-74.1 (audio).
  • The framework's largest absolute gains are on the weakest modality — +7.8 (A2T) and +8.1 (T2A) Mac.F1 over the best Self-Check baseline, vs +4.1 (I2T) and +3.4 (T2I) over GPT-4.1 UNIHD.
  • Atomic claim decomposition is the biggest single contributor: removing it costs 6.58-7.93 Mac.F1, consistently more than removing multi-expert voting (4.47-5.31).
  • The GRPO-trained 7B verifier in filter mode hits 80.52 Mac.F1 while invoking the full expert ensemble on only 34.2% of samples; GRPO beats SFT by +7.5 and DPO by +2.1.
  • Cost/latency drop from 5-8 API calls, ~15s, ~$0.12 per sample to 1.7-2.8 calls, ~6s, ~$0.05 — ~$1,200 to ~$500 for the full benchmark.
  • OmniHallu-Bench has a 42.8% overall hallucination rate (48.1% generation, 39.2% comprehension), with Fleiss' kappa 0.89/0.86/0.83 before consensus filtering.
  • Difficulty by type is object > attribute > event > relation everywhere; the framework improves most on relation hallucinations (+9.2 points on average).

Limitations

  • The four-type taxonomy compresses modality-specific phenomena and compound errors — video events could be split into temporal ordering, action omission, and causal errors.
  • The text-centric formulation excludes non-text pairings such as image-to-audio and video-to-image.
  • Results depend heavily on the reasoning backbone and available experts: vision experts are far larger than audio experts, so part of the reported gradient reflects tool capability rather than modality difficulty, and majority voting cannot correct errors shared by all experts.

Open questions

  • How much of the image > video > audio gradient would survive if audio experts matched vision experts in scale — is the gap intrinsic or a tooling artifact?
  • Can a confidence-calibrated or unequal-weight voting scheme beat equal-weight majority voting, which the authors avoided only because no reliable cross-model calibration metric exists?
  • Since decomposition quality is the primary bottleneck, what would a purpose-trained (rather than GPT-4.1-prompted) claim decomposer buy — especially for audio, where coverage is lowest at 0.89?
