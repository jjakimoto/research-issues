The Reconfiguration Premium: Co-movement Structure as an Unspanned Dimension of the Variance Risk Premium

📰 Latest · 2026-08-24 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-20
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20020

Every priced co-movement measure in use (index variance, average pairwise correlation, option-implied correlation, absorption ratio) is a function of correlation-matrix eigenvalues only and is blind to rotation of the eigenbasis; this paper builds an eigenspace-rotation index that is essentially unspanned by the traded implied-correlation surface (5.0-6.7% R-squared) yet couples to the variance risk premium at t = +5.40, and shows the premium is prepaid two to three quarters ahead of the volatility it anticipates.

Executive summary

The paper measures how fast the S&P 500's co-movement structure is rewritten as the mean squared sine of the principal angles between the subdominant (modes 2-4) eigenspaces of consecutive twelve-month correlation matrices, using a dual-space Gram decomposition on a June 1994 - December 2025 monthly constituent panel. A typical month rewrites about a fifth of the classification (mean 0.194, roughly a 26-degree tilt) and carries four-fifths forward. Standardized and smoothed over three months, the index prices the log variance risk premium at beta = +0.195, Newey-West t = +5.40, R-squared = 0.504 (n = 365), while correlating no higher than 0.316 with any level gauge and being only 5.0-6.7% recoverable from COR3M/COR1M/DSPX. Only the persistent component prices (t = +4.92 vs +0.30 for the high-frequency residual); the mechanism is prepayment -- the whole contemporaneous association sits in the implied leg (t = +4.57) with realized variance unmoved (+0.62), and forward realized variance rises in a hump peaking at +0.155 around h = 7-8 months. Three pre-registered nulls hold: no timing alpha, no crash protection, and a downside variant that collapses once downside intensity is controlled.

How the reconfiguration (REC) index is constructed

Start from the 12-month correlation matrix. Because T << N, the eigenstructure is taken in the dual (Gram) space: a 12x12 Gram matrix whose nonzero spectrum coincides with that of the correlation matrix. Stock-space loadings on the subdominant modes 2-4 are QR-orthonormalized. Mode 1 is dropped by construction because its loading vector is near-uniform and positive in essentially every window (the market factor). Rotation is measured only on the intersection of consecutive cross-sections; the singular values of A'B are the cosines of the principal angles, and REC = (1/3) * sum of sin^2(theta_i), which lies in [0,1].

  • Invariant to basis choice within the subspace, sign flips, and relabeling of modes inside the 3-mode block -- it tracks the subspace, not any single eigenvector.
  • Immune to index membership changes: entering and exiting names contribute zero rotation because only the shared cross-section is used.
  • 1 - REC is exactly the share of last month's structure retained, so the metric is a proportion, not an abstract distance.
  • Rotation is concentrated in one axis: median principal angles 5.9, 11.8, 42.8 degrees; in March 2022 two axes moved 7 and 10 degrees while the third moved 71.
  • K = 3 justified against a DEFLATED Marchenko-Pastur edge: modes 2 and 3 sit above bulk in 100% and 97.3% of windows, mode 4 in 25.5%, mode 5 in 0.8%.
  • The reported series is a three-month moving average chosen at the LEFT edge of a K = 3-6 plateau rather than at its maximum; the raw series alone already prices.

What it is orthogonal to, and why level measures cannot see it

Simple correlations with every available level gauge top out at +0.316 (smoothed REC vs VIX). Projecting the index on the traded implied-correlation surface recovers R-squared = 0.059 (raw) / 0.050 (smoothed) on COR3M+COR1M (n = 240) and 0.052 / 0.067 adding DSPX (n = 139) -- at most 6.7%. The theoretical reason: any level functional is a Rayleigh quotient with a near-positive weight vector, so it overlaps the market mode almost entirely and the sign-balanced body modes barely at all -- median squared overlap 0.750 on mode 1 for average correlation, with body-mode overlaps 0.013, 0.004, 0.002.

  • The Davis-Kahan alternative (rotation as a mechanical artifact of compressing spectral gaps) is tested, not waved away: gaps enter with the predicted negative signs (t = -3.14, -2.15), but the full mechanical model explains only R-squared = 0.121 of index variance, and adding the whole battery leaves the coupling at t = +4.92.
  • Conditional sorts kill the 'shadow of stressed states' reading: coupling in the calmest quintile of market-mode variance share is +3.96; the narrow-gap quintile is among the WEAKER cells (+2.02).
  • Absolute calibration: with K = 3, N = 421, chance retention is 0.0071 (verified with 3,000 simulated pairs), against observed one-month retention of 0.8056 -- about 113x chance.
  • The option market feels the state variable without carrying it: regressing the COR3M-minus-COR1M slope on smoothed REC gives t = -3.46, beta = -0.910 index points per s.d. against a mean slope of +3.409.

The pricing evidence: which component of rotation is compensated

Baseline regression of the log variance risk premium on the standardized smoothed index plus realized volatility and average correlation: beta_hat = +0.195, Newey-West (12 lags) s.e. 0.036, t = +5.40, 95% CI [0.124, 0.266], R-squared = 0.504, n = 365; raw series beta_hat = +0.126, t = +4.02. Both controls enter negatively and significantly. Splitting the index into a persistent part (3-month trailing average) and a high-frequency residual, only the persistent part prices: t = +4.92 (logs) vs +0.30.

  • Dose-response in subspace dimension matches the spectral classification: t = +4.31, +5.40, +2.24, +1.97 for K = 2, 3, 4, 5.
  • Smoothing sweep over K = 1..24 gives t from 3.09 to 5.46 -- significant at every window, so no smoothing choice is load-bearing.
  • Pace, not distance: coupling decays from t = +5.40 (k = 1) to +1.93 (k = 12) and +0.16 (k = 24); entered jointly, the one-month index holds t = +4.76 while the twelve-month index falls to +1.38.
  • A permanent structural core -- retained alignment plateauing near 0.07 (about 9-10x chance) between k = 12 and 24 -- is NOT priced; only the transient component's revision rate is.
  • Levels-vs-logs: baseline R-squared 0.504 in logs vs 0.149 in levels; Oct 2008-Dec 2009 alone is 46% of the total squared deviation of the levels premium.

Mechanism: prepaid fear, and how the forward inference is done

The premium's coupling sits entirely in the implied leg on impact: t = +5.12 (log VRP), +4.57 (log implied variance), +0.62 (log realized variance) at h = 0. Over the following quarters implied variance decays while realized variance builds, the legs crossing around h = 3, and the premium turns negative and troughs eight months out (t = -2.68 at h = 8) before dying at h = 11-12. Forward log realized variance regressed on the smoothed index with two of its own lags gives a smooth hump: beta_hat = +0.050 (h = 1) rising to +0.155 at h = 8 (about 17% in variance terms) and falling to +0.098 at h = 12.

  • Because Newey-West alone over-rejects here, significance is judged against a SIMULATED null in which innovation pairs are drawn jointly and i.i.d. so the Stambaugh channel survives while all lead-lag structure is destroyed; empirical 95% critical values run 2.13-2.73.
  • The paper reports a bootstrap design it REJECTED: block resampling preserves within-block lead-lag structure -- the alternative itself -- pushing critical values to 3.63-5.59 so nothing would be significant.
  • Analytic Stambaugh check: index AR(1) 0.719, innovation correlation +0.025, implied bias 0.2% of the coefficient at n = 365.
  • AR(4) robustness: peak +0.148 at h = 8, clearing the null at h = 1-9 (p <= 0.027), so the forecast claim is made for 1-9 months only.
  • Predictability expires exactly where the subspace decorrelates (h = 11-12), matching the k-horizon saturation.
  • Scope condition stated up front: against horizon-matched forward index variance-swap payoffs the index registers |t| <= 1.2 at 1/3/6 months and |t| <= 1.9 against forward one-month cap-weighted index realized variance.

Pre-registered negative results (the part most papers omit)

A downside index built identically from a both-down realized-semicovariance Gram prices alone at t = +3.18 smoothed but loses the horse race (+2.23 vs Pearson +4.83) and collapses under mechanical controls: its persistent component goes +2.19 -> +0.08 once down-breadth and realized semivariance are included, while the Pearson persistent component survives (+4.92 -> +4.16). A cross-sectional-shuffle placebo (100 rebuilds) prices at mean t = +1.14, s.d. 0.29, against the actual +1.97 in the same spec -- so something structural exists but is not separable from intensity at T = 12. A 1 + 0.5*z exposure overlay on the short-variance payoff gives a paired t of -1.85 over 329 months; step tilt -0.94, top-quartile-only -2.23. Over the same months the worst 5% carry a mean standardized index reading of -0.47.

  • No timing alpha, no crash protection, and no separable tail channel -- all three specified before testing.
  • Two further nulls (no out-of-sample covariance-matrix improvement; no cross-sectional pricing of firm-level exposure) are disclosed as coming from the underlying thesis and explicitly NOT claimed as results of this paper.
  • Generative-AI disclosure in the front matter: analysis code, figures and drafting used LLM assistance; design, hypotheses, pre-registered nulls and interpretation are the author's, with every reported quantity regenerated from source.

Economic magnitude and what is actually rotating

A variance-harvest attribution sells variance at each month-end over July 1998 - November 2025 (329 months, overall capture 34.0%), conditioned on the EXPANDING-window standardized index so nothing is look-ahead. Sixty-four percent of 27 years of accumulated premium sits in the highest-rotation quartile, which is simultaneously the safest: loss frequency 0.15 vs 0.29 in Q1, mean loss given loss -0.0102 vs -0.0265, worst month -0.032 vs -0.099. A VIX-median double sort shows the rotation spread is flat when implied vol is low and wide when it is high. The author frames this as separating 'explained fear' from 'unexplained fear' and insists it is attribution and conditioning, not alpha.

  • Identity of the rotating structure: labeling each subdominant mode by its dominant sector, mode 2 is utilities in 63.6% of the 368 windows and energy in 17.7%; mode 3 is diffuse in 52.2%, mode 4 in 71.2% -- independent corroboration of K = 3.
  • The permanent core is economically recognizable as a duration-and-defensives axis, with a commodity axis as its most frequent alternative.
  • Three eliminations narrow the priced motion to continuous, diffuse revision of which firms load on the operative axes: discrete label switching correlates only +0.187 and leaves the index at t = +4.89; spectral-gap compression is bounded at 12.1%; volatility-rank churn is orthogonal and unpriced.
  • Practitioner framing: the market-removed residual of the correlation matrix is what the crowding literature measures, so the priced object is the turnover of crowding structure.

Key findings

  • The measure is a pure eigenvector-motion statistic -- mean squared sine of the principal angles between consecutive modes 2-4 eigenspaces, computed via a 12x12 dual Gram and evaluated only on the shared cross-section -- so it is invariant to basis rotation, sign flips, mode relabeling and index membership changes, and orthogonal by construction to every eigenvalue-only level measure.
  • Sample mean 0.194 (s.d. 0.119, max 0.526 in July 2002): a typical month rewrites about one fifth of the classification, roughly a 26-degree tilt; observed one-month retention of 0.8056 is about 113x the random-subspace chance level of 0.0071.
  • Pricing: beta_hat = +0.195 (t = +5.40, 95% CI [0.124, 0.266]), R^2 = 0.504, n = 365, with realized vol and average correlation entering NEGATIVELY -- the coupling is identified against, not through, volatility level. Level gauges correlate at most +0.316 and the traded implied-correlation surface spans only 5.0-6.7%.
  • Only the persistent (3-month) component prices: t = +4.92 vs +0.30 for the high-frequency residual. Cumulative distance is not priced either -- the coupling falls to +1.93 at k = 12 and +0.16 at k = 24, and jointly the one-month index (+4.76) drives out the twelve-month index (+1.38).
  • Prepayment mechanism: the whole contemporaneous association sits in implied variance (t = +4.57) with realized variance unmoved (+0.62); forward log realized variance follows a hump peaking at +0.155 at h = 8, clearing a simulated null at h = 1-9 under both AR(2) and AR(4) controls, and dying at h = 11-12 -- exactly where the subspace has decorrelated.
  • The Davis-Kahan 'mechanical rotation' alternative is bounded rather than dismissed: gaps enter with predicted negative signs but explain only 12.1% of index variance, and the coupling is numerically unchanged (t = +4.92) with the full mechanical battery as controls.
  • Attribution, not alpha: 64% of 27 years of variance-selling premium sits in the top rotation quartile (capture 52.7%) which is also the safest, yet every timing rule fails (linear overlay paired t = -1.85, top-quartile-only -2.23) and the worst 5% of months carry a mean standardized reading of -0.47.

Notable quotes

The controls are not incidental: both enter negatively and significantly, so the coupling is identified against, not through, the level of volatility.
Short-horizon rotation drives out cumulative rotation, not the reverse.
Contemporaneously the entire association sits in implied variance; realized variance has not moved.
Whatever the premium is paying for, it is not insurance against the left tail of its own payoff.

Limitations

  • The premium object has mismatched legs in horizon and weighting (one-month cap-weighted VIX-squared implied variance against twelve-month equal-weighted constituent realized variance); the author states outright that this is not the canonical variance risk premium, and against horizon-matched forward variance-swap payoffs the index has |t| <= 1.2 (1/3/6 months). Log is primary; the levels premium has R^2 of only 0.149 and Oct 2008-Dec 2009 accounts for 46% of its squared deviation.
  • The estimation universe is survivor-tilted by the coverage filters (effective cross-section averaging 421 names), so the realized leg describes the persistent large-cap cross-section rather than the index as constituted at each date; evidence is one market and one premium, and the implied-correlation tests rest on only 240 and 139 monthly observations.
  • The downside/tail verdict is an estimator statement, not a claim about the world: at T = 12 with roughly six down-observations per name, downside structure and downside intensity are not identified apart. The priced object is identified only by ELIMINATION, one elimination relies on a licensed daily Bloomberg panel and is therefore not reproducible from the public repo, and part of the K = 3 -> 5 dose-response decline could be subspace-dimension mechanics.

Open questions

  • Direct measurement of loading drift -- the priced object is pinned down only by eliminating discrete axis substitution, spectral-gap compression and volatility-rank churn; a constructive estimator of continuous loading revision is the natural next step and would test the 'turnover of crowding structure' reading.
  • Does the result survive on a genuinely horizon- and weighting-matched premium (one-month cap-weighted index variance-swap payoffs) and on markets other than the S&P 500, where the |t| <= 1.9 scope condition currently blocks the canonical VRP claim?
  • Can the tail hypothesis be adjudicated at a resolution where the observation count is not binding, and is there a T large enough to separate downside co-movement structure from downside intensity?
