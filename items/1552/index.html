Coding Agents Have Converged: Why the SWE-bench Leaderboard Can No Longer Order Its Top Entries, and What to Measure Instead

📰 Latest · 2026-09-16 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-16

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-15
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394

An audit of 254 public SWE-bench submissions shows the top of the Verified leaderboard is statistically unorderable — no adjacent pair in the top thirty is separable — and that a submission's score reflects the model-scaffold pair, not the model, so small leaderboard gaps should not drive model selection or procurement.

Executive summary

The authors re-analyse the per-instance verdicts SWE-bench publishes for 254 submissions across four splits (no models are run) to ask whether the displayed ranks can be read. On Verified the top ten share 285 successes and 51 failures, leaving only 164 discriminating instances; frontier solution sets are nested at median 0.935 versus a score-implied baseline of 0.774. Exact paired McNemar tests separate none of the 29 adjacent top-thirty pairs, while the wider-ability Test split separates 14 of 23 — so irresolvability is a property of the converged comparison set, not of the benchmark family. They also show within-model scaffold ranges up to 29.8pp against an 8.8pp top-thirty spread, and release a five-step audit protocol plus design recommendations.

Data and provenance

Every public SWE-bench split publishing per-instance verdicts, retrieved 30 July 2026: Verified (134 submissions, 500 instances), Lite (84, 299), Test (24, 2,294) and Multimodal (12 of 22 usable, 301). All entries were graded by the same official harness on the same instances, which is what licenses the paired tests.

  • Verified and Lite share only 93 instances (31% of Lite), so Lite is a largely independent replication.
  • Only 61 of 134 Verified submissions (46%) carry a single usable model tag; tags are inconsistent.
  • Hand normalisation yielded 34 scaffolds, 30 models, 55 occupied cells, five with more than one submission.

Effective size: most instances cannot separate the leaders

An instance is degenerate for a comparison set if all or none of its members resolve it; n_eff counts the non-degenerate instances. Over all 134 Verified submissions n_eff/n = 0.94, but over the top ten it is 0.33 (285 solved by all, 51 by none, 164 left) and over the top two 0.07. KR-20 is 0.994 over all 134 submissions but 0.475 over the top ten.

  • n_eff is a property of the (benchmark, comparison set) pair, not of the benchmark alone.
  • The collapse holds on all four splits, including the 2,294-instance Test set.

Nesting, not complementarity

Nesting coefficient cov(A,B)=|A∩B|/|B| is compared against the score-implied baseline |A|/n. Median nesting is 0.935 at the frontier (baseline 0.774, excess +0.161) and exceeds baseline in every ability band. The union of the top two systems' solutions is 414 instances against 396 for the best single system, and 449 for the top ten.

  • Excess over baseline is largest in the weakest band (+0.342) but absolute nesting peaks at the frontier, exactly where the ranking is read.

Alternative explanations tested (IRT, defects, contamination)

A 2PL model on the 134x468 non-constant matrix converges (accuracy 0.868 vs 0.550 base rate; corr(theta, score)=0.971) and peaks at theta = -0.62, carrying 2.3x more information about a median system (I=278) than a top-ten one (I=118). Degenerate instances have higher mean discrimination (2.52 vs 1.82).

  • Of the 285 instances all top-ten systems resolve, 49% were rated '<15 min' and none '>4 hours'; of the 51 none resolves, 14% '<15 min' and 33% over an hour.
  • Correlation between PR year and top-ten solve rate is -0.069 (95% bootstrap CI [-0.148, +0.012]).

The score belongs to the model-scaffold pair

Within-model scaffold range has a median of 78 instances (15.6pp); claude-3-5-sonnet spans 168-317 across nine scaffolds (149 instances, 29.8pp). Within-scaffold model range median is 60 instances (12.0pp). The whole top-thirty spread is 8.8pp. An additive fit on the connected core gives R2=0.989 with a scaffold-effect range of 37.6pp against 47.9pp for models (ratio 0.78). Paired difference-in-differences bootstraps (100,000 resamples) leave six of nine interactions Holm-significant.

  • Repeat submissions of the same scaffold+model differ by 13, 15, 27, 41 and 116 instances (median 27, 5.4pp).
  • epam-ai-run leads sweagent by 95 instances at claude-3-5-sonnet and 51 at claude-4-sonnet (interaction +44, Holm p=0.0035).
  • agentless vs epam-ai-run reverses across models (-75.5 instances); autocoderover likewise (-83); both adjusted p<0.001.
  • The design is observational; 54% of submissions cannot be placed in the factorial.

Tiers and design implications

Exact McNemar separates none of the 29 adjacent Verified top-thirty pairs (median gap one instance, median discordance 61); the same holds on Lite and Multimodal, but 14 of 23 on Test. A leader-based rule gives three descriptive tiers (8/12/10 systems). Retiring degenerate instances widens the visible spread from 8.8 to 18.7pp and reorders three pairs but adds no power.

  • Across all 435 top-thirty pairs, 194 (44.6%) are separable uncorrected and 41 (9.4%) survive Holm; among adjacent pairs it is 0 either way, smallest adjacent p = 0.545.
  • Ten leading adjacent pairs need an upper-median 52x more instances (~26,000).
  • Recommendations: report n_eff, add nesting-breaking instances, record (model, scaffold, version) machine-readably, publish tiers, grade from machine-readable artefacts.

Key findings

  • Over the SWE-bench Verified top ten, only 164 of 500 instances are discriminating (n_eff/n = 0.33): 285 are solved by all ten and 51 by none; over the top two n_eff/n falls to 0.07.
  • Frontier solution sets are strongly nested (median cov 0.935 vs a score-implied baseline of 0.774); the top two together solve 414 instances vs 396 for the best single system.
  • Exact paired McNemar separates 0 of 29 adjacent top-thirty pairs on Verified (and on Lite and Multimodal), but 14 of 23 on the 2,294-instance Test split.
  • Within-model scaffold ranges reach 149 instances (29.8pp) for claude-3-5-sonnet across nine scaffolds, versus an 8.8pp spread across the entire top thirty; the replication floor is a median of 27 instances (5.4pp).
  • Six of nine paired difference-in-differences interaction tests survive Holm correction, and two scaffold comparisons reverse sign across models (adjusted p<0.001).
  • Rescoring on only the 209 instances non-degenerate for the top twenty widens the spread to 18.7pp yet separable pairs remain 0 of 29.

Notable quotes

A leaderboard whose purpose is comparison does not record what was compared in machine-readable form
Degeneracy could in principle coexist with specialisation: systems might solve overlapping-but-different sets. They do not.
The observed scaffold ordering thus depends on the model. A fixed scaffold correction cannot recover a model ranking from these submissions.
Retirement is worth doing for evaluation cost and to stop scores drifting into a compressed range that invites false precision, but it adds no power, and advertising the wider spread as sharper discrimination would mislead readers.

Limitations

  • The model x scaffold design is observational and reconstructed from incomplete public metadata — 54% of Verified submissions cannot be placed in the factorial, so scaffold effects are not causal.
  • One run per submission: between-system differences cannot be separated from run-to-run variance, and non-rejection does not establish equivalence.
  • Scope is one benchmark family, one harness, and voluntarily submitted entries.

Open questions

  • Would a controlled factorial running the same scaffolds over the same models under one harness confirm that scaffold and model contribute comparably?
  • What kinds of curated instances actually break frontier nesting?
  • Do the measured magnitudes replicate on multi-domain suites and private internal benchmarks?
