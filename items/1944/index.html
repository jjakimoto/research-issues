    The Teacher Is a Direction, Not a Destination: Extrapolating RL-Induced Representation Residuals in On-Policy Distillation

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-02

    Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-09-29
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36484

    If you distill an RL run back into its own base model (or merge RL experts that share a base), RIDE regresses the student's hidden states toward teacher + 0.25 x (teacher - base) at every layer. That costs one extra frozen forward pass, and the student matches or beats the RL teacher on AIME/AMC. Logit-space reward extrapolation (ExOPD) does the opposite: it pushes the student below its own teacher on all four model pairs.

    Executive summary

    Standard on-policy distillation (OPD) treats the RL-trained teacher as the endpoint. Output-space extrapolation methods (ExOPD) try to overshoot the teacher by up-weighting the implicit log-ratio reward, but they get unstable. RIDE instead measures the RL-induced change as the layerwise hidden-state difference between the teacher and its pre-RL checkpoint on the student's own prefixes. It then regresses the student toward targets displaced beyond the teacher along that residual, using a single coefficient lambda (lambda=1 recovers OPRD). The test covers four base/RL-teacher pairs (1.5B-4B; Qwen, Llama and Phi lineages) on AIME24/AIME25/AIMO. RIDE is the only method whose mean Avg@16 lies above the teacher on every pair. It beats OPRD by 0.97-4.06 points and ExOPD by 9.1 points on average.

    Problem: the LM head hides most of what RL changed

    In the same-initialization setting (teacher = RL(base), student initialized at base), the teacher-minus-base difference measures what RL contributed. Generalized OPD / ExOPD extrapolate this difference in log-probability space. The paper argues this is measured in the wrong place, for two reasons. First, the LM head reweights hidden-state changes according to its anisotropic singular spectrum. Second, the sampled-token log-ratio is a noisy estimate, and the extrapolation coefficient amplifies that noise.

    • On R1-Distill-1.5B, the head's 512 weakest directions carry 79.8% of the teacher-to-base residual's hidden-state energy (vs 33.3% under isotropy) but only 30.0% of its centered-logit energy.
    • The residual keeps only 0.59 of the head gain of an equal-norm isotropic direction. The centered head's singular values span 71.7 to 1.8, so the residual is attenuated by a factor of up to about 40.
    • A logit target puts no constraint on layers below the final one.
    • Proposition 4.1: the ExOPD advantage variance includes a (lambda-1)^2 Var[rho] term. This term does not vanish even when the student matches the teacher, unless RL left that next-token distribution unchanged.

    Method: RIDE (RL-Induced Direction Extrapolation)

    On each student rollout, the frozen teacher and the frozen pre-RL base run on identical tokens. The residual at layer l and position t is Delta = h_T - h_B. The target is h* = h_T + (lambda-1)*Delta = lambda*h_T + (1-lambda)*h_B. The loss is a dimension-normalized squared error between student and target hidden states, averaged over all L layers and the last k=2,000 response positions. Under a shared linear head, the ExOPD logit target is exactly the head image of the RIDE target, so ExOPD is a lossy projection of RIDE.

    • lambda=1 recovers OPRD (on-policy representation distillation) exactly, so any gain over OPRD comes from the residual term alone.
    • Reward-and-penalty view (Prop. 4.2): per position, RIDE maximizes (lambda-1)<h-h_T, Delta> - 0.5||h-h_T||^2, a linear directional reward with a quadratic proximity penalty around the teacher.
    • Given a rollout, the gradient is deterministic (zero conditional variance for any lambda), unlike the sampled-token score-function estimator.
    • The loss is multiplied by lambda^-2 so that the initial loss equals OPRD's (Remark G.1).
    • Cost vs OPRD: one extra frozen forward pass of the base on the teacher worker. h* is formed in place, so transfer volume is unchanged (a T x L x d hidden-state tensor).

    Training and evaluation setup

    Four base -> RL-teacher pairs: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B -> JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B, and Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.2-3B and Phi-4-mini, each RL-trained with the same JustRL recipe (Just-* teachers). Within a pair, base, teacher and student share architecture, tokenizer and head. Across pairs, depth, width, vocabulary and pre-training lineage differ. Prompts come from DAPO-Math-17K, and every method uses the same 500-step budget. The paper evaluates only math; it reports no code benchmarks.

    • Rollouts: 8 prompts/step x 2 responses, temperature 1.0, max response 16,384 tokens.
    • Optimizer: AdamW, peak LR 1e-5, 3% linear warm-up then cosine decay; bf16, FSDP (verl); 4x H200.
    • Eval: Avg@16 at temperature 0.7 on AIME24 (30 problems), AIME25 (30) and AIMO (83; AMC 2022-2023); boxed-answer exact match; 3 training seeds (14, 42, 2027).
    • Baselines: sampled-token OPD (top-1), top-16 OPD, OPRD, and ExOPD with the same lambda=1.25 and the same pre-RL reference. Weight-space extrapolation is omitted because ExOPD was stronger in the same setting.

    Main results

    RIDE has the best mean Avg@16 among distillation methods on every pair and sits above the RL teacher on all four. On three pairs the margin over the teacher is within one across-seed SD. ExOPD falls below its teacher on every pair. When RL moved the teacher only a little from the base (2.8-6.0 points), ExOPD also falls below the untouched student, by 14.1 points on Qwen3-4B, as the variance analysis predicts.

    • R1-Distill-1.5B: RIDE 56.38 vs teacher 55.30, OPRD 54.50, OPD top-1 52.90, ExOPD 49.87 (student 39.00).
    • Qwen3-4B: RIDE 66.07 vs teacher 65.59, OPRD 62.01, ExOPD 48.75 (student 62.82); OPD top-1 (59.24) also falls below the student.
    • Llama-3.2-3B: RIDE 13.33 vs teacher 13.01. The AIME scores are near the floor, so this pair's evidence rests mainly on AIMO (RIDE 25.20 vs teacher 24.85).
    • Phi-4-mini: RIDE 18.30 vs teacher 17.96, OPRD 17.33, ExOPD 12.22.

    Extrapolation-coefficient sweep, mechanism and direction controls

    Sweep on R1-Distill-1.5B (one seed per lambda). RIDE improves from 48.2 at lambda=0.5 to 54.3 at lambda=1 (OPRD) and peaks at 55.4 at lambda=1.25. Every lambda in [1.15, 1.35] beats OPRD, and past 1.35 the decline is gradual (52.3 at 1.5, 52.4 at 2). ExOPD peaks at lambda <= 1 (52.9) and is hurt by every lambda > 1: it collapses to 46.2 at lambda=2, its format score drops to 64%, and responses grow to about 8.7k tokens. RIDE with lambda >= 1 instead shortens responses to 5.3-5.7k tokens.

    • The ExOPD conditional variance fits 0.0096 - 0.0421(lambda-1) + 0.153(lambda-1)^2. It has a minimum near lambda=1.14 and is 12.6x larger at lambda=2 than at lambda=1. RIDE's per-position gradient is unchanged when the next token is resampled.
    • The student's update aligns with the residual (cosine 0.954), and its projection along the residual is 1.69, above the target coefficient of 1.25.
    • The teacher's head drifts only 1.65% (relative Frobenius norm) from the base head during RL, which supports the shared-head assumption.
    • Direction controls at the same displacement magnitude all stay within one point of OPRD (54.50): random 55.04, reversed 53.90, mismatched origin (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct) 54.75, trajectory-mismatched 55.12. The true RL residual reaches 56.38.

    Why it matters for post-training pipelines

    The method is a small change to an existing hidden-state OPD pipeline: one extra frozen base forward pass and a different target, with no reward model and no partition function. It turns the teacher-minus-base delta into a usable training signal, which is especially useful when RL moved the teacher only slightly. In that regime logit-space extrapolation is harmful. The approach fits workflows where you keep the pre-RL checkpoint, such as distilling an expensive RL run back into its base or merging RL experts that share a base.

    • Requires access to the pre-RL checkpoint and a shared representation space (same architecture, tokenizer and head).
    • lambda around 1.15-1.35 was a stable range on the tested pair; lambda=1.25 is the default.

    Key findings

    • RIDE is the only method whose mean Avg@16 exceeds the RL teacher on all four pairs, e.g. 56.38 vs 55.30 on R1-Distill-1.5B and 66.07 vs 65.59 on Qwen3-4B. On three pairs the margin is within one across-seed SD.
    • Since RIDE differs from OPRD only in its target, its 0.97-4.06 point margin over OPRD isolates the benefit of residual extrapolation. It beats ExOPD by 9.1 points on average.
    • ExOPD falls below its teacher on every pair. When RL moved the teacher only 2.8-6.0 points, it falls below the untouched student too, by 14.1 points on Qwen3-4B (48.75 vs 62.82).
    • Every RIDE lambda in [1.15, 1.35] beats OPRD (peak 55.4 at 1.25, single seed). ExOPD degrades for every lambda > 1, down to 46.2 Avg@16 and a 64% format score at lambda=2.
    • The ExOPD sampled-advantage variance grows 12.6x from lambda=1 to lambda=2, driven by the (lambda-1)^2 Var[rho] term. RIDE's gradient is deterministic given the rollout.
    • Direction controls at equal displacement magnitude (random, reversed, unrelated-origin, trajectory-mismatched) all land within one point of OPRD (53.90-55.12), vs 56.38 for the true RL residual. The gain comes from the RL direction, not from regularization or loss scale.

    Notable quotes

    A displaced target could help for reasons unrelated to the RL-induced residual, for instance by acting as a regularizer or by enlarging the loss scale.
    On the Llama-3.2-3B pair the AIME benchmarks lie near the evaluation floor, so the evidence there rests mainly on AIMO.
    Relaxing these constraints and studying the safety and calibration of students trained on hidden-state targets are left for future work.
    An RL-trained teacher supplies a direction as well as a destination.

    Limitations

    • Narrow scope: math reasoning only (no code or agentic benchmarks), one RL recipe (JustRL), models of at most 4B parameters, a short 500-step budget, and a single global lambda.
    • Needs the pre-RL checkpoint and a shared representation space (same architecture, tokenizer and head), so it does not apply to cross-family teacher -> student distillation.
    • Gains over the teacher are within one across-seed SD on three of four pairs. The lambda sweep and ablations use single seeds on one pair. Code and teacher checkpoints are promised only 'upon publication'.

    Open questions

    • Does residual extrapolation carry over to code, tool-use or agentic RL teachers, and to larger models where RL deltas may be structured differently?
    • Could a per-layer or per-token adaptive lambda (e.g. scaled by residual norm or confidence) extend the stable range beyond about 1.35?
    • Since the targets are hidden states no model ever produced, does pushing beyond the teacher amplify RL-induced biases or hurt calibration and safety behavior?
