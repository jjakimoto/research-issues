When AI Co-Scientists Fail: SPOT—a Benchmark for Automated Verification of Scientific Research

📚 Foundations · 2026-08-27 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-27

Theme: Empirical verification of autonomous research
Autonomous research must be evaluated through hidden tests, executable outcomes, and confirmed scientific errors—not merely the plausibility of generated ideas or papers. These benchmarks expose large gaps in implementation, debugging, calibration, multimodal reasoning, and scientific verification.
Source: arxiv
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11855

Shows that frontier LLMs remain too inaccurate, inconsistent, and poorly calibrated to serve as dependable full-paper scientific verifiers.

Executive summary

SPOT evaluates whether LLMs can perform the verification half of automated science by finding confirmed errors in complete scientific manuscripts. Its 83 papers contain 91 author-acknowledged or retraction-linked errors across ten fields and six error categories. Leading systems perform poorly: o3 reaches only 6.1% precision, 21.1% recall, and 18.4% pass@1.

Benchmark scope

SPOT presents models with full papers averaging roughly 12,887 tokens and 17.5 images, requiring them to locate and explain scientific errors rather than answer isolated questions.

  • The benchmark includes 83 manuscripts and 91 confirmed errors.
  • Papers span ten scientific fields and six error categories.
  • Errors include flawed proofs, duplicated figures, inconsistent data, statistical errors, reagent errors, and experimental-setup errors.

Curation and validation

Candidates originate from WithdrarXiv and PubPeer, then undergo model filtering, a post-2024 publication filter, confirmation from authors or retraction notices, and human auditing. PDFs are converted into interleaved text and images and manually checked.

  • Only self-contained errors detectable from the manuscript are retained.
  • Author acknowledgment is required because many apparent issues occur in unsettled fields.
  • Fifty-nine errors prompted errata and 32 were associated with retractions.

Evaluation protocol

Models return structured candidate errors with locations and descriptions. A prediction is correct only when its location matches an annotation and a separate model determines that the description identifies the same error. Precision, recall, and bootstrapped pass@K are reported over eight runs.

  • Unmatched predictions are false positives under an exhaustive-annotation assumption.
  • Pass@K measures whether any of K runs discovers an error.
  • Repeated trials reveal substantial instability.

Results and significance

Every evaluated model falls far short of dependable manuscript verification. Images often distract models, shorter relevant segments improve performance, and expert review finds that many false positives arise from missed conventions, implicit calculations, or long-range dependencies.

  • o3 obtains 6.1% precision, 21.1% recall, and 37.8% pass@4.
  • o3 reaches 62.6% pass@4 on proof errors but 0% on figure duplication.
  • Only one unannotated model finding is judged a genuine error.
  • The results challenge research pipelines that rely on unvalidated LLM judges.

Key findings

  • The strongest model finds only about one-fifth of annotated errors per run.
  • Repeated sampling raises o3 from 18.4% pass@1 to only 37.8% pass@4.
  • Reasoning performance is error-category-specific rather than general.
  • Images frequently reduce verification performance.
  • Expert review remains necessary for validating model-reported scientific errors.

Notable quotes

We treat the error annotations included in SPOT as exhaustive: any model-reported error not matching an annotation is counted as a false positive.
First, most models improve in recall and pass@4 when removing images, suggesting that figures usually act as distractors rather than helpful context.
While not severe, this error is the only instance in which we observe an LLM identifying an unannotated but genuine error.
We therefore recommend a secondary expert review, particularly for domains with complex logical dependencies or deep specialization, to validate and refine model-flagged errors.

Limitations

  • SPOT is relatively small because construction prioritizes high-confidence annotation and contamination control.
  • The exhaustive-annotation assumption may classify genuine unannotated findings as false positives.
  • GPT-4.1 mediates candidate-to-annotation alignment, although it does not judge scientific correctness directly.

Open questions

  • Can executable checks, symbolic tools, retrieval, and specialist agents outperform monolithic review?
  • How should benchmarks score genuine unannotated discoveries reproducibly?
  • Can hierarchical evidence routing reduce long-context distraction while preserving cross-section dependencies?
