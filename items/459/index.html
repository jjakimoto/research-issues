SeekBrain: An Autonomous Multi-Agent System for Accelerating Neuroscience Discovery

📰 Latest · 2026-08-04
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-04

Source: arXiv cs.MA (Shanghai AI Lab and collaborators; ~30 authors) · 2026-07-31
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29347

An AI-scientist system whose measured advantage comes almost entirely from a curated repertoire of 68 executable 'analysis recipes' — removing them costs 12.6 of its 75.8 benchmark points — but its scoring is LLM-as-judge against reference figures, not wet-lab validation.

Executive summary

SeekBrain embeds distributed domain knowledge as retrievable analysis recipes (crystallized by aligning paper method text to matching repository code) inside a planning-plus-execution agent architecture, and evaluates on BrainArena, a 32-task benchmark built from 10 source neuroscience studies. It scores 75.8 against 64.1 for Claude Code and 58.1 for Codex on the same backbone family, and a recipe ablation costs 12.6 points. Two case studies reanalyse real zebrafish and IBL mouse data. For a lab tracking autoresearch systems, the honest read is: strong execution-grounded scaffolding, LLM-as-judge scoring, no external validation, and no cost or human-intervention numbers reported at all.

Architecture: recipes as the knowledge substrate

A Crystallization Agent aligns paper method text to code blocks in the matching repository, producing a recipe directory (RECIPE.md, metadata.json, references, run_analysis.py, utils.py) structured as Objective / Input / Processing / Output. A Distillation Agent converts completed execution traces plus user feedback into new or updated recipes, stripping local paths and dataset-specific names.

  • 68 foundational recipes at launch, organized as unimodal (dynamics, behavior, structure, molecular) plus multimodal.
  • Motivation: neuroscience has no scanpy-equivalent unified toolkit, so existing science agents that assume clean structured input transfer poorly and hallucinate methods.
  • Claude Opus 4.7 for coding, review, task generation, validation and interpretation; Gemini 3.1 Pro for sub-question generation, long-text synthesis and reporting.

Loop topology

A Research Planning Engine (Survey -> Orchestrator -> Reviewer, capped at 3 iterations) emits a tree with the query at the root and analysis tasks at the leaves. An Analysis Execution Engine then runs Formulation (retrieve and adapt a recipe), Code (ReAct write-execute-debug, dummy data prohibited), Validation (4 dimensions, max 3 cycles) and Interpretation.

  • Verifier grounding is mixed: internal verification is execution-grounded (code must run and produce figures and statistics) plus LLM critique, but benchmark scoring is rubric-based LLM-as-judge using Claude Opus 4.6.
  • Human involvement is researcher-in-the-loop at every stage — refining plans, judging feasibility, tuning pipelines — with traces distilled back into recipes.
  • No wet-lab or external experimental validation is performed.

BrainArena and the head-to-head

32 tasks from 10 source studies, rubrics of 3-5 items scored to 100 points with over 700 deduction subitems. Organisms span zebrafish (31%), mouse (34%), C. elegans (16%), macaque (12%) and Drosophila (6%); 59% of tasks involve two modalities and 53% pair behavior with neural data.

  • Friedman test p<0.0001; Dunn post hoc adjusted p=0.0002 versus Claude Code and p<0.0001 versus Codex.
  • SeekBrain led all seven analysis types, with the largest gaps over Codex in neural decoding (+28.7) and brain-network/anatomical mapping (+28.5).
  • Systems are blinded to the source paper, reference figures and conclusions, and one post-release bioRxiv preprint is included to limit leakage.

Case studies

Case study 1 (researcher-led) clusters 1,546 zebrafish swim bouts from 18 larvae into three behavioral types and decodes them from 8-D CEBRA latents at 0.949 accuracy. Case study 2 (agent-proposed hypotheses) reanalyses IBL mouse decoder tables, finding a single component explaining 63.2% of variance across 201 regions and 5 decoders.

  • Zebrafish: 64,032 +/- 8,575 ROIs per fish reduced to 3,000 region-aware supervoxels; Procrustes alignment paired Wilcoxon p = 2.44e-4 over 13 larvae.
  • IBL: PC1 tested against a 5,000-permutation column-shuffle null, empirical p ~ 2.0e-4; rank-PCA and value-PCA PC1 correlate at r = 0.96.
  • Movement regression explains R^2 = 0.61 / 0.39 / 0.15 of choice / feedback / stimulus decoding, leaving task-specific residual maps.

Key findings

  • SeekBrain scored 75.8 on 32 BrainArena tasks against 64.1 for Claude Code and 58.1 for Codex, with Dunn adjusted p=0.0002 and p<0.0001.
  • The curated recipe repertoire is the dominant mechanism: removing it costs 12.6 points (75.8 to 63.2, Wilcoxon p<0.0001).
  • The largest deltas over Codex were in neural decoding (+28.7) and brain-network/anatomical mapping (+28.5); SeekBrain led all seven analysis types.
  • Benchmark scoring is rubric-based LLM-as-judge (Claude Opus 4.6) against reference figures — there is no wet-lab or externally checked validation anywhere in the paper.
  • In the IBL reanalysis a single component captured 63.2% of variance across 201 regions and 5 decoders, and movement alone explained 61% / 39% / 15% of choice / feedback / stimulus decoding variance.
  • No cost, wall-clock or human-intervention numbers are reported — only structural caps (at most 3 planning-review iterations and 3 validation cycles).

Limitations

  • Scoring is LLM-as-judge against reference figures; leakage is only partly mitigated by including one post-release preprint, and no result is externally or experimentally validated.
  • The Research Planning Engine was ablated for the head-to-head benchmark, so the reported scores test execution quality, not planning.
  • The repertoire is small (68 recipes) and omits spatial transcriptomics, large-scale connectomics and closed-loop behavior; baselines Robin, AI Scientist and AI Co-Scientist were excluded for architectural incompatibility, narrowing the comparison.

Open questions

  • Does the recipe advantage survive when the repertoire must be built by a community rather than curated by the authors — i.e. is it a method or a dataset?
  • How would the system score under a non-LLM judge, or against results with an external experimental check rather than figure reproduction?
  • What are the actual cost, wall-clock and human-intervention rates — none are reported, and reliability rather than capability is the usual binding constraint for these systems.
