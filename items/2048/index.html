    FactorBench: A Portfolio-Aware Benchmark for Automated Factor Mining

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-07

    Source: arxiv · 2026-10-03
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06947

    Run under one shared data/target/split/execution contract, about 5,000 factors from 9 GP, RL, generative and LLM-agent miners give no winning paradigm. The LLM agents' smaller train-to-test decay comes from weaker in-sample fit, not better generalization, and their pools sit close to Alpha101. Once five style exposures are neutralized, AlphaQCM is the only automated miner whose long-short book stays profitable. For anyone building an autonomous factor researcher, this is strong evidence that pushing up a training-IC proxy is not evidence of alpha.

    Executive summary

    FactorBench (King's College London) re-runs 9 author-released factor miners (GP, AlphaGen, AlphaCFG, AlphaQCM, AlphaForge, AlphaSAGE, R&D-Agent, AlphaAgent, QuantaAlpha) plus the fixed Alpha101 library on 5 large-cap equity markets. All use the same inputs, the same 20-day forward-return target and the same 2017-21 / 2022-23 / 2024-Oct 2025 split, giving 135 runs and 4,914 factors. Each miner is scored at three levels: factor (validity, temporal decay, IC left after removing style exposures), pool (redundancy, similarity to other miners and to Alpha101) and portfolio (selection, ridge combination, after-cost long-only and long-short books). No paradigm dominates. In-sample advantages disappear out of sample, larger pools are not broader, and most composite predictiveness and long-short profit goes away once beta, volatility, momentum, reversal and liquidity are neutralized.

    Problem: the search objective is only a proxy

    Published factor-mining results come from method-specific data, objectives and backtests, so they cannot be compared across studies. The score a miner optimizes during discovery is an intermediate proxy. What matters is how the delivered pool behaves out of sample and once it is combined into investment decisions. The authors ask whether newer paradigms (RL, generative search, LLM agents) actually deliver more generalizable, distinct and economically useful signals than older GP.

    • Financial signals are weak and non-stationary, and repeated search over the same history easily finds patterns that do not persist.
    • Earlier benchmarks (AlphaBench, AlphaEval, AlphaForgeBench) evaluate LLM capabilities or individual formulas. FactorBench follows the whole path from discovery to an after-cost portfolio.

    Benchmark design and shared contract

    There is a four-block pipeline: shared setting, heterogeneous miners, an execution contract that turns symbolic expressions or Python programs into date-by-asset signal matrices, and portfolio-aware evaluation. The data are daily yfinance OHLCV for fixed constituent lists (S&P 100, Hang Seng 85, CSI 300 subset of 272, FTSE 100, Nikkei 225; 782 securities in total). Inputs are OHLCV, a VWAP proxy of (H+L+C)/3, the 1-day return and 20-day average volume. The target is the 20-trading-day forward close-to-close return. Each miner keeps its native algorithm and objective, is run for 3 seeds per market on one A100, and test-period metrics are never fed back. All three LLM agents share the same Qwen3.8-27B endpoint and their released prompts.

    • Search budgets follow each paper's released configuration. They are deliberately NOT equalized, so runtime is a footprint, not a controlled efficiency comparison.
    • LLM budgets are small: R&D-Agent runs 5 research-development-feedback loops, AlphaAgent 3 rounds, and QuantaAlpha 10 directions over at most 11 evolutionary rounds.
    • A factor counts as scorable only with at least 100 valid dates in every split. Failed and unscorable outputs stay in the validity analysis instead of being silently dropped.
    • Alpha Jungle and CogAlpha were implemented but excluded: neither has official code, and paper-scale LLM call budgets could not be reproduced.

    Factor level: validity, decay, and style exposure

    Of 4,914 delivered factors, 98.5% execute and 95.6% are scorable. All 76 execution failures come from the LLM pipelines (R&D-Agent 19, AlphaAgent 24, QuantaAlpha 33). Symbolic miners lose factors to numerical degeneracy instead (AlphaSAGE has 47 IC-incomplete factors). GP, RL and generative miners post much higher training IC, but most of that advantage is gone by validation and does not come back in test. LLM agents start lower and so decay less, but their held-out IC is not consistently higher. Neutralizing against beta, volatility, momentum, short-term reversal and liquidity cuts IC for most methods, most consistently for GP, AlphaForge and AlphaGen. GP and R&D-Agent show the highest and most persistent adjusted-R2 overlap with the styles.

    • Factor sign is fixed on training IC, so negative held-out IC is a true out-of-sample sign reversal.
    • The authors attribute the decay gap to feedback: GP, RL and generative miners repeatedly optimize training IC (adaptive overfitting), while LLM agents use IC feedback in a few multi-objective revise cycles.
    • R&D-Agent's neutralized IC turns negative in some markets. The authors suggest pretrained financial priors lead it to rediscover known style factors.
    • The decay result holds under RankIC and ICIR and under paired within-factor train-minus-test degradation, which is strongest in HSI, CSI 300 and FTSE 100.

    Pool level: redundancy and where each paradigm searches

    Pool size is not signal breadth. AlphaGen and AlphaQCM have the highest within-pool redundancy, GP is also highly correlated in several markets, and AlphaSAGE is redundant in CSI 300 but diverse elsewhere. Across methods there is a family structure: non-LLM miners resemble each other (AlphaGen and AlphaQCM overlap most), and the three LLM agents resemble each other more than they resemble symbolic search. LLM pools are closer to the published Alpha101 factors than non-LLM pools, most strongly for R&D-Agent. These patterns persist across train, validation and test, and hold under Pearson as well as Spearman correlation.

    • Similarity among the LLM agents plausibly comes from the shared foundation model and common financial-language priors.
    • Pearson similarity is below Spearman for AlphaGen, AlphaQCM and AlphaSAGE: their factors agree on stock ordering, and hence on trades, more than on magnitudes.
    • Distinctness does not improve monotonically from GP to RL to generative to LLM miners.

    Portfolio level: long-only flatters, long-short plus neutralization discriminates

    Selection uses validation IC with a 0.8 Spearman redundancy cap. Composites are built by ridge regression (weights fit on train, regularization chosen on validation), rebalanced every 20 days with 10 bp per dollar traded, and traded as top-20% long-only or top/bottom-20% dollar-neutral long-short. Every long-only book is profitable (CAGR 22.4-31.2%, Sharpe 1.03-1.31), but these are fully invested and ride the 2024-25 market. Long-short returns are much smaller: R&D-Agent (Sharpe 0.679) and AlphaQCM (0.668) lead and AlphaAgent is negative (-0.087). RankIC of the composites is small and negative for several methods. After style neutralization, AlphaQCM is the only automated miner with a profitable long-short book. R&D-Agent's 6.5% CAGR becomes -1.6%. Long-short results also swing with costs (0-50 bp), rebalance interval (1-20 days) and combination rule, and some change sign.

    • Alpha101, the fixed, non-mined 2016 library, is competitive throughout: long-only Sharpe 1.241, raw composite IC 0.0238 against 0.0237 residual, and its long-short Sharpe rises from 0.319 to 0.540 after neutralization.
    • Cross-market dispersion is larger than the gaps between methods. Every method has a positive long-short Sharpe in the S&P 100, while several are negative in HSI, CSI 300 or FTSE 100.
    • No combination rule (equal, IC-weighted or ridge) dominates, and the choice reorders methods.

    Implications for an autonomous factor researcher

    The paper's own conclusion is that progress in factor mining is multidimensional and cannot be established by a search objective or an isolated predictive score. For an LLM-agent miner, the evidence argues for gating candidates on held-out, style-neutralized IC and on correlation to a known library such as Alpha101 rather than on raw training IC. Execution failures should be reported as a system property, and long-short after-cost, neutralized books should be the acid test. Long-only CAGR is mostly market beta.

    • Agentic pipelines carry real execution risk: 80.5-82.2% executable for AlphaAgent and R&D-Agent, against 100% for every symbolic miner.
    • Wall-clock cost differs by orders of magnitude (GP 0.14 h/run vs QuantaAlpha 27.90 h/run) with no matching downstream gain.
    • The LLM results come from one mid-size open model (Qwen3.8-27B) at small loop budgets. Frontier models and larger budgets were not tested.

    Key findings

    • 135 mining runs delivered 4,914 factors; 98.5% execute and 95.6% are scorable. All 76 execution failures are in the LLM pipelines (R&D-Agent 19, AlphaAgent 24, QuantaAlpha 33), and only 81.3% of R&D-Agent's and 80.5% of AlphaAgent's factors are scorable, against 92.7-100% for the symbolic miners.
    • GP, RL and generative miners have much higher training IC that mostly disappears by validation. LLM agents decay less only because they start lower; their held-out IC is not consistently higher. The result holds under RankIC, ICIR and paired within-factor degradation.
    • R&D-Agent has the highest raw test composite IC (0.0253), but its residual IC after style neutralization falls to 0.0011. AlphaQCM keeps most of its IC (0.0191 to 0.0175), and the fixed Alpha101 library is nearly unchanged (0.0238 to 0.0237). GP, AlphaGen, AlphaForge, AlphaSAGE and AlphaAgent go negative after neutralization.
    • Every long-only book is profitable (net CAGR 22.4-31.2%, Sharpe 1.03-1.31), which mostly reflects being fully invested. Long-short is much weaker: R&D-Agent has Sharpe 0.679 and 6.5% CAGR, AlphaQCM 0.668, and AlphaAgent -0.087.
    • With styles neutralized, AlphaQCM is the only automated miner with a profitable long-short book (CAGR 3.7% to 1.3%, Sharpe 0.668 to 0.283). R&D-Agent falls from 6.5% to -1.6% CAGR and from 0.679 to -0.449 Sharpe.
    • LLM-agent pools look like one another (shared Qwen3.8-27B priors) and sit closer to Alpha101 than non-LLM pools, most strongly for R&D-Agent. AlphaGen and AlphaQCM are the most internally redundant, and they also overlap each other the most.

    Notable quotes

    Their narrower train-to-test gap therefore reflects less in-sample performance to lose rather than stronger out-of-sample predictiveness.
    Thus, high raw training IC does not necessarily represent predictive information beyond familiar risk and style exposures.
    Similarity among the LLM methods may instead reflect their shared foundation model and common financial-language priors.
    Removing failed factors before reporting results would hide the execution risk of agentic pipelines, while assigning zero IC to constant signals would conflate an undefined prediction statistic with evidence of no predictiveness.

    Limitations

    • Narrow evidence base: five large-cap universes with fixed constituent lists from Yahoo Finance (survivorship bias acknowledged), one 20-day target, a single chronological split with a test window of about 22 months (Jan 2024-Oct 2025), and three seeds. Cross-market dispersion is larger than the gaps between methods, so no reliable global ranking exists.
    • Search budgets are not equalized, and every LLM agent uses one mid-size open model (Qwen3.8-27B) at small loop budgets (3-11 rounds). The LLM-agent conclusions may not carry over to frontier models or larger agent budgets, and there is no compute-controlled comparison.
    • Residual IC controls only five price/volume styles with no industry classification. Costs and borrowing are simplified, with no capacity, market-impact or borrow-availability modeling, so even the surviving AlphaQCM and Alpha101 signals are not established as pure alpha.

    Open questions

    • How do factor quality and out-of-sample decay change with model and search compute, for example frontier LLMs, longer agent loops, or GP and RL given matched candidate-evaluation budgets? The authors list this as future work.
    • Would rolling-origin evaluation, point-in-time universes and a richer risk model (industries, more styles) keep AlphaQCM's residual edge, or would no automated miner beat the non-mined Alpha101 reference?
    • Can an LLM agent's search be steered away from the Alpha101 and style region its priors favor, for example by gating on residual IC and correlation to known libraries, without reintroducing the adaptive overfitting seen in IC-hill-climbing miners?
