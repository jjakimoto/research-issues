Point-in-Time Audit Before Alpha: Public-Archive Availability and a Negative Matched-Budget Study on BTC Perpetual Futures

📰 Latest · 2026-08-29 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-29

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-26
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25348

A rare, fully-instrumented negative result: after building a deterministic point-in-time auditor for Binance BTCUSDT perpetuals, an adaptive 'audited agent' proposer tied uniform random search exactly (39 vs 39 qualified candidates under matched valid-candidate budgets), and every one of 23 holdout runs had positive IC but negative net Sharpe across all 460 fee-slippage cells. If you build agentic search loops, this is the control experiment you should be running: the audit machinery is what paid off (false passes 0.2910 -> 0.0625), not the agent.

Executive summary

Four authors (Tsinghua, BUPT, Nanjing) audit two years of public Binance BTCUSDT USD-M perpetual-futures archives under a three-timestamp discipline (event / publication / availability) and then run a preregistered, matched-budget comparison of an adaptive proposal policy against uniform random search and tree GP. Their original premise -- a gapless five-minute trade/mark/index/OI grid for 365+ days -- was falsified by the data itself (longest unrepaired intersection: 304.57 days), and they published the failure instead of repairing it. The audit pipeline demonstrably cut false discoveries in null controls, but the agent did not beat random search and no candidate survived transaction costs in the one-time historical holdout. The paper is explicitly framed as an audit-methods contribution plus a scoped negative result, not a profitability or agent-superiority claim.

Problem: file-complete is not point-in-time-complete

The core argument is that a crypto archive can look usable simply because the files exist, while factor research needs observations that were actually retrievable and executable at each decision instant. A candidate can appear predictive because the underlying record was unavailable, the trade used an unfinished bar, realized funding was moved backward, or a gap was filled with future information. The study window is [2024-08-01, 2026-08-01).

  • The project's stated original premise -- trade, mark, index, and open interest all on one exact five-minute grid continuously for at least 365 days -- was falsified by the checksum-closed archive audit.
  • Longest unrepaired exact-grid intersection: 87,717 rows = 304.5729166666667 days, i.e. it missed the 365-day admission gate.
  • Rather than silently repairing, the authors disclosed the failure and revised the admission rule; the retired and revised rules are called 'different sample definitions, not two estimates of one quantity.'
  • Conclusion framing: 'A file-complete public archive was not automatically a point-in-time-complete research dataset.'

Data and the three-timestamp availability discipline (Sec. 3.1-3.3)

Historical OKX probes returned candles but empty funding and OI, so a frozen fallback rule permitted a whole-study venue switch (never cross-venue stream mixing) to public Binance archives: 826 official-checksum-verified ZIPs. For a bar, event time identifies the interval, publication cannot precede the explicit close, and availability is close_time + 1 ms; a decision at time t may read only records whose availability_time is <= t. Realized funding has no publication-time field in the archive, so the primary analysis assumes event time + 5 minutes, with 0/5/10/15-minute sensitivity.

  • Missing observations are masked, never filled: interpolation, backward filling, cross-gap forward filling, timestamp snapping, deleting conflicting duplicates to manufacture continuity, and venue mixing are all prohibited.
  • Open interest is demoted to optional AND disabled as a factor input because its publication time is unverified.
  • Revised core (trade, mark, index, native-frequency funding after availability masking) yields 727 complete UTC days.
  • Split is timestamp-only 60/20/20: 436 train / 145 validation / 146 test days, with a 60-minute purge and embargo.
  • The test partition is honestly labelled a 'one-time historical holdout,' not a prospective test, because the protocol was hash-locked after the interval had already ended.

The deterministic auditor and its conformance benchmark (Sec. 3.4, 5.2)

Search methods may only propose expressions. A separate deterministic evaluator exclusively parses the expression, constructs lineage, applies availability masks, audits execution, computes labels and metrics, performs selection, and writes an append-only ledger. This proposer/auditor separation is the structural leakage control. The auditor targets five known leakage classes: future source shift, same-bar execution, future realized funding, incorrect OI availability alignment, and backward fill.

  • Benchmark: 40 illegal + 40 legal executable templates, eight violations per class; acceptance required >=38/40 detection, <=2/40 false rejections, and 8/8 exact reason matches per class.
  • Result: 40/40 illegal detected (Wilson 95% [0.9124, 1.0000]); 0/40 legal falsely rejected ([0.0000, 0.0876]); 8/8 per class; all 40 rejection reasons matched the injected class.
  • The authors immediately discount their own win: the intervals do not establish population recall above 95% or false rejection below 5%, and because the generator exercises known auditor rules, unknown leakage is outside the evidence.

Null controls: the audit works, but the masking mechanism is not identified (Sec. 3.5, 5.3)

Ten null-signal paths (five moving-block label permutations of public 15-minute BTC data plus five synthetic heavy-tailed, volatility-clustered paths), each evaluating 100 candidates (80 legal, 20 known-violation), crossed over three audit levels x three missing-data policies x 2/5/10% missingness. Selection uses BH-FDR q=0.10 with PBO and Deflated Sharpe diagnostics; the primary cell is 5% missingness with availability masking.

  • Mean false passes at the primary cell: 0.2910 (no audit) -> 0.2479 (basic shift audit) -> 0.0625 (full five-class audit); a 78.5% relative reduction.
  • Full vs no audit improved all 10 paths; paired path-bootstrap difference -0.2285, 95% interval [-0.2595, -0.2012], exact two-sided sign-test p=0.001953.
  • PBO barely moved (-0.001429, interval [-0.004286, 0], nine ties); DSR was zero where computable and missing when no candidate was selected.
  • Honest negative: exact-grid deletion and availability masking produced identical numbers, and backward fill was not uniformly worse -- so only the JOINT effect of the full audit pipeline is supported, not an independent masking effect.

Matched-budget search: the agent ties random search (Sec. 3.6, 5.4)

All three methods share one expression DSL (max AST depth 4, at most 5 operator nodes, lookbacks of 3/6/12/24/48/96/288 five-minute bars). Crucially the budget is matched on VALID candidates (100 per method-seed-horizon run, 1,000 per method), not on wall-clock or attempts. The 'audited agent' is a deterministic adaptive proposal policy using training-only failure and validation feedback -- the paper states explicitly it is not an external LLM. Seeds 11/23/37/53/71, horizons 15 and 60 minutes.

  • Qualified candidates: audited agent 39, random 39, tree GP 29 -- an exact tie between agent and random.
  • Raw attempts to fill the valid budget: agent 1,648, random 2,658, tree GP 1,804 -- the agent was cheaper per valid candidate.
  • The authors refuse to bank that efficiency win: evaluations to first qualified factor and resource-normalized runtime were not frozen outcomes, so the preregistered discovery-efficiency claim is rejected.
  • All successful, invalid, duplicate, and rejected proposals are retained in the ledger (append-only accounting).

Holdout: positive IC, uniformly negative economics (Sec. 3.7, 5.5)

At most five qualified factors per run are direction-corrected on validation, z-scored, and equally weighted; position is the sign of the composite, delayed by one completed decision bar and sampled non-overlapping at the target horizon. Primary costs are 6 bp taker fee + 2 bp slippage per side, with fee grid 4/5/6/8/10 bp and slippage grid 1/2/5/10 bp per side. The holdout evaluator was invoked once, after selection and economic rules were frozen.

  • Prediction held up: all 23 evaluated runs retained validation-to-test IC sign with individual moving-block IC intervals excluding zero. Mean test IC 0.2352 (agent), 0.1907 (random), 0.1550 (GP).
  • Economics did not: zero runs of 23 had positive net Sharpe at primary costs. Median Sharpe -28.04 / -21.16 / -30.11 with turnover ~5,158-6,653.
  • Robustness checks made it worse: an additional one-bar delay gave 0/23 positive Sharpe, and no run survived the frozen extreme-observation deletions.
  • Across the full fee-slippage grid (160 agent + 160 random + 140 GP = 460 cells) positive-Sharpe counts were 0, 0, 0; the best observed Sharpe values were only -6.3148, -5.5009, -5.4938.
  • The authors state they could have rescued the result by changing the combiner, holding period, or cost model after opening the holdout, and deliberately did not.

Key findings

  • The preregistered data premise failed outright: the exact five-minute trade/mark/index/OI intersection ran only 304.5729166666667 continuous days (87,717 rows), short of the 365-day gate; the disclosed revision to trade/mark/index/funding retained 209,951 eligible decisions, masked 288, and yielded 727 complete UTC days, stable at 727 across funding lags of 0/5/10/15 minutes.
  • Archive defects were retained rather than repaired: trade had no exact-grid gap, mark and index each lacked 288 bars on 2026-06-29, and OI had 9 missing records, 3 off-grid records, one conflicting duplicate group, and 501 archive-order reversals.
  • The deterministic auditor scored 40/40 violation detection and 0/40 false rejection on 80 frozen templates with 8/8 correct reason codes per leakage class, but the Wilson intervals ([0.9124, 1.0000] and [0.0000, 0.0876]) are too wide to establish population-level recall.
  • Full five-class auditing cut mean false passes in null controls from 0.2910 to 0.0625 (78.5% relative), improving all 10 paths (paired difference -0.2285, 95% CI [-0.2595, -0.2012], sign-test p=0.001953) -- but PBO moved only -0.001429, so the gain is in pre-selection filtering rather than overfitting diagnostics.
  • Under exactly matched valid-candidate budgets the adaptive audited agent produced 39 qualified candidates vs random search's 39 (tree GP 29) -- a dead tie; its lower raw attempt count (1,648 vs 2,658) was explicitly not counted because first-discovery and runtime metrics were not frozen outcomes.
  • Prediction and economics decoupled completely: all 23 holdout runs kept IC sign with intervals excluding zero (mean test IC up to 0.2352), yet 0/23 had positive net Sharpe, median Sharpe was -28.04 to -30.11 at turnover ~5,000-6,700, and all 460 fee-slippage cells were negative with a best-case Sharpe of only -5.4938.

Notable quotes

The audited agent tied random search on qualified candidates and therefore did not beat both baselines.
At 5% missingness with availability masking, mean false passes were 0.2910 without auditing, 0.2479 with the basic audit, and 0.0625 with the full audit.
The best observed Sharpe values were -6.3148, -5.5009, and -5.4938, respectively.
Changing the combiner, holding period, or cost model after opening the holdout could have produced a more attractive retrospective specification, but would invalidate the one-time-access design.

Limitations

  • Single asset and single venue (Binance BTCUSDT USD-M) with no second asset, exchange, or genuinely future interval; the holdout interval ended before the protocol lock, so one-time access is retrospective, not prospective validation.
  • The audit benchmark only covers known, self-injected leakage rules -- it is not an unseen-adversary test -- and the null simulation could not distinguish exact-grid deletion from availability masking, so no independent masking effect is identified.
  • Substantial planned work was not executed: budgets 30 and 300, first-discovery counts, resource-normalized comparison, direction accuracy and trade counts were not emitted, and the LightGBM / nonlinear / manual baselines were unavailable; aggregated bars plus assumed slippage cannot support capacity or live-tradability claims.

Open questions

  • Would an actual LLM-driven or externally-guided proposer beat random search where this deterministic adaptive policy tied it, or does the matched valid-candidate budget design neutralize proposal intelligence in general?
  • Is the negative economic result a property of BTC perpetuals at 5-minute/15-60-minute horizons under 8 bp round-trip-per-side costs, or would a lower-turnover combiner, longer holding period, or a maker-fee execution model flip it -- and how can that be tested without burning another one-time holdout?
  • Can a leakage auditor be validated against unknown or adversarially-constructed violations rather than self-injected known rules, so that recall estimates carry beyond the finite template set?
