GST-Bench: Can VLMs Develop Global Spatial Awareness from Video?

📰 Latest · 2026-08-09
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-09

Source: arXiv · 2026-08-06
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05747

GST-Bench exposes a large gap between local visual competence and globally consistent spatial reasoning over long videos. Targeted training more than doubles Qwen3-VL-8B's score, but it still remains far below humans and is trained on synthetic data from the benchmark's own generation pipeline.

Executive summary

GST-Bench evaluates whether vision-language models can integrate long-horizon egocentric observations into a global scene representation and answer from unseen viewpoints. It contains human-verified questions derived from 6,790 minutes of synthetic video and evaluates 22 VLMs across 12 subtasks. The best zero-shot model scores 42.68 versus 79.08 for humans; controlled local variants attribute much of the deficit to cross-frame integration.

Task design

Questions cover object localization, self-localization, and scene-structure understanding through distance, angle, point, and multiple-choice formats. Inputs combine exploration video, a novel current viewpoint, and either visual or semantic target specifications.

Model evaluation

The study covers proprietary, open-source, and embodied-understanding models from 2B to 38B parameters. Gemini-3-Pro leads zero-shot evaluation at 42.68, GPT-5 scores 40.85, and the best open model, InternVL3.5-38B, scores 30.71.

Local-versus-global controls

Local-Video makes the target visible in the current view while retaining the video; Local-Image removes the video entirely. Gemini-3-Pro's semantic egocentric-direction score rises from 22.11 globally to 61.20 locally, showing strong local perception but weak cross-frame consolidation.

Open-model bottleneck

Open-source models often remain weak even in local controls. For example, InternVL3.5-2B reaches 25.40 on local-image semantic direction, far below Gemini-2.5-Pro's 66.49, indicating both perception and integration limitations.

Targeted supervision

Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuned on scene-disjoint GST-Train plus general multimodal instructions improves from 25.89 to 53.52. This exceeds every evaluated zero-shot model but remains 25.56 points below the reported human score.

Key findings

  • Gemini-3-Pro scores 42.68, leaving a 36.4-point gap to humans.
  • Nine of 17 open-source and embodied models score within three points of random guessing.
  • Embodied-tuned models do not outperform similarly scaled general-purpose VLMs on global spatial reasoning.
  • Removing cross-frame integration raises top proprietary-model direction scores by 34–43 points in the Local-Image setting.
  • Targeted training raises Qwen3-VL-8B from 25.89 to 53.52 but does not close the human gap.

Notable quotes

Human evaluators achieve an average score of 79.08, whereas the best model, Gemini-3-Pro, reaches only 42.68, leaving a gap of 36.4 points.

Limitations

  • Videos are synthetically generated, so performance may not transfer directly to noisy real-world embodied perception.
  • The human baseline uses 20 samples per task rather than exhaustive annotation of the full benchmark.
  • GST-Train and GST-Bench use the same generation pipeline despite scene-disjoint splits, leaving open the possibility of pipeline-specific transfer.
  • The fine-tuned result is not zero-shot and therefore should not be compared as a pure base-model capability ranking.

Open questions

  • Will GST-trained models transfer to real robot video and independently generated environments?
  • Which memory representation best supports globally consistent maps over very long visual streams?
  • Can models improve cross-frame integration without sacrificing local perception or general multimodal instruction following?
