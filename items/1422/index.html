Signal Correlation, IC, and PnL Dependence

📰 Latest · 2026-09-10 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-10

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-09
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09588

Quant teams routinely use the cross-sectional signal-correlation matrix as a stand-in for the PnL-correlation matrix; this note proves by an exact date-by-date orthogonal decomposition that neither bounds nor orders the other, and shows exactly which component each diagnostic sees and where the discarded 'transverse' geometry re-enters once signals are combined into a normalized or risk-budgeted book.

Executive summary

The paper decomposes each date's unit-norm, cross-sectionally neutral signal into a longitudinal coordinate along the realized demeaned return direction — exactly the realized Pearson IC, so PnL = return dispersion x IC (the Qian-Hua identity) — and a transverse component orthogonal to it. Signal correlation equals the uncentered IC cross-moment plus transverse similarity, where the normalized transverse similarity is precisely the cross-sectional partial correlation controlling for realized returns; Pearson PnL correlation sees only the IC series, dispersion-weighted and mean-centered. Theorem 9 is a non-identifiability result: with no constraint on transverse geometry, neither correlation bounds, determines, or universally orders the other. For ensembles the transverse Gram matrix re-enters through the normalization denominator, and the Euclidean and risk-metric optima agree for every longitudinal exposure iff the risk metric is isotropic on the signal span.

Setup and the longitudinal-transverse decomposition (Sections 1-2)

Signals are cross-sectionally demeaned unit vectors in the neutral subspace H = {x : 1'x = 0}. Lemma 1 shows p_{i,t} = <S_{i,t}, Q_t> is exactly the realized cross-sectional Pearson IC, and realized PnL is a_t p_{i,t}. Lemma 2 gives <S_1,S_2> = p_1 p_2 + <U_1,U_2> with ||U_i||^2 = 1 - p_i^2. Averaging over dates: C_sig = C_IC + C_perp.

  • Signal correlation is an inner product across assets at each date; PnL correlation is across dates after collapsing each signal to a scalar — different index sets, not two estimators of one population quantity.
  • The 'moving frame' sends Q_t to e_1; the residual freedom is an O(d-2) gauge and the rotation is never computed in practice.
  • Scope condition: the identity needs the traded portfolio to BE the Euclidean-normalized signal; rank IC does not satisfy it unless the rank-transformed vector is what is traded.

The transverse statistic is a partial correlation (Sections 3-4)

Normalizing the transverse inner product by its attainable radius gives Z_t = <U_1,U_2> / sqrt((1-p_1^2)(1-p_2^2)) = rho_{12.R,t}: the one-control cross-sectional Pearson partial correlation given the realized return vector. Proposition 4 shows two ordered configurations share a gauge orbit iff they have the same longitudinal vector p and the same transverse Gram matrix B.

  • Both coordinates carry classical statistical meaning: longitudinal = realized IC, normalized transverse = partial correlation given returns.
  • The Gram-matrix orbit result is the elementary transverse-subspace case of Weyl's first fundamental theorem for O(n).
  • O(m) rather than SO(m) is used deliberately, to discard the economically meaningless handedness invariant.

What PnL correlation actually does (Section 5)

Three transformations separate signal similarity from the allocator's PnL correlation matrix: each date is weighted by realized dispersion a_t^2, the shared expected-alpha component is centered out, and normalization is by time-series volatility rather than cross-sectional norm. The uncentered PnL second moment is M_pnl = mean(a^2) C_IC + Cov_t(a_t^2, p_1 p_2).

  • Remark 5: two profitable signals can share positive expected IC yet have nearly uncorrelated PnL innovations.
  • The covariance term asks whether the two signals agree longitudinally precisely when cross-sectional opportunities are large.
  • Attribution targets: transverse geometry, dispersion weighting, implementation scaling, Pearson centering.

Ensembles: Euclidean vs. risk metric (Section 6)

For K signals with Gram A_t = p_t p_t' + U_t'U_t, the normalized combination IC is p_t'v / sqrt(v'A_t v); Proposition 6 gives max = p_t' A_t^{-1} p_t. Corollary 6.1 identifies this with the squared multiple correlation of the return direction on the signal span. Under a risk metric Sigma_t with B_t = S_t' Sigma_t S_t, Proposition 7 gives max |E_t(v)|^2 = a_t^2 p_t' B_t^{-1} p_t.

  • Transverse structure, inert for a single fixed-norm signal, re-enters through the normalization denominator.
  • Under an independent rank-K span and isotropic returns, R_t^2 ~ Beta(K/2, (d-1-K)/2) with E[R_t^2] = K/(d-1), hence R_adj^2 = 1 - (1-R^2)(d-1)/(d-1-K).
  • Both optima are ex post envelopes — p_t contains the realized forward return — and are span-efficiency diagnostics, not trading rules.

Sharp bounds and non-identifiability (Sections 7-8)

Proposition 8: |rho_12 - p_1 p_2| <= sqrt((1-p_1^2)(1-p_2^2)). Theorem 9: for d-2 >= 2, without restrictions on transverse geometry, return magnitudes, or temporal dependence, neither average signal similarity nor PnL correlation determines, nontrivially bounds, or universally orders the other. Proposition 10 gives the exact isotropic null for Z_t: density proportional to (1-z^2)^{(m-3)/2}, m = d-2, Var(Z)=1/m, t-statistic Z sqrt(m-1)/sqrt(1-Z^2) ~ t_{d-3}.

  • At a realized IC of 0.03, ||U_i|| is approximately 0.99955.
  • The geometric isotropic null and the classical Gaussian partial-correlation null are observationally equivalent for Z_t.
  • When d - 2 = 1 only a weaker discrete non-identifiability remains.

Synthetic illustration and empirical program (Sections 9, 11-12)

A 12-signal, d=500, T=1000 design (seed 20260906) imposes independence between transverse feature-family structure and longitudinal IC dependence, with IC innovation sd 0.045 matching the zero-alpha per-date IC noise floor. Across the 66 pairs, C_IC ranks PnL dependence almost perfectly while full signal correlation carries almost none of that ranking information. A 50-signal library yields 1,225 pairs of serially dependent estimates, requiring block bootstrap or HAC inference and FDR control.

  • Reproduction NumPy code is in Appendix A; the author discloses ChatGPT-assisted development and Claude-generated critical reviews.
  • The dispersion-regime term has median absolute magnitude roughly one-half of the pure scale term.
  • Five-step program: decompose, attribute disagreement, compare Z to its isotropic scale, identify feature families, and use R^2 only as a null-relative span diagnostic.

Key findings

  • Exact decomposition: C_sig = C_IC + C_perp; Pearson PnL correlation sees only the IC series, dispersion-weighted by a_t^2 and mean-centered.
  • The normalized transverse statistic is exactly the one-control cross-sectional partial correlation, with exact null t = Z sqrt(m-1)/sqrt(1-Z^2) ~ t_{d-3}, m = d-2.
  • Theorem 9 (non-identifiability, d-2 >= 2): neither signal correlation nor PnL correlation determines, nontrivially bounds, or universally orders the other.
  • In the synthetic design, Spearman(C_IC, rho_pnl) = 0.974 while Spearman(C_sig, rho_pnl) = -0.070; across IC innovation sd 0.012/0.025/0.045/0.070 the C_sig Spearman is -0.165/-0.160/-0.070/+0.150 versus C_IC 0.388/0.877/0.974/0.989.
  • Concrete inversions: pair 3-12 has C_sig = 0.4766 but rho_pnl = -0.405, while pair 11-12 has C_sig = -0.0002 and rho_pnl = 0.616.
  • Raw ex post multiple correlation is null-biased upward: E[R_t^2] = K/(d-1) = 0.0240, against a measured mean of 0.0334 and adjusted mean of only 0.0096.
  • The Euclidean and risk-metric optima agree for every longitudinal exposure iff the risk metric is isotropic on the signal span.

Notable quotes

Allocators do not size positions on signal correlation.
Two signals can look nearly orthogonal while their PnLs are strongly correlated, or look strongly similar through a shared transverse feature family while their PnLs are negatively correlated.
The isotropic/Gaussian null is not a realistic generative model for signals that come out of a shared feature pipeline.
This is a mechanism illustration, not calibrated evidence.

Limitations

  • The only empirical content is a synthetic simulation in which the key independence is imposed rather than discovered.
  • All central quantities contain the realized forward return, so they are ex post diagnostics, not deployable trading rules.
  • Turnover and transaction costs are not derived, because the target-aligned frame rotates between dates.

Open questions

  • How much of real-world signal/PnL correlation disagreement is due to Pearson centering versus transverse geometry versus dispersion weighting?
  • Does the independence between transverse feature-family structure and longitudinal IC dependence hold in a production alpha library?
  • Can a temporal connection transport the moving frame between dates, so turnover and cost-aware implementation fall out of the same decomposition?
