    LoopVL: Recurrent Visual Intelligence

    ← Go backHacker News — Front Page: HN front page turned over…NextThe AI Theorist reveals excitonic structure in α-RuCl3 →
    📰 Latest · 2026-10-05 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-05

    Source: huggingface-daily-papers · 2026-09-29
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38426

    A roughly 1B-scale looped VLM, built from 32 unique layers executed as 128 layer calls and trained on only about 0.14T tokens, matches or beats dense 4B baselines trained on the same budget, and beats 2-4B open VLMs trained on 11-36T tokens on reasoning-heavy benchmarks. It saves parameters, not compute: it only works with the exact loop schedule it was trained on, and it has not been shown to scale with extra test-time loops.

    Executive summary

    LoopVL extends a hierarchical looped Transformer (HRM-Text style, with coupled L and H stacks) to vision-language modeling, so that visual tokens keep being updated by shared modules on every loop. With 32 unique layers (two 16-layer stacks, hidden size 1536) run on an H2L3 schedule (128 layer calls), it beats a 32-layer dense twin by a wide margin and roughly matches 78-layer and wide 4B dense baselines on the same 0.14T-token budget, using somewhat fewer training FLOPs. The authors also report a 'Visual Aha Moment': at the boundary between the two model cycles, visual attention concentrates sharply (Gini 0.405 to 0.858). Throughout, they are careful not to treat attention maps as causal explanations.

    Problem: does looping work for VLMs?

    Recurrent-depth work has mostly been language-only. In a VLM, the instruction decides which image regions matter, and after one round of vision-language interaction both the visual representations and their relative importance can change. The paper asks whether Loop Transformers can be extended to VLMs. Because image patches give a spatial coordinate system, it also uses them to watch what the recurrence actually does.

    • Central question: can Loop Transformers be effectively extended to vision-language models?
    • Visual tokens keep a correspondence with image patches, so the same physical layer can be compared across loops.

    Architecture: Module-Loop inside Model-Loop

    A frozen Penguin Vision Encoder (1024-d per patch) feeds a 2-layer GELU projector (1024 to 1536). The backbone has separate L and H stacks of 16 layers each, with gated self-attention, SwiGLU, 2D spatial RoPE for visual tokens and a PrefixLM mask. Module-Loop repeats the L stack under a fixed H condition. Model-Loop repeats the whole L/H cycle. The default H2L3 order is LLLHLLLH.

    • 32 unique layers yield 128 executed layer calls per forward pass; the language backbone is roughly 1B-scale.
    • At the start of each cycle, a visual anchor from the initial projected embeddings is re-injected into the H state, controlled by a query-conditioned token-wise gate and a cycle-specific scale.
    • Selective backprop: gradients flow through the last 2 invocations, growing to the last 5 during warmup; L1-L3 run only in the forward pass.
    • An ablation that freezes visual states during the second cycle, or a retrained read-only variant, underperforms normal LoopVL on all four tested benchmarks.

    Training recipe and data (about 0.14T tokens total)

    The authors pretrain their own language backbone from scratch with HRM-Text's framework (not its checkpoint). They then align, mid-train, run SFT, and finish with GRPO. Settings: AdamW (0.9, 0.95), gradient clipping 1.0, 3% warmup, weight decay 0.

    • Text pretraining: 75B tokens (60B from the HRM-Text base recipe plus 15B curated).
    • Stage 1 alignment: LLaVA-559K (0.33B tokens). Only the projector is trained (LR 1e-3, batch 256, sequence length 2048, 256-1024 visual tokens).
    • Stage 2 mid-training: OneVision-1.5 Mid-Training-85M (49B tokens). LM LR 2e-5, projector LR 1e-4, batch 128, sequence length 3072.
    • SFT: 16.81B tokens (OneVision-1.5 Instruct-22M 13B, Spatial-4M 3B, LLaVA Mix665K 0.55B, Vision-R1-Cold 0.26B).
    • RL: GRPO on Vision-R1-RL (0.006B tokens) with an outcome reward, and a maximum generation length increased from 2048 to 4096.

    Results and benchmark caveats

    Results cover 16 benchmark families, using greedy decoding and direct answers (usually a 32-token budget, no chain of thought). LoopVL leads the compact-VLM table on LogicVista, MMMU-Pro, MMStar, MathVision and MMK12-Math. It trails clearly on ChartQA, AI2D, VMCBench, HallusionBench and MMEval-Pro.

    • Caveat: the comparison table mixes in-house evaluations with selected public results, so protocols are not fully matched.
    • Caveat: the 0.14T figure counts LoopVL's own stages; the frozen Penguin encoder was pretrained separately.
    • Caveat: the efficiency claims concern training FLOPs. Inference still executes 128 layer calls, and the paper reports no latency or throughput numbers.

    Analysis: Visual Aha Moments and state dynamics

    On a 32-sample diagnostic set, attention Gini over visual tokens jumps from 0.405 to 0.858 across the cycle boundary (layer index 63 to 64), and spatial entropy is generally lower in cycle 2. Some patches move from the bottom half of the attention ranking in cycle 1 to the top quartile in cycle 2. The pattern survives full backprop and removal of the anchor/gate, in models pretrained from scratch.

    • Visual hidden states keep substantial L2 updates in cycle 2, so the second cycle is not just copying the first.
    • Logit-lens KL to the final distribution stays high until it drops sharply inside the final H call.
    • Linear recoverability of the initial visual features falls by the end of the recurrence.

    Significance

    This is a working looped-VLM recipe with public 1B models. The main practical lesson is that how the loops are organized matters as much as how many there are. Equal unrolled depth with a different L/H order gives very different accuracy, and adding loops at inference beyond the trained schedule hurts.

    • Same-depth controls: H2L1 beats H1L3 on all five benchmarks in the training-time ablation (both 64 layer calls).

    Key findings

    • With the same 32 unique layers as a dense twin, looping to 128 layer calls lifts MMStar from 55.33 to 63.47, RealWorldQA from 55.29 to 70.98 and ChartQA from 51.12 to 74.52.
    • At 2.47e21 training FLOPs, LoopVL beats the 4B Deep (2.89e21) and 4B Wide (2.99e21) dense baselines on MMStar, RealWorldQA, MathVision, VisuLogic and MMK12, but trails them slightly on VMCBench, AI2D and ChartQA.
    • Training-time ablation: H2L1 (64 calls) beats H1L3 (64 calls) on all five benchmarks (e.g., ChartQA 65.12 vs 60.40), and H2L3 (128 calls) is best (74.52).
    • Inference-time over-looping hurts: on MMStar, H3L3 scores 58.20 and H4L3 scores 24.00, vs 63.47 for the trained H2L3. Early readout (H1) gives near-zero scores.
    • Visual Aha Moment: mean visual-attention Gini rises from 0.405 to 0.858 at the cycle boundary. The effect persists with full backprop and without the visual anchor/gate.
    • Against compact VLMs trained on 11-36T tokens, LoopVL-1B (0.14T) leads on LogicVista (45.19 vs 36.91 for the next best) and MMK12-Math (61.60 vs 54.80), but trails on ChartQA (74.52 vs 86.00) and HallusionBench (44.71 vs 51.59).

    Notable quotes

    Model quality therefore depends not only on how much computation is executed, but also on how that computation is organized between low-level state refinement and high-level state updates.
    Additional model cycles therefore do not automatically improve the results of this checkpoint, despite increasing the amount of executed computation.
    Taken together, these results show that the model-cycle transition is a stable point at which the organization of visual attention changes markedly.
    Raw attention is therefore treated here as an allocation diagnostic rather than a standalone causal explanation.

    Limitations

    • The interpretability evidence rests on a 32-sample diagnostic set and hand-picked heatmaps. There is no matched general Model-Loop control VLM, so it is unclear whether Visual Aha Moments are intrinsic to Model-Loop or specific to LoopVL's design.
    • The efficiency story is about parameters and training FLOPs only: inference runs 128 layer calls (KV cache disabled in the sweep) with no latency or memory reporting. The baseline table mixes in-house and public numbers under a direct-answer, no-CoT protocol.
    • Only image tasks with a frozen encoder are covered. Reasoning post-training is math-focused and sometimes produces repetitive reasoning loops.

    Open questions

    • Can a looped VLM be trained to benefit from extra loops at test time (adaptive depth or extrapolation), instead of collapsing off its trained schedule?
    • Does the training-FLOP advantage over dense 4B models survive real wall-clock and serving costs, and at larger scales?
    • Is the cross-cycle attention reallocation causally linked to correct answers, as opposed to just correlated with them?
