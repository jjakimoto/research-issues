    MLR-Copilot: Autonomous Machine Learning Research based on Large Language Models Agents

    Date: 2026-09-24

    Theme: Autonomous machine-learning research through retrieval and execution
    DS-Agent and MLR-Copilot treat machine-learning research as an iterative, executable workflow rather than a single generation task. DS-Agent emphasizes case-based reuse of successful competition solutions, while MLR-Copilot connects literature-grounded ideation to implementation and debugging. Both show gains from retrieved experience and execution feedback, but neither establishes reliable fully autonomous science under strict held-out evaluation.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14033

    Connects literature-grounded idea generation to prototype retrieval, implementation, execution, and iterative debugging.

    Executive summary

    MLR-Copilot turns an existing machine-learning paper into a proposed research method, experimental plan, executable implementation, and measured result. IdeaAgent is trained with supervised learning and multidimensional reinforcement-learning feedback, while ExperimentAgent retrieves prototype code and optional models or datasets before iteratively debugging experiments. Across five tasks, IdeaAgent generally receives stronger ratings than BaseLLM and ResearchAgent, while Claude-3.7 achieves 44.16% average improvement over retrieved prototypes and a 50.0% success rate.

    Problem and scope

    The paper addresses the gap between reading prior work, proposing a research idea, implementing it, and obtaining executable results. MLR-Copilot accepts selected content from a research paper and attempts the complete workflow.

    • It extracts the task, gaps, and keywords before retrieving recent literature.
    • The three stages are idea generation, experiment implementation, and implementation execution.
    • The intended output is executable research rather than a hypothesis alone.

    IdeaAgent

    IdeaAgent generates a methodology and experiment plan grounded in the source paper and retrieved work. It receives supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning from novelty, feasibility, and effectiveness signals.

    • The training corpus includes 4,271 ICLR and NeurIPS papers from 2023 and 2024.
    • A supervised dataset is derived from 1,000 papers with extracted ideas and plans.
    • Novelty, feasibility, and effectiveness are scored separately.

    ExperimentAgent

    ExperimentAgent retrieves the original paper's prototype implementation, edits it to realize the plan, optionally retrieves models and datasets, executes the experiment, and revises failures using observations and optional human feedback.

    • Dataset suitability is checked against experimental requirements.
    • Implementation follows repeated reasoning, action, observation, and feedback.
    • The ELLIPSE case study combines CNN, BiLSTM, BERT, linguistic features, cross-validation, and iterative refinement.

    Evaluation and results

    Evaluation separates idea quality from implementation success across five tasks. IdeaAgent has the strongest or tied-strongest reported ratings, while iterative execution decisively outperforms a one-pass baseline that fails on every task.

    • IdeaAgent's automated validity rating is 4.7, versus 4.2 for ResearchAgent and 3.2 for BaseLLM.
    • Claude-3.7 averages 44.16% improvement over the retrieved prototype.
    • Claude-3.7 succeeds in 50.0% of trials under the paper's threshold of at least 10% improvement.
    • Trials use human instructions, so the evidence does not establish unattended autonomy.

    Key findings

    • A staged architecture connects literature-grounded ideation to executable experiments.
    • IdeaAgent achieves the strongest or tied-strongest reported ratings while generating hypotheses less similar to the source work.
    • Iterative execution and debugging are decisive against the one-pass baseline, which records no successful trials.
    • Performance varies greatly by task, from 14.5% improvement on Identify-Contrails to 76.2% on IMDB for Claude-3.7.
    • The current system remains human-augmenting because execution trials use human instructions and optional human feedback.

    Notable quotes

    MLR-Copilot automates the generation and implementation of research ideas using LLM agents, organized into three integrated phases: research idea generation, experiment implementation, and implementation execution.
    Notably, Our method with Claude-3.7 achieves the highest average improvement, with a success rate of 50.0% compared to GPT-4 with 40% and Claude-2.1 with 27.5%, highlighting its superior effectiveness.
    A tighter integration between stages, particularly enabling backward transitions from implementation or execution back to ideation, would better reflect the iterative nature of real-world scientific discovery.

    Limitations

    • The pipeline is largely sequential and lacks systematic transitions from failed execution back to revised ideation.
    • Evaluation covers only five tasks, with eight implementation trials per task aided by human instructions.
    • The sole implementation baseline is one-pass prompting, while automated ratings rely partly on GPT-4 and no uncertainty estimates are reported.

    Open questions

    • Can execution failures trigger principled revisions of the hypothesis and experimental plan without human intervention?
    • How much gain comes from each component under matched compute and token budgets?
    • Can the framework incorporate preregistration, uncertainty, replication, statistical testing, and disciplined negative-result handling?
