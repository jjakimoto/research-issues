Studying Image Tokenizers as Visual Languages in Unified Multimodal Models

📰 Latest · 2026-09-09
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-09

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-08
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09143

A controlled pure-autoregressive testbed shows that the standard way of picking image tokenizers — reconstruction FID — can be actively misleading for unified multimodal models, and that the choice of image token space even changes how well the model models plain text.

Executive summary

The authors (Amazon FAR + University of Washington) extend Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B into unified autoregressive multimodal models by expanding the vocabulary with discrete image tokens, then continually pretrain on up to 60M mixed-modal samples and track four task-specific validation losses. The four losses scale differently, rank tokenizers differently, and only I2T loss — over the shared text vocabulary — is a consistent cross-tokenizer signal. Reconstruction fidelity diverges from 'multimodal learnability', and the image token space perturbs text loss via interference from the image-token prediction objective. Three design axes are revisited: a DINO discriminator (better rFID, no downstream gain), semantic supervision (worse rFID, better everything else), and vocabulary size (non-monotonic on loss, largest best downstream).

Testbed: pure-AR continual pretraining on Qwen3

Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B are extended with new learnable image-token embeddings plus begin/end-of-image markers; the LM head is extended to predict image tokens. Continual pretraining uses 60M samples at the largest scale, then SFT on 4.9M instruction-following samples. Loss is computed only on the target modality span.

  • Data: 6.6M pure-text (DataComp-LM) + 53.3M image-text (LAION-Aesthetics, JourneyDB, BLIP3o-Pretrain-Short-Caption)
  • Images fixed at 256x256 with 16x16 = 256 tokens, so vocabulary size is the only compression axis
  • 80% of image-text samples go to T2I, 20% to I2T; 10% of T2I samples become unconditional for CFG
  • Recipe validation: an 8B model with the Chameleon tokenizer roughly matches Liquid-7B

Task-specific losses scale, but differently

All four validation losses fall roughly as power laws under both data and model scaling, but their behaviour and tokenizer rankings diverge, so an averaged loss is too coarse.

  • Text loss barely moves under data scaling — largely inherited from the backbone; image-side losses move a lot
  • T2I loss closely tracks the unconditional image loss; I2T loss behaves unlike text loss
  • Rankings conflict: UniTok has the highest text loss but the lowest T2I loss; GigaTok the reverse
  • Annealing mainly reduces text loss

Which loss predicts benchmark performance

With the tokenizer fixed, T2I loss aligns strongly with GenAI-Bench VQAScore and MJHQ-30K gFID. Across tokenizers the T2I curve shifts because each tokenizer defines a different image-token space; I2T loss is the more consistent signal.

  • Vocabulary normalization L* = L / log2(B) reduces the cross-tokenizer shift among IBQ variants
  • Residual gap at fixed B=16384 tracks rFID: worse reconstruction reaches the same generation quality at a lower T2I loss
  • Pre-SFT I2T loss correlates moderately with post-SFT VQAv2 and GQA; weaker for OCR-heavy TextVQA

Reconstruction fidelity vs multimodal learnability

Within tokenizer families, rFID and downstream quality come apart. The DINO discriminator halves rFID with no downstream benefit, while semantic supervision hurts rFID yet improves every reported loss and metric.

  • GigaTok-DINO: rFID 0.81 -> 0.51, GenAI unchanged at 0.720, VQAv2 52.25 -> 51.31
  • UniTok-sem: rFID 1.86 -> 2.23 (worse) but text 2.883 -> 2.873, I2T 1.670 -> 1.655, T2I 6.264 -> 5.848, GenAI 0.670 -> 0.690, VQAv2 57.21 -> 61.28
  • Conclusion: rFID alone is insufficient for tokenizer selection in unified models

The image token space leaks into text modeling

With text data, text tokenizer and backbone all fixed, swapping the image tokenizer changes text loss. Ablating the image-generation objective makes the text-loss gaps largely vanish — while the I2T gap persists, implying a second, independent pathway.

  • UniTok-sem beats UniTok on text loss under joint training; IBQ-1024 beats IBQ-8192 on text loss
  • Text+I2T ablation removes the text-loss gap in both pairs but not the I2T gap
  • An appendix check verifies the effect is not caused by the I2T objective

Why semantic supervision helps, and vocabulary size

The I2T improvement concentrates on visually grounded words rather than making sequences locally easier to predict; vocabulary size is non-monotonic on loss yet the largest still wins downstream.

  • Largest I2T gains on COCO object-category tokens; higher PMI between image tokens and COCO object words for UniTok-sem
  • n-gram entropy: UniTok-sem lower at 1- and 2-grams but HIGHER at 3- and 7-grams — no 'simpler visual grammar'
  • IBQ-16384 attains the best I2T loss; IBQ-8192 the lowest normalized T2I and image losses
  • IBQ-16384 is best on post-SFT generation and understanding; beyond B=16384 is future work

Key findings

  • Swapping GigaTok's PatchGAN discriminator for a DINO discriminator improves rFID from 0.81 to 0.51 but leaves GenAI unchanged at 0.720 and drops VQAv2 from 52.25 to 51.31.
  • Semantic supervision moves rFID the wrong way (1.86 to 2.23) yet improves text loss, I2T, T2I, GenAI (0.670 to 0.690) and VQAv2 (57.21 to 61.28).
  • The four task-specific losses rank tokenizers inconsistently, so an averaged loss is uninformative.
  • I2T loss over the shared text vocabulary is the only cross-tokenizer-consistent signal.
  • Vocabulary normalization L* = L/log2(B) restores a consistent T2I loss-performance relation across IBQ B in {1024, 8192, 16384}.
  • Vocabulary size is non-monotonic: IBQ-16384 wins on I2T loss and all post-SFT benchmarks, but IBQ-8192 attains the lowest normalized T2I and image losses.
  • The image tokenizer changes TEXT loss under joint training; ablating image-token prediction removes the text-loss gap but not the I2T gap.

Notable quotes

The T2I loss closely tracks the unconditional image loss, indicating that image-token prediction accounts for much of the difficulty in T2I.
Together, these results show that a reconstruction metric such as rFID alone is insufficient for tokenizer selection: we must also consider how the tokenizer affects multimodal learnability.
Interestingly, the I2T gap in both pairs persists, suggesting that tokenizer choice affects how informative image tokens are for captioning through a pathway independent of image token prediction.
This association suggests that reconstruction fidelity may help explain the residual differences in the T2I loss–performance relationship at a fixed vocabulary size.

Limitations

  • Resolution fixed at 256x256 with 16x16 tokens and only single-codebook tokenizers.
  • Image-token loss is computed over lossy discrete codes, so it is only comparable within a fixed visual language.
  • Model scale tops out at 0.6B/1.7B/4B for the main experiments, and loss-to-benchmark correlations are only moderate.

Open questions

  • Beyond B=16384, is reconstruction fidelity or multimodal learnability dominant?
  • What is the second, image-token-prediction-independent pathway affecting captioning?
  • Do these conclusions hold when resolution, token count, or multi-codebook tokenization vary?
