End-to-End Neural Shrinkage of Indefinite Pairwise Correlation Matrices for Small-Cap-Inclusive Portfolios

📰 Latest · 2026-09-01 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-01

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-31
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30446

RIEnet learns to repair and shrink indefinite pairwise correlation matrices while conditioning on heterogeneous observation overlap. In a cost-aware 2000–2025 U.S. equity backtest, it cut five-session volatility by 20.7% versus the next-best covariance estimator and delivered the highest CAGR and Sharpe ratio.

Executive summary

This paper addresses covariance estimation for portfolios containing recent listings, intermittently traded stocks and other securities with incomplete histories. Its dimension-agnostic RIEnet maps signed eigenvalues and factor-specific overlap information to a positive-definite covariance matrix, trained end-to-end on future global-minimum-variance risk. Across a point-in-time universe of up to 1,500 U.S. equities and a detailed broker simulation spanning 2000–2025, RIEnet achieved substantially lower realized volatility than incomplete-panel estimators, complete-row shrinkage and equal weighting.

Problem and data construction

Pairwise-complete estimation preserves fragmented histories by estimating each correlation entry from the maximum history shared by that asset pair, but the resulting matrix may be indefinite because entries use different samples and denominators. The experiment uses point-in-time U.S. common-equity and ADR universes with up to 1,500 stocks and a requested 1,200-day lookback.

  • 17.15% of stock–rebalance observations have fewer than 1,200 valid returns, 8.45% have fewer than 600, and 3.48% have fewer than 252.
  • Signals are produced every five trading days.
  • The continuous out-of-sample period contains 6,537 sessions from January 2000 through December 2025.

RIEnet architecture

RIEnet combines a learned five-parameter lag transformation, a shared marginal-volatility MLP and a bidirectional-GRU spectral module. For each spectral factor, the network receives its signed eigenvalue, square-root dimension, factor-aligned effective sample length and concentration ratio.

  • The network has approximately 7,400 trainable parameters, independent of asset count and history length.
  • The marginal-volatility MLP has one hidden layer with eight leaky-ReLU units.
  • Each direction of the bidirectional GRU has 32 hidden units.
  • Positive inverse eigenvalues are used to reconstruct a unit-diagonal positive-definite correlation matrix.

End-to-end training

Training computes the analytical unconstrained GMV portfolio from RIEnet's covariance estimate and minimizes its realized five-session variance, differentiating through the complete estimation pipeline. Deployment deliberately uses a different long-only optimizer.

  • Training samples contain 50–500 assets, while evaluation reaches 1,500 assets.
  • The authors train 26 expanding-window models: 1990–1999 predicts 2000, and 1990–2024 predicts 2025.
  • Optimization uses Adam, a 0.0001 initial learning rate, gradient clipping at one, 500 batches per epoch and 100 epochs.

Evaluation and execution realism

Benchmarks include nearest-correlation projection, bootstrap, factor imputation, ridge reconstruction, complete-row MLE, Quadratic Inverse Shrinkage and Equal Weight. A continuous broker simulation models integer shares, closing-auction execution, commissions, regulatory fees, financing, corporate actions and market impact.

Performance and implementation costs

RIEnet had the lowest annualized five-session volatility at 11.17%, versus 14.08% for Factor. It also posted the highest CAGR and Sharpe ratio. Its total modeled CAGR drag was 1.49 percentage points—higher than QIS but well below the 3.06–4.03 range of several alternatives.

Key findings

  • RIEnet reduced annualized five-session volatility from 14.08% for Factor to 11.17%, a 20.7% reduction.
  • RIEnet achieved a 9.30% CAGR and 0.814 Sharpe ratio, the highest values among evaluated methods.
  • RIEnet was the only method retained by the Model Confidence Set at the 0.1% level across all tested block lengths.
  • The architecture generalized from training samples containing at most 500 assets to universes containing up to 1,500.
  • RIEnet's modeled total CAGR drag was 1.49 percentage points.

Notable quotes

The loss is differentiated through the complete estimation pipeline, from the lag transformation to the reconstructed covariance matrix.
Every covariance estimator is combined with the same long-only GMV optimizer.
RIEnet is therefore not the least costly estimator to implement, but its total drag remains well below those of MLE, Bootstrap, Anderson, Factor, and Ridge, which range from 3.06 to 4.03 percentage points.
We leave this broader analysis to future work.

Limitations

  • The paper evaluates one fixed RIEnet specification and leaves controlled synthetic experiments and component-level attribution to future work.
  • Economic conclusions depend on a broker simulation and assumed impact coefficients rather than live execution.
  • The study is confined to U.S. equities and a five-session GMV setting.

Open questions

  • Which architectural component accounts for most of the improvement?
  • How robust is RIEnet under controlled missingness mechanisms and structural covariance changes?
  • Would training through deployment-specific constraints improve realized portfolios further?
