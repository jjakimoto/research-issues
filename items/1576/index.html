    Model-Free Passive Execution via Order-Level Shadowing

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-17

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-16
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18019

    A passive execution algorithm that places orders by copying the price, venue and lifecycle of individual third-party limit orders — no order-book model, no fill prediction — and, over a full year of replayed CME ES futures, matches a standard aggressive POV on cost while degrading four times more slowly with latency. It gives desks a model-free reference line that any predictive placement model must beat.

    Executive summary

    Shadow-PPOV is a passive percentage-of-volume execution method whose order-placement rate is driven by observed order-flow adds rather than traded volume. On each qualifying third-party add the algorithm transmits its own limit order at that add's price and venue, stores only a (venue, exchange_order_id) -> shadow_order_id association, and cancels when the followed order dies. Evaluated on 246 sessions of 2025 CME ES market-by-order data in the open-source kaspar-hft deterministic replay simulator, passive Shadow-PPOV and an aggressive POV baseline at nearly matched participation cost statistically identical amounts (+0.0955 vs +0.0952 ticks/contract), but latency hurts the aggressive arm about four times more. A 30-second-delay control raises passive cost by 3.9x, showing the inherited information is real and perishable.

    Problem: passive placement normally needs a model

    Every schedule-based algorithm (TWAP, VWAP, POV, Implementation Shortfall) is model-based: it derives child orders from a forecast, schedule or control loop. Wherever a slice is worked passively, the same placement question arises — how far into the book to quote, trading fill probability against impact and adverse selection. Conventional answers build an order-book model and predict where a passive order will fill; posting blindly at the touch needs no model but surrenders control and invites adverse selection.

    • A conventional POV runs a feedback loop comparing executed x_t against target rho*V_t, crossing the spread when behind.
    • Shadow-PPOV replaces the control loop with per-order following; participation is emergent, not a control input.
    • The paper is explicit that 'model-free is not information-free': the algorithm reads every add/cancel/trade message but computes nothing from them.

    Method: participant shadowing by identifier

    On each qualifying add on the target side, Shadow-PPOV transmits a shadow limit order at the add's price to the add's originating venue and records exactly one association. It retains no price, quantity, queue position, book state or timestamp for the followed order, so a state-based cancellation decision is impossible by construction and lookup/removal is expected O(1). Selection is either probabilistic or deterministic (every N-th add); the experiments use the probabilistic mode.

    • Cancel mirroring: a delete for the followed order cancels the shadow immediately; a trade arms a delayed cancel over a grace window of B end-of-burst events so the in-flight sweep can still reach the shadow.
    • A modify is treated as lifecycle termination — cancel and drop the association; re-shadowing requires a fresh qualifying add.
    • Flow-inherited venue selection reproduces the observed cross-venue add mix in expectation with no routing optimizer. Argued analytically but NOT evaluated — the corpus is single-venue.
    • Under FIFO price-time priority the shadow rests behind the order it follows, so a sweep must clear that order and the depth ahead of it first.
    • Shadow size is a randomized function of recent average trade sizes, capped by the followed order's size and remaining parent quantity.

    Reference implementation: kaspar-hft

    Shadow-PPOV is implemented as execution components in kaspar-hft, an open-source C++ actor-model trading system and order-book simulator. The simulator reconstructs a full order-by-order (MBO) book from exchange PCAPs, so queue position and hence fill inference are definable; three latencies (outbound order, outbound cancel, inbound feed) are independently configurable.

    • Two-band place/cancel hysteresis prevents place/cancel/replace churn on one-tick oscillations.
    • Completion and over-hedge guards pull orders when the target is reached, the position goes flat, or per-level caps are exceeded.
    • The paper argues the guards are risk limits, not a model: none forecasts a fill, values queue position, or chooses where to place.
    • Market impact is NOT modelled — orders fill against the recorded feed as if they had not been there.
    • The aggressive baseline is the same C++ class driven by trade prints instead of adds, sending marketable orders with a 1 ms time-to-live.

    Experiment design

    CME ES E-mini futures, market-by-order channel 310, every regular session of calendar 2025 with available PCAPs (245-246 sessions). The algorithm runs as a two-sided market maker: at 10-minute boundaries from 09:29 to 16:00 ET it is given 100 contracts to buy and, independently, 100 to sell, worked by separate books; a window closes only when the 10-minute minimum has elapsed AND both sides completed 100 contracts.

    • Relative slippage = 0.5*(vwap_buy - vwap_sell), which cancels the arrival mid so intraday drift drops out.
    • One tick = 0.25 index points = $12.50/contract. The ES book is one tick wide at 96.3% of window openings, mean spread 1.038 ticks, so immediate-execution slippage is +0.519 ticks/contract.
    • Participation is the aggregate sum(F)/sum(F+V) over all sides of all windows, not the mean of per-window ratios.
    • 100 contracts arrive as roughly 85 executions of 1.18 contracts on average; clip size is set by the market's add-size distribution, not a parameter.

    Results and significance

    At nearly matched participation (4.46% passive vs 4.86% aggressive), the two styles cost the same to the fourth decimal on both relative slippage and cost versus immediate execution, over more than 9,500 completed windows each — despite one resting for 99% of its quantity and the other crossing for half. Post-fill mark-outs at 1/5/30 s are flat rather than decaying, the signature of adverse selection rather than temporary impact. The paper frames the small positive cost as a Glosten-Milgrom prediction, making Shadow-PPOV a benchmark against which a predictive placement model can be scored.

    • The VWAP column (-0.621 passive vs -0.404 aggressive) is explicitly disclaimed: legs run for different lengths.
    • Latency converts style: passive goes from 1.1% to 27.3% of quantity filling on arrival at 5 ms; aggressive ends at 74.6%.
    • A stylized Gamma model shows realized participation is effectively random for tiny parents and concentrates as 1/sqrt(Q-2).

    Key findings

    • At nearly matched participation (4.46% vs 4.86%), passive and aggressive cost +0.0955 ± 0.0130 vs +0.0952 ± 0.0135 ticks/contract — agreement to the fourth decimal across >9,500 windows each, even though passive crossed on only 1.04% of quantity versus 51.86%.
    • Latency dominates: passive rises +0.097 -> +0.150 ticks/contract from 0 to 5 ms, aggressive +0.087 -> +0.296; slopes are 0.011 vs 0.042 per millisecond, and aggressive flips from cheaper at zero delay to 0.055 ticks more expensive by 500 µs.
    • Latency converts execution style: passive quantity filling on arrival rises 1.1% -> 27.3%, aggressive ends at 74.6%; passive participation rises 4.51% -> 6.87% while aggressive falls 3.18% -> 2.02%.
    • The 30-second staleness control raises passive cost 3.9x (+0.0953 -> +0.3673 ticks/contract) and cuts child fills from 85.5 to 37.2 per leg.
    • Post-fill mark-outs (1/5/30 s) are indistinguishable between arms and flat, indicating adverse selection rather than temporary impact. Corpus: 1.63M passive and 1.64M aggressive fills.
    • Under the stylized Gamma model, realized participation has a 10th-90th band of [0.44, 9.48] at Q=1, narrowing to [0.96, 1.04] at Q=1000.

    Limitations

    • The simulator does not model market impact: fills are added to the day's volume rather than displacing anyone, so participation should be read as an upper bound.
    • Single instrument and single venue: ES is a one-tick book for most of the session, and flow-inherited venue selection is entirely unexercised.
    • Parent size is fixed at 100 contracts and no competing predictive placement baselines are evaluated — the benchmark role is proposed rather than demonstrated.

    Open questions

    • Does flow-inherited venue selection actually reproduce the add distribution in a fragmented multi-venue market?
    • How does Shadow-PPOV compare against TWAP, VWAP, Almgren-Chriss and predictive placement models?
    • How much of the latency-driven cost increase is style conversion versus worse execution within a style?
