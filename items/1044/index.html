Deep Learning in Asset Pricing

📚 Foundations · 2026-08-24
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-24

Theme: Economically Grounded Deep Learning for Quantitative Trading
These papers show how deep learning can become more credible in quantitative finance when its architecture and objective encode economic structure. One learns a nonlinear stochastic discount factor from no-arbitrage conditions; the other learns residual-based statistical-arbitrage signals and portfolio allocations under explicit trading constraints.
Source: arxiv
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00745

Combines no-arbitrage moments, adversarial test assets, and recurrent macroeconomic states to estimate a tradable nonlinear stochastic discount factor.

Executive summary

Chen, Pelger, and Zhu estimate a nonlinear conditional stochastic discount factor for U.S. equities using linked feedforward, recurrent, and adversarial neural networks. With monthly data from 1967–2016, 46 firm characteristics, and 178 macroeconomic series, the model substantially outperforms linear and unconstrained deep-learning benchmarks out of sample. The results show the value of economic restrictions, characteristic interactions, informative test assets, and dynamic macroeconomic states.

Problem and economic framework

The paper addresses conditional asset pricing with many firm characteristics and macroeconomic variables. Rather than predict returns alone, it estimates a traded stochastic discount factor directly from conditional no-arbitrage moment restrictions.

  • SDF portfolio weights depend on observable firm-specific and macroeconomic information.
  • No-arbitrage links expected returns to stocks' conditional SDF exposures.
  • The formulation avoids directly estimating and inverting a large conditional covariance matrix.

Neural and adversarial method

A feedforward network learns nonlinear SDF weights, an LSTM compresses 178 macroeconomic series into latent states, and an adversarial network selects conditioning instruments that expose large pricing errors.

  • The minimax objective alternates between finding difficult characteristic-managed assets and improving the SDF.
  • The selected specification has two layers, four macroeconomic states, and eight adversarial instruments.
  • Estimates ensemble nine fits and use dropout and validation-based tuning.

Data and evaluation

The sample contains monthly CRSP returns from 1967–2016, merged with 46 firm characteristics and 178 macroeconomic series. Training covers 1967–1986, validation 1987–1991, and out-of-sample testing 1992–2016.

  • Metrics include SDF Sharpe ratio, explained time-series variation, and cross-sectional R².
  • Benchmarks include least squares, elastic net, and a neural return-forecasting model.
  • Evaluation also covers beta-sorted and 46 anomaly-sorted portfolio families.

Results and significance

The GAN specification delivers the best overall out-of-sample performance, with a monthly Sharpe ratio of 0.75, explained variation of 8%, and cross-sectional R² of 23% for individual stocks.

  • Beta-sorted portfolio regressions have R² values of 0.98, 0.97, and 0.95 for 5, 10, and 20 portfolios.
  • GAN obtains cross-sectional R² around or above 90% across the 46 anomaly portfolio sets.
  • Using only current macro observations causes performance to collapse; the LSTM's dynamic states materially improve performance.
  • Adversarial test assets raise the SDF Sharpe ratio by roughly 20%.

Key findings

  • No-arbitrage-constrained learning outperforms an equally flexible network trained only to forecast returns.
  • The GAN's annualized out-of-sample Sharpe ratio is approximately 2.6.
  • Firm-characteristic interactions matter more than isolated nonlinear characteristic effects.
  • Macroeconomic variables become useful when their histories are compressed into latent recurrent states.
  • Adversarial construction of informative test assets materially improves SDF identification.

Notable quotes

Our crucial innovation is the use of the no-arbitrage condition as part of the neural network algorithm.
The “kitchen-sink” prediction approach with deep learning does not outperform a linear model with no-arbitrage constraints.
However, the multi-dimensional challenge of asset pricing cannot be solved with linear models and requires a different set of tools.

Limitations

  • The evidence is limited to U.S. equities and a sample ending in 2016.
  • The objective does not directly enforce SDF positivity, although the estimated SDF is positive in the sample.
  • The investment analysis does not provide a fully re-estimated net-of-transaction-cost trading strategy.

Open questions

  • Does the learned SDF generalize after 2016 and across international markets or other asset classes?
  • Can trading costs, liquidity, leverage, and capacity constraints be incorporated directly into adversarial training?
  • Can newer sequence architectures improve macro-state identification without sacrificing stability?
