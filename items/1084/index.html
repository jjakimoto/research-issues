Short-horizon mean reversion in cryptocurrency markets: a matched cross-market measurement

📰 Latest · 2026-08-25 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-25

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-22
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21888

Under a matched out-of-sample protocol, cryptocurrency markets exhibit pervasive but economically small 15-minute directional mean reversion. The effect follows aggressive order flow and remains detectable after conservative robustness adjustments, but its roughly 1.3-basis-point peak gross edge does not overcome benchmark spot-trading costs.

Executive summary

The authors compare 183 Binance USDT pairs with 187 liquid US stocks and ETFs under identical walk-forward models and inference. Mean crypto AUC is 0.531 versus 0.499 for US-listed instruments, with 90% versus 2.7% of assets jointly significant after false-discovery correction. The effect survives a six-month holdout, venue replication, factor removal, alternative estimators, and adjustments for flat bars and bar-boundary artifacts. Listed funds tend to inherit the reversal behavior of their underlying price process, while the strongest conditioning variable is aggressive taker flow rather than measured order-book depth consumption.

Matched cross-market design

Both markets are evaluated at 15-minute frequency using constrained distributed-lag and unrestricted AR(12) logistic models. Models train on 5,760 candles and are evaluated on non-overlapping 960-candle blocks; significance requires both models to pass moving-block-bootstrap tests followed by Benjamini-Hochberg correction.

  • Crypto mean AUC is 0.531, with 164 of 183 pairs jointly significant.
  • US-listed mean AUC is 0.499, with 5 of 187 instruments jointly significant.
  • A one-lag model reaches crypto mean AUC 0.5267, showing that most of the signal is reversal of the preceding candle.

Robustness and holdout

The headline class gap shrinks under increasingly conservative handling of flat bars and timing boundaries but stays positive. The frozen February-August 2026 holdout also preserves the effect, though at reduced magnitude.

  • Primary class-mean AUC gap: +0.031, 95% CI [+0.027,+0.035].
  • Non-flat bars plus a one-bar gap: +0.011, 95% CI [+0.008,+0.014].
  • Holdout gap: +0.020, 95% CI [+0.010,+0.028].
  • Coinbase, OKX, and Bybit replications produce significant results in all 12 focal venue-coin cells.

Market mechanism evidence

Reversal strengthens after moves aligned with aggressive taker flow and with greater flow intensity. Measured depth consumption contributes no comparable conditioning effect, which is consistent with compensated liquidity provision but does not causally establish it.

  • AUC is 0.540 after flow-driven bars versus 0.519 after flow-opposed bars.
  • The flow-driven-minus-flow-opposed sign-flip difference is +0.021, CI [0.015,0.026].
  • Depth-consuming versus depth-replenished moves differ in flip rate by -0.002, CI [-0.007,+0.004].

Economic significance

Higher confidence thresholds improve directional accuracy but reduce opportunity count. The maximum reported gross edge remains below public spot-market fee schedules throughout the cross-section.

  • Peak gross edge is approximately 1.3 basis points per trade.
  • The cheapest benchmark maker round trip is approximately 5 basis points.
  • None of the 183 crypto pairs clears the 5-basis-point band at any tested threshold.

Key findings

  • Short-horizon reversal is much broader in liquid cryptocurrencies than in matched US-listed instruments.
  • Most predictive information resides in the sign of the preceding return rather than its magnitude.
  • The conservative artifact-adjusted effect is smaller than the headline result but remains above zero.
  • Listed wrappers appear to inherit the process of their NAV-linked underlying rather than the average behavior of their listing venue.
  • The signal is statistically detectable but not profitable under the paper's benchmark public spot-fee assumptions.

Notable quotes

The population fact is therefore predominantly a lag-one reversal with a short memory tail.
Everything in this section is therefore a conditioning consistent with the provision reading, not a design that selects it.
The precise claim is that the edge is not exploitable under benchmark spot-cost assumptions: a public-schedule taker or maker paying explicit spot costs cannot capture it anywhere in the cross-section.

Limitations

  • The primary cryptocurrency universe comes from one exchange and is exposed to survivor and selection effects.
  • Cross-pair dependence is strong; the estimated effective sample size is roughly 2.5 rather than 183 independent replications.
  • The six-month holdout is short and already shows attenuation.
  • Order-flow evidence is observational, and the available depth snapshots do not capture tick-level book dynamics.
  • The authors disclose operating an automated cryptocurrency trading system using a variant of the model.

Open questions

  • Does the reversal continue to erode over longer prospective holdouts?
  • Would forced-liquidation flow provide a cleaner test of temporary price pressure?
  • Can tick-level book data identify depth effects hidden by 30-second snapshots?
  • What costs and capture rates do professional market makers face for this signal?
