Scores Alone Do Not Prove Discovery: The Discovery Certification Protocol for Auditing AI Research Agents

📰 Latest · 2026-09-10 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-10

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-07
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09219

DCP replaces "the agent scored X" with an executable three-gate audit: sealed-utility validation, a matched challenger given the same starting information (but not the run's research history) whose recovery refutes the claim, and an optional randomized truthful-vs-neutral feedback experiment. Two full audits recorded 0/96 recoveries with a 0.0468 upper bound and 30/30 vs 0/30 feedback recoveries, for about $56-61 each, with a deterministic LLM-free verifier replaying every decision offline.

Executive summary

The paper argues that a benchmark score does not establish that an AI research agent discovered anything, and proposes the Discovery Certification Protocol (DCP) to turn discovery claims into executable tests. Gate 1 validates useful improvement over a baseline on a sealed evaluator; Gate 2 runs fresh matched challenger agents given the registered background K, initial observations E0, and the exact Web bytes the target saw (W_obs) while withholding the run's research history L*, where any valid artifact scoring at least x - epsilon is a recovery witness that vetoes certification; optional Gate 3 randomizes truthful versus a neutral feedback channel from a shared checkpoint. Verification is execution-grounded and LLM-free.

The information boundary: K, E0, L*, A*, P

An audit is organized around five registered objects. K is background fixed before the run; E0 is task observations fixed independently of the run's research actions; L* is the research history created during the run; A* is the final output selected by a rule fixed before sealed testing; P is the machine check for recovery. The E0/L* split follows information provenance.

  • Core targets adaptive research with at least one new observation consumed by a later research action
  • A pool of independent candidates plus final score-based selection is only a search-and-selection baseline
  • Fixed human guidance enters E0; guidance chosen after intermediate results enters a human-agent lineage

Gate 1 and Gate 2 mechanics

Gate 1 requires LCB[mu(A*) - mu(b)] >= delta_min on a registered [0,1] scale. Gate 2 gives a fresh matched agent the same model, interface, K, complete E0, and W_obs, with a frozen non-directional response policy in place of new task scores. Recovery is P(a) = Valid(a) AND score(a) >= x - epsilon. With zero recoveries in n episodes the bound is p_upper = 1 - alpha_recovery^(1/n); Core requires p_upper <= rho, zero witnesses, and a complete adequate audit including positive controls.

  • Any valid method is eligible: recombination of known modules, cross-domain transfer, or a different implementation
  • A best-of-k procedure counts as one k-candidate episode, so search budget is inside the statistical unit
  • A witness closes the audit as 'recovered, Core refuted'

Gate 3: randomized feedback effect with null calibration

From a registered checkpoint, fresh paired branches receive either truthful feedback from their own actions or messages from a neutral policy that preserves timing, schema and approximate length while withholding information about the correct next action. Independent null tasks compare the neutral policy against a second non-directional policy; their interval must lie inside a registered equivalence band +/- delta_sham.

  • Both audits used delta_evidence = 0.34 and delta_sham = 0.17, so the required lower bound was 0.51
  • Both arms share model, tools, remaining budget, starting state and output checks; assignment and stopping frozen in advance

Verifier grounding, threat model, and provenance

DCP treats the claimant and the evidence producer as potentially strategic. Verification is execution-grounded, not LLM-as-judge: task adapters specify validity, scoring and feedback, and a shared deterministic verifier evaluates the resulting records offline. A recording gateway stores every Web query, response time and exact model-visible bytes, so the Gate 2 bound conditions explicitly on W_obs.

  • Every started confirmatory audit consumes a preallocated error budget, including recovered and incomplete audits
  • Model, information or tool-contract violations make the audit inconclusive
  • Both audits split alpha to a total of 0.05 across seven registered checks

Audit cases and cost

SQLite-Web (DeepSeek-v4-flash) models an event service with 16 query families scored by traffic-weighted VM-step reduction; the final plan scored 0.8855. Virtual catalyst (DeepSeek-v4-pro) has five controls with eight levels each (32,768 recipes) and a final recipe reaching 1.0 against a 0.5990 baseline. Multidimensional knapsack produced two witnesses above the recovery line and refutes Core; a low-sample affine case stays audit-incomplete.

  • SQLite-Web: 507 model sessions, $61.17; catalyst confirmatory: 419 sessions, $54.27 ($56.40 including development)
  • SQLite with Web disabled: 130 sessions, $25.56; device calibration: $19.08 recorded provider cost

Key findings

  • Both complete audits returned 0/96 challenger recoveries with a zero-hit upper bound of 1 - 0.01^(1/96) = 0.0468381 < rho = 0.05; all 45 positive controls succeeded, giving a recall lower bound of 0.8889268.
  • Best challenger scores stayed well below the recovery lines: 0.6733847 vs 0.8805470 for SQLite-Web, and 0.814649 vs 0.95 for the catalyst task.
  • Both paired feedback studies gave 30/30 truthful and 0/30 neutral recoveries, with an exact paired 99% interval of [0.6379275, 1], and 60-pair null studies with interval [-0.0950339, 0.0950339] inside the registered +/-0.17 band.
  • The knapsack case shows the veto working: one challenger episode returned two legal artifacts scoring 0.9362833 and 0.9355607, both above the 0.9328968 recovery line.
  • Episode counts follow from the bound: alpha_recovery = 0.01 with rho = 0.05 requires 90 episodes (96 registered).
  • Costs are modest for the evidence produced: $61.17 / 507 sessions (SQLite-Web) and $56.40 / 435 sessions (catalyst).

Notable quotes

The central design choice is to admit every valid route to the same numerical outcome.
Evidence production and decision checking have separate interfaces.
A model failure under the correct contract remains a failed attempt.

Limitations

  • Only two complete three-gate audits, both on synthetic/virtual tasks with a single model family, so external validity to real scientific research is untested.
  • The reported bundles record formal_certificate_issued=false; independent provenance verification and registry countersigning were not performed.
  • Non-recovery is evidence about the challenger distribution, not proof of novelty; historical priority is explicitly recorded as not_assessed.

Open questions

  • Does the protocol scale to open-ended research outcomes where no crisp validity predicate and numerical threshold exist?
  • How does the challenger distribution need to grow as models improve?
  • What does retrospective auditing cost for already-published claims?
