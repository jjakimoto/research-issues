    Model-agnostic noise reduction for high-dimensional time series data

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-23
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27614

    The paper introduces a linear denoiser that learns a dynamic factor space from lagged covariances and uses correlations between noise inside and outside that space to remove more noise than ordinary factor projection. For 459 S&P 500 stocks, it attributes only 10.22% of variance to dynamic signal versus 39.96% under unbiased orthogonal projection, but it finds no reliable forecasting gain—making this a useful preprocessing and measurement result, not evidence of trading alpha.

    Executive summary

    The method assumes that all serial dependence lives in a low-dimensional linear subspace while observational noise is white over time but can have arbitrary cross-sectional covariance and overlap the signal directions. It estimates the dynamic space from nonzero-lag autocovariances, recovers orthogonal noise from residual covariance, and uses that noise to predict and subtract correlated noise inside the dynamic space. Theory gives an MSE-optimal projection and root-T convergence under fixed-dimensional regularity conditions; simulations are favorable, but applications show that stronger denoising does not automatically improve forecasts.

    Problem and geometric insight

    Ordinary factor reconstruction orthogonally projects observations onto an estimated dynamic space, leaving all noise aligned with that space untouched. The proposed denoiser instead uses covariance between the observable orthogonal-noise component and parallel noise to regress out part of the latter. At population level it is the MSE-minimizing linear projection that preserves the dynamic space; when signal and noise spaces share no nonzero direction, the noise can be removed perfectly in principle.

    Estimation pipeline

    • Form K as a weighted sum of products of nonzero-lag sample autocovariances; because temporally white noise has zero lagged covariance, K identifies the dynamic space without the asymptotic bias of the contemporaneous covariance matrix.
    • Estimate the dynamic dimension with sequential bootstrap tests and take the leading eigenvectors of K as a basis.
    • Project the contemporaneous covariance into the dynamic-space complement, retain enough orthogonal-noise eigenvectors to exceed an explained-variance threshold, and insert the resulting covariance estimates into the optimal projection.
    • Experiments use lags 1 and 2, 300 bootstrap samples, significance level 0.05, and an orthogonal-noise explained-variance cutoff of 0.95.

    Theory and assumptions

    For known dynamic and orthogonal-noise dimensions, the estimated denoiser approaches its population target at O_P(T^-1/2). The result assumes a fixed finite dimension, stationary mixing behavior, independence between signal and noise across time, at least one informative full-rank factor autocovariance, and distinct nonzero eigenvalues of K.

    Synthetic evidence

    The default simulation uses a four-factor VAR(2), 50 observed dimensions, eight structured-noise modes, 20% noise variance, no unstructured noise, and T=400. Across 100-replication experiments, optimal denoising beats orthogonal projection for all tested VAR specifications, makes VAR parameter estimates consistent, and drives one-step forecasting error toward its theoretical lower bound. Even at a 99% noise fraction it removes about 70% of noise on average, although short series with weak signal can suffer harmful overfitting and dimension-selection errors.

    Financial and macroeconomic applications

    The stock panel contains 459 complete S&P 500 return histories over 2,795 trading days; the macro panel contains 20 FRED indicators with 802 monthly observations. Because the bootstrap dimension counter was unstable, the authors visually selected four dynamic factors for both applications. Optimal denoising sharply lowers estimated signal variance, especially for stocks, but stock forecasts do not meaningfully beat total variance and are not reported. In 65 leave-one-year-out macro folds, optimal denoising has average MSE 23.47 versus 22.36 for unbiased orthogonal denoising, with heavily overlapping standard errors.

    Key findings

    • Correlated orthogonal noise contains information about otherwise unremovable noise aligned with the dynamic factors, so orthogonal factor projection is generally not MSE-optimal.
    • When the dynamic and noise spaces have no nonzero common direction, the population denoiser achieves perfect linear recovery; the estimated result converges at the parametric root-T rate under the paper's assumptions.
    • In simulations, optimal denoising improves reconstruction, VAR coefficient estimation, and one-step forecasting; at a 99% noise fraction it still removes roughly 70% of noise on average.
    • For S&P 500 returns, estimated signal variance falls from 39.96% under unbiased orthogonal denoising to 10.22% under the proposed method; for the macro panel it falls from 73.72% to 46.29%.
    • The stronger denoising does not improve empirical macro forecasting: average MSE is 23.47 for the optimal method versus 22.36 for unbiased orthogonal projection, and the standard errors overlap substantially.

    Notable quotes

    The method uses only second-order properties and, seen as a pre-processing step, can be paired with many downstream models on the denoised series.
    On average, this method does not remove any noise from the observed time series.
    That this reclassification is so pronounced in both datasets suggests that real-world noise is often not orthogonal to the dynamic space, reinforcing the relevance of the optimal denoising approach developed in this paper.
    Because the fraction of the variance of the S&P 500 returns identified as signal by MSE-optimal denoising is so small, any downstream model fitted to the corresponding denoised factors is estimated on what is effectively very little signal.

    Limitations

    • The core model assumes additive temporally white noise, signal-noise independence, stationary mixing data, and fixed finite dimensions; financial noise with autocorrelation, stochastic volatility, heavy tails, or regime shifts can violate these conditions.
    • The sequential bootstrap dimension estimator is unstable in the real datasets, so the authors select d=4 by visual inspection and set the orthogonal-noise dimension through a practitioner-chosen 0.95 variance threshold.
    • The empirical evidence does not establish predictive or trading value: S&P 500 forecasts are omitted after showing no meaningful improvement, while macroeconomic forecast errors are statistically similar across methods.

    Open questions

    • Can shrinkage or ridge regularization prevent the harmful finite-sample overfitting observed for short, low-signal series?
    • How robust is the method to serially correlated, heteroskedastic, heavy-tailed, or time-varying noise typical of asset returns?
    • Can dynamic-dimension and noise-rank selection be made stable and fully data-driven while optimizing downstream nonlinear forecasts or trading objectives?
