Artificial Intelligence in Equity and Crypto Markets: Progress, Profitability Evidence, and the Limits of Automated Investing

📰 Latest · 2026-09-13
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-13

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-04
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04917

A critical review that grades every 'AI beats the market' claim against an alpha-translation chain and concludes that, in the public record through 31 August 2026, no AI architecture is shown to deliver persistent, cross-regime, capacity-aware net alpha — progress is real but sits upstream of the profitability evidence.

Executive summary

Zhu and Cai audit the public evidence (cutoff 31 August 2026) that AI — classical ML, deep asset-pricing models, time-series foundation models, financial LLMs, RL policies and agent frameworks — produces investable profit in listed equities/ETFs, centralized crypto spot, perpetual futures and on-chain markets. They organize claims along an 'alpha-translation chain' (point-in-time information -> stable signal -> feasible positions -> executable orders -> net risk-adjusted return -> persistence at scale) and score each study on a seven-dimension evidence profile rather than a leaderboard. Documented progress is concentrated upstream (representation, prediction, text extraction, workflow integration) while the demanding profitability claims are downstream, where the record is thin. The authors are explicit that 'insufficient' is not 'negative'.

Method and evidentiary boundaries

A critical state-of-the-art review, deliberately not a systematic review or meta-analysis: the authors argue pooling is misleading because reported Sharpe ratios differ in sampling frequency, annualization, universe, leverage, cash rate, benchmark, risk model, cost assumptions and model-selection budget. The unit of evidence is a bounded claim, not a paper-level endorsement.

  • Seven coded dimensions: temporality, selection, portfolio mapping, implementation, risk/benchmark, external validity, operational provenance
  • Unreported items are coded as unknown, never favourable; 'net' means net only of the disclosed cost model
  • Positive and negative evidence are read symmetrically
  • 'Sustainable net alpha' is defined as positive risk-adjusted performance surviving time-valid external or prospective evaluation across regimes at a stated capital scale

The alpha-translation chain and evidence archetypes

Realized net return is written as portfolio return minus fee, spread, impact, delay, financing and venue-specific deductions (funding, liquidation, gas, failed transactions, MEV); alpha is the intercept of a stated risk model fitted to that net return, not the return itself. The paper insists 'out of sample' is not the same as point-in-time.

  • Eight evaluation archetypes: CAP, TASK, OOS, GROSS, NET, PROSP, LIVE, EXT — explicitly not a total ordering
  • A linear cost model of the form k times traded notional 'silently assumes away much of the capacity problem'
  • Difficulty of beating 1/N diversification out of sample is the baseline warning against elaborate optimization layers

Prediction, foundation models and text

Nonlinear cross-sectional ML and deep SDF models add information under their protocols, but their support is strongest on historical prediction and disclosed simulations. Time-series foundation models show genuine zero-shot transfer while their direct profitability evidence is much weaker, and numerical pretraining corpora are hard to audit for contamination. Post-training-cutoff designs are treated as the strongest LLM profitability-related evidence.

  • Rolling-origin tests on five liquid US equities: pretrained TSFMs take eight of ten task-level wins, but gains over a random walk are significant in only two model-asset comparisons
  • FinVerse compares 43 public foundation models across 116,897 financial series and finds generic forecasting rank need not translate into useful financial forecasts
  • Ablations where removing the LLM component preserves or improves performance are cited as a demand for equal-budget non-pretrained baselines
  • Agents are an integration layer, not a new asset-pricing law; debate among correlated agents on one base model can produce 'verbose agreement rather than independent evidence'

Market structure: four strata, four P&L equations

The authors refuse a single return equation and cost scalar across markets. Equities carry survivorship, corporate-action and borrow issues plus highly cross-sectional capacity; centralized crypto spot adds changing token universes, wash volume, custody and prefunding; perpetuals add funding, mark-price liquidation and contract-spec leakage; on-chain markets add gas auctions, failed calls, MEV and loss-versus-rebalancing.

  • Perpetual P&L is written with a funding term and a discontinuous liquidation loss — a last-traded-price backtest 'can therefore report a path that the margined account could not survive'
  • Arbitrage-related losses can exceed fee income in many large Uniswap pools, so a perfect pool-price prediction can capture no surplus
  • The AI-specific crypto record is thinner than the equity record

The bounded verdict and research agenda

Affirmative evidence exists for nonlinear prediction, decision-focused portfolio learning, post-cutoff news LLM signals and point-in-time language-model checkpoints. Against that, four cumulative erosions: incomplete time validity, repeated search/publication selection, implementation and equilibrium decay, and restrained prospective/field evidence.

  • A corrected look-ahead alignment erased a reported monthly ML alpha, with the journal subsequently issuing an expression of concern
  • Controls are prescribed against delegated authority, not model quality: allow-lists, notional/leverage caps, price collars, stale-data rejection, transaction simulation, kill switch outside the model's control
  • The agenda is presented as falsifiable conditions that 'can improve evidence and implementation; they do not guarantee profit'

Key findings

  • Across the public record through 31 August 2026, no general AI architecture, foundation model or agent family is shown to deliver persistent, cross-regime, capacity-aware net alpha.
  • Time-validity failures are economically decisive: a reported monthly ML alpha vanished once predictors were aligned to their actual availability, and the journal later issued an expression of concern.
  • Prospective evidence is materially weaker than backtests: daily precommitted LLM stock recommendations tilt toward large, growth, momentum and media-attention names yet earn no statistically significant characteristic-adjusted abnormal returns; AI-labelled hedge funds outperformed early but the advantage declined.
  • Time-series foundation models win eight of ten task-level comparisons on five liquid US equities, but beat a random walk significantly in only two; a 43-model, 116,897-series benchmark finds generic forecasting rank does not translate into useful financial forecasts.
  • An audit of 77 agentic trading studies found only 19 with both action output and closed-loop evaluation — within that subset, two time-consistent splits, one explicit transaction-cost model, one documented survivorship treatment, and no fully replayable record.
  • The most cited agentic upstream win is explanatory, not tradable: an optimized agentic system raised explained contemporaneous return variation around earnings from ~8% to close to 20%.
  • A July 2026 SEC exhibit reported nearly 100,000 opened Agentic Trading accounts and more than USD 100 million in custody — adoption figures, not profitability evidence.

Notable quotes

Absence of a reported item is coded conceptually as unknown, not favourable.
Those are company-reported adoption figures, not counts of active or profitable accounts, evidence of safe use, or an independent audit.
The successful demonstration of a buy order is CAP-type operational evidence and a governance event, not a profitability experiment.
For independently audited live-capital performance that persists across regimes and capital scales, that public record is sparse.

Limitations

  • Not a systematic review: no database-complete retrieval, no duplicate independent screening, no registered protocol and no formal risk-of-bias scoring, so study selection is itself a judgement call.
  • The verdict is conditional on disclosure — proprietary firms may hold live records that are never published, so public insufficiency is not evidence of private absence.
  • The AI-specific crypto evidence base is explicitly thinner than the equity base, so cross-market conclusions are unevenly supported.

Open questions

  • Can shared, versioned point-in-time testbeds (including chronological model checkpoints) make claims cumulative rather than leaderboard entries?
  • Would prospective, fill-aware, risk-adjusted agent arenas run long enough to separate skill from beta and style exposure change the verdict?
  • How much of any measured AI edge is self-decaying through reflexive diffusion, and can that decay rate be estimated rather than assumed?
