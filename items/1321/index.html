Price manipulation in nonlinear transient impact models: rigidity before memory and complete positivity after memory

📰 Latest · 2026-09-05 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-05

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-02
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02447

Closes a 15-year-old open question in market-impact modelling: applying a concave impact law to the TRADING RATE and then convolving with a decaying kernel admits explicit, arbitrarily small round-trip money pumps at every exponent pair except exactly linear — so every calibrated (delta, gamma) pair in the literature is manipulable. The fix is architectural, not parametric: put the concavity on the impact STATE after the memory, where safety is exactly complete positivity of the kernel and power-law decay passes.

Executive summary

Minhyeok Lee classifies when nonlinear transient (propagator) impact models admit price manipulation, and shows the answer is decided entirely by the ORDER in which the nonlinearity and the memory kernel are composed. Rate-inside (nonlinearity applied to the trading rate, then convolved — Gatheral's 2010 model) is rigid: for ANY nonzero L^1 Volterra kernel, nonnegative cost on all finite piecewise-constant round trips forces the impact law to be affine, hence linear for any nonzero convolution kernel. With power-law impact sgn(x)|x|^delta and decay t^-gamma the safe set collapses from Gatheral's region delta+gamma>=1 to the single line delta=1, so the square-root law is always manipulable. State-outside (a monotone readout applied to the accumulated impact state) is instead classified by complete positivity of the kernel, and t^-gamma is completely positive — so square-root impact AFTER power-law memory is manipulation-free for every input. The negative result is backed by interval-arithmetic-certified finite witnesses at the calibrated exponents.

The open problem: two stylized facts that may not coexist

The propagator model of Bouchaud et al. (2004) and Gatheral (2010) writes the price response to a trading rate v as a memory kernel convolved with an instantaneous impact law. Two empirical regularities are usually imposed: single-trade response is concave and roughly square-root (Toth et al. 2011), and the response decays as a power law. Whether both can hold without allowing a profitable round trip has been open since Gatheral (2010) derived a slow-rate bound delta+gamma>=1; Curato et al. (2017) found negative-cost strategies numerically INSIDE that supposedly allowed region, and Schneider-Lillo (2019) and Abi Jaber et al. (2025) recorded the question as open.

  • Three architectures: rate-inside D=int H(t,s) f(v(s)) ds (Gatheral 2010); state-outside D=G*v with cost int v h(D) (nonlinear propagator, Abi Jaber et al. 2025); mixed D=G*f(v) with readout h.
  • Economic test: nonnegative impact cost on every round trip int_0^T v = 0, imposed only on ORDINARY controls with finitely many constant pieces — no relaxed or distributional strategies are admissible.
  • Definition 3.1: v is a price manipulation for (H,f) if C_{H,f}[v] < 0; (H,f) is 'safe' if cost is nonnegative on every round trip in the class.
  • Readout class H = {h continuous, nondecreasing, h(0)=0}. Quantifying over all monotone readouts is what makes complete positivity, rather than positive definiteness, the relevant kernel condition.

Main negative result: affine rigidity before the memory

Theorem 3.2 (Affine rigidity): let H in L^1(Delta_T) be nonzero and f arbitrary. Then C_{H,f}[v] >= 0 for every finite piecewise-constant round trip IFF f(x)=c+lambda*x with c*A_H(t)=kappa a.e. and lambda*Q_H[v]>=0 on round trips. Corollary 3.3: for a nonzero CONVOLUTION kernel the intercept must vanish, so safety forces f(x)=lambda*x on all of R. Theorem 3.4 shows a common speed cap only localizes the rigidity to the accessible rate interval — it does not make a nonlinear law safe there.

  • Theorem 3.5: for H_gamma(t,s)=(t-s)^-gamma, 0<gamma<1, safety holds IFF f(x)=lambda*x with lambda>=0.
  • Corollary 3.6: for f_delta(x)=sgn(x)|x|^delta there is NO price manipulation iff delta=1. For every delta != 1 a finite bounded piecewise-constant manipulation exists, even under any prescribed positive common speed cap.
  • Corollary 3.15: witnesses are smooth-approximable and scale as C[a*w] = a^(1+delta) C[w], so every neighbourhood of zero in C_c^inf contains a manipulation — bounds on speed, total variation, Sobolev/C^k seminorms or L^p norms do not restore safety.
  • Net effect on the exponent plane (Figure 1): Gatheral's region delta+gamma>=1 shrinks to the single safe line delta=1. The calibrated pairs (0.6,0.4), (0.5,0.5) and Curato et al.'s (0.55,0.45) all lie in the manipulable set.

The mechanism: a zero-volume chattering pump plus two thin baseline trades

Earlier rigidity theorems (Huberman-Stanzl 2004; Gatheral 2010 Lemma 4.1; Schneider-Lillo 2019 Lemma 3.5; Hey-Neuman-Tuschmann 2025b Thm 2.4) all compress a two-block round trip onto a horizon where the kernel is effectively permanent, so they need a finite G(0) or continuity at the origin — unavailable for t^-gamma. Lee's proof instead uses Lemma 3.7 (two-rate separation): if g is not proportional to the identity there exist x>0>y with duty theta=-y/(x-y) such that the mixture has ZERO mean rate but nonzero mean impact n_0. Repeating that pair over M cells gives a pump u_M with u_M -> 0 weak-star but g(u_M) -> n_0 * 1_P, and a product weak-star lemma valid for every L^1 kernel kills the pump's self-cost. Two thin baseline trades placed before and after then read out the nonzero impact moment.

  • Proposition 3.11 assembles this into a finite, bounded round trip with strictly negative cost: v = sigma*a*1_{I-} + u_M*1_P - sigma*a*1_{I+}, with sigma = sgn(n_0*d).
  • Only INTEGRABILITY of the kernel is used, so signed, non-convolution and integrably singular kernels are all covered, with no regularity assumption on f at all.
  • Section 3.5 reframes the linear side: sufficiency is conditional positive semidefiniteness of Q_H on the zero-integral subspace, weaker than full positive semidefiniteness.

Main positive result: complete positivity after the memory

Theorem 4.1 (scalar kernel converse): for real G in L^1(0,T), nonnegative state-outside cost for EVERY piecewise-constant input and every h in H is equivalent to complete positivity of G — i.e. both Volterra resolvents r_a + a G*r_a = a G and s_a + a G*s_a = 1 are nonnegative for every a>0. The converse is constructive: two hinge probes force r_a>=0 and s_a>=0, and a single fixed dead-zone readout suffices without a cap. Theorem 4.5 does the mixed case: universal safety forces f=lambda*id AND lambda*G completely positive.

  • Complete positivity forces G>=0 a.e. and rules out an initial delay, but does not force complete monotonicity or pointwise decrease (explicit oscillatory counterexample, eq. 4.17).
  • Corollary 6.3: for a decaying locally-integrable completely monotone transient — e.g. H(t)=t^-gamma, 0<gamma<1 — and ANY monotone readout such as h(x)=sgn(x)|x|^delta, the cost is bounded below by a storage quotient and is nonnegative for every input.
  • Theorem 5.2 / 5.4: for kernels with uniformly vanishing tails, round-trip safety on all horizons equals all-input safety; for permanent memory the two differ by an explicit storage quotient.
  • Section 6 classifies two-mode Prony kernels completely; Section 7 gives rho>=0 and b'+rho*b>=0 for time-inhomogeneous first-order memory; Section 8 gives a conservativity + drift-dissipation criterion for stable matrix memory.

Certified witnesses, friction, and how many blocks a manipulation needs

Section 3.6 displays explicit 'sparse comb' witnesses at the calibrated exponents: slow selling at unit rate over [0,1] with M brief intense buying bursts concentrated in a final span — exactly the burst pattern Curato et al. (2017) found numerically. Every block interaction is a finite combination of K(r)=r^(2-gamma)/((1-gamma)(2-gamma)), so the cost is a finite sum of fractional powers of rationals, evaluated in directed interval arithmetic with enclosure width below 1e-30 and reproduced in the ancillary supplement (verify.py). Section 9 then asks what actually saves the rate-inside model in practice.

  • Theorem 9.7 (two-block phase): a TWO-block manipulation exists iff delta < 1-gamma (making Gatheral's slow-rate bound sharp) or delta > delta_+(gamma), with delta_+(gamma) = +infinity exactly when gamma >= 2 - log_2 3 ~ 0.415. In the intermediate strip EVERY manipulating strategy needs at least three blocks.
  • Proposition 9.8: near delta=1 an explicit pump achieves negative cost with at most C_gamma * |delta-1|^(-2/(1-gamma)) blocks, on any horizon and inside any rate cap.
  • Theorem 9.5 (friction): under a common rate cap, a power penalty kappa*int Psi(v) with Psi = sum a_j |x|^p_j repairs the model with a finite coefficient iff p_min <= 1+delta; without a cap iff p_min <= 1+delta <= p_max.
  • Remark 9.6: a bid-ask spread alone (p=1) repairs only inside a cap, with kappa growing like ||G||_1 * B^delta — it converts manipulation into a size threshold rather than removing it. A quadratic cost alone (p=2) cannot repair concave impact. Spread + quadratic together repair every concave law 0<delta<=1.
  • Theorem 9.9: for permanent memory at cubic-critical friction (q=1+delta=3), the exact critical repair coefficient is kappa_* = 0.0881331300961391335081086746213..., attained by a one-hump inventory whose rate changes sign once.

Why a systematic-trading researcher should care

This is a specification theorem, not a trading signal. Any execution optimizer built on a rate-inside concave propagator is optimizing over a cost functional that is unbounded below on arbitrarily small, arbitrarily fast round trips — the optimizer will chase chattering artifacts rather than real alpha, and no amount of positivity checking on a coarse grid or over two-block schedules will detect it. The prescribed fix is to keep both stylized facts but move the concavity to the impact state, where the kernel condition is complete positivity and t^-gamma passes.

  • Calibration guidance: a completely monotone kernel is automatically completely positive, so fitting the decay as a mixture of exponentials with nonnegative weights guarantees the state-outside model is safe.
  • Diagnostic warning: for H=(t-s)^-1/2 with f(x)=sgn(x)x^2, every nonzero two-piece round trip has strictly positive cost even though a finite manipulation exists — two-rate checks and coarse grids cannot certify safety.
  • Mitigants that DO bite: a fixed minimum dwell time plus a fixed block count gives a quantitative safe neighbourhood of delta=1 (Proposition E.2), which is why real venues with latency and tick constraints do not obviously bleed money.

Key findings

  • Affine rigidity (Theorem 3.2): for ANY nonzero H in L^1 on the Volterra triangle and ANY f, nonnegative cost on every finite piecewise-constant round trip forces f(x)=c+lambda*x; for a nonzero convolution kernel the intercept must vanish, leaving f(x)=lambda*x (Corollary 3.3).
  • For power-law decay t^-gamma (0<gamma<1) and power impact sgn(x)|x|^delta, there is no price manipulation IFF delta=1 (Corollary 3.6). Gatheral's allowed region delta+gamma>=1 collapses to a single line, and the calibrated pairs (0.6,0.4), (0.5,0.5) and (0.55,0.45) are all manipulable — inside any rate cap and at arbitrarily small amplitude.
  • The new mechanism is a zero-volume 'chattering pump' (two rates x>0>y with duty theta=-y/(x-y): zero mean rate, nonzero mean impact) read out by two thin baseline trades. It needs only kernel integrability, so unlike Huberman-Stanzl / Gatheral / Hey-Neuman-Tuschmann it covers singular kernels such as t^-gamma with no G(0).
  • Certified finite witnesses exist at the calibrated exponents: at (delta,gamma)=(1/2,1/2) a 129-block sparse comb has cost <= -0.10963; at (3/5,1/2), 513 blocks give <= -0.17409; at (1/2,3/5), 513 blocks give <= -0.044262. Enclosures have width below 1e-30.
  • Reversing the composition order fixes it: state-outside safety for every input and every continuous nondecreasing readout is EXACTLY complete positivity of G (Theorem 4.1). Since t^-gamma is completely monotone hence completely positive, square-root impact applied after power-law memory is manipulation-free (Corollary 6.3).
  • Complexity and friction quantifiers: two-block manipulation exists iff delta<1-gamma or delta>delta_+(gamma), with delta_+(gamma)=infinity exactly when gamma >= 2-log_2 3 (~0.415); O(|delta-1|^(-2/(1-gamma))) blocks suffice near delta=1. A power penalty repairs the capped model iff p_min <= 1+delta; a spread alone only creates a size threshold, a quadratic cost alone fails for concave impact, and the two together repair every 0<delta<=1.

Notable quotes

For the fractional kernel, positivity is strict and can be seen directly.
The nonlinear necessity is the new part: the quadratic problem on the round-trip subspace is the only one that survives the rate-inside no-manipulation axiom.
This rules out a tempting but invalid inference: checking all two-rate schedules, or observing positivity on a coarse numerical grid, does not establish no manipulation for the unrestricted finite class.
For the empirical question that motivated the paper the answer is concrete: a concave power law applied to the trading rate before power-law memory is manipulable at every exponent pair, and the same law applied to the impact state after the same memory is not.

Limitations

  • Frictionless and deterministic: only the impact component of implementation shortfall on finite piecewise-constant controls, with no risk aversion, inventory penalty or probabilistic price model. Section 1.3 is explicit that nothing here rejects empirically concave impact — it locates where concavity can sit.
  • The witnesses require unbounded switching: finitely many blocks but no uniform bound on the count and no minimum dwell time. With both fixed, a quantitative safe neighbourhood of delta=1 reappears (Proposition E.2), so latency, tick size and dwell constraints materially blunt the practical exploit. Rate-inside rigidity also needs H in L^1.
  • Coverage is partial outside the scalar case: the finite-Prony hierarchy assumes nonzero instantaneous gain, the conic-complement and storage-matching conditions of Section 8 are only sufficient, and general nonlinear matrix convolution is not treated. Single-authored by an independent researcher, and Section 11 discloses substantive generative-AI assistance across proof development and drafting, so the proofs merit independent checking (ancillary verify.py certificates are provided).

Open questions

  • Is the block-count upper bound O(|delta-1|^(-2/(1-gamma))) tight? It is an upper bound for one explicit pump architecture, not a matching lower bound; the only general lower bound is 'at least three blocks' in the intermediate strip.
  • The critical repair coefficient is computed in closed form only for permanent memory at q=1+delta=3 (kappa_* = 0.08813...). What is it for general q and for transient kernels — i.e. what exact spread/quadratic coefficient does a desk need for a given calibrated (delta,gamma)?
  • Empirically: can a state-outside propagator be calibrated to the same square-root response and power-law decay data as the rate-inside models in use, and does it change optimal execution schedules or out-of-sample cost prediction enough to matter?
