    LiveOption: Evaluating LLM Agents in Structured Option Trading with Nonlinear Payoffs

    📰 Latest · 2026-10-04
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-04

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-27
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33470

    This is a carefully run negative result rather than an alpha claim. Six mid-tier open-weight LLMs (no frontier closed models) traded options on 2025 data and showed no edge. Median returns are negative in every episodic task, no difference between models survives Holm correction, and the three slightly positive 0DTE means disappear once the paper charges a realistic half-spread (pooled mean goes from -0.216pp to -0.452pp, p=0.013). The main value is the evaluation method: paired sign-flip tests with Holm correction, re-pricing fills at the spread, context ablations and Greek attribution. Keep the caveats in mind: one rollout per case, five overlay assets, proprietary data, and only 2 of the 6 models have training cutoffs before 2025.

    Executive summary

    LiveOption is a backtesting benchmark for LLM trading agents in US equity options. It has three task suites: portfolio overlays (covered call / protective put on 5 names), earnings-event trades (95 events, 24 names) and SPY 0DTE intraday trading (102 sessions), all on 2025 data. Each agent is an analyzer-executor pair. It sees a compressed option chain and IV surface, and every order passes a backend margin and risk verifier. Across six open-weight models and 1,235 selected runs, no model shows a statistically meaningful edge. The paper argues for reporting distributions, participation and tail risk, and for testing what information an agent uses by intervention (ablation) rather than inferring it from mean returns.

    Problem: equity-only LLM trading benchmarks miss derivative structure

    Existing LLM trading benchmarks focus on equities or crypto with buy/hold/sell actions and linear payoffs. They also suffer from look-ahead bias and ignore costs, a critique the authors cite from Kong et al. 2026. Options add nonlinear payoffs, theta decay, vega exposure, margin rules and a combinatorial choice over strike, expiry and structure. The authors claim this is the first systematic study of LLM agents in option markets.

    • Three use cases map to the roles of options: hedging/income (overlay), event speculation (earnings) and intraday leverage (0DTE).
    • Users can define their own scenarios with different objectives, constraints and trading frequencies.

    Architecture: analyzer-executor agent plus option-aware backtester

    The analyzer interprets the market and outputs high-level decisions. The executor turns those decisions into orders. Two representations shrink the option chain to fit the context window: AVSR summarises the IV surface with 3x3 anchor points over moneyness/delta and tenor, and QOCR quantizes actions into expiry buckets, moneyness levels and option types. A backend verifier enforces margin, position limits and contract validity. The backtester has two layers. Scenario Replay provides a deterministic step function over daily or 1-minute bars, serving prices, option-chain snapshots, news and event calendars that are time-gated to information timestamped before each step. The Trading Simulator keeps a brokerage-style account with multi-leg positions, expiry/assignment and forced closes.

    • Frictions in the shared engine: $0.65 per option contract, $0.0005 per share, 1.0 bp slippage, mid-quote fills (0 bp explicit spread).
    • A full decision trace (thesis text, intents, fills, Greek snapshots) is logged for behavioural analysis and BSM Greek PnL attribution.

    Experimental setup: 2025 data, six open-weight LLMs, explicit statistics

    Data is proprietary (MASSIVE Inc.) and cannot be redistributed. The framework ships converters so users can plug in their own licensed data. The evaluation period is full-year 2025. The six models are DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, MiniMax-M3 and GLM-5.3-Flash, all served by one hosted provider at temperature 0.5. Each case gets one selected rollout, giving 1,235 main runs (202-207 per model) plus 684 paired ablation runs on three models. Mean returns carry 10,000-resample bootstrap 95% CIs. Cross-model and ablation comparisons use paired two-sided sign-flip tests with Holm correction, and overlay Sharpe ratios use a stationary block bootstrap.

    • Overlay: QQQ, NVDA, AMD, GOOG, TSLA; 10,000 shares + 20% cash buffer; weekly decisions; Income DTE 7-45d, Hedge DTE 7-60d; no naked short premium.
    • Earnings: 24 single names, 95 events (90-95 valid per model); entry at T-2/T-1 close, every 30 min T to T+2, force-flat at T+2; $50k account.
    • 0DTE: SPY, every Wednesday and Friday of 2025 (102 sessions), decisions every 15 min, force-flat 15:55 ET.
    • Contamination: the authors state the 2025 window is NOT a contamination control. Only GPT-OSS-120B (Jun 2024) and Llama-3.3 (Dec 2023) have cutoffs before 2025. Qwen3's falls inside the window, MiniMax-M3's is Jan 2026, and two vendors publish none.

    Results: no edge in any task, and models are not statistically separable

    Overlay: active return against matched buy-and-hold is negative for all six models under hedging and for five of six under covered calls. Information ratio is negative in every cell. Hedging reduces max drawdown for five models (1.45-8.33pp). Earnings: every model has a negative mean and median and a profit factor below 1. Only MiniMax-M3's CI excludes zero, and it is negative. No pairwise difference survives Holm correction across the 15 comparisons. 0DTE: means range from -0.73% to +0.14%, every median is negative (-3.20% to -0.26%) and every CI includes zero. Charging the quoted half-spread (median $0.005, about 50x the simulator's applied slippage) erases all positive means.

    • Against a deterministic 0.30-delta rule, the LLM panel is +17.5pp ahead under covered calls and roughly level under hedging (+0.69pp). The rule cuts drawdown about twice as much (+8.20 vs +3.78). Both still trail passive buy-and-hold.
    • Returns are right-skewed. Every model loses on 62-76 of 102 0DTE sessions. Positive means rest on a handful of tail sessions (30 upside tails vs 3 downside across both tasks).
    • Turnover correlates 0.79 with the spread charge, so a zero-spread leaderboard partly ranks how much a model trades.

    Diagnostics: Greek attribution and targeted context ablations

    In Earnings, theta is the dominant attributed cost: -$1,178 to -$3,108 per case over a 1.5-2.4 day hold. Vega is small (+$11 to +$658), and the unexplained residual is large and noisy (-$31,340 to +$1,043). In 0DTE, theta runs -$98 to -$271 per session and delta straddles zero, so picking direction is not what separates the models. Ablations on three models, with 24 paired cases per profile, find no return effect significant after Holm correction. The large effects are on behaviour. Removing Greeks and multi-timeframe inputs cuts 0DTE fills per session from 29.5 to 4.4 and pushes DeepSeek-V4-Flash to 95.8% no-trade. Removing portfolio state raises mean max drawdown from 4.4% to 12.4%, for every model.

    • 0DTE risk-rejection rates span 7.0% to 66.1% across models.
    • Llama-3.3-70B under the hedge mandate let its equity leg sit below target for most of the year on 4 of 5 assets (e.g. 246 of 250 days on TSLA).

    Significance for agent builders

    The paper is most useful as a methodology template for evaluating trading agents skeptically. It conditions on a selection manifest, pairs episodes and applies multiple-comparison correction. It re-prices recorded fills against OPRA NBBO quotes (99.91% of 15,200 0DTE fills matched), and it uses ablations to test whether an agent actually uses an input. Its three design principles: put risk verification in the execution layer as infrastructure rather than prompt guidance; report no-trade and rejection rates and drawdown distributions next to returns; and test information dependence by intervention.

    • The result does not test frontier closed models (GPT-5-class, Claude, Gemini) or tool-augmented / multi-agent designs, which the authors list as unexplored.
    • Code and a data sample are claimed to be public, but the full results depend on licensed vendor data.

    Key findings

    • Overlay: active return vs buy-and-hold is negative for 6/6 models under hedging (-3.05 to -13.02pp) and 5/6 under covered calls. The information ratio is negative in every cell, so there is no risk-adjusted edge over passive exposure.
    • Earnings: all six models have negative mean and median returns and a profit factor below 1. No pairwise model difference survives Holm correction across 15 comparisons.
    • 0DTE: three models show small positive means (+0.11% to +0.14%), but every median is negative and every CI includes zero. Charging the quoted half-spread moves the pooled mean from -0.216pp (p=0.250) to -0.452pp (p=0.013) and leaves no model positive.
    • The simulator's applied slippage was $0.0001 per contract against a median quoted half-spread of $0.0050 (about 50x larger). Turnover correlates 0.79 with the spread charge, so GPT-OSS-120B falls from tied-first to third once spreads are charged.
    • LLM overlays beat a fixed 0.30-delta covered-call rule by 17.5pp of active return (the rule sold the AMD/GOOG rallies) but only tie the protective-put rule (+0.69pp), which buys about twice the drawdown reduction.
    • Context ablations change behaviour, not returns. No return effect is significant after Holm correction. Removing Greeks/multi-timeframe inputs drops 0DTE fills from 29.5 to 4.4 per session (95.8% no-trade for DeepSeek-V4-Flash), and removing portfolio state raises mean MDD from 4.4% to 12.4%.

    Notable quotes

    Any leaderboard computed under a zero-spread execution model partly ranks turnover.
    The evaluation window is therefore not a contamination control for this panel, and we do not present it as one.
    The execution model is therefore directionally right and economically negligible, and the returns reported elsewhere in this paper should be read as excluding spread cost.
    Prompt engineering alone cannot prevent the generation of tail-risk strategies, and in option markets, a single unconstrained position can exceed the entire capital budget.

    Limitations

    • Each case gets one selected rollout and the analysis conditions on valid completed runs. The CIs therefore reflect variation across market episodes, not variation between repeated generations or API reliability. The universe is also small: 5 overlay assets, 24 earnings names, SPY only for 0DTE, 2025 only.
    • Look-ahead is controlled only by within-run time-gating. Four of the six models have cutoffs inside or after 2025 or publish none, so recall of 2025 outcomes from pretraining is not ruled out. Spread costs are modelled only for 0DTE (mid-quote fills elsewhere), and the re-pricing does not model behavioural adaptation, queue position or market impact.
    • The model panel has no frontier closed models and only one agent architecture (analyzer-executor). The data is proprietary. There are also small internal inconsistencies: 0DTE capital is listed as $100,000 in Table 10 but as $10,000 episodes in Appendix I, and the overlay cadence is weekly in Table 10 but Mon+Wed for the mechanical baselines.

    Open questions

    • Would frontier closed models, or tool-augmented / multi-agent designs, close the gap, or is the negative result structural to LLM-driven option selection under these constraints?
    • If quoted spread and depth were visible at decision time (the L1 replay the authors plan), would agents trade less and reduce the turnover penalty?
    • How much do the model rankings and ablation effects vary across repeated rollouts at temperature 0.5, given that only one rollout per case was evaluated?
