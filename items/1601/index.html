SAiFE-gym: Model-based Environments for Automated Market Making with Concentrated Liquidity

📰 Latest · 2026-09-18
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-09-18

Source: arxiv · 2026-09-15
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17788

An open-source, Gymnasium/SB3-compatible, NumPy-vectorized simulator for Uniswap-v3-style concentrated-liquidity AMMs that makes LP strategy an RL control problem with realistic microstructure (liquidity-reactive order arrivals, depth-dependent impact, gas, latency) — with a ~30x rollout speedup and domain randomization for robustness to market-parameter shift.

Executive summary

SAiFE_gym is a Python module of model-based RL environments for constant product markets with concentrated liquidity, decomposing AMM microstructure into swappable components: external mid-price process, liquidity-taker arrival intensities, price impact, blockchain frictions (gas, latency), agents, and reward functions. Environments are natively vectorized over a leading batch dimension with NumPy rather than process-level multiprocessing, giving roughly 30x faster rollouts on an 8-core/16GB machine and much lower variance in mean episode reward. Gymnasium wrappers connect the AMM engine to Stable Baselines 3. The demonstration trains PPO liquidity-provider agents under fixed market parameters and under episode-level domain randomization over volatility and baseline arrival intensity, evaluating in- and out-of-distribution.

Motivation: the gap between stylized AMM math and RL

AMMs have facilitated $4 trillion in trading volume across Ethereum and compatible networks. Mathematical-finance treatments of LP problems stay low-dimensional to remain analytically solvable and abstract away gas and latency. The authors argue model-based simulation for AMM microstructure is fragmented with no unified open-sourced collection, and position SAiFE_gym as the AMM analogue of a modular limit-order-book gym.

  • Concentrated liquidity (Uniswap v3) massively expands the LP action space, which was previously passive
  • The CL pool implementation mirrors real deployed pools: tick grid, per-tick depth, pro-rata fees
  • Positioned as an alternative to agent-based simulators, which generate dynamics from agent interaction rather than specified processes

Market mechanics and state representation

Reserves satisfy the constant product invariant with marginal rate Z and a proportional fee on the input amount. A CL position occupies an interval whose endpoints come from a finite ordered tick grid; each tick range carries a translated constant-product invariant, so the CL pool is a segmented CPM. The state is reparameterized from asset quantities to the per-tick depth profile plus Z — an LP action changes only the depth profile while a liquidity-taker action changes only Z. Fees are stored per tick range and distributed pro-rata by depth share.

  • Below the range the position is all risky asset; above it, all numeraire (single-sided = a synthetic limit order)
  • Every LP action withdraws holdings, collects accrued fees, and reinvests full wealth into the new position

Stochastic components: prices, arrivals, impact, frictions

The external mid-price is exogenous, with Brownian motion, geometric Brownian motion, and an arithmetic model with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck alpha signal implemented. The marginal rate Z is an endogenous pure jump process on the tick grid. Arrival intensities follow a linear model in which one term couples arrivals to nearby liquidity through exponential kernels over neighbouring ticks and another captures arbitrage pressure pulling Z toward the external price — so the LP's own action influences future order flow. The paper claims this queue-reactive-style liquidity kernel is a first in the CPM-with-concentrated-liquidity context.

  • Depth-independent impact: each trade moves Z exactly one tick
  • Depth-dependent impact: smoothed forward/backward depth over H ticks; trade size Q jumps Z by floor(Q / D) ticks
  • Gas modelled as a fixed per-rebalance cost, so cost scales with rebalancing frequency; latency modelled deterministically as a decision budget

RL layer: wrappers and vectorization

Gymnasium APIs keep the wrapper layer algorithm-agnostic and compatible with Stable Baselines 3. StructuredMultiDiscreteVecEnv reparameterizes the Uniswap-v3-native [lower_offset, upper_offset, hold] action into the more separable [center, half_width, hold]. DecisionStrideEnv decouples market step frequency from agent decision frequency. ReduceStateSizeWrapper offers standard observation features: time remaining, mispricing, current position, accrued fees. All state, actions, rewards and stochastic processes carry a leading batch dimension in NumPy, so one step produces many transitions in a single amortized pass.

  • Vectorization is native to the AMM structure, distinct from Gymnasium's generic VectorEnv
  • Contrasts with process-level rollout parallelism across CPU cores

Experiments: nominal PPO vs domain randomization

A PPO LP agent trades in a GBM market (mu=0, sigma=0.03) with intensity parameters alpha_1=450, alpha_2=10, alpha_3=4000 and depth-dependent impact. Initial price 100, LP wealth 1000 in numeraire, 100,000 liquidity units per tick range, trade size 250 (about a 5-tick impact), gas 2. The agent acts once every 100 steps within +/-50 tick ranges under a running-inventory-penalty reward (phi=0.4, eta=0), against a baseline that rebalances symmetrically every 100 steps. Domain randomization samples sigma ~ U(0.01, 0.05) and alpha_1 ~ U(300, 600) per episode; the parameters are not observed, so the agent faces a latent-parameter POMDP. Evaluation spans 9 parameter points, in- and out-of-distribution.

  • Larger domain-group count broadens coverage per PPO update; smaller gives more repeated trajectories per domain
  • The domain-randomized policy is feedforward; a recurrent policy inferring dynamics from history is flagged as an alternative

Key findings

  • Vectorized NumPy rollouts of 1000 trajectories take approximately 0.05s versus approximately 1.6s for the multiprocessing approach — roughly a 30x speedup on one machine with 8 CPU cores and 16 GB RAM.
  • Raising parallel trajectories from 10 to 10,000 reduces the variance of mean episode reward across training updates, but performance differs little between n=100 and n=1000, suggesting ~100-1000 is the practical sweet spot.
  • The arrival-intensity model makes order flow endogenous to the LP's own liquidity placement via exponential liquidity kernels — described as the first queue-reactive-style coupling for CPMs with concentrated liquidity.
  • Depth-dependent impact converts an order of size Q into floor(Q / D) tick jumps using smoothed depth, versus the one-tick-per-trade reduced form used in prior Uniswap v3 work.
  • Benchmark configuration: sigma=0.03, alpha_1=450, alpha_2=10, alpha_3=4000, initial price 100, LP wealth 1000, 100,000 liquidity units per tick, Q=250 (~5 ticks of impact), gas 2, decision stride 100 steps, action range +/-50 ticks, phi=0.4, eta=0.
  • Domain randomization over sigma ~ U(0.01,0.05) and alpha_1 ~ U(300,600) is evaluated at 9 parameter points including out-of-distribution stress points, against a nominal PPO trained only at (0.03, 450) and a periodic-rebalancing baseline.

Notable quotes

This wrapper separates the market decision frequency from the agent’s decision frequency.
The restrictions on the agent’s action space are natural, but, importantly, they improve the agent’s exploration.
Consequently, the domain randomized agent must learn behaviors that perform well across the domain distribution.
Gas fees are mostly a flat fee paid to the blockchain and do not depend on LP’s specific position.

Limitations

  • Single-agent only: liquidity provision is strategic with multiple LPs competing for order flow, but multi-agent RL environments are left to future work.
  • An 8-page tool note — results are figures of sampled trajectories and regime heatmaps rather than tabulated PnL/Sharpe statistics; the only comparator is a naive symmetric periodic-rebalancing agent.
  • Latency is deterministic, trading fees are static, and the mid-price is exogenous, so MEV, adversarial ordering, and fee competition are outside the model.

Open questions

  • How much of the robustness gain survives with a recurrent policy that infers the latent market domain from history, rather than the feedforward domain-randomized policy used here?
  • Do policies trained in this environment transfer to real Uniswap v3/v4 pool data, given the intensity and kernel specification is calibrated rather than learned from chain data?
  • What equilibria emerge once multiple strategic LPs and dynamic fee setting are added?
