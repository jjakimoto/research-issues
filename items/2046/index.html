    SeeClick: Harnessing GUI Grounding for Advanced Visual GUI Agents

    ← Go backOpen gaps — 2026-10-07NextScreenAI: A Vision-Language Model for UI and Infographics… →
    📚 Foundations · 2026-10-07 Full Article
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-10-07

    Theme: Screenshot-native GUI understanding and control
    Screen-native agents must first parse heterogeneous interfaces, then ground language in precise visual targets, and finally act over changing states. ScreenAI establishes scalable screen understanding and annotation, while SeeClick isolates GUI grounding as a transferable bottleneck for visual automation.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10935

    A foundational screen-native agent paper that turns language-conditioned GUI element localization into a pre-training objective, introduces ScreenSpot, and quantifies how grounding quality affects downstream automation.

    Executive summary

    SeeClick identifies GUI grounding—locating a screen element from a natural-language instruction—as a central bottleneck for screenshot-only agents. The authors continually pre-train Qwen-VL-Chat on roughly one million web, mobile, and general vision-language examples, then evaluate the resulting model on the new ScreenSpot benchmark and three downstream automation datasets. SeeClick reaches 53.4% average ScreenSpot accuracy and improves screenshot-only task performance, although it still trails HTML-based methods on real websites and requires task-specific data for multistep workflows.

    Problem and grounding formulation

    The paper formulates GUI grounding as predicting an element location from a screenshot and textual description. Rather than adding coordinate tokens, SeeClick generates normalized numerical coordinates as ordinary language and is later adapted into an agent that consumes the current screenshot, the instruction, and four previous actions.

    • Grounding targets are represented as either a center point or a bounding box, with coordinates normalized to the range [0,1].
    • A representative grounding target is generated in the form "click (0.49, 0.40)" and optimized with standard cross-entropy loss.
    • The downstream action space covers clicking, typing, dropdown selection, directional swipes, Back, Home, Enter, and task-completion states.

    GUI-focused data and continual pre-training

    SeeClick combines automatically mined web interfaces, reorganized mobile-interface datasets, and general LLaVA instruction data. It continually pre-trains Qwen-VL-Chat for about 10,000 steps while applying LoRA to both the visual encoder and language model.

    • Approximately 300,000 Common Crawl web pages supply visible-text and title-attribute elements for grounding and inverse OCR tasks.
    • Mobile data includes nearly 20,000 screenshots, 40,000 widgets, and 100,000 descriptions, augmented with RICO elements, UI summarization, and reversed widget-caption grounding pairs.
    • The final mixture contains about one million examples: 433K web localization/OCR samples, 420K mobile samples, and 145K general LLaVA samples.
    • Training uses AdamW, cosine annealing from a 3e-5 initial learning rate, a global batch size of 64, and about 24 hours on eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

    ScreenSpot grounding benchmark

    ScreenSpot evaluates whether a predicted click falls inside the target element bounding box across mobile, desktop, and web screenshots. Its more than 600 screenshots and 1,200 instructions deliberately include both text and harder icons/widgets, with interfaces collected separately from the training resources.

    • SeeClick obtains 53.4% average click accuracy, compared with 47.4% for the 18B CogAgent, 16.2% for GPT-4V, and 5.2% for its Qwen-VL starting point.
    • SeeClick is strongest on mobile text at 78.0%, but its 55.7% web-text score and 72.2% desktop-text score remain below CogAgent's 70.4% and 74.2%.
    • All evaluated models perform substantially worse on icons/widgets than text, exposing non-text localization as a persistent bottleneck.
    • Error analysis shows that many incorrect predicted points lie near the correct element, suggesting recognition without sufficiently precise localization.

    Downstream visual-agent results

    The model is fine-tuned and tested on MiniWob, Android In The Wild, and a screenshot-derived version of Mind2Web. Across all three, the GUI-grounding pre-training materially improves the Qwen-VL baseline, while an instruction-wise AITW split is introduced to reduce leakage from repeated trajectories.

    • On MiniWob, SeeClick scores 73.6% over the 45-task comparison using 2.8K episodes, versus 65.5% for WebGUM trained on the same amount of data.
    • In the vision-only MiniWob comparison, SeeClick reaches 67.0%, versus 48.4% for Qwen-VL and 64.6% for Pix2Act trained on 1.3M examples.
    • On AITW, SeeClick reaches 59.3 overall and 66.4 click accuracy, versus Qwen-VL's 54.3 and 57.4.
    • On Mind2Web, SeeClick nearly doubles Qwen-VL step success across cross-task, cross-website, and cross-domain splits, but remains below HTML-based MindAct.

    Significance and remaining constraints

    Checkpoint analysis reports that improvements on ScreenSpot consistently accompany better downstream scores, supporting grounding as a transferable capability rather than a benchmark-only objective. Joint training across all three agent datasets works but causes a modest decline relative to separate fine-tuning, and the paper explicitly limits the current system's action coverage and multistep generalization.

    • Separate SeeClick models score 67.0, 59.3, and 20.9 on MiniWob, AITW, and Mind2Web; the unified model scores 64.1, 57.1, and 19.5.
    • The screenshot-only design avoids dependence on platform-specific HTML or accessibility trees, but coordinate prediction remains harder than choosing among structured candidates.
    • The stated action-space limitation excludes complex interactions such as dragging and double-clicking.
    • The authors report that agent-specific training is still necessary for multistep mobile and computer tasks.

    Key findings

    • A one-million-example GUI-focused continual-pre-training stage raises average ScreenSpot accuracy from 5.2% for Qwen-VL to 53.4% for SeeClick.
    • SeeClick exceeds the larger CogAgent on average ScreenSpot accuracy, although CogAgent remains stronger for desktop and web text localization.
    • Grounding improvements transfer to three distinct automation settings: synthetic web tasks, Android interaction, and real-website navigation.
    • The instruction-wise AITW split exposes unseen-instruction generalization more directly than the original episode-wise split with repeated instructions and similar trajectories.
    • Screenshot-only operation is viable across platforms, but structured HTML candidates still confer a large advantage on complex real-world websites.

    Notable quotes

    Subsequently, we normally compute the cross-entropy loss between the model output and the ground truth “click (0.49, 0.40)” to optimize the LVLM.
    This split avoids overfitting and reflects the performance of agents on unseen instructions.
    This highlights the difficulty of grounding in intricate interfaces, suggesting substantial room for improvement in visual agents for real-world application.
    Our preliminary experiments indicated that predicting points was slightly better than bounding boxes, likely due to the variable sizes of UI elements.

    Limitations

    • SeeClick mainly models clicking and typing and excludes complex actions such as dragging and double-clicking.
    • The underlying open-source LVLM still needs agent-specific fine-tuning to perform multistep mobile and computer tasks.
    • The model struggles with fine-grained icon/widget localization and remains behind HTML-based agents on Mind2Web; the authors also attribute weaker desktop/web text grounding partly to resolution and training-data scale.

    Open questions

    • Can richer high-resolution visual representations close the icon/widget and small-target localization gap without relying on HTML or accessibility metadata?
    • Can one GUI model learn heterogeneous platforms and full interaction vocabularies without the negative transfer observed under unified downstream training?
    • How can screenshot-native agents execute long, safety-sensitive workflows without task-specific demonstrations while preserving privacy and preventing unintended actions?
