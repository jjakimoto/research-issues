    Deep Learning of Robust Market Making under Regime-Switching Order Flow

    📰 Latest · 2026-09-19
    Read full article →View on GitHub

    Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
    Date: 2026-09-19

    Source: arxiv · 2026-09-10
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11614

    A distributional-RL market maker beats the classical GLFT closed form across the whole stationary risk-return frontier, but both collapse under persistent directional order flow; adding a Bayesian change-point flow belief plus a queue-adjusted quote-exposure signal, then a scenario-bandit fine-tune, restores profitability and trims the stressed left tail.

    Executive summary

    Moret and Lillo (Scuola Normale Superiore) train a Rainbow-style distributional DQN (C51) market maker inside a queue-faithful, event-driven zero-intelligence (Santa Fe) limit order book calibrated on 28 days of LOBSTER Level-3 AMZN data, and benchmark it against a censored-waiting-time-calibrated GLFT policy and an at-best baseline under paired seeds. Under stationary symmetric flow the RL frontier dominates GLFT everywhere; under static asymmetry GLFT turns loss-making beyond |p_buy-0.50|~0.08 while the RL agent stays profitable over p_buy in [0.40,0.60]. Under regime-switching flow the stationarily trained controller (Algorithm A) saturates its inventory at the hard cap and falls to roughly -46% of its symmetric-flow PnL; fine-tuning with a Bayesian online change-point belief and a queue-adjusted quote-exposure imbalance (Algorithm B) returns it to about +37% with tighter inventory. A final scenario-bandit robust fine-tuning stage (Algorithm C) improves stressed mean PnL by ~16-17% and cuts stressed PnL dispersion to ~70-76%.

    Setup: a queue-faithful ZI limit order book and a calibrated GLFT benchmark

    The simulator is a continuous-time, event-driven zero-intelligence (Santa Fe) LOB with per-order queue tracking and price-time (FIFO) priority, driven by three Poisson flows (limit orders per side per level, market orders of total intensity 2mu with buy probability p_buy, and per-unit cancellations). GLFT quotes are computed from an exponential fill-intensity model and discretized onto the tick grid; the authors stress GLFT is used as a strong analytical benchmark, not a structural model of a FIFO book.

    • Santa Fe parameters calibrated from LOBSTER Level-3 AMZN data, 28 trading days 1 Aug 2025 - 10 Sep 2025, ~2.9e7 message events, first/last 60 min excluded: lambda=0.06, mu=0.10, theta_cxl=0.02 per simulated second.
    • Fill intensity Lambda(delta)=A*exp(-kappa*delta) estimated by censored waiting-time probes with window 0.5 s, giving A=0.1507 s^-1 and kappa=2.335 ticks^-1.
    • Volatility taken as the plateau of the volatility signature plot, sigma ~ 0.30 ticks/sqrt(s), avoiding microstructure-noise contamination.
    • Calibration episodes are disjoint from evaluation episodes; all policies run on matched paired seeds inside the same simulator.

    RL formulation: throttled SMDP, six actions, Rainbow/C51

    Control is posed on a 1-second throttle clock rather than on raw book events, making the problem a semi-Markov decision process with discount Gamma_t = gamma^{N_t}. The state holds the touch spread, near-touch depths (K=1), signed inventory, and (only in the non-stationary experiments) an auxiliary feature vector. The reward is dampened fill PnL minus a quadratic inventory penalty plus a one-sided inventory-wall term active beyond q_w = q_max/2, with a hard cap enforced by post-hoc dropping of one quote leg.

    • Backbone combines DQN, Double DQN, dueling heads, prioritized replay, NoisyNet exploration, n-step targets and C51 with N=101 atoms.
    • Action set is the one-tick grid minus three economically extreme cells: {(-1,-1),(-1,0),(0,-1),(0,0),(0,+1),(+1,0)}.
    • A smart-quoting layer keeps a resting order in place when its target price is unchanged, preserving FIFO queue priority.
    • Action-space ablation: under stationary flow the six- and nine-action grids tie (+0.339 vs +0.331 mean terminal PnL over 300 paired episodes, extra cells chosen 10.3% of the time), but under the short non-stationary fine-tune the six-action grid dominates at every checkpoint (best training moving average +0.097 vs -0.005).

    Stationary results and the asymmetry breakdown

    Over 1000 paired-seed episodes the Rainbow DQN risk-return frontier (sweeping the inventory penalty phi) lies above the GLFT frontier (sweeping gamma_GLFT) across the whole observed risk range, with the at-best baseline as a lower reference. GLFT itself beats the at-best baseline mainly through inventory control, not mean PnL: paired Wilcoxon median differences are +0.02, -0.015 and -0.10 for gamma_GLFT = 1e-6, 1e-3, 1e-1. Under static flow asymmetry both GLFT variants cross into losses once |p_buy-0.50| exceeds about 0.08, while the RL maker remains profitable on p_buy in [0.40,0.60].

    • The closed-form misspecification analysis absorbs FIFO queue competition into an effective rate A_eff = eta*A with eta ~ 0.82.
    • The resulting stationary controller is named Algorithm A.

    The robustness failure: inventory saturation under persistent regimes

    Non-stationary flow is modelled as piecewise-constant p_buy on the market-order clock, with regime lengths L_k ~ Exp(1/tau_r) and p_buy,k ~ Uniform[0.50-omega, 0.50+omega], omega fixed at 0.30 (so p_buy in [0.20,0.80]). A single-episode diagnostic at tau_r=60 shows Algorithm A pinned at the +/-8 inventory cap during sustained directional regimes, accruing adverse-selection losses; the population sweep over tau_r in {15,30,60,120,240} shows monotone PnL decay for both a less risk-averse (phi=1e-3) and a more risk-averse (phi=5e-3) controller.

    • The latent p_buy schedule is never observed by the agent.
    • GLFT shows the same monotone collapse in tau_r, and no parameter of the closed form can absorb it because the quotes are flow-blind.

    Algorithm B: Bayesian change-point belief + queue-adjusted quote exposure

    Fine-tuning activates an auxiliary feature vector e_t = (b_t, x_t) without changing the action space, reward or simulator. b_t is the Bayesian online change-point filter's summary (posterior directional bias iota_hat = 2*p_hat-1 and posterior expected run length), updated on the market-order clock; x_t is a queue-adjusted quote-exposure imbalance built from inverse queue-ahead opportunities on each live side, ranging in [-1/2, 1/2]. Value-function heatmap slices confirm the learned logic: value peaks near zero inventory, and a long position is judged less dangerous when the filter detects buy-heavy flow.

    • Evaluation over 100 paired episodes of 100,000 LOB events at tau_r=60, omega=0.30.
    • Relative to the Algorithm A symmetric-flow mean, terminal PnL is -46% for non-adapted Algorithm A under regimes and +37% for Algorithm B, a paired recovery of about 80% of the stationary benchmark (paired t-test p < 1e-4).
    • Time-averaged inventory standard deviation moves from 1.89x the symmetric baseline (non-adapted) to 0.55x (Algorithm B).

    Algorithm C: scenario-bandit robust fine-tuning

    A pool of M=256 exogenous regime scenarios (128 from a random-persistence family, 128 from a correlated-direction family with sign persistence rho_side=0.85 and imbalance intensity Uniform[0.00,0.30]) is treated as bandit arms. Each arm carries an EWMA difficulty score (eta=0.05) of its penalized terminal loss; difficulties are standardized, softmaxed with inverse temperature beta_sb, mixed with a uniform epsilon term, capped at w_max=0.05 per arm, and the easiest 10% of the pool is regenerated every 500 bandit updates. The network update stays the ordinary Rainbow/C51 rule, warm-started from Algorithm B.

    • beta_sb ramps 0.00 to 0.50 and epsilon decays 1.00 to 0.50 over the first half of fine-tuning.
    • Random-persistence stress: mean terminal PnL +16%, empirical std down to ~70% of the Algorithm B level.
    • Correlated-direction stress: mean terminal PnL +17%, std down to ~76%.
    • No new state information is added to the agent - only the replay distribution over regimes changes.

    Key findings

    • Calibration: Santa Fe triple (lambda, mu, theta_cxl) = (0.06, 0.10, 0.02) per simulated second from ~2.9e7 LOBSTER Level-3 AMZN messages over 28 days; censored waiting-time fit with a 0.5 s window gives A = 0.1507 s^-1 and kappa = 2.335 ticks^-1, and the volatility signature plateau is ~0.30 ticks/sqrt(s).
    • Under stationary symmetric flow over 1000 paired-seed episodes, the Rainbow/C51 frontier lies above the GLFT frontier across the entire observed risk range; GLFT's edge over the at-best baseline is inventory control, with paired Wilcoxon median PnL differences of +0.02, -0.015, -0.10 at gamma_GLFT = 1e-6, 1e-3, 1e-1.
    • Static asymmetry: GLFT (both empirical and closed-form) turns loss-making once |p_buy - 0.50| exceeds about 0.08, whereas the RL maker stays profitable over p_buy in [0.40, 0.60]; the FIFO queue-competition factor is eta ~ 0.82.
    • Regime-switching flow (tau_r = 60, omega = 0.30) drives the stationarily trained controller to -46% of its symmetric-flow mean PnL with inventory dispersion 1.89x baseline; Algorithm B recovers to +37% with dispersion 0.55x (paired t-test p < 1e-4), an ~80% paired recovery.
    • Persistence sweep: Algorithm A decays from about 30% at tau_r = 15 to -91% at tau_r = 240, while Algorithm B only falls from about 46% to 23% and never becomes loss-making.
    • Scenario-bandit fine-tuning (pool M = 256, EWMA rate 0.05, per-arm probability cap 0.05) lifts stressed mean terminal PnL by about 16% (random persistence) and 17% (correlated direction) while cutting empirical std to about 70% and 76% of the Algorithm B level.
    • Action-space ablation: the six-action grid ties the nine-action grid under stationary flow (+0.339 vs +0.331 over 300 paired episodes) but dominates under the 4000-episode non-stationary fine-tune (best training moving average +0.097 vs -0.005).

    Notable quotes

    This smart-quoting layer is essential: canceling and reposting at the same price every decision would reset queue priority under price–time priority and erase the spread-capture edge, so queue priority is treated as an economic resource the agent earns by holding.
    Third, robustness rather than raw profitability is the central open problem: agents trained in a stationary order-flow simulator are strongly unprofitable under persistent directional flow, and this fragility is not resolved by larger networks or more training data alone.
    The bandit changes which stored regimes are replayed more often, directing updates toward adverse realizations.

    Limitations

    • All evaluations use simulated order flow from a calibrated zero-intelligence Santa Fe model with no strategic counterparties, so adverse selection from informed traders and endogenous reactions to the maker's quotes are absent.
    • The maker quotes unit lots with at most one resting order per side, so order sizing and multi-level quoting are out of scope.
    • The regime model fixes width omega = 0.30 with exponential durations and two directional modes; heavy-tailed durations, more modes, and intraday seasonality are not explored.

    Open questions

    • Does the Bayesian-belief plus quote-exposure state survive transfer to historical or agent-based LOBs with strategic counterparties, where the maker's own quotes change the flow it is trying to infer?
    • How much of the Algorithm C gain comes from the bandit reweighting itself versus simply training on the wider mixture P0, and would a formal distributionally-robust objective do better?
    • Can the approach scale to multi-level, size-aware quoting without losing the convergence advantage that motivated the deliberately compact six-action grid?
