Same Model, Different Harness: Different Coding-Agent Results

📰 Latest · 2026-08-28 Full Article
Read full article →

Topic: Quantitative trading; machine learning for quant & trading; agentic coding techniques; LLMs & multimodal LLMs; and autonomous research (autoresearch / AI-scientist) systems — methods, papers, tools
Date: 2026-08-28

Source: arxiv · 2026-08-26
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26218

Holding model weights, tasks and time budget fixed and changing only the harness configuration swings SWE-bench Verified complete solutions from 43 to 72 (mean per-task F2PF 28% to 49%) at a 20,480-token window — a harness effect as large as the spread between four different open-weight models. The catch: the gains are not compute-matched (2-3x prompt tokens, 3.5-5.9x wall time) and vanish once context is effectively unconstrained.

Executive summary

A strictly paired control-vs-treatment study using Yuj, an open-source harness, on SWE-bench Verified (169 tasks), SWE-bench Pro (316) and FeatureBench (183). Control shows the full chronological transcript until it fills the window; treatment mechanically shortens older tool results as the window fills and adds a stall detector plus command safeguards. Under context pressure the treatment raises mean per-task fail-to-pass fraction on all three benchmarks (exact paired sign tests p<0.0001) and nearly doubles complete solutions on Verified (43→72); the same frozen package transfers without retuning to Devstral, Nemotron and Qwen3.8. At a 262,144-token window the Verified and Pro advantages collapse to zero (F2PF difference -0.3 pp, 95% CI [-4.5, +3.9]). The conclusion for evaluators: the tested solver is the model AND the harness together.

What was actually compared: two configurations of one harness

This is not a cross-vendor bake-off. Both arms are the same harness (Yuj, github.com/sydches/yuj) differing by exactly one checked-in configuration package, so the estimate is the total effect of assigning the treatment condition as run, not the effect of any single rule.

  • Control: full chronological model-visible conversation; the run ends at the window limit even with an incomplete patch.
  • Treatment bundles three mechanisms: a 'half-life' working-view rule, a stall detector plus interventions, and command safeguards.
  • Half-life rule: shortening activates only after the estimated prompt reaches half the window; the newest 4 tool results stay full; as tool-result age doubles across tiers the character cap halves.
  • The stall detector reads fixed facts already in the execution record — no model call, no extra tokens.
  • Cross-harness sanity check (untreated Yuj vs mini-SWE-agent v2.2.8, GPT-5.5 via Codex CLI, 500 Verified tasks): 87.6% outcome agreement, Cohen's kappa 0.66 — even 'equivalent' harnesses disagree on roughly 1 task in 8.

Design, sample size and statistical treatment

Every selected task runs once under control and once under treatment, sharing task, model weights, context capacity, tool interface, serving setup, evaluator, harness code and protocol. Primary model: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B served by llama-server with four-bit Q4_K_XL weights, greedy decoding, network-disabled per-task containers, fixed 480-second attempt cap.

  • REPEATED RUNS: none. One greedy trajectory per arm per task, so the design measures the task-paired difference and explicitly does not measure run-to-run variance or pass@k.
  • Resolution tested with the exact two-sided McNemar test; F2PF direction with the exact two-sided sign test; paired-task bootstrap CIs; repository-cluster sensitivity via 20,000 whole-repository resamples.
  • Holm correction applied across six repository-level tests plus a separate pre-registered Devstral-Nemotron transfer family.
  • Author-flagged confound: campaigns were collected successively, so cross-window ordering does not isolate window size from run-era change.

Results: a large effect, but only under context pressure

The effect shrinks monotonically as the window widens: +21.1 pp F2PF at 20,480 tokens, +6.4 pp at 43,008, and -0.3 pp at 262,144, where fewer than 1% of tasks in any arm hit the context limit. FeatureBench is the exception, keeping a +6.8 pp gain even at the wide window.

  • Verified tight window: F2PF 28.0% → 49.1% (ratio 1.75; 42 tasks better under T vs 6 under C, 121 ties); solutions 43 → 72.
  • Pro (49,152): F2PF 15.2% → 32.7%; solutions 31 → 72.
  • After Holm correction, all three F2PF increases and the Pro resolution increase survive; the Verified resolution test (p=0.0625) and FeatureBench resolution test (p=1) do not.
  • Mechanism evidence: the fitted 'reading boundary' — the demand/window ratio at which half the runs stop before any source change — more than doubles under treatment on every benchmark (Verified 0.71 → 1.81, stretch 2.53x).
  • Shortening fires in 82.8% of Verified 20k runs, typically first at turn 10, removing a median 45% of characters.

Cross-model transfer: harness effect vs model effect

With the treatment package frozen after the Qwen3.6 study, the same 169-task cohort and 20,480-token window were rerun on three additional locally served four-bit checkpoints spanning different architectures. Treatment raised both endpoints for every model with no harness-code fork and no retuning.

  • Under control the four models span 12.0%-28.0% F2PF (a 16 pp spread from model choice), while flipping only the harness moves a single model by 6.4-21.1 pp.
  • Treated Nemotron (18.3%) beats untreated Devstral (17.2%) — the harness knob is of the same order as the model knob at this budget.
  • Devstral gains most proportionally: 2.1x the control F2PF and 2.4x the complete solutions.

Cost: the gains are NOT compute-matched

No dollar figures (all models are locally served open-weight four-bit checkpoints), but the resource table is unambiguous and is the most important caveat for a harness builder. Treatment used more model work in every pressure comparison, largely because control frequently died at the context limit while treatment kept going.

  • Verified: 3,280 → 6,517 model turns; 37.8M → 80.7M prompt tokens; 1.3 → 4.6 solver wall-clock hours.
  • Pro: 8,357 → 25,750 turns; 210.6M → 657.1M tokens; 5.4 → 21.8 hours. FeatureBench: 2.5 → 14.7 hours.
  • The only clean efficiency win is at the wide window: 7.2% fewer prompt tokens per turn (paired geometric-mean ratio 0.928, 95% CI [0.878, 0.979]) for statistically indistinguishable outcomes — free compression, not free accuracy.

Tuning probes: the frozen operating point was not beaten

An exploratory 15-task-per-model probe varied the age-tier character caps (depth) and the activation threshold / protected-result count (timing). No altered setting improved resolution beyond the observed rerun-to-rerun difference, and doubling all caps — a bigger, less-aggressive view — was the worst depth setting.

  • Frozen caps baseline on the probe: 6/15 then 7/15 solutions, 45.3% then 47.5% F2PF — that ~2 pp gap is the rerun noise floor.
  • All caps x0.5: 5/15, 45.0%. All caps x2.0: 4/15, 33.3%.
  • The operating point was frozen before the reported campaigns but was NOT chosen by a task-disjoint hyperparameter study.

Key findings

  • Changing only a checked-in harness configuration raised Verified complete solutions from 43 to 72 of 169 and mean per-task F2PF from 28.0% to 49.1% at a 20,480-token window (+21.1 pp, 95% CI [+14.1, +28.3]).
  • The harness effect is the same order as the model-choice effect at this budget: control models span 12.0%-28.0% F2PF while the harness flip moves one model by +6.4 to +21.1 pp; treated Nemotron outscores untreated Devstral.
  • It is specifically a context-pressure effect: +21.1 pp at 20,480 tokens, +6.4 pp at 43,008, -0.3 pp at 262,144 where fewer than 1% of tasks hit the limit.
  • The frozen treatment transferred to three architecturally different open-weight models with no retuning, raising both endpoints for all of them.
  • The gains are explicitly not compute-matched — 2.0-3.1x model turns, 2.1-3.1x prompt tokens and 3.5-5.9x wall time, mostly because control kept dying at the context limit.
  • One greedy trajectory per arm per task: no repeated runs anywhere in the main results, so no run-to-run variance is estimated.

Notable quotes

A model name alone does not identify the solver behind measured coding-agent performance.
Treatment used more model work in every comparison and more often remained active after control reached its context limit.
Each task contributes one greedy trajectory per arm, so this design does not measure run-to-run variation.
Less model work can mark an earlier stop rather than greater efficiency: under pressure, control often reached the context limit while treatment continued.

Limitations

  • No repeated runs — one greedy trajectory per arm per task, so run-to-run variance, pass@k and end-to-end determinism are all unmeasured, and the entire effect estimate rests on paired single trajectories.
  • Not compute-matched and not decomposable: the treatment's extra 2-3x tokens and 3.5-5.9x wall time are baked into the reported effect, and the three bundled mechanisms cannot be attributed individually.
  • Single-author work with a self-built harness whose control is a configuration of the author's own system — mitigated but not eliminated by the mini-SWE-agent agreement check — and the mechanical rule was never compared against model-written summaries or retrieval-based context views.

Open questions

  • What does the stall detector alone contribute, with the context policy and command safeguards held fixed?
  • How much of the effect is time rather than context — an experiment varying only the time budget, with repeated runs, would separate the two.
  • Does the result hold for hosted frontier models and full-precision weights, where raw capability may absorb the context pressure the treatment relieves?
