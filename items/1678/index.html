    An analysis of Uniswap markets

    Date: 2026-09-22

    Theme: CFMM mechanics and optimal routing
    These papers establish the mathematical foundation for automated-market-maker execution: constant-product pricing, fee-dependent no-arbitrage bands, manipulation and liquidity properties, followed by convex optimization for routing basket trades across heterogeneous pools and mixed-integer extensions for fixed execution costs.
    Source: arxiv
    Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03380

    A foundational formal treatment of constant-product and constant-mean AMM mechanics, showing how fees, reserves, and convex arbitrage determine price tracking and efficient routing across markets.

    Executive summary

    Angeris et al. formalize Uniswap’s constant-product mechanism, formulate optimal arbitrage as a convex problem, and derive a fee-dependent band within which arbitrage keeps its marginal price near an external reference price. They establish reserve, liquidity, manipulation-cost, and liquidity-provider-return properties, then extend much of the framework to multiasset constant-mean markets. An agent-based simulation using a deployed Uniswap contract supports the predicted price tracking across varied simulated conditions, while revealing weak liquidity-provider utility outside near-zero-drift markets.

    Constant-product mechanics

    The market holds two positive reserves whose product constrains feasible trades, with only a fraction γ of the input counted toward the invariant because the remaining fraction is charged as a fee. Its marginal no-fee price is the reserve ratio, so every trade changes both inventory and the quoted price without maintaining an order book.

    • With zero fees, trades preserve the reserve product exactly; with fees, the post-trade product increases.
    • The mechanism cannot be depleted solely by exchanging the two assets because total reserves retain a lower bound derived from the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality.
    • Greater reserves reduce marginal price impact, giving a formal sense in which deeper pools provide more liquidity.

    Convex arbitrage and price tracking

    Against an infinitely liquid reference market, the authors maximize the proceeds of buying from one venue and selling to the other subject to the AMM invariant. Eliminating one trade variable turns the problem into a convex optimization problem with an analytic optimum. Under no arbitrage, the AMM’s no-fee marginal price must lie between γ times and γ⁻¹ times the reference price, so higher fees widen the admissible discrepancy.

    • Convexity makes the global optimum tractable and extends to strategies involving several interacting constant-product markets.
    • Convex risk penalties can model failed execution, price impact, delay, or other costs while preserving tractability.
    • A quadratic risk penalty is later used for arbitrageurs in the simulation.

    Robustness, manipulation, and liquidity-provider economics

    The paper proves that the product constant is nondecreasing, that splitting sequential trades is worse than executing the aggregate trade at once when fees are positive, and that manipulation cost grows linearly with pool reserves. Small oracle distortions can nevertheless be comparatively cheap because the lower bound is quadratic in the desired deviation near zero. In the no-fee benchmark, a liquidity provider’s pool value follows the square root of the reference-price return rather than the underlying asset return itself.

    • The manipulation lower bound is proportional to reserve size and to min{ε², √ε} for a relative price displacement ε.
    • Oracle-dependent protocols should not react too sensitively to small instantaneous deviations.
    • Under geometric Brownian reference-price dynamics, the appendix identifies a volatility drag in the expected no-fee pool payoff.

    Constant-mean generalization

    The analysis generalizes from two reserves with a constant product to n reserves whose weighted geometric mean is invariant. The corresponding multiasset arbitrage program has an equivalent convex inequality relaxation because the weighted geometric mean is concave and the invariant constraint is tight at an optimum. Nondecreasing invariants, no depletion, greater liquidity with greater reserves, and convex risk extensions largely carry over.

    • Constant-product markets are the two-asset, equal-weight special case.
    • The optimization jointly represents external purchases and pairwise trades among pool assets.
    • The paper does not obtain a general closed-form necessary-and-sufficient no-arbitrage condition for constant-mean markets.

    Agent-based validation

    The authors implement a simulated Ethereum environment around Uniswap contract commit c10c08d and combine it with a stochastic reference market. Agents include quadratic-risk arbitrageurs, Markowitz-optimizing liquidity providers implemented with CVXPY and ECOS, and traders who transact only within a price-tolerance rule. Simulated AMM prices generally remain within the theoretical bounds under noise and drift when trades are not large, although discrete action ordering can create temporary violations.

    • The simulations use Uniswap’s γ = 0.997 input multiplier.
    • Initial liquidity providers have negative utility in almost all reported simulated conditions and positive expected utility only when mean market return is near zero.
    • The exercise supports the analytical mechanism under modeled conditions but is not an empirical study of live on-chain order flow.

    Key findings

    • Optimal arbitrage against a constant-product AMM can be expressed as a convex optimization problem with an analytic solution in the basic two-asset case.
    • No-arbitrage confines the AMM’s no-fee marginal price to a multiplicative band determined by the fee parameter; larger fees permit wider deviations from the reference price.
    • Pool depth simultaneously lowers price impact and raises the cost of sustained price manipulation, although small distortions remain relatively inexpensive.
    • Most structural results extend to weighted, multiasset constant-mean markets through convex optimization.
    • Agent-based simulations reproduce the predicted tracking behavior across varied modeled conditions, while initial liquidity-provider utility is usually negative outside near-zero market drift.

    Notable quotes

    In this section, we derive bounds on the marginal price of the market relative to a reference market and show that this market maker has other desirable properties.
    In the current simulation, we have two possible markets for agents to trade with: one is given by the Uniswap contract and the other is given by a simple stochastic market model.
    The results of our simulations suggest that the theoretical results derived above hold in practice under a wide variety of market conditions.
    Additionally, we suspect that there is an even larger class of automated market maker mechanisms which satisfy the above properties, and it would be interesting to further explore its mathematical properties.

    Limitations

    • The sharpest tracking and manipulation results rely on no-arbitrage and an infinitely liquid external reference market; actual latency, market impact, and failed execution weaken those assumptions.
    • The simulation uses a simplified stochastic reference market, stylized agents, discrete action ordering, and one Uniswap contract snapshot rather than empirical mainnet order flow or strategic adversaries.
    • The liquidity-provider return formula is derived for the no-fee case, and the paper describes the constants in its manipulation bound as very loose and mainly theoretical.

    Open questions

    • Which broader class of AMM invariants preserves convex or log-log-convex arbitrage, useful price bounds, and efficient multi-pool routing?
    • Can necessary-and-sufficient closed-form no-arbitrage conditions be derived for general multiasset constant-mean markets?
    • How do gas costs, latency, front-running, transaction ordering, finite external liquidity, and empirical order flow change the theoretical tracking and manipulation guarantees?
